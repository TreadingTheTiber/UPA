*Note on this entry: this is the collection's earliest specimen of the anti-UPA argument deployed by a* ***Protestant*** *controversialist — and of a Catholic theologian answering it as such. George Turnbull of Tranent (c. 1568/69–1633), a Scottish Jesuit and professor of theology at the Brûlart College of Reims, spent his last decade in a running duel with Robert Baron (c. 1596–1639) of Marischal College, Aberdeen — the most formidable of the "Aberdeen Doctors." Baron's doctoral disputation on the formal object of faith (1627) charged Catholic theology with a vicious circle (Scripture believed for the Church, the Church for Scripture); Turnbull answered with the* Imaginarii Circuli Quadratura Catholica *("The Catholic Squaring of the Imaginary Circle," Reims 1628); Baron counter-replied with the massive* Apodixis Catholica *(1631); and Turnbull closed the exchange with the present* Sententia Iuris *(Reims 1632), arraigning Baron as a "slanderer" before a mock court. In the* Apodixis *(Tract. V, pp. 230–237) Baron attacks the thesis that the Church's supreme definition is the infallible Word of God with five uncertainty-arguments, and the fourth is the anti-UPA argument entire: even granting in general that a true pope defines infallibly, "it is not established in particular" that Martin V, Eugene IV, Leo X, or Pius IV — the popes who confirmed Constance, Basel–Ferrara–Florence, Lateran V, and Trent — "were true Pontiffs and successors of Peter," since no one can have the certitude of faith that they were bishops, priests, or even baptized (the intention of the minister being hidden, as Bellarmine himself grants for the sacraments); therefore Catholics have no certitude of faith about the authority of Trent itself. The argument is thus, in its cradle, a weapon* ***against*** *the councils' authority — Baron runs precisely the contrapositive of the inference that Murga (1684, n. 84) would later run forward: both agree that de fide certainty about Trent requires de fide certainty that Pius IV was true pope; Baron concludes "therefore no certainty about Trent," Murga "therefore it is of faith that Pius IV was pope, since the contrary is well-nigh heretical." Turnbull's response (Chapter 8, n. 6) concedes that "not all Catholics respond in the same manner" to the singular question — whether it is established by Catholic faith "that this man by number — for example, Urban VIII — is the true Pontiff before God" — names the negative party (Cajetan, Bañez, Torquemada) and the affirmative (John Azor, Suárez), and sides with the affirmers: "or,* ***better****, of those who affirm it." On that path, he says, "this knot is none at all." He adds that he has treated the question fully in another of his books, "On the Spiritual Kingdom of the Christian Church" — probable Latin title* De Regno Spirituali Ecclesiae Christianae*, a work of which no copy or printed record has been located: the standard bibliographies (De Backer–Sommervogel; DNB) know only the two printed replies to Baron and one unpublished manuscript, the* Commentarii in Universam Theologiam*, "ready for the press when the author died" (Reims, 11 May 1633), of which the* De Regno *may have formed part — and then supplies a fallback that defeats Baron even on the negative path: the assistance of the Holy Spirit is promised "not to the person of the definer, but to the office and the see from which he speaks," so that God could use even the ministry of a putative pope occupying the true see to confirm the faithful — as he used Caiaphas to prophesy, the ass to instruct Balaam, and the Pythoness to warn Saul.*

*The structure of the reply deserves particular notice, because it is the very disjunction by which this repository defines the doctrine (see the root README): UPA* simpliciter*, the genus, divides into the* ***fact-guarantee*** *(School 1) and the* ***effect-guarantee*** *(School 2), and no school in the tradition stops short of the genus. Turnbull's answer is built on exactly that fork, and ranked exactly that way. He* ***affirms the fact-guarantee*** *as the better opinion ("or,* ***better****, of those who affirm it… in the path of Suárez and of the others who there affirm, this knot is none at all"), and then* ***hedges with the effect-guarantee*** arguendo*: even if the singular title were only morally certain, the infallible assistance attaches to the office and the see, not the person, so that a putative pope occupying the true see cannot be used to bind the Church to error — the doctrinal-providence mechanism of School 2, deployed twelve years after Laymann (1620) formulated its juridical twin. On either arm of the fork the Protestant's conclusion — no certitude of faith about Trent — fails: "whichever opinion on that matter we follow… the present knot is an easy one." A Catholic controversialist answering a Protestant in 1632 thus treats fact-or-effect as the exhaustive Catholic alternative — a Church that could be harmed by adhering to a false claimant (School 3) appears on neither arm — which is precisely the census this collection documents. Classification is* ***1*** *(fact-guarantee affirmed as the better opinion; the note is not determined in this excerpt), with the School-2 hedge recorded here: he agrees with the fact-guarantee while hedging to the effect-guarantee, never below it.*

## Part Two, Chapter 8. On behalf of the same truth, against another line of objections.

**1.** In a second way of assailing it, the adversary objects that our Catholic assertion labors under a varied and manifold uncertainty. [...] **Fourth, because, although it were established in general that the judgment of a true Pope is the supreme definition of the Church, yet in particular we do not know whether this or that individual man is the true Pope. Indeed, it is not established with the certitude of divine faith that he is truly baptized; for the validity of Baptism depends upon the intention of the one baptizing, which is hidden from us.** [...]

**6.** In the fourth point alone is touched a common difficulty, to which not all Catholics respond in the same manner: **whether it is established by Catholic faith, not only in general, that he who is canonically elected is the true Pontiff, but also in the individual case, that this man by number — for example, Urban VIII — is the true Pontiff before God.** But because the author of the Squaring treats and resolves this question diligently enough in another of his books, which he entitles *On the Spiritual Kingdom of the Christian Church*, it will be enough in this place to show that, whichever opinion on that matter we follow — whether of those who deny it with Cajetan, Bañez, and Torquemada, **or, better, of those who affirm it with others, among whom of our own men John Azor and Suárez follow** — the present knot is an easy one. **And indeed, in the path of Suárez and of the others who there affirm, this knot is none at all. For if by the same faith by which the authority of a true Pope is believed in general, there be believed also in particular the truth of this individual Pope, no uncertainty remains.** But in the path of the others, who distinguish a twofold certitude in this matter — one altogether of divine faith, by which the authority of a true Pontiff is believed in general; the other a moral certitude of human faith, by which the truth of this Pontiff by number is believed [...] — the whole difficulty is easily resolved by those who advert that **the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit promised to the supreme Judge and definer of matters of faith is not promised or bound to the person of the definer, but to the office and the see from which he speaks.** But the see is always the same rule and matter of divine faith among all Catholics, granted that it happen to be occupied for a short time by one having a hidden impediment to legitimate possession. For an impediment of this kind does not bind the omnipotence of God, so as to keep him from using the ministry of a putative Pope occupying the true see to confirm the faithful — just as of old he used the ministry of Caiaphas the Pontiff, although unbelieving in Christ, to prophesy concerning the necessary death of one man, lest the whole nation should perish; and the ministry of the ass to instruct Balaam; and the ministry of the Pythoness to foretell death to King Saul.

## Appendix — Baron's objection in his own words (*Apodixis Catholica*, 1631, Tract. V, pp. 236–237)

> Quartò, quamvis in genere constaret Papæ ex Cathedra docentis sententiam esse supremam Ecclesiæ definitionem, **non tamen constat in particulari sententias Martini V. aut Eugenii IV. aut Leonis X. aut Pii IV. Pontificum Romanorum [...] esse pronunciatas ab iis, qui erant veri Pontifices, & Petri Successores. Vnde necessariò sequitur nos plenam & firmam fidei certitudinem de authoritate Constantiensis, Basileensis, Lateranensis, & Tridentini Conciliorum non habere.** Quis enim mortalium omnium certus est aut esse potest Martinum V. Eugen. IV. Leonem X. & Pium IV. verè Episcopos, immò vel Sacerdotes, vel baptizatos fuisse [...]?

> "Fourthly: although it were established in general that the judgment of a Pope teaching from the Chair is the supreme definition of the Church, **yet it is not established in particular that the judgments of Martin V, or Eugene IV, or Leo X, or Pius IV [...] were pronounced by men who were true Pontiffs and successors of Peter. Whence it necessarily follows that we do not have a full and firm certitude of faith concerning the authority of the Councils of Constance, Basel, the Lateran, and Trent.** For who of all mortals is certain, or can be certain, that Martin V, Eugene IV, Leo X, and Pius IV were truly Bishops — nay, that they were even Priests, or baptized [...]?"
