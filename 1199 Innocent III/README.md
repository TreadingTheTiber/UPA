*Note on this entry: Innocent III preached this sermon on the first anniversary of his episcopal consecration. He was consecrated on 22 February 1198, the feast of the Chair of St. Peter, and the sermon says the anniversary is being kept "for the first time" — which places it in February 1199 (a dating inferred from the text itself). Its theme is John 3:29, "He that has the bride is the bridegroom": the pope is the bridegroom of the Roman Church, and the sermon works through the three goods of marriage — fidelity, offspring, sacrament. It enters this collection as a* ***witness (W)***, *for three things.* ***(1) The sheep and the stranger.*** *Under* fides *Innocent applies John 10 to pope and Roman Church: they "have always kept such fidelity to one another" that the sheep "do not follow a stranger but flee… the strangers are heretics and schismatics, whom the Roman Church does not follow, but pursues and puts to flight.* ***But their own they know and hear — not an apostate, but an apostolic one; not a Cathar, but a catholic one.***" *The Church's hearing of her own shepherd and her flight from the stranger are stated as a single fact — the fathers' recognition-idiom, here on a pope's own lips.* ***(2) Election makes the pope.*** *"The Roman pontiff, when he is elected, is confirmed"; the elect "as true pope obtains the authority of ruling the Roman Church" (the election decree of Nicholas II, 1059: Gratian, D. 23 c. 1); and "consent alone between legitimate persons makes a marriage… by reason of the mutual consent of the electors and the elected he is assuredly made their bridegroom."* ***(3) The hypothesis every later disputant quotes.*** *Under* sacramentum *comes this: the bond is dissolved only by death, the pontiff "does not withdraw, is not deposed" — yet "for the cause of fornication the Roman Church could dismiss the Roman pontiff… that is, on account of the error of unbelief," followed at once by Innocent's own rider: "I, however, would not readily believe that God would permit the Roman pontiff to err against the faith."* *Three cautions fix the classification. The John 10 passage is a homiletic accommodation ("may fittingly be applied"), not a thesis; it speaks of the* Roman *Church, the pope's own bride, and not in terms of the universal Church; and it asserts a fidelity always kept in fact, without formulating a guarantee that the Church's acceptance proves the man. The sentence about strangers is sometimes quoted alone, to argue that a heretic can never truly be accepted; the selection is therefore given continuously, so that its opening ("such fidelity have the Roman pontiff and the Roman Church always kept to one another") and its close ("but their own they know and hear") can be read with it — together with the fornication-hypothesis and its rider, which follow in the same column. The Latin is transcribed from the page images of Migne's Patrologia Latina 217; Migne's evident misprints are kept in the Latin and flagged in the translation.*

## The spiritual marriage: election, confirmation, consecration (col. 663)

[...] So too the spiritual marriage, which is between a bishop and his Church, is said to be initiated in the election, ratified in the confirmation, consummated in the consecration. But that marriage which I, the Bridegroom, contracted with this my bride was at once initiated and ratified: **for the Roman pontiff, when he is elected, is confirmed, and when he is confirmed, is elected.** Do you not recall what you have read of him in the canon? — **that the one elected, as true pope, obtains the authority of ruling the Roman Church** and of disposing of all her resources. [...]

## The first good, fidelity: the sheep and the stranger (coll. 663–664)

Now there are principally three goods of marriage: fidelity, offspring, and sacrament. Fidelity refers to chastity, offspring to fruitfulness, sacrament to stability. **For the Roman pontiff and the Roman Church have always kept such fidelity to one another that what the Truth says in the Gospel may fittingly be applied to them: "I know my sheep, and mine know me" (John 10): they do not follow a stranger but flee, because they do not know the voice of strangers. Now the strangers are heretics and schismatics, whom the Roman Church does not follow, but pursues and puts to flight. But their own they know and hear — not an apostate, but an apostolic one; not a Cathar, but a catholic one** — [she] receiving and rendering the conjugal debt: receiving from him the debt of providence, and rendering the debt of reverence. [...]

Further, since **the Roman Church pays the debt of reverence to no one at all except the Roman pontiff, who after God has no other superior**: how is it that the Roman pontiff is bound to render the debt of providence not only to the Roman Church, but to all the Churches altogether? [...] So too the Roman pontiff has the Roman Church as his bride, who nevertheless brings in to him the Churches subject to her, that they may receive from him the debt of providence. [...]

## The third good, the sacrament: indissoluble, save for spiritual fornication (coll. 664–665)

But the sacrament between the Roman pontiff and the Roman Church endures so firm and stable that they are never separated from one another except by death [...] But this husband, bound to a wife, does not seek to be loosed, **does not withdraw, is not deposed**; for "to his own lord he stands or falls" (Rom. 14). — "But he that judges is the Lord" (1 Cor. 4). **Yet for the cause of fornication the Roman Church could dismiss the Roman pontiff. I do not mean carnal fornication, but spiritual** — because the marriage is not carnal but spiritual — **that is, on account of the error of unbelief; for "he that does not believe is already judged"** (John 3): and in this article is understood what is read in the Gospel which you have heard: "You are the salt of the earth; but if the salt lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted?" (Matt. 5). **I, however, would not readily believe that God would permit the Roman pontiff to err against the faith, for whom he prayed in a spiritual manner in Peter**: "I," he says, "have prayed for thee, Peter," etc. (Luke 22). Therefore *he that has the bride is the bridegroom.*

## Consent makes the marriage (coll. 665–666)

[...] She sought one who knew nothing of it; she took one who resisted — gainsaying at the beginning, but consenting at the end; **because consent alone between legitimate persons makes a marriage.** [...] But when he is received by election, **by reason of the mutual consent of the electors and the elected he is assuredly made their bridegroom, especially when the election is confirmed.** Yet before he is consecrated he will claim neither the name of pontiff nor the office. But whether this is so, let our care inquire. [...]
