| **SERMO III. IN CONSECRATIONE PONTIFICIS.** *De quatuor speciebus desponsationum, et præconiis Romanæ Ecclesiæ: de Spirituali conjugio episcopi cum Ecclesia sua, et bonis conjugii.* — *Qui habet sponsam, sponsus est. Amicus autem sponsi, qui stat et audit eum, gaudio gaudet propter vocem sponsi* (Joan. III). [...] | | **SERMON III. ON THE CONSECRATION OF THE PONTIFF.** *On the four kinds of espousals, and the praises of the Roman Church; on the spiritual marriage of a bishop with his Church, and the goods of marriage.* — *He that has the bride is the bridegroom. But the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice* (John 3). [...] |
| **[col. 663]** [...] Solet dici carnale conjugium, quod est inter virum et feminam, initiatum, ratum, et consummatum. Initiatum in desponsatione, ratum in consensu, consummatum in copula. Sic et spirituale conjugium, quod est inter episcopum et Ecclesiam, initiatum dicitur in electione, ratum in confirmatione, consummatum in consecratione. Illud autem conjugium, quod ego Sponsus cum hac mea sponsa contraxi, simul fuit initiatum et ratum: quia Romanus pontifex cum eligitur, confirmatur, et cum confirmatur, eligitur. Nonne recolitis quod de ipso legistis in canone? quoniam electus, sicut papa verus, auctoritatem obtinet regendi Romanam Ecclesiam, et disponendi omnes facultates illius. Certe cum ego contraherem, filius ducebat matrem in conjugem: ubi vero contraxi, pater habuit filiam in uxorem. In carnali conjugio excluduntur propinqui, et admittuntur extranei; sed in spirituali conjugio prima facie regulariter excluduntur extranei, et admittuntur propinqui. De propinquis excludentis in carnali conjugio, legistis in canone cautum: « Omnes affinitate propinquos ad conjugalem copulam accedere denegamus. » De extraneis autem a spirituali conjugio excludentis canonica tradit auctoritas, ut sit facultas clericis remittendi, si se viderint prægravari, et quos sibi ingeri ex adverso contigerit, non timeant refutare. Propterea reperitur cautum in canone, ut in apostolatus culmine de carnalibus presbyteris aut diaconis nemo consecretur. | | **[col. 663]** [...] The carnal marriage, which is between man and woman, is commonly said to be initiated, ratified, and consummated: initiated in the betrothal, ratified in the consent, consummated in the union. So too the spiritual marriage, which is between a bishop and his Church, is said to be initiated in the election, ratified in the confirmation, consummated in the consecration. But that marriage which I, the Bridegroom, contracted with this my bride was at once initiated and ratified: for the Roman pontiff, when he is elected, is confirmed, and when he is confirmed, is elected. Do you not recall what you have read of him in the canon? — that the one elected, as true pope, obtains the authority of ruling the Roman Church and of disposing of all her resources. Certainly, when I was contracting, a son was taking his mother to wife; but once I had contracted, a father had his daughter as wife. In carnal marriage kin are excluded and outsiders admitted; but in spiritual marriage, at first sight and as a rule, outsiders are excluded and kin admitted. Concerning the exclusion of kin in carnal marriage, you have read it provided in the canon: "We forbid all who are near by affinity to approach the conjugal union." Concerning the exclusion of outsiders from spiritual marriage, canonical authority hands down that clerics are to have the power of resisting [the print has *remittendi*; the canon reads *renitendi*] if they see themselves overburdened, and are not to fear to refuse those who happen to be thrust upon them from outside. Hence it is found provided in the canon that at the summit of the apostolate no one be consecrated [unless] from the cardinal [the print has *carnalibus*] priests or deacons. |
| Anniversarium ergo diem, quo fuit hoc conjugium spirituale consummatum, hodie mecum primum celebratis, licet ipso die fuerim in sede apostolica consecratus, quo beatus Petrus apostolus in episcopali fuit cathedra constitutus. Sed sicut lux solis, lucem stellæ secum videri non patitur: sic illa solemnitas hanc secum non sustinet celebrari. Cedit ergo minor majori, quia minor majori succedit. Ego igitur conjugium contraxi, consecratione nuptias celebravi. | | You therefore celebrate with me today, for the first time, the anniversary of the day on which this spiritual marriage was consummated — although I was consecrated in the Apostolic See on the very day on which blessed Peter the Apostle was established in the episcopal chair. But as the light of the sun does not allow the light of a star to be seen together with it, so that solemnity does not suffer this one to be celebrated together with it. The lesser therefore yields to the greater, because the lesser follows after the greater. I, then, contracted the marriage; by the consecration I celebrated the nuptials. |
| Tria vero principaliter sunt bona conjugii, fides, proles, et sacramentum. Fides ad castitatem, proles ad fecunditatem, sacramentum ad stabilitatem refertur. Tantam enim fidem Romanus pontifex et Romana Ecclesia sibi semper invicem servaverunt, ut eis congrue valeat coaptari, quod Veritas inquit in Evangelio: « Cognosco oves meas, et cognoscunt me meæ (Joan. X): » alienum non sequuntur sed fugiunt, quia non noverunt vocem alienorum. Alieni autem sunt hæretici et schismatici, quos Ecclesia Romana non sequitur, sed persequitur et fugat. Suum autem cognoscunt et audiunt, non apostaticum, sed apostolicum: non Catarum, sed catholicum, recipiens et reddens debitum conjugale, recipiens ab eo debitum providentiæ, et reddens debitum reverentiæ. Quia « vir non habet potestatem sui corporis, sed mulier. Similiter mulier non habet potestatem sui corporis, sed vir (I Cor. **[col. 664]** VII). » | | Now there are principally three goods of marriage: fidelity, offspring, and sacrament. Fidelity refers to chastity, offspring to fruitfulness, sacrament to stability. For the Roman pontiff and the Roman Church have always kept such fidelity to one another that what the Truth says in the Gospel may fittingly be applied to them: "I know my sheep, and mine know me" (John 10): they do not follow a stranger but flee, because they do not know the voice of strangers. Now the strangers are heretics and schismatics, whom the Roman Church does not follow, but pursues and puts to flight. But their own they know and hear — not an apostate, but an apostolic one; not a Cathar, but a catholic one — [she] receiving and rendering the conjugal debt: receiving from him the debt of providence, and rendering the debt of reverence. For "the husband has not power over his own body, but the wife; likewise the wife has not power over her own body, but the husband" (1 Cor. **[col. 664]** 7). |
| Porro, cum Ecclesia Romana debitum reverentiæ nulli prorsus impendat, nisi Romano pontifici, qui post Deum alium superiorem non habet: quid est hoc, quod Romanus pontifex debitum providentiæ non utique tantum Romanæ Ecclesiæ, sed omnibus omnino tenetur Ecclesiis exhibere? « Sapientibus et insipientibus debitor sum (Rom. I), » inquit Apostolus; et: « Instantia mea quotidiana sollicitudo omnium Ecclesiarum (II Cor. XI). » Quid judicatur ad paria, ut secundum quod legitur in Veteri testamento, unus posset habere plures? Nonne legistis quod Abraham Saram habebat uxorem, quæ tamen Agar famulam suam introduxit ad ipsum: nec commisit propter hoc adulterium, sed officium adimplevit (Gen. XVI). Sic et Romanus pontifex sponsam habet Romanam Ecclesiam, quæ tamen Ecclesias sibi subjectas introducit ad ipsum, ut ab eo recipiant debitum providentiæ: quia quanto plus redditur, tanto magis debetur. Sed nunc fit in spiritu, quod tunc fiebat in carne; quia « Spiritus est qui vivificat, caro non prodest quidquam (Joan. VI). » At nonne potest unus episcopatus habere duos episcopos, et unus episcopus duos episcopatus habere? Ne longe exempla petantur, unus et idem est Hostiensis et Vellucensis [f. Vercellensis] episcopus, ut utraque simul Ecclesia nupsit eidem. Rursus Hipponensis Ecclesia, quæ conjuncta erat Valerio, ipso vivente etiam nupsit beato Augustino: qui non tam successit, quam accessit Valerio. Sed qua ratione possunt hæc fieri salva lege conjugii, vos exquirite, quos delectat inquisitio quæstionum: me alia sollicitudo detinet occupatum. | | Further, since the Roman Church pays the debt of reverence to no one at all except the Roman pontiff, who after God has no other superior: how is it that the Roman pontiff is bound to render the debt of providence not only to the Roman Church, but to all the Churches altogether? "To the wise and to the unwise I am a debtor" (Rom. 1), says the Apostle; and: "My daily pressing care, the solicitude for all the Churches" (2 Cor. 11). Is it to be judged a like case, that, as is read in the Old Testament, one man could have several [wives]? Have you not read that Abraham had Sarah to wife, who nevertheless brought in her handmaid Hagar to him; nor did he on that account commit adultery, but fulfilled a duty (Gen. 16). So too the Roman pontiff has the Roman Church as his bride, who nevertheless brings in to him the Churches subject to her, that they may receive from him the debt of providence: for the more is rendered, the more is owed. But now that is done in the spirit which then was done in the flesh; for "it is the Spirit that gives life, the flesh profits nothing" (John 6). But cannot one bishopric have two bishops, and one bishop have two bishoprics? Not to seek examples far off: one and the same man is bishop of Ostia and of Velletri [the print has *Vellucensis*, with Migne's conjecture *Vercellensis*], so that both Churches at once are wedded to the same man. Again, the Church of Hippo, which was joined to Valerius, while he was still living was wedded also to blessed Augustine, who did not so much succeed Valerius as accede to him. But by what reasoning these things can be done with the law of marriage preserved, do you inquire, whom the investigation of questions delights: another care keeps me occupied. |
| Contra hoc conjugium inter episcopum et Ecclesiam, ut religiosam prolem Christo generet, quatenus uxor illius sicut viris abundans in lateribus domus suæ. « Filii sui sicut novellæ olivarum, » etc. (Psal. CXXVII.) Propter quod dicit Apostolus: « Filioli mei, quos iterum parturio, donec formetur Christus in vobis (Galat. IV). » Et Lia, datis quondam mandragoris, « conduxit Jacob, ut ad illam intraret, quæ concepit et peperit (Gen. XXX). » Eos autem quo Christus regenerat, doctrinis instruit salutaribus, et monitis informat honestis, « cibat eos pane vitæ et intellectus, et potat eos aqua sapientiæ salutaris (Eccli. XV.) » — « Venite, inquit, et comedite panem meum, et bibite vinum meum, quod miscui vobis (Prov. IX). » Panem cœlestem et calicem salutaris, de quo si quis gustaverit, vivet in æternum: « Omne delectamentum in se habentem et omnis saporis suavitatem (Sap. XXI). » | | This marriage between bishop and Church is contracted [the print has *Contra hoc conjugium*] that it may bear religious offspring to Christ, so that his wife may be "as a fruitful vine [the print has *viris*] on the sides of his house. His children as olive plants," etc. (Ps. 127). For which reason the Apostle says: "My little children, of whom I am in labour again, until Christ be formed in you" (Gal. 4). And Leah, having once given the mandrakes, "hired Jacob, that he should go in unto her, and she conceived and bore" (Gen. 30). And those whom Christ regenerates he instructs with saving doctrines and forms with honourable admonitions; he "feeds them with the bread of life and understanding, and gives them the water of saving wisdom to drink" (Ecclus. 15). — "Come," he says, "eat my bread, and drink the wine which I have mingled for you" (Prov. 9): the heavenly bread and the chalice of salvation, of which if any man taste he shall live for ever, "having in it all that is delicious, and the sweetness of every taste" (Wis. 16 [the print has *Sap. XXI*]). |
| Sacramentum autem inter Romanum pontificem et Romanam ecclesiam tam firmum et stabile perseverat, ut non nisi per mortem unquam ab invicem separentur; quia mortuo viro mulier, secundum Apostolum, « soluta est a lege viri (Rom. VII). » Vir autem iste alligatus uxori, solutionem non quærit, non cedit, non deponitur; nam « suo domino aut stat, aut cadit (Rom. XIV). » — « Qui autem judicat, Dominus est (I Cor. IV). » Propter **[col. 665]** causam vero fornicationis Ecclesia Romana posset dimittere Romanum pontificem. Fornicationem non dico carnalem, sed spiritualem; quia non est carnale, sed spirituale conjugium, id est propter infidelitatis errorem; quoniam « qui non credit, jam judicatus est (Joan. III): » et in hoc articulo intelligitur, quod legitur in Evangelio, quod audistis: « Vos estis sal terræ, quod si sal evanuerit in quo salietur? (Matth. V.) » Ego tamen facile non crediderim, ut Deus permitteret Romanum pontificem contra fidem errare: pro quo spiritualiter oravit in Petro: « Ego, inquit, pro te rogavi, Petre, etc. (Luc. XXII), » Ergo *qui habet sponsam, sponsus est.* | | But the sacrament between the Roman pontiff and the Roman Church endures so firm and stable that they are never separated from one another except by death; because, the husband being dead, the woman, according to the Apostle, "is loosed from the law of her husband" (Rom. 7). But this husband, bound to a wife, does not seek to be loosed, does not withdraw [*non cedit*], is not deposed; for "to his own lord he stands or falls" (Rom. 14). — "But he that judges is the Lord" (1 Cor. 4). Yet for **[col. 665]** the cause of fornication the Roman Church could dismiss the Roman pontiff. I do not mean carnal fornication, but spiritual — because the marriage is not carnal but spiritual — that is, on account of the error of unbelief; for "he that does not believe is already judged" (John 3): and in this article is understood what is read in the Gospel which you have heard: "You are the salt of the earth; but if the salt lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted?" (Matt. 5). I, however, would not readily believe that God would permit the Roman pontiff to err against the faith, for whom he prayed in a spiritual manner [*spiritualiter*] in Peter: "I," he says, "have prayed for thee, Peter," etc. (Luke 22). Therefore *he that has the bride is the bridegroom.* |
| Hæc autem sponsa non nupsit vacua, sed dotem mihi tribuit absque pretio pretiosam, spiritualium videlicet plenitudinem et latitudinem temporalium, magnitudinem et multitudinem utrorumque. Nam cæteri vocati sunt in partem sollicitudinis, solus autem Petrus assumptus est in plenitudinem potestatis. In signum spiritualium contulit mihi mitram, in signum temporalium dedit mihi coronam; mitram pro sacerdotio, coronam pro regno, illius me constituens vicarium, qui habet in vestimento et in femore suo scriptum « Rex regum et Dominus dominantium (Apoc. XIX): sacerdos in æternum, secundum ordinem Melchisedech (Psal. CIX). » Amplam mihi tribuit dotem, sed utrum ego donationem aliquam sibi fecerim propter nuptias, vos videritis. Ego nolo asseverare jactanter. Ignorantem quæsivit, renitentem accepit; sed contradicentem in principio, sed consentientem in fine; quia solus consensus inter legitimas personas efficit matrimonium. | | Now this bride did not wed empty-handed, but bestowed on me a dowry precious beyond price: namely the fullness of spiritual things and the breadth of temporal things, the greatness and the multitude of both. For the others were called to a share of the solicitude, but Peter alone was taken up into the fullness of power. As a sign of spiritual things she conferred on me the mitre; as a sign of temporal things she gave me the crown: the mitre for the priesthood, the crown for the kingdom — constituting me the vicar of him who has written on his garment and on his thigh "King of kings and Lord of lords" (Apoc. 19), "a priest for ever according to the order of Melchisedech" (Ps. 109). She bestowed on me an ample dowry; but whether I have made her any gift on account of the nuptials, you shall see. I do not wish to assert it boastfully. She sought one who knew nothing of it; she took one who resisted — gainsaying at the beginning, but consenting at the end; because consent alone between legitimate persons makes a marriage. |
| Inde videtur contingere, quod licet mirabile videatur, quod aliquis possit esse alicujus Ecclesiæ, antequam sit sponsus ipsius: **[col. 666]** sicut aliquis posset esse alicujus Ecclesiæ sponsus, antequam pontifex esset illius. Cum enim per provisionem majoris injuste retinentibus juste datur episcopus, antequam in ipsum consentiant, utique pontifex est eorum propter auctoritatem concessionis. Sed videtur fortasse, quod nondum sit sponsus eorum propter defectum consensus, unde consentire teneatur, et cum eo conjugale fœdus inire. Cum autem per electionem accipitur, propter mutuum consensum eligentium et electi, utique sponsus eorum efficitur, præsertim cum electio confirmatur. Sed antequam consecretur, nec nomen pontificis, officium vindicabit. Sed an ita sit, sollicitudo nostra disquirat. [...] | | Hence it seems to come about — though it may seem strange — that someone can be [pontiff] of a Church before he is its bridegroom, **[col. 666]** just as someone could be the bridegroom of a Church before he was its pontiff. For when, by the provision of a superior, a bishop is justly given to those unjustly resisting [the print has *retinentibus*], before they consent to him, he is assuredly their pontiff by the authority of the grant. But it seems perhaps that he is not yet their bridegroom, for want of consent — to which they are bound to consent [the print has *teneatur*], and to enter the conjugal covenant with him. But when he is received by election, by reason of the mutual consent of the electors and the elected he is assuredly made their bridegroom, especially when the election is confirmed. Yet before he is consecrated he will claim neither the name of pontiff nor the office. But whether this is so, let our care inquire. [...] |
