| **DISPVTATIO XXVIII.** | | **DISPUTATION XXVIII.** |
| *De iis, quæ prærequiruntur ad actum fidei.* | | *On those things which are prerequired for the act of faith.* |
| POstquam declarata est natura fidei, consultum est,vt ostendatur,quænam sunt necessaria , vt aliquis eliciat actum ipsius , loquor autem de iis , quæ particulariter requiruntur ad hoc:nam de gratiæ necessitate, quæ non magis ad actum fidei , quàm ad alios actus supernaturales requiritur;& de vsu rationis,sine quo nullũs actus moralis elici possit, non est facienda hic particularis mentio.De duobus ergo principaliter in disputatione agendum est. *Primò*, de propositione mysteriorum credendorum : *Secundò* de voluntate, seu volitione credendi,quæ communiter apud Theologos vocatur pius credulitatis affectus. | | After the nature of faith has been declared, it is fitting that it be shown what things are necessary in order that someone may elicit an act of it. I speak, moreover, of those things which are particularly required for this: for of the necessity of grace, which is not more required for the act of faith than for other supernatural acts, and of the use of reason, without which no moral act can be elicited, no particular mention is to be made here. Therefore, two things principally are to be treated in this disputation. *First*, of the proposal of the mysteries to be believed; *Second*, of the will, or volition, of believing, which is commonly called among Theologians the pious affection of credulity. |

| *Quæritur* ergo de authoritate tali,ratione cuius,quis teneatur credere habenti illam , quando proponit res fidei. | | *It is asked*, therefore, concerning such authority, by reason of which someone would be bound to believe the one having it, when he proposes the things of faith. |

Nor do I believe that every act of faith must have such certitude of adhesion, but only those which are proposed in a morally infallible manner. *To the confirmation* I respond by conceding the consequence, speaking of a proposal which is made only by a particular person, and which is not known to be common to the Church, except through her authority. |

| **CONCLVSIO V.** | | **CONCLUSION V.** |
| **6.** {marg. Non sufficit ipsa scriptura pro regula.} *NOn sufficit ipsamet scriptura pro regula infallibili ad cognoscenda mysteria fidei, etiam ipsa, quæ reuera continentur in scriptura sic , quin,ea non obstante,rationabiliter posset quis dubitare de multis, nisi haberet aliam regulam.* Hæc est communissima apud Catholicos omnes contra hæreticos. | | **6.** {marg. | **9.** *Obiicies primò* : Sequeretur scripturam esse diminutam , si non contineret satis expressè , & clarè omnia necessaria : sed hoc videtur absurdum. | | **9.** *You will object first*: It would follow that Scripture is incomplete, if it did not contain sufficiently expressly and clearly all necessary things; but this seems absurd. |
| *Respondeo*,distinguendo maiorem,quantum ad hoc,quod non contineret illa omnia sic,concedo, nec hoc est absurdum;quia ita voluit Deus:sicut enim ipsemet Christus non proposuit per se doctrinam suam in scriptura aliqua per se,nec tradidit etiam omnia, quæ dixit tã clarè,quin sit difficile intelligere multa,quæ dixit,propter finem sine dubio bonum:ita non erat necessarium quod scriptura omnia contineret, aut tam clarè quàm volunt hæretici, propter eundem finem,aut alium quemcunque bonum , quem prætenderat Deus; Quantum ad hoc , quod non contineret omnia necessaria , & eadem claritate,qua videbatur Deo expedire ad finem ab ipso intentum , nego maiorem. | | *I respond* by distinguishing the major: as to this, that it would not contain all those things in that manner, I concede it, nor is this absurd, because God so willed; for just as Christ Himself did not propose His doctrine by Himself in any scripture of His own, nor did He even deliver all the things He said so clearly but that it is difficult to understand many things He said — for an end without doubt good — so it was not necessary that Scripture should contain all things, or so clearly as the heretics would have it, for the same end, or any other good end whatsoever, which God had intended. As to this, that it would not contain all necessary things, and with that same clarity with which it seemed to God expedient for the end intended by Him, I deny the major. |
| *Itaque certum est*,quod nulla scriptura, aut doctrina debet videri diminuta , quando continet omnia , & eodem modo, quo expediens est ad finem,ab ipsius authore intentum,licèt non contineret omnia necessaria , aut eodem modo , quo cõduceret ad alios fines.Certè quæ dixerat Christus parabolicè & obscuriùs, poterat dicere absque ænigmatibus,aut parabolis,& lõgè clariùs,si vellet,& tota scriptura poterat à Deo clariùs proponi,quàm proposita est de facto,idque esset longè melius in ordine ad hoc,quod facilius intelligeretur,& vitarentur variæ hæreses exortæ circa sensum ipsius;& tamen ita non fecit,quia non erat expediens,vt ita faceret,in ordine ad alios fines, ab ipso intentos. | | *And so it is certain* that no scripture or doctrine ought to seem incomplete when it contains all things, and in that same manner, which is expedient for the end intended by its author, even though it did not contain all necessary things, or in the same manner which would conduce to other ends. Certainly the things which Christ had said parabolically and rather obscurely, He could have said without enigmas or parables, and far more clearly, if He had wished; and the whole of Scripture could have been proposed by God more clearly than it has in fact been proposed, and that would have been far better in order to this, that it might be more easily understood, and that the various heresies arisen about its sense might be avoided; and yet He did not do so, because it was not expedient that He should do so, in order to other ends intended by Him. |
| *Obiicies secundò* : Scriptura prohibet aliquid ipsi addendum Deuteronom.4.& Apocalyps.vltimo. | | *You will object secondly*: Scripture forbids anything to be added to it, Deuteronom.4. & Apocalyps. vltimo. |
| *Respondeo* ad obiectionem friuolam , & quam mirum est vllum virum vllius iudicij proponere: scripturam tantum velle,quod nihil addatur ipsi, quod sit contrarium sensui eius,aut tanquam pars ipsius:non verò quod non habeatur pro re reuelata à Deo aliquid amplius, quàm habetur in illa scriptura , in qua hoc proponitur. Et quod non velit aliud,manifestum est;quia certum est,& admissum ab ipsis hæreticis , multa alia addita scripturæ fuisse post Deuteronomium,& certum est S. Ioann. voluisse tantùm , nihil addendum esse ipsimet Apocalypsi ipsius , tanquam partem ipsius Apocalypsis. Hoc autem euidentissimum est non esse ad propositum , nec impedire vim traditionum. | | *I respond* to this frivolous objection — one which it is a wonder that any man of any judgment should propose: that Scripture means only that nothing be added to it which is contrary to its sense, or as a part of it; but not that nothing more be held for a thing revealed by God than is contained in that scripture in which this is proposed. And that it does not mean anything else is manifest; because it is certain, and admitted by the heretics themselves, that many other things were added to Scripture after Deuteronomy; and it is certain that St. John meant only that nothing was to be added to his own Apocalypse itself, as a part of the Apocalypse itself. But it is most evident that this is not to the point, nor does it impede the force of traditions. |
| *Similis est obiectio tertia* ex Isaiæ 29.Matth.15. Galat.1.1.Petr.2.vbi reprobantur traditiones hominum. | | *Similar is the third objection*, from Isaiæ 29. Matth.15. Galat.1. 1.Petr.2., where the traditions of men are reproved. |
| *Respondeo*,hoc intelligendum de traditionibus hominum , non habentibus Deum pro authore. Sed neque de omnibus similibus hominum traditionibus intelligendum est , sed de iis , quæ essent contra ius diuinum , aut naturale ; aut quæ præferentur legi diuinæ , aut naturali:quid enim absurdius , quàm quod existimaretur hoc intelligi de traditione , quæ esset in Republica aliqua, quod filius primogenitus deberet succedere in hæreditatem patris, aut similibus aliis ex se non malis? | | *I respond* that this is to be understood of the traditions of men not having God for their author. But neither is it to be understood of all similar traditions of men, but of those which would be against divine or natural law, or which would be preferred to the divine or natural law: for what would be more absurd than that this should be thought to be understood of a tradition which might exist in some Commonwealth, that the first-born son should succeed to the inheritance of his father, or of other similar things not evil of themselves? |
| *Obiicies quartò*:Traditiones esse subiectas defectibus incertitudinis, & obliuionis ; & consequenter eas non posse obligare. | | *You will object fourthly* that traditions are subject to the defects of uncertainty and of oblivion, and consequently that they cannot oblige. |
| *Respondetur* , negando consequentiam : quia saltem antequam cadant in obliuionem , & est incertum moraliter,an sint veræ,obligabunt. | | *It is responded* by denying the consequence: because, at least before they fall into oblivion, and before it is morally uncertain whether they are true, they will oblige. |
| *Respondeo secundò*,Traditiones rerum necessariò credendarum non esse incertas moraliter , nec posse tradi obliuioni apud Ecclesiam vniuersalem ; quia est impossibile , moraliter loquendo, quod penes,maiorem partem Ecclesiæ non haberetur cura earum conseruandarum ; sicut est impossibile, moraliter loquendo, quod in Ecclesia, vniuersaliter loquendo, non conseruetur memoria perpetua Symboli Apostolici. | | *I respond secondly* that the traditions of things necessarily to be believed are not morally uncertain, nor can they be consigned to oblivion in the universal Church; because it is impossible, morally speaking, that among the greater part of the Church care should not be had of preserving them; just as it is impossible, morally speaking, that in the Church, universally speaking, the perpetual memory of the Apostles' Creed should not be preserved. |
| *Confirmatur*, quia eodem modo posset scriptura esse subiecta incertitudini, & obliuioni, etiam quoad verum sensum eius : nam si loquamur de scriptura autographa , scripta ab ipsis Apostolis; illa certè non potest peruenire ad omnes nationes, quotquot credunt,& credere debent sub periculo perdendæ salutis æternæ,propter defectum rerum necessariò credendarum, quæ in ipsa continentur ; nec potest ab omnibus illis in ea lingua, qua scripta est intelligi ; vnde necessariò transferenda erat ab aliquibus , & illi in interpretatione facilè possent errare, nisi haberent assistentiam particularem Spiritus sancti ; quam eos habere certè scriptura antecedens non testificabatur, & consequenter probanda esset aliunde. Quod si etiam omnes intelligerent aliam linguam , adhuc cum eadem autographa ad omnes non possent peruenire, deberet sine dubio transcribi ; in ipsa autem transcriptione tam multiplici , quæ deberet fieri,bone Deus!quot errores , vel litterarum, vel punctorum,vel commatum,quæ sensum totaliter inuertere possent , facilè irreperent, vt sanè quicunque, vel mediocris ingenij,meritò dubitare posset,an correctè transcriberentur,etiam quoad partem necessariam, si non haberet aliam regulam, cui deberet se conformare. De qua re plura dici possent , si breuitati non esset vacandum, & non esset propositum mihi,si Deus vitam largiretur,& gratiam,plura in alia occasione,de controuersiis fidei agere. | | *It is confirmed*: because in the same way Scripture could be subject to uncertainty and oblivion, even as to its true sense: for if we speak of the autograph Scripture, written by the Apostles themselves, that certainly cannot reach all the nations — as many as believe, and must believe under peril of losing eternal salvation, on account of the want of the things necessarily to be believed which are contained in it — nor can it be understood by all of them in that language in which it was written; whence it necessarily had to be translated by some, and they could easily err in the interpretation, unless they had the particular assistance of the Holy Spirit — which assistance the antecedent Scripture certainly did not testify that they had, and consequently it would have to be proved from elsewhere. And even if all understood another language, still, since the same autographs could not reach all, it would without doubt have to be transcribed; but in that transcription, so manifold, which would have to be made — good God! how many errors, whether of letters, or of points, or of commas, which could totally invert the sense, would easily creep in — so that truly anyone, even of middling intelligence, could deservedly doubt whether they were correctly transcribed, even as to the necessary part, if he did not have another rule to which he ought to conform himself. Of which matter more could be said, if brevity were not to be attended to, and if it were not my purpose, should God grant life and grace, to treat more fully of the controversies of faith on another occasion. |

| **10.** {marg. Cõcilia particularia non sunt infallibilis regula proponẽdorum mysteriorum fidei.} *COncilia particularia non sunt infallibilis regula proponendorum mysteriorum fidei , seu reuelationum diuinarum.* Hæc est communis inter Catholicos , & multò magis inter hæreticos , qui eam authoritatem negant Conciliis generalibus , quibus euidens est,quod magis attribui deberet. | | **10.** {marg. Particular Councils are not an infallible rule of proposing the mysteries of faith.} *Particular Councils are not an infallible rule of proposing the mysteries of faith, or divine revelations.* This is common among Catholics, and much more among the heretics, who deny that authority to general Councils, to which it is evident that it ought rather to be attributed. |
| *Intelligo autem* , per Concilia particularia , non solum Episcopalia,vbi congregatur Clerus vnius Episcopatus, eodem Episcopo præside;nec etiam Prouincialia , vbi conuocantur omnes clerici vnius Prouinciæ Archiepiscopalis , præside Archiepiscopo eiusdem Prduinciæ; sed etiam nationalia ( quæ vocantur ) consistentia ex congregatione omnium Archiepiscoporum , & Episcoporum,& Cleri vnius nationis integræ , verbi gratia , Hibernicæ , Hispanicæ , aut Gallicæ , sub quocunque præside , cui id conueniret de iure, aut ex electione. | | *Now I understand* by particular Councils not only episcopal ones, where the Clergy of one Episcopate is gathered, with the Bishop presiding; nor only provincial ones, where all the clerics of one Archiepiscopal Province are convoked, with the Archbishop of the same Province presiding; but also national ones (as they are called), consisting of the congregation of all the Archbishops and Bishops and Clergy of one entire nation — for example, the Irish, the Spanish, or the French — under whatever president to whom that would belong by right or by election. |
| *Probatur primò*, quia huiusmodi Concilia errauerunt tempore sancti Cypriani,& posse errare, traditur in Concilio Romano sub Symmacho: quam probationem non adduco hic,vt rem ex authoritate alio modo probem, quàm quatenus inde constat illum fuisse tum sensum magis communem Ecclesiæ,qui sensus sine dubio præferendus est sensui huiusmodi Conciliorum,aut eorum terminationibus. | | *It is proved first*: because Councils of this kind erred in the time of St. Cyprian, and that they can err is handed down in the Roman Council under Symmachus — which proof I do not adduce here to prove the matter from authority in any other way than insofar as from it it is established that that was then the more common sense of the Church, which sense without doubt is to be preferred to the sense of Councils of this kind, or to their determinations. |
| *Probatur secundò*,& magis ad propositum meum, qui ago hic rationibus , vt debet, nisi fallor, agi in hac materia,in qua probari debet regula infallibilis fidei,non supponi : quia talia Concilia non repræsentant totam Ecclesiam , nec constat authoritate,cui hic credi deberet : nec ratione;quod competat ipsis talis authoritas : nisi autem id constet , semper incertum erit , etiam moraliter, de veritate rerum ab ipsis , etiam de fide , propositarum. | | *It is proved secondly* — and more to my purpose, who proceed here by reasons, as one ought, unless I am mistaken, to proceed in this matter, in which the infallible rule of faith must be proved, not supposed: because such Councils do not represent the whole Church; nor is it established, either by an authority which ought here to be believed, or by reason, that such authority belongs to them; but unless that be established, it will always be uncertain, even morally, concerning the truth of the things proposed by them, even as de fide. |
| *Hinc* tamen non sequitur:quin subditi, & quicunque alij intelligunt huiusmodi Conciliorum determinationem , quoad res fidei , non debeant multum authoritatis deferre ipsis , & etiam sub peccato credere,nisi constet ipsis aliunde ratio dubitandi ; nec sufficiet, quod iam doceo , eos non habere infallibilem assistentiam Spiritus sancti, sed aliam debent habere:sufficiet autem viris doctis, quod sciant motiua,quibus adducuntur ad id definiendum,non esse sufficientia;sed multò magis, si sciant rem illam non ita teneri ab aliis nationibus,& esse nouã : Simplicioribus autem nullam existimo suffecturam,nisi res determinata esset contraria illis,quæ semper habuerunt sibi proposita pro rebus fidei,tunc enim meritò dubitare possent , & etiam prudentissimè non credere, nisi consultis aliis;& præsertim, si audirent contradici illis determinationibus à Conciliis similis qualitatis,& multò magis à Pontifice Romano : sed oporteret, vt illud audirent cum aliqua euidentia morali de veritate; quia , si aliqui duo, vel tres, etiã viri docti,& pij id dicerent,non deberet suf ficere ; quia potius credendum est illi Concilio, quàm ipsis. | | *Hence*, however, it does not follow but that subjects, and whoever else understand the determination of Councils of this kind as to the things of faith, ought to defer much authority to them, and even to believe under pain of sin, unless a reason for doubting be established for them from elsewhere; nor will it suffice — as I now teach — that they do not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, but they must have another reason. Now it will suffice for learned men that they know that the motives by which they are led to define it are not sufficient; but much more, if they know that that matter is not so held by other nations, and is new. For the simpler folk, however, I judge that no reason will suffice, unless the thing determined were contrary to those things which they have always had proposed to them as things of faith; for then they could deservedly doubt, and even, most prudently, not believe, except after consulting others; and especially if they heard that those determinations were contradicted by Councils of similar quality, and much more by the Roman Pontiff; but it would be necessary that they hear that with some moral evidence of its truth; because, if some two or three, even learned and pious men, said it, it ought not to suffice; because that Council is rather to be believed than they. |
| Sed & ipsi doctiores, qui cognoscerent motiua huiusmodi Conciliorum ad determinandas tales res, non esse sufficientia , deberent cauere, ne publicè , aut priuatim aliis significarent determinationes illas non esse credendas, antequam possent id meliori authoritate , aut ratione confirmare ; sed priùs deberent summum Pontificem informare,& si res esset talis , ex cuius fide posset periculum aliquod imminere saluti aliorum, consultius esset , vt aliis proponerent illud periculum, & eos propterea, quantum possent,cautè, & secretè auerterent à fide illis determinationibus adhibenda. | | But even the more learned themselves, who might know that the motives of Councils of this kind for determining such things are not sufficient, ought to beware lest, publicly or privately, they signify to others that those determinations are not to be believed, before they could confirm this by better authority or reason; but they ought first to inform the supreme Pontiff; and if the matter were such that from belief in it some danger could threaten the salvation of others, it would be more advisable that they propose that danger to others, and on that account, as far as they could, cautiously and secretly turn them away from giving credence to those determinations. |
| **11.** *Reuera* autem vix apparet moraliter probabile, vt aliquid immediatè necessarium ad salutem , & cuius ignorantia inuincibilis non liberaret quemcunque à periculo salutis,posset in talibus Cõciliis determinari ; quia illa,quæ sunt taliter necessaria ad salutem,sunt ita proposita sufficienter, vt moraliter videatur impossibile , quod à talibus Conciliis oppositum determinetur sic,quin ipsismet simplicioribus occurrat ratio dubitandi,meritò sufficiens,ex eo scilicet,quod sit contra illud quod proponebatur ipsis , antea absque vlla controuersia tanquam necessarium ad salutem. | | **11.** *In truth*, moreover, it scarcely appears morally probable that anything immediately necessary for salvation — and invincible ignorance of which would not free anyone from danger to salvation — could be determined in such Councils; because those things which are in such manner necessary for salvation are so sufficiently proposed that it seems morally impossible that the opposite should be determined by such Councils in such a way but that to the simpler folk themselves there would occur a reason for doubting, deservedly sufficient — namely, from the fact that it is against that which was proposed to them before, without any controversy, as necessary for salvation. |
| *Hoc totum confirmo* exemplo:Nam quando deficiebat Henricus VIII.Angliæ Rex ab Ecclesia,si tum ita præualeret apud Prælatos totius Angliæ, vt conuenirent cum ipso ( quod posset optimè contingere propter non necessariam assistentiam promissam ipsis à Spiritu sancto ) vt declararent summum Pontificem non esse verum caput Ecclesiæ in terris,& Vicarium Christi, & confessionem auricularem non esse necessariam ad salutem; nec inuocandos Sanctos,profecto quilibet particularis Catholicus istius regni posset, & deberet non præstare fidem isti determinationi ; quia erat directè contra doctrinam , quam ante habuerunt communiter ab ipsismet Prælatis,& multò magis quilibet doctus posset persuadere ipsis,& exponere etiam suam vitam pro hoc,si esset necessarium, vt multi fecerunt : sed si determinaretur ab ipsis aliquid aliud circa intelligentiam alicuius loci scripturæ , quod non esset contra doctrinam ab ipsis ante pro fide receptam , etiamsi à parte rei falsum esset,simpliciores deberent id credere, antequam sufficienter intelligerent contrarium doceri ab Ecclesia vniuersali, aut à Pontifice,& doctiores cum cautela prædicta deberent procedere , quod totum mihi videtur , ex ipsis terminis , satis apparens. | | *All this I confirm* by an example: For when Henry VIII., King of England, was falling away from the Church, if he had then so prevailed with the Prelates of all England that they agreed with him (which could very well happen, on account of the assistance promised to them by the Holy Spirit not being necessary), so as to declare that the supreme Pontiff is not the true head of the Church on earth and the Vicar of Christ, and that auricular confession is not necessary for salvation, nor the Saints to be invoked — assuredly any particular Catholic of that kingdom could, and ought to, withhold credence from that determination, because it was directly against the doctrine which they had before received commonly from those very Prelates; and much more could any learned man persuade them of this, and even expose his own life for it, if it were necessary, as many did. But if something else were determined by them concerning the understanding of some passage of Scripture, which was not against the doctrine received by them before as of faith, even if in point of fact it were false, the simpler folk ought to believe it, until they sufficiently understood that the contrary was taught by the universal Church or by the Pontiff; and the more learned ought to proceed with the aforesaid caution — all of which seems to me, from the very terms, sufficiently apparent. |

| **12.** {marg. Authoritas Concilij generalis est talis regula.} *AVthoritas Concilij generalis,& vniuersalis, nomine totius Ecclesiæ legitimè congregati,& approbati, est infallibilis regula fidei , quantum ad illa, quæ proponit de fide credenda.* Hæc est communis Catholicorum omnium contra hæreticos. | | **12.** {marg. The authority of a general Council is such a rule.} *The authority of a general and universal Council, legitimately congregated in the name of the whole Church, and approved, is an infallible rule of faith, as to those things which it proposes as de fide to be believed.* This is common to all Catholics against the heretics. |
| *Probatur*,quia ex traditione Apostolica cõstat, huiusmodi Concilia habere talem authoritatem, & non posse proponere pro rebus reuelatis , nisi quæ sunt tales ; vnde ab ipsomet initio Ecclesiæ ad hæc vsque tempora fiebat recursus in grauioribus difficultatibus ad illa, & ipsamet Concilia congregata existimabant semper se habuisse talem authoritatem : quod certè fieri non posset , nisi per traditionem satis manifestatam id constaret ipsis. | | *It is proved*: because from Apostolic tradition it is established that Councils of this kind have such authority, and cannot propose as revealed things any but such as are so; whence from the very beginning of the Church down to these times recourse was had to them in the graver difficulties, and the congregated Councils themselves always judged that they had such authority — which certainly could not happen unless it were established for them through a tradition sufficiently manifested. |
| *Confirmatur*,si, quod decerneretur ab huiusmodi Conciliis, non esset infallibiliter de fide,Pontifex errare posset in proponendis rebus fidei,tanquam Pontifex ; quia, vt determinationes horum Conciliorum essent de fide,ipse deberet illas approbare , & sic approbando , proponeret esse de fide,quod non est de fide:sed vt statim dicam, ipse in hoc nequit errare. | | *It is confirmed*: if what was decreed by Councils of this kind were not infallibly de fide, the Pontiff could err in proposing the things of faith, as Pontiff; because, in order that the determinations of these Councils be de fide, he would have to approve them, and, so approving, he would propose to be de fide what is not de fide; but, as I shall say presently, he cannot err in this. |
| *Confirmatur secundò* ; quia non negatur authoritas huiusmodi Conciliorum,nisi ab hominibus, aut congregationibus millies minoris authoritatis : ergo imprudentissimum esset, propter talium authoritatem , negare authoritatem huiusmodi Conciliorum:præsertim cùm non possit ostendi, quod vnquam defecerit vllum ex tot huiusmodi, quot fuerunt à tempore Christi, in proponendis rebus fidei,vt patet ex facili solutione aliquarum instantiarum, quas proponunt hæretici videndas apud Bellarminum , apud quem etiam videri potest, quid ad huiusmodi Concilium requiratur, quis ipsum posset conuocare, quis in eo præsidere , an debeat confirmari à Papa , an sit supra Papam, & alia, quæ non habent difficultatem vllam speculatiuam, sed tantùm historicam, aut Theologicam facilis expeditionis. | | *It is confirmed secondly*: because the authority of Councils of this kind is not denied except by men or congregations of a thousand times less authority; therefore it would be most imprudent, on account of the authority of such, to deny the authority of Councils of this kind — especially since it cannot be shown that any one of so many of this kind as there have been from the time of Christ has ever failed in proposing the things of faith, as is evident from the easy solution of certain instances which the heretics propose, to be seen in Bellarmine — in whom also may be seen what is required for a Council of this kind, who could convoke it, who preside in it, whether it must be confirmed by the Pope, whether it is above the Pope, and other matters which have no speculative difficulty, but only a historical or Theological one of easy dispatch. |
| **CONCLVSIO X.** | | **CONCLUSION X.** |
| **13.** *POntifex Romanus, qui est Summus Pontifex totius Ecclesiæ,habet huiusmodi authoritatem etiam extra Concilium.* | | **13.** *The Roman Pontiff, who is the Supreme Pontiff of the whole Church, has authority of this kind even outside a Council.* |
| Hæc est communissima apud Catholicos , & probatur ; quia id constat ex continua traditione Ecclesiæ, semper attribuentis ipsi huiusmodi authoritatem,quod non est verisimile eam facturam, nisi illa traditio esset Apostolica ; nec etiam est verisimile,quod ipsimet Pontifices, quorum plurimi erant sanctissimi , & doctissimi , eam authoritatem sibi assumerent, nisi ita esset. Authoritates Patrum,& Conciliorum,quibus constat illa traditio , & sensus Ecclesiæ communiter hac de re,videndæ sunt apud Bellarminum,lib.3.de Pontifice , & alios controuersistas , qui soluunt facilè hæreticorum obiectiones , quarum duas tantum hic proponere placet. | | This is most common among Catholics, and it is proved: because it is established from the continual tradition of the Church, always attributing to him authority of this kind — which it is not likely she would do unless that tradition were Apostolic; nor is it likely that the Pontiffs themselves, very many of whom were most holy and most learned, would assume that authority to themselves, unless it were so. The authorities of the Fathers and Councils by which that tradition is established, and the common sense of the Church on this matter, are to be seen in Bellarmine, lib.3.de Pontifice, and other controversialists, who easily solve the objections of the heretics, of which it pleases me to propose only two here. |
| *Obiicies primò* : Si Pontifex haberet huiusmodi authoritatem,frustra congregarentur Concilia generalia ad definiendas res fidei,cum tot sumptibus, & difficultatibus. | | *You will object first*: If the Pontiff had authority of this kind, general Councils would be congregated in vain for defining the things of faith, with so many expenses and difficulties. |
| *Respondeo*,negando sequelam.*Primò*,quia Pontifex non solet aliquas difficultates definire ; nec vllas absque præuio diligenti examine ; nullum autem magis opportunum fieri potest , quàm quod fit congregatis huiusmodi Conciliis;vnde ad hoc vtilissimè congregantur. | | *I respond* by denying the consequence. *First*, because the Pontiff is not wont to define certain difficulties, nor any without a previous diligent examination; but none more opportune can be made than that which is made when Councils of this kind are congregated; whence for this they are most usefully congregated. |
| *Deinde*, per consensum tot Prælatorum , faciliùs obuiaretur Schismati,& occurreretur oppositionibus hæreticorum contra decisiones in Concilio factas. | | *Further*, through the consent of so many Prelates, Schism would be more easily obviated, and the oppositions of heretics against the decisions made in a Council would be met. |
| *Obiicitur secundò*:Vt esset infallibilis authoritatis , quod proponeretur à Papa , deberet credi, quod ipsemet esset verus Papa, & sic esset de fide, quod Innocentius X. qui hodie regnat , esset canonicè, & verè electus: sed hoc non est verum, quia non constat , quod Deus id vnquam reuelauerit;& quia aliqui Theologi putant quem eligi posse in Papam,& pro tali haberi,qui verè talis non esset. | | *It is objected secondly*: In order that what was proposed by the Pope should be of infallible authority, it would have to be believed that he himself is the true Pope; and thus it would be de fide that Innocent X., who reigns today, was canonically and truly elected; but this is not true, because it is not established that God ever revealed it; and because some Theologians think that one could be elected Pope, and held for such, who was not truly such. |
| *Respondeo primò*, negando sequelam maioris, quia sufficit moralis certitudo , quæ haberetur, quod ipse esset verus Papa ad hoc,vt quis credat fide diuina rebus ab ipso propositis ; & certè existimo satis probabile esse,quod traditio Apostolica, quæ habetur de infallibili authoritate Papæ, extendat se ad omnes Papas , qui cognoscuntur cum morali euidentia esse tales , siue credantur fide diuina esse tales,siue non ; & etiam siue reuera sint tales,siue non ; modo possit fieri,quod aliquis cum tali certitudine haberetur pro Papa,qui non esset reuerà Papa. | | *I respond first* by denying the consequence of the major: because the moral certitude which would be had that he is the true Pope suffices for this, that someone may believe by divine faith the things proposed by him; and certainly I judge it sufficiently probable that the Apostolic tradition which is had concerning the infallible authority of the Pope extends itself to all Popes who are known with moral evidence to be such, whether they be believed by divine faith to be such or not, and also whether they truly be such or not — provided it could happen that someone should be held for Pope with such certitude who was not truly Pope. |
| **14.** {marg. Qui habetur communiter pro Papa absque sufficiẽti ratione dubitandi , credendus est de fide esse verus Papa.} *Respondeo secundò* , negando minorem : nam sanè existimo esse articulum fidei,quod quicumque habetur communiter ab Ecclesia pro canonicè,& legitimè electo, absque rationabili causa dubitandi,sit verus Papa,præsertim postquam actu exerceret suam authoritatem,quantum ad decidendas aliquas fidei controuersias , aut conuocationem Concilij generalis. | | **14.** {marg. He who is commonly held for Pope without sufficient reason for doubting is to be believed de fide to be the true Pope.} *I respond secondly* by denying the minor: for truly I judge it to be an article of faith that whoever is commonly held by the Church as canonically and legitimately elected, without reasonable cause of doubting, is the true Pope — especially after he should actually exercise his authority as to deciding some controversies of faith, or the convocation of a general Council. |
| *Probatur hoc* ; quia de fide est Deum dedisse Petro,& successoribus huiusmodi infallibilem authoritatem : sed si non crederetur in particulari huic,& illi successori esse concessam talem authoritatem,& hunc & illum esse verum successorem eius,ad nihil deseruiret , & frustra communicaretur talis authoritas successoribus Petri;quia semper posset dubitari,quis esset successor verus eius : ergo id credendum est. | | *This is proved*: because it is de fide that God gave to Peter and to his successors infallible authority of this kind; but if it were not believed in particular that to this and to that successor such authority has been granted, and that this one and that one is his true successor, it would serve for nothing, and such authority would be communicated in vain to the successors of Peter, because it could always be doubted who was his true successor; therefore this must be believed. |
| *Probatur secundò* : quia non sufficit credere in vniuersali,quod Ecclesia à Deo fundata sit infallibilis veritatis , & incapax erroris profitendi in materia fidei ; sed requiritur in particulari credere determinatam aliquam Ecclesiam esse talem:ad quid enim deseruiret fides illa in vniuersali præstita ? Ergo non sufficit credere in genere, quod successores legitimi Petri sint infallibilis authoritatis ; sed requiritur credere in particulari hunc successorem illius habere talem authoritatem , & consequenter esse verum successorem ipsius , saltem aliquando ; nunquam autem potius , quàm in casu conclusionis : & sanè aliàs inutilis prorsus esset illa fides, & inutiliter reuelaretur à Deo, aut communicaretur authoritas talis successoribus ipsius. | | *It is proved secondly*: because it does not suffice to believe in universal that the Church founded by God is of infallible truth, and incapable of professing error in matter of faith; but it is required to believe in particular that some determinate Church is such: for to what would that faith rendered in universal serve? Therefore it does not suffice to believe in general that the legitimate successors of Peter are of infallible authority; but it is required to believe in particular that this successor of his has such authority, and consequently is his true successor — at least at some time; but never rather than in the case of the conclusion: and truly, otherwise that faith would be altogether useless, and uselessly would it be revealed by God, or such authority communicated to his successors. |
| *Probatur tertiò* : Quia de tempore in tempus ab initio Ecclesiæ hucvsque,vt constat ex Historiis Ecclesiasticis , fideles credebant illos, qui habebantur absque ratione dubitandi pro Pontificibus, fuisse verè tales;in quo certè non tam generaliter conueniretur, nisi esset traditio Apostolica ea de re. Itaque sine dubio Christus reuelauit Apostolis,& illi aliis hanc veritatem ; vndè constat primam probationem minoris esse falsam , & quoad secundam partem dico illos Theologos fuisse deceptos. | | *It is proved thirdly*: Because from time to time, from the beginning of the Church until now, as is established from the Ecclesiastical Histories, the faithful believed that those who were held, without reason for doubting, for Pontiffs, were truly such — in which there certainly would not be such general agreement, unless there were an Apostolic tradition on that matter. And so without doubt Christ revealed this truth to the Apostles, and they to others; whence it is established that the first proof of the minor is false; and as to the second part, I say that those Theologians were deceived. |
| *Dixi* in hac responsione , *sine rationabili causa dubitandi* ; Quia non esset in tali casu obligatio credendi eum, de quo dubitaretur ,esse verum Papam. Casus autem, in quo posset esse talis dubitatio, posset contingere, quando Principes Ecclesiæ , penes quos esset electio , non solùm non conuenirent in electione vnius ; sed ex iis aliqua pars eligeret vnum, & alia alium, vt quandoque contigit ; aut quando manifesta vis adhiberetur illis,qua cogerentur eligere vnum,quem non vellent aliàs eligere. | | *I said* in this response, *without reasonable cause of doubting*; because in such a case there would be no obligation of believing that he, of whom there was doubt, is the true Pope. Now the case in which there could be such a doubt could occur when the Princes of the Church, with whom the election lay, not only did not agree in the election of one man, but some part of them elected one, and another part another, as has sometimes happened; or when manifest force were applied to them, by which they were compelled to elect one whom they would not otherwise wish to elect. |
| *Dixi etiam , præsertim , quando actu exerceret suam authoritatem, decidendo aliquid de fide*,vt abstraherem à difficultatibus aliquorum casuum admissorum à nonnullis Theologis, vt quod mulier , aut non baptizatus eligerentur, & haberentur pro Papis absque vlla ratione dubitandi, cum tamen non essent capaces Papatus, & consequenter non essent verè Papæ. Quamvis enim hi casus essent possibiles , non solum absolutè ; sed etiam vt subest Ecclesia prouidentiæ particulari Dei circa ipsam , tamen omninò existimo Deum nunquam permissurum , vt procederent ad decisionem vllius doctrinæ fidei. | | *I said also, especially when he should actually exercise his authority, deciding something de fide*, in order to abstract from the difficulties of certain cases admitted by some Theologians — as that a woman, or one not baptized, might be elected and held for Pope without any reason for doubting, although nevertheless they would not be capable of the Papacy, and consequently would not truly be Popes. For although these cases would be possible, not only absolutely, but even as the Church is subject to the particular providence of God concerning her, nevertheless I altogether judge that God would never permit that they should proceed to the decision of any doctrine of faith. |
| *Reuera* tamen existimo dicendum,quod ad prouidentiam particularem Dei circa suam Ecclesiã, quam collocauit supra firmam petram, & contra quam non præualebunt vnquam portæ inferi, spectet, vt non permittat vnquam fieri tales electiones , aut alias inualidas , quin statim in ipso initio appareant rationes dubitandi rationabiles, ob quas electio pro suspecta , aut pro manifestè inualida haberi debeat : sicut enim ad illam prouidentiam spectaret impedire , ne tales personæ, quæ non essent veri Papæ,sed pro talibus haberentur absque aliqua ratione dubitandi,procederent ad decisionem rerum fidei,vt fatentur authores tenentes illos casus posse contingere de facto: ita multò suauius,magis congruum,& æquè facile esset impedire,ne eligerentur tali modo,vt non dubitaretur de eius vera electione. | | *In truth*, however, I judge it must be said that it pertains to the particular providence of God concerning His Church — which He has placed upon a firm rock, and against which the gates of hell shall never prevail — that He never permit such elections, or others invalid, to take place, but that immediately, in the very beginning, reasonable reasons for doubting appear, on account of which the election ought to be held as suspect, or as manifestly invalid: for just as it would pertain to that providence to prevent such persons — who would not be true Popes, but would be held for such without any reason for doubting — from proceeding to the decision of things of faith, as the authors holding that those cases can in fact occur confess: so it would be much more sweet, more congruous, and equally easy to prevent their being elected in such a manner that there would be no doubt of his true election. |
| **15.** *Quæres* autem circa hoc , an sequatur ex eo, quod sit de fide , Papam , de cuius vera electione non dubitatur,esse verum Papam, electionem ipsius esse legitimam. | | **15.** *You will ask*, however, concerning this: whether it follows, from the fact that it is de fide that a Pope of whose true election there is no doubt is the true Pope, that his election is legitimate. |
| *Respondeo* , Electionem legitimam Papæ posse considerari dupliciter. *Primò* ita , vt sit talis,qua mediante , reuera confertur verus Papatus personæ electæ,siue ipsamet sit peccaminosa,& inualida, & siue virtute illius solius conferebatur verus Papatus , siue ea mediante per Deum supplentem eius defectum,& in hoc sensu sequitur manifestè, electionem debere esse legitimam ; quia aliàs non esset electus verus Papa. | | *I respond* that the legitimate election of a Pope can be considered in two ways. *First*, in such wise that it be such that, by its mediation, the true Papacy is truly conferred upon the person elected — whether the election itself be sinful and invalid, and whether the true Papacy was conferred by virtue of it alone, or by its mediation through God supplying its defect — and in this sense it manifestly follows that the election must be legitimate; because otherwise the one elected would not be the true Pope. |
| *Secundò* sic , vt ipsamet electio non sit ex se peccaminosa , ratione peccatorum , quæ solent inualidare electiones, vt ratione simoniæ,aut coactionis. | | *Second*, in such wise that the election itself not be of itself sinful, by reason of the sins which are wont to invalidate elections, as by reason of simony or coercion. |
| *In hoc autem sensu* existimo, non sequi electionem esse bonam, quia , quamvis non esset adhuc Papa,esset verus Papa,quando non constaret publicè de talibus defectibus , & ratione eorum statim opponeret se aliqua pars principalis electioni ; quia cum,nisi constarent illi defectus publicè,aut fieret oppositio propter illos electioni,haberetur pro Papa absque ratione dubitandi rationali sic electus, omnino dicendum est hos defectus non impedire validitatem electionis , licèt impedirent validitatem aliarum electionum,& illam etiam , si innotescerent publicè, & propterea statim fieret oppositio ipsi. | | *But in this sense* I judge that it does not follow that the election is good; because, although he would not as yet be Pope, he would be the true Pope when such defects were not publicly established, and when some principal party did not, by reason of them, immediately oppose itself to the election; because since — unless those defects were publicly established, or opposition were made to the election on account of them — the man so elected would be held for Pope without rational reason for doubting, it must altogether be said that these defects do not impede the validity of the election, although they would impede the validity of other elections, and of this one also, if they became publicly known, and opposition were therefore immediately made to it. |
| *Dicis* , illos defectus posse tum etiam reddere electionem secundùm se inualidam ; nam licèt, qui ea mediante fieret Papa,esset verè talis, id non sequeretur vi electionis,sed Deo ipsi ascribendum esset, supplenti defectum electionis. | | *You say* that those defects can even then render the election, according to itself, invalid; for although he who by its mediation became Pope would be truly such, this would not follow by force of the election, but it would have to be ascribed to God Himself, supplying the defect of the election. |
| *Contra :* quia hoc est reducere difficultatem ad quæstionem de nomine:nam per electionem validam intelligimus illam , qua posita, electus sit verè Papa;talis autem esset illa,non minus,quàm si non haberet illos defectus. | | *Against this*: because this is to reduce the difficulty to a question about a name: for by a valid election we understand that election which, being posited, the one elected is truly Pope; but such would that election be, no less than if it did not have those defects. |
| *Posset tamen* satis probabiliter dici , quod docui in meo Commentario,electionem semper futuram absque his defectibus,quando publicè non constat contrarium , & non fit oppositio propterea ; quia id videtur spectare ad prouidentiam particularem Dei : & etiam si aliquæ actiones eligentium viderentur arguere tales aliquos defectus ; tamen quando electio ipsa facienda esset, & fieret pacificè , & sine strepitu , verisimilius est, quod illi defectus auferetentur per retractationem, aut pœnitentiam, aut aliqua alia via. | | *It could, nevertheless,* be said with sufficient probability — what I taught in my Commentary — that the election will always be without these defects when the contrary is not publicly established, and no opposition is made on that account; because this seems to pertain to the particular providence of God: and even if some actions of the electors should seem to argue some such defects, nevertheless, when the election itself was to be made, and was made peacefully, and without tumult, it is more likely that those defects would be removed through retractation, or penance, or some other way. |
| **16.** {marg. An Papa possit condere nouũ articulum fidei.} *Quæres secundò* : An Papa , vel Concilium generale possit condere aliquem nouum articulum fidei. | | **16.** {marg. Whether the Pope can institute a new article of faith.} *You will ask secondly*: Whether the Pope, or a general Council, can institute some new article of faith. |
| Certum est,si per articulum nouum fidei intelligeretur aliqua propositio,quæ ante determinationem Papæ non habebatur pro articulo fidei,& post esset , posse eos condere articulum nouum fidei,& sæpè eos tales condidisse in hoc sensu:& certè aliàs authoritas infallibilis, à Deo ipsi concessa ad determinandas fidei controuersias nihil deseruiret. Verùm de huiusmodi non agitur hic, sed an Papa, aut Concilium possint declarare,aut proponere aliquid de fide credendum , ob reuelationem aliquam nouam , quæ fieret ipsis , quod nec in generali, nec in particulari fuit reuelatum ante à Christo Apostolis aut aliis , siue obscurè, siue clarè. | | It is certain that, if by a new article of faith were understood some proposition which before the determination of the Pope was not held for an article of faith, and afterwards would be, they can institute a new article of faith, and have often instituted such in this sense: and certainly otherwise the infallible authority granted to him by God for determining the controversies of faith would serve for nothing. But it is not of this kind that we treat here, but whether the Pope or a Council can declare or propose something to be believed as de fide on account of some new revelation which might be made to them — something which neither in general nor in particular was revealed before by Christ to the Apostles or to others, whether obscurely or clearly. |
| *Communis* autem, & vera Theologorum sententia est, quod non possunt ; quia ex praxi , & vsu Pontificum, & Conciliorum habetur , quod nunquam tentauerint hoc facere ; quia nunquam proposuerunt aliquid innixum tali reuelationi nouæ;nec cum dubitaretur de veritatibus aliquibus fidei,vsi sunt vlla diligentia,nec quidem orationum , aut bonorum operum ad impetrandum à Deo huiusmodi reuelationem,& licèt verisimile sit ex sanctis Pontificibus multos habuisse reuelationes particulares, tamen nunquam proposuerunt illas credendas fidelibus,tanquam propositiones fidei diuinæ , sed semper vtebantur diligentia scrutandi Scripturas,& indagandi traditiones Ecclesiæ de re decidenda tanquam de fide, quod signum euidentissimum est,nec eos,nec Ecclesiam vnquam sensisse,quod talis authoritas resideret apud ipsos. | | *But the common* and true opinion of Theologians is that they cannot; because from the practice and usage of the Pontiffs and Councils it is had that they have never attempted to do this; because they have never proposed anything relying upon such a new revelation; nor, when there was doubt about certain truths of faith, did they use any diligence, not even of prayers or of good works, to obtain from God a revelation of this kind; and although it is likely that of the holy Pontiffs many had particular revelations, nevertheless they never proposed them to be believed by the faithful as propositions of divine faith, but always used the diligence of searching the Scriptures, and of investigating the traditions of the Church, concerning the matter to be decided as de fide — which is a most evident sign that neither they nor the Church ever judged that such authority resided with them. |
| **17.** *Dices primò* : Pontifices, & Concilia sæpè determinauerunt loca aliqua scripturæ habere hunc, vel illum sensum,& non habere sensum,quo hæretici abutebantur illis ; sed post illam definitionem illud erat de fide , ante non erat ; nec tamen fuit olim reuelatio , quod ille esset sensus ipsius: ergo Pontifices, & Concilia vel habuerunt reuelationem nouam ea de re , vel proposuerunt aliquid de fide credendum , quod non erat de fide: nihil enim potest esse de fide,quod non fuit reuelatum aliquando. | | **17.** *You will say first*: The Pontiffs and Councils have often determined that certain passages of Scripture have this or that sense, and do not have that sense in which the heretics abused them; but after that definition, that was de fide, before it was not; nor yet was there of old a revelation that that was its sense: therefore the Pontiffs and Councils either had a new revelation on that matter, or proposed something to be believed as de fide which was not de fide: for nothing can be de fide which was not revealed at some time. |
| *Confirmatur* hoc,quia ipsamet propositio Pontificum,& Conciliorum de tali sensu est reuelatio noua : nam quandoquidem Deus promiserit illis assistentiam suam in propositionibus rerum fidei, sequitur euidenter, quòd astiterit ipsis,dum proponebant talem sensum ; vnde illa propositio est reuelatio Dei, tam benè, quàm si significaret alio quocumque modo suam mentem. | | *This is confirmed*: because the very proposal of the Pontiffs and Councils concerning such a sense is a new revelation: for since God has promised them His assistance in the proposals of the things of faith, it evidently follows that He assisted them while they were proposing such a sense; whence that proposal is a revelation of God, just as well as if He signified His mind in any other manner whatsoever. |
| *Respondeo* , hanc obiectionem non videri mihi ad propositum difficultatis ; quia non agitur de sensu scripturæ;sed de aliis rebus, quæ non continentur in scriptura,nec constant ex traditione;ille autem sensus continetur in scriptura,& colligitur ex scriptura ipsa, aut ex traditione esse verum ipsius loci sensum;ac propterea determinatur esse talis à Pontifice, aut Concilio. | | *I respond* that this objection does not seem to me to the point of the difficulty; because the question is not about the sense of Scripture, but about other things which are not contained in Scripture, nor established from tradition; but that sense is contained in Scripture, and is gathered from Scripture itself, or from tradition, to be the true sense of that passage; and therefore it is determined to be such by the Pontiff or Council. |
| *Vnde directè respondeo*,negando minorem;quia ille sensus,vel reuelatur in scriptura, quatenus ex scriptura colligi potest,quòd sit verus sensus eius, vel reuelabatur Apostolis, & ab illis Ecclesiæ per traditionem. | | *Whence I respond directly* by denying the minor: because that sense either is revealed in Scripture, insofar as it can be gathered from Scripture that it is its true sense, or it was revealed to the Apostles, and by them to the Church through tradition. |
| *Ad confirmationem respondeo*, Illam propositionem Pontificis , ac Conciliorum esse reuelationem nouam Dei , sed dependentem à reuelatione antiqua ipsius ,tam de sensu illo scripturæ, quàm de promissione, quam fecerat assistendi in huiusmodi propositionibus Ecclesiæ ; vndè non est ad propositum. | | *To the confirmation I respond* that that proposal of the Pontiff and of Councils is a new revelation of God, but one dependent upon His ancient revelation, both concerning that sense of Scripture, and concerning the promise which He had made of assisting in such proposals of the Church; whence it is not to the point. |
| *Dices secundò* : Pontifex , dum canonizat Sanctos.proponit ipsos esse in cœlo,tanquam de fide credendum : sed Deus non reuelauit olim, quod S.Franciscus esset in cœlo, aut quod esset moriturus in statu tali , in quo sequeretur ipsum post mortem futurum in cœlo : ergo. | | *You will say secondly*: The Pontiff, when he canonizes Saints, proposes that they are in heaven, as a thing to be believed de fide; but God did not reveal of old that St. Francis would be in heaven, or that he would die in such a state, from which it would follow that he would be in heaven after death: therefore. |
| *Respondeo primò* , satis probabile esse,quod non proponatur de fide credendum Sanctos canonizatos esse in cœlo,sed tanquam quid moraliter certum,cognitione Theologica , innixa huic principio Theologico certo de fide , Quod quicumque vixerit benè , & fecerit pœnitentiam correspondentem suis imperfectionibus, si quas commisit, sit statim donandus beatitudine post mortem: & huic propositioni naturali,moraliter certæ, quod S. Franciscus sic vixerit , & fecerit talem pœnitentiam,cuius propositionis certitudo dependet à motiuis illis,quibus ad id colligendum mouebatur Ecclesia, post tam exactam diligentiam,quam in hac re adhibere solet ; quæ sanè est maxima, & satis ad id moraliter persuadendum cuicumque benè disposito, eam cognoscenti , & consideranti. | | *I respond first* that it is sufficiently probable that it is not proposed as to be believed de fide that canonized Saints are in heaven, but as something morally certain, by Theological knowledge, relying upon this Theological principle certain as de fide: That whoever has lived well, and has done penance corresponding to his imperfections, if he has committed any, is to be endowed with beatitude immediately after death; and upon this natural proposition, morally certain, that St. Francis so lived, and did such penance — the certitude of which proposition depends upon those motives by which the Church was moved to gather this, after such exact diligence as she is wont to employ in this matter; which truly is very great, and sufficient to persuade of it morally anyone well disposed who knows and considers it. |
| *Nec refert*, quod in Bulla canonizationis præcipere soleat Pontifex fidelibus , quod non dubitent de canonizatorum existentia in cœlo ; nam posset hoc præcipere,quamvis non putaret id esse de fide ex eo tantùm , quod esset moraliter certum,& quod dubitatio de illa posset cedere in detrimentum fidelium. Sed nec prohibet dubitationem speculatiuam, quæ compati posset cum ipsa fide , & quæ non est dependens à voluntate fidelium , nisi quatenùs est in potestate eorum , eam quantùm possunt reiicere, sicut debent deponere cogitationes malas. Sed de dubitatione quasi practica, ex qua sequeretur,quòd vellent omittere orare illos Sanctos,& eos colere propter illam dubitationem; hæc enim dubitatio est, quæ officeret illis. | | *Nor does it matter* that in the Bull of canonization the Pontiff is wont to command the faithful not to doubt of the existence of the canonized in heaven; for he could command this, although he did not think it to be de fide, from this alone, that it is morally certain, and that doubt about it could redound to the detriment of the faithful. But neither does he forbid speculative doubt, which could be compatible with faith itself, and which is not dependent upon the will of the faithful, except insofar as it is in their power to reject it as far as they can, just as they ought to put away evil thoughts. But he does forbid the quasi-practical doubt, from which it would follow that they would wish, on account of that doubt, to omit praying to those Saints, and venerating them; for this is the doubt which would harm them. |
| *Respondeo secundò* , satis etiam probabile esse, quòd sit de fide, canonizatos esse in cœlo: sed nego tamen inde sequi, posse condi nouum articulum fidei in sensu controuerso hic;quia illa veritas dependet à reuelatione antiqua verisimiliter facta Apostolis,quòd quicumque esset canonizatus à Papa,esset sine dubio in cœlo,aut ab illa altera,quòd assisteret Pontifici, ne proponeret aliquid de fide,quòd non esset de fide ; & sanè existimo hoc esse longè probabilius ; quia cùm posset esse de fide hoc modo , & cùm existimatio communis Ecclesiæ sit, quod ita fiat,probabilius longè videtur , quòd ita sit. | | *I respond secondly* that it is also sufficiently probable that it is de fide that the canonized are in heaven; but I deny, nevertheless, that it thence follows that a new article of faith can be instituted in the sense controverted here; because that truth depends upon an ancient revelation, likely made to the Apostles, that whoever should be canonized by the Pope would without doubt be in heaven; or upon that other, that He would assist the Pontiff, lest he propose something as de fide which was not de fide; and truly I judge this to be by far the more probable; because, since it could be de fide in this way, and since the common estimation of the Church is that it so happens, it seems by far more probable that it is so. |
