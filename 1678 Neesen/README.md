*Note on this entry: Laurent Neesen (c. 1611–1679) of Sint-Truiden, licentiate of Louvain, was appointed in 1639 by Archbishop Jacob Boonen as* ***President of the Archiepiscopal Seminary at Mechelen***, *and held that office for* ***forty years***, *while serving as* ***Canon Theologian of the metropolitan church of St Rombout, synodal examiner, and censor of books for the diocese***. *He left his whole estate to the seminary and was buried in a chapel he had founded there. This volume — the third part of a complete* Universa Theologia *— announces itself on the title page as "especially useful* ***for pastors, confessors***, *and all students of sacred theology," written "for the use of the theologians of the same Seminary." It is therefore not a speculative disputation but* ***the teaching manual of the primatial see of the Low Countries***, *by the man who ran its clergy formation, printed under his own censorship.*

***Why the entry exists.*** *At Q. II dub. VIII Neesen puts the question of this collection in its sharpest individuated form, naming the reigning pope, and answers it in the negative:*

> *An sit de fide* ***Innocentium XI Romanum Pontificem feliciter regnantem*** *esse Petri verum successorem & Christi in terris Vicarium?* {marg. ***Non est fidei divinæ modernum Pontificem esse verum Pontificem***} ***REsp. negat.*** *sed id solum esse certum* ***prudentiâ humanâ cui speculative potest subesse falsum***, *prout rectè ait Cajetanus 2. 2. quæst. 1. art. 10.*

*He is, so far as this census has found, the* ***only author in the collection who both poses the question of a peacefully reigning, uncontested pope and denies that the answer is of faith***. *That alone would earn him a place: a repository arguing a thesis must print its sharpest opposing witness in full, and this is he.*

***What his argument actually is — and what it is not.*** *The proof is a single syllogism about the* ***election***: *"every legitimately elected Pontiff is the successor of St Peter and Vicar of Christ; but Innocent XI is a legitimately elected Pontiff; therefore…" — and "the antecedent is not certain to us by faith,* ***at least as to the minor***." *The minor fails because canonical election depends on four conditions of divine law — that the Pontiff be* ***a man, baptized, legitimately ordained, and legitimately elected by legitimate electors*** *— "all of which we cannot, humanly speaking, evidently know, much less believe." He then answers three objections drawn from the affirmative side, and it is worth naming which ones. Against the argument from the canon of Scripture and from papal definitions — the* ***definition-certitude argument*** *that Turrianus reports as having carried the affirmative side after Clement VIII — he replies that "***it is quite consistent that a thing proposed be certain by faith, even though the proposer himself be certain not by divine faith but only by human faith***," since what applies the divine testimony need only be "***a human testimony, which renders the divine testimony itself evidently credible***." Against the rule that a universal of faith carries its particulars, he answers that "***this rule fails in many cases***," with the host and baptism parallels. And he closes the dubium by grading his own result:*

> *conclusio aliqua educta ex assumpto de fide & subsumpto* ***moraliter tantum certo***, *quantumvis per necessariam consequentiam,* ***non potest esse de fide*** *… adeoque* ***solum erit conclusio Theologica***.

*That last clause governs the whole entry. Neesen is not asserting that the reigning pope's title is practically doubtful; he is* ***assigning a theological note***, *and the note he assigns is* ***conclusio theologica***. *Correspondingly, "one who should deny that this individual Roman Pontiff is Peter's successor* ***would not be a heretic***, provided he did not deny that a legitimately elected Roman Pontiff is Peter's successor."*

***The decisive limitation: he never engages acceptance.*** *A full-text sweep of all 384 pages for the acceptance vocabulary —* accepta ab universa Ecclesia, pacifice, universali consensu, receptus ab Ecclesia *— returns* ***nothing*** *in any papal sense; every occurrence is the word-sense "acceptation," or accepting the faith, or accepting a duel. The only certifier of the minor he ever considers is the* ***election***. *This matters more than it may appear, because* ***the affirmative school agrees with him about the election***: *Gregory of Valencia holds that "not from this fact alone — that someone has been elected by the Church to the place of Saint Peter — is it certain with infallible faith that he is the true Pontiff," and Turrianus writes that "***election alone, and the acceptance of the Church without any controversy, suffice***," the second conjunct doing the work. Neesen's syllogism defeats the election-alone route,* ***which nobody in this collection defends***. *He therefore contradicts the affirmative* ***conclusion*** *without ever meeting its* ***ground***. *He is a negative by omission rather than by rebuttal, and the entry says so plainly rather than presenting him as an opponent he never was.*

***Why he is nevertheless classified School 2, and not as a bare negative.*** *Read whole, his system is emphatically papalist and his providence is robust. He holds the pope above councils (Lateran V, Florence), councils fallible without papal confirmation (Ephesus II, Basel), no infallibility for a council* sede vacante *or in schism — "***the whole Church is not understood without her head***" — and he defines the Church herself as the body united "under the government of the one Vicar of Christ on earth, the Roman Pontiff." He ranks the Sorbonne–Adrian VI–***Castro*** *opinion first among the* sententiae *and rejects it, adopting Cano's third view: the pope defining* ex cathedra *cannot err, but only proceeding* more humano, *a congregation of learned men having first examined the question — a* conditio sine qua non *"without which the Holy Spirit will not assist him defining." Most importantly he operates a* ***three-tier structure of fallibility***: *decrees depending on particular facts are fallible, the pope there acting "***as a mere Ecclesiastical Prince relying on human testimonies***" (Innocent III: the Church's judgment "sometimes follows opinion, which it often happens both to deceive and be deceived by"); but universal moral decrees and canonizations are* ***infallible***, *and his reason for the second tier is verbatim the argument of this collection: "***since the Pontiff's attestation is a public testimony for the whole Church, the providence of God is bound to assist him, lest, if it were false, the whole Church should err***," and "***if the Pontiff were to err in this, the whole Church would likewise so err***." That is the effect-guarantee — the fact left at moral certitude, the universal acts divinely protected — which is precisely* ***School 2***, *the Laymann–Muratori line, in its doctrinal-providence form.*

***The internal tension, stated without exaggeration.*** *Neesen's own criterion for tier-two protection —* a public testimony for the whole Church, such that were it false the whole Church would err *— fits the identity of the pope at least as well as it fits a canonization. Every infallibility he grants presupposes the fact he grades as a theological conclusion: the whole Church is bound by Christ's precept to obey* this man. *He protects every downstream act while leaving the upstream fact speculatively fallible, and he never confronts the asymmetry. This is not a refutation of him — it is the pressure the affirmative school applies, exhibited inside his own text.*

***Bearing on the modern controversy.*** *Two things follow, and they cut in opposite directions. Against the affirmative thesis, Neesen is a real cost: a printed seminary manual at a primatial see, teaching in 1678 that the denial is not heresy — the direct contradictory of [Carena](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1668%20Carena)'s verdict ten years earlier that such a denier is "***not to be held for a Schismatic, but for a true heretic***," and a standing counter-instance to [Turrianus](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1634%20Turrianus)'s judgment that after Clement VIII the negative "cannot be defended without scandal." Against the* ***sedevacantist and sedeprivationist*** *use of him, however, his system gives nothing: the universal acts of the man the whole Church obeys are providentially protected* lest the whole Church err, *so a claimant whose acts are void or whose magisterium is without authority is unthinkable in his framework. He is also explicit that "***not every schismatic must be a heretic; for it can happen that someone refuse obedience to the Supreme Pontiff, even though he believe him to be Christ's vicar***" — and, at dub. IX, that a pope may be deposed "***for the crime of heresy alone***," by a general council or, none being convened, by the cardinals, "since the two powers, of electing and of deposing, are reckoned naturally connected," but "***not for any other sins however scandalous***," the first see being judged by no one.*

***Textual history: the position was maintained after his death.*** *The volume was reprinted for half a century as part of the collected* Universa Theologia. *Three later recensions were collated for this entry against the 1678:* ***Lille 1687*** *(Fievet; BSB `bsb10325579`), where the dubium stands unaltered as Dub. VIII naming* ***Innocent XI***; ***Lille 1693*** *(BSB `bsb10626600`), where the dubium is renumbered* ***VII*** *and the editor has* ***updated the pope throughout the body to Innocent XII*** *— "sed* ***Innocentius XII*** *est Pontifex legitimè electus … hic individuus Pontifex* ***Innocentius XII***" *— while the running index still reads XI; and* ***Antwerp 1730*** *(Verdussen, "editio correcta"), which reverts to Innocent XI as a fossil; and the* ***1715*** *reissue (Google Books* JgfJUKIy4K8C*, printed 23–24) still reads Innocent XII, sixteen years into Clement XI, so that by then the name was no longer being kept current. The wording is otherwise identical in every recension, and* ***no elaboration and no acceptance-language was ever added***. *The companion volume* De Sacramentis *(1693, `bsb10626601`, 366 scans) was swept in full and contains nothing on the question. The 1693 name-update is the significant datum: it shows the thesis being maintained as* ***live teaching at Mechelen fourteen years after the author's death***, *by an editor who thought it worth keeping current — and who evidently felt no need to answer the acceptance argument.*

***A lead this entry opens.*** *Neesen's editor and eventual successor in the same chair was* ***Peter Dens*** *(1690–1775), President of the Mechelen seminary 1735–1775, whose first publication was the* Supplementum theologiae Rev. Adm. D. Laur. Neesen *(Mechelen 1758). Dens's own* Theologia ad usum seminariorum *(Mechelen 1777; Hanicq 1828–30; Dublin, Coyne, 1832) became the standard Irish seminary manual — though the Catholic Encyclopedia cautions that Dens "is not the author of the complete course" and that it "continues to be published by Mechlin seminary professors with modifications," so that "Dens" names a* ***house tradition*** *of which this volume is the ancestor.* ***That course reversed it.*** *See the [1829 Dens](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1829%20Dens) entry: the Mechelen course, in print under Pius VI by 1790 and almost certainly in the 1777 first edition, holds it "plausibly of faith" that the modern Pontiff is Peter's successor, states Neesen's own baptism parity as the objection, and denies it because "the legitimate election of the Pontiff rests on the proposition of the universal Church acknowledging the modern Pontiff as legitimate, in which she cannot err" — while withholding the note "catholic" for want of sufficient proposition. Between Neesen and Dens stand the Louvain lectures of Daelman (printed 1735), which raise the same question and answer that "in the Pontiff there is had the judgment of the Church, which on this point does not seem able to be deceived."*

## Q. II, Dub. VIII — the question, and the answer (printed 67)

**DUBIUM VIII.** *Whether it is of faith that **Innocent XI, the happily reigning Roman Pontiff**, is the true successor of Peter and the Vicar of Christ on earth?*

{marg. *That the modern Pontiff is the true Pontiff is not of divine faith.*} **I answer: no.** But that this is certain only by **human prudence, beneath which falsehood can speculatively lie**, as Cajetan rightly says, *2. 2. quæst. 1. art. 10.* The reason is: because the canonical election of the Roman Pontiff depends upon several conditions, which we are neither bound to believe by divine faith, nor can we, humanly speaking, know them all; and therefore in this syllogism — every legitimately elected Pontiff is the successor of St Peter and the Vicar of Christ; but Innocent XI is a legitimately elected Pontiff; therefore Innocent XI is Peter's successor and the Vicar of Christ — **the antecedent is not certain to us by faith, at least as to the minor; therefore neither is the consequent.** The consequence is proved: for since by divine law it is required that the Pontiff be **a man**; secondly **baptized**; thirdly **legitimately ordained**; fourthly **legitimately elected by legitimate electors** — all these we cannot, humanly speaking, evidently know, much less believe, since nevertheless upon them depends the legitimate election of the Pontiff.

## Q. II, Dub. VIII — the denier is not a heretic (printed 67)

**It is proved fourthly**, because **one who should deny that this individual Roman Pontiff is Peter's successor would not be a heretic**, provided he did not deny that a legitimately elected Roman Pontiff is Peter's successor: therefore that this modern Pontiff is Peter's true successor is not of faith.

## Q. II, Dub. VIII — the reply to the definition-certitude argument (printed 68)

**You will say:** if it be not certain by divine faith that this Pontiff is the true Pontiff, then neither will it be established by divine faith that this Scripture is Canonical Scripture, nor that this tradition is true. For that this Scripture is Canonical and that this tradition is Apostolic depends upon the definition and declaration of the Supreme Pontiff.

**I answer, denying the consequence**, since **it is quite consistent that a thing proposed be certain by faith, even though the proposer himself be certain not by divine faith but only by human faith** [...] therefore we can likewise believe by divine faith the things which this Pontiff proposes to us as to be believed by divine faith, **even though we do not believe by divine faith that he himself is the true Pontiff, but only by human faith**; for it is not true, as is clear from what has been said, that in order for a thing to be believed by divine faith it is necessary that the proposer himself be believed by divine faith; since it is not necessary that the proposition which merely applies the divine testimonies be itself the testimony of God, but **it suffices that it be a human testimony, which renders the divine testimony itself evidently credible**.

## Q. II, Dub. VIII — the closing grade: *only a theological conclusion* (printed 69)

And therefore the aforesaid rule is true only when the particular is contained under the universal as an individual under its species, or as a species under its genus [...] And so, as regards the confirmation, **a conclusion drawn from a premise assumed of faith and a premise subsumed only morally certain, however necessary the consequence, cannot be of faith**, as is clear in the examples cited; **and therefore it will be only a theological conclusion.**

## Q. II, Dub. VII — canonization: providence bound, lest the whole Church err (printed 66)

**You will say:** in the canonization of saints the Pontiff uses human testimony, which is fallible, and consequently he is not infallible in an act of this kind.

**I answer**, granting the antecedent and denying the consequence, because **he does not use human testimony as a motive of believing, but only as a certain means by which he inquires**; and the inquiry made, the true sanctity of the deceased is established to him: and that inquiry having been made and sure signs of the sanctity of the one to be canonized having been had, **since the Pontiff's attestation is a public testimony for the whole Church, the providence of God is bound to assist him, lest, if it were false, the whole Church should err.**

## Q. II, Dub. V — the three tiers, and the universal decrees (printed 64)

As regards a decree of the first kind, namely one depending upon a fact, **all agree that the Pontiff can err in it**; because in respect of it he does not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, inasmuch as in respect of it he proceeds **as a mere Ecclesiastical Prince relying on human testimonies** — which Innocent III not obscurely declares [...] "the judgment of the Church sometimes follows opinion, which it often happens both to deceive and to be deceived by."

As regards decrees of the second kind, **the Pontiff cannot err in handing down and teaching *ex cathedra* a doctrine of morals necessary to salvation for the whole Church** [...] For if he could err in framing laws of this kind as to their substance, **the whole Church could also err in the business of salvation**; but that this should happen is impossible, since according to the saying of the Saviour the gates of hell shall not prevail against her. The assumption is proved: because the whole Church is bound by Christ's precept to obey the Supreme Pontiff forbidding or commanding something necessary to salvation; and so **if the Pontiff were to err in this, the whole Church would likewise so err.**

## Q. II, Dub. IX — whether the Pope can be deposed (printed 70–71)

**You will ask, whether the Pope can be deposed from the papacy.**

**I answer in the affirmative**: and first indeed, by spontaneous and free abdication, as Celestine V did; secondly, **by coercion and a just sentence**; which is proved from *cap. si Papa distinct. 40*, where it is said that **the Pope is to be judged by no one unless he be found deviating from the faith**, and so **for the crime of heresy alone** [...] **not, however, for any other sins whatsoever, however scandalous**, concerning which the common axiom holds good, **the first see is judged by no one**.

Further, the deposition of a Pontiff in the case of heresy — or rather the declaration by which he is declared deprived of the pontifical dignity — will pertain to a **General Council**, or, that not being assembled, **to the Cardinals having the power of electing a Pontiff**; for the reason that both those powers, namely of electing and of deposing, are reckoned to be connected by natural right.

## *De Haeresi* — not every schismatic is a heretic (printed 162)

**I answer** that every heretic is a schismatic, inasmuch as he withdraws from the universal Church, refusing to give assent to the Articles of faith proposed by her: but conversely **not every schismatic must be a heretic; for it can happen that someone refuse obedience to the Supreme Pontiff, even though he believe him to be Christ's vicar, and assent to all the Articles of faith.**
