| **Discip.** Quae recitasti circa quaesita, ad praesens sufficiunt mihi, et ideo ad aliud, quod magis cordi habeo, festino. Volo enim de haeresibus multa inquirere, sed quia nonnumquam cognitio unius contrariorum ad cognitionem alterius conferre dignoscitur, quaero primo, quae veritates sunt catholicae censendae. **Mag.** Quaestio tua unum videtur supponere, et aliud quaerere; videtur enim supponere quod non omnes veritates sunt catholicae iudicandae, quod beatus Augustinus *in Enchiridion* expresse determinat. Quaerit autem quae sunt illae veritates quae catholicae sunt censendae. **Discip.** Cum beato Augustino illud quod supponit firmiter teneamus, et circa quaesitum sententiam unam vel plures enarra. **Mag.** Circa quaesitum sunt diversae et adversae sententiae. | | Disciple. What you have recounted concerning the matters inquired into suffices me for the present, and so I hasten to another thing which I have more at heart. For I wish to inquire much about heresies, but because sometimes the knowledge of one of two contraries is found to help toward the knowledge of the other, I ask first which truths are to be reckoned catholic. Master. Your question seems to suppose one thing and to ask another. For it seems to suppose that not all truths are to be judged catholic, which the blessed Augustine expressly determines in the Enchiridion. But it asks which are those truths that are to be reckoned catholic. Disciple. With blessed Augustine let us firmly hold what he supposes, and set out concerning the matter asked one opinion or several. Master. Concerning the matter asked there are diverse and opposed opinions. |
| Quarum una est: quod illae solae veritates sunt catholicae reputandae et de necessitate salutis credendae, quae in canone Bibliae explicite vel implicite asseruntur; ita quod si aliquae veritates in Biblia sub forma propria minime continentur, ex solis tamen contentis in ea consequentia necessaria et formali possunt inferri, sunt inter catholicas connumerandae. Sicut haec veritas: *Christus est verus Deus et verus homo*: in tota scriptura divina sub hac serie verborum nullatenus invenitur; quia tamen ex contentis in scriptura sacra consequentia necessaria et formali concluditur, catholica est censenda, et eam credere est necessarium ad salutem. Omnes autem aliae veritates, quae nec in Biblia sunt insertae, nec ex contentis in ea consequentia formali et necessaria possunt inferri, licet in scripturis sanctorum et in diffinitionibus summorum pontificum asserantur, et etiam ab omnibus fidelibus teneantur, non sunt catholicae reputandae; nec est necessarium ad salutem eis per fidem firmiter adhaerere, vel propter eas rationem vel humanum intellectum captivare. | | Of these one is: that those truths alone are to be reckoned catholic and to be believed of necessity for salvation which are asserted in the canon of the Bible explicitly or implicitly; so that if any truths are not contained in the Bible under their proper form, yet can be inferred from what is contained in it alone by a necessary and formal consequence, they are to be counted among the catholic ones. As this truth, "Christ is true God and true man": it is nowhere found in the whole of divine scripture under this series of words; yet because it is concluded from what is contained in sacred scripture by a necessary and formal consequence, it is to be reckoned catholic, and to believe it is necessary for salvation. But all other truths, which are neither inserted in the Bible nor can be inferred from what is contained in it by a formal and necessary consequence — although they be asserted in the writings of the saints and in the definitions of the supreme pontiffs, and even be held by all the faithful — are not to be reckoned catholic; nor is it necessary for salvation to adhere to them firmly by faith, or to take captive one's reason or human understanding on their account. |
| Hanc suam sententiam rationibus et auctoritatibus confirmare nituntur. Auctoritas prima est Salomonis, *Proverbio* 30: *omnis sermo Dei ignitus clypeus est omnibus sperantibus in se; ne addas quicquam verbis illius, et argueris inveniarisque mendax*. Ex quibus colligitur quod verbis divinis, quae in divina scriptura habentur, nihil penitus est addendum tamquam necessarium ad credendum. Hoc etiam testari videtur Moyses *Deuteronomio* quarto capite, et beatus Ioannes *Apocalypsi ultimo*, quorum verba *capitulo secundo* sunt adducta *primi libri*. Ex quibus datur intelligi quod, sicut de scriptura sacra nihil penitus est auferendum, ita nihil est penitus addendum tamquam necessarium ad salutem. | | They strive to confirm this opinion of theirs by reasons and authorities. The first authority is Solomon's, Proverbs 30: "every word of God is a fiery shield to all that hope in him; add nothing to his words, lest thou be reproved and found a liar." From which it is gathered that to the divine words which are had in divine scripture nothing whatever is to be added as necessary to be believed. Moses too seems to testify this, Deuteronomy chapter four, and blessed John, Apocalypse last chapter, whose words are adduced in the second chapter of the first book. From which it is given to understand that, just as nothing whatever is to be taken away from sacred scripture, so nothing whatever is to be added as necessary for salvation. |
| Hanc sententiam auctoritate Augustini conantur ostendere. Unde Augustinus *in quadam epistola ad Hieronymum*, et recitatur *in decretis distinctione 9 capite* "Ego", ait: *Ego solis scripturarum libris (qui iam canonici appellantur) didici hunc timorem honoremque deferre, ut earum nullum auctorem aliquid errasse firmissime credam. Si aliquid autem in eis invenero quod videatur contrarium veritati, nihil aliud quam mendosum codicem esse, vel non esse assecutum interpretem, quod dictum est, vel minime me intellexisse non ambigam:* *alios autem* scriptores ita lego, ut, quantavis sanctitate quantaque doctrina polleant, non ideo verum putem, quia ita ipsi senserunt: sed quia per alios auctores vel canonicas vel probabiles rationes, quod a vero non aberrent, mihi persuadere potuerunt. Ex verbis istis colligitur quod solum canonibus qui in Biblia continentur necesse est firmiter adhaerere; et quod assertionibus aliorum non est necessarium ad salutem firmiter adhaerere. | | They try to show this opinion by the authority of Augustine. Whence Augustine in a certain letter to Jerome, and it is recited in the Decretum, distinction 9, chapter "Ego," says: "I have learned to defer this fear and honour to those books of the scriptures alone which are now called canonical, that I most firmly believe no author of them to have erred in anything. But if I find anything in them that seems contrary to the truth, I shall not doubt that it is either that the manuscript is faulty, or that the translator has not attained what was said, or that I have failed to understand it: but other writers I so read that, with however great holiness and learning they abound, I do not therefore think a thing true because they so held it, but because they were able to persuade me, through other authors or canonical or probable reasons, that they do not stray from the truth." From these words it is gathered that it is necessary to adhere firmly only to the canonical books contained in the Bible; and that it is not necessary for salvation to adhere firmly to the assertions of others. |
| Hoc idem videtur Augustinus *in libro de unico Baptismo*, ut recitatur *distinctione 9 capite* "Quis nesciat", aperte determinare, cum ait: *Quis nesciat sanctam scripturam canonicam tam veteris quam novi testamenti certis suis terminis contineri, eamque posterioribus omnibus episcoporum libris ita praeponi, ut de illa omnino dubitari non possit, utrum verum vel utrum rectum sit, quicquid in ea scriptum esse constiterit; episcoporum autem litteras, quae post confirmatum canonem vel scriptae sunt vel scribuntur, et per sermonem forte sapientiorem cuiuslibet in ea re peritioris, et per aliorum episcoporum graviorem auctoritatem doctioremque prudentiam, et per concilia reprehendi licere, si quid in eis forte a veritate est deviatum.* Ex his verbis colligitur quod de sola sacra scriptura novi et veteris testamenti est illicitum dubitare, utrum sit verum vel rectum, quicquid in ea scriptum esse constiterit. Ergo de omnibus scripturis conciliorum generalium, et consimilium, et quorumcumque aliorum expositorum scripturae divinae, ac etiam Romanorum pontificum, et quorumlibet historiographorum, post canonem editis confirmatum, licet dubitare et discrepare, an a veritate exorbitent, quaecumque scriptura constiterit in eis, antequam scripturae novi et veteris testamenti consona demonstretur. | | The same Augustine seems clearly to determine in the book "On the one Baptism," as it is recited in distinction 9, chapter "Quis nesciat," when he says: "Who does not know that the holy canonical scripture, both of the Old and of the New Testament, is contained within its own fixed limits, and that it is so set above all later books of bishops that of it there can be no doubt at all whether whatever is found written in it be true or right; but that the letters of bishops, which are written after the confirmed canon, may be corrected — whether by the perhaps wiser discourse of anyone more skilled in the matter, or by the weightier authority and more learned prudence of other bishops, or by councils — if anything in them has perhaps deviated from the truth." From these words it is gathered that it is unlawful to doubt only concerning sacred scripture of the New and Old Testament, whether whatever is found written in it be true or right. Therefore concerning all the writings of general councils, and the like, and of any other expositors of divine scripture, and also of the Roman pontiffs, and of any historiographers whatever, drawn up after the confirmation of the canon, it is permitted to doubt and to differ as to whether any writing found in them strays from the truth, until it is shown consonant with the scripture of the New and Old Testament. |
| Item Augustinus *in epistola ad Vincentium*, et allegatur *dist. praedicta capite* "noli", loquens de scripturis posterioribus novo et veteri testamento, ait: *hoc genus litterarum ab auctoritate canonis distinguendum est, non enim sic leguntur, tamquam ex eis testimonium proferatur, ut contra sentire non liceat, ubi forte aliter sapuerint quam veritas postulat.* Ex his habetur quod contra omne genus litterarum post canonem Bibliae licet sentire. His concordare videtur Augustinus *in epistola ad Fortunatum*, et habetur *dist. praefata capite* "Neque", qui ait: *neque quorumlibet disputationes* (id est, exponens secundum glossam, quamvis catholicorum et laudatorum hominum) *velut scripturas canonicas habere debemus, ut nobis non liceat (salva honorificentia quae illis debetur hominibus) aliquid in eorum scriptis improbare atque respuere, si forte invenerimus quod aliter senserint quam veritas habet divino adiutorio vel ab aliis intellecta, vel a nobis.* Ex his concluditur quod nullis assertionibus quorumcumque quae in scripturis canonicis non habentur firmiter assentire quis constringitur. | | Again Augustine, in the letter to Vincentius (cited in the aforesaid distinction, chapter "noli"), speaking of writings later than the New and Old Testament, says: "this kind of writing must be distinguished from the authority of the canon; for they are not read as though testimony were adduced from them such that one may not think otherwise, where perhaps they have thought otherwise than the truth demands." From this it is held that against every kind of writing after the canon of the Bible it is permitted to hold a contrary view. With this Augustine seems to agree in the letter to Fortunatus (had in the aforesaid distinction, chapter "Neque"), who says: "nor ought we to hold the disputations of anyone whatever" (that is, expounding according to the gloss, however catholic and praised the men) "as canonical scriptures, so that it be not lawful for us — saving the honour owed to those men — to disapprove and reject something in their writings, if perchance we find that they have thought otherwise than the truth holds, whether understood by others with God's help, or by ourselves." From this it is concluded that no one is bound to assent firmly to any assertions whatever which are not had in the canonical scriptures. |
| Item de veritatibus quas docuit Augustinus, quod nullus teneatur eas de necessitate salutis recipere nisi in scripturis canonicis habeantur, secundum eundem Augustinum ostendunt. Nam in libro *de Trinitate*, ut *in dist. 19 recitatur capite* "noli", ait: *Noli meis litteris quasi canonicis scripturis inservire: sed in illis quae non credebas, cum inveneris, incunctanter crede. In istis autem quod certum non habeas (nisi certum intellexeris) noli firmum tenere.* Et in epistola *ad Vincentium Victorem, libro 2*, et habetur *dist. 9 capite* "negare", ait: *negare non possum nec debeo, sicut in ipsis maioribus est, ita esse multa in tam multis opusculis meis quae possunt iusto iudicio et nulla temeritate culpari.* Ex his concluditur quod scripturis beati Augustini non est necesse incunctanter adhaerere. Ergo eadem ratione nec scripturis aliorum quorumcumque qui inter scriptores Bibliae non habentur. | | Again, concerning the truths that Augustine taught, they show, according to the same Augustine, that no one is bound to receive them of necessity for salvation unless they are had in the canonical scriptures. For in the book "On the Trinity," as is recited in distinction 19, chapter "noli," he says: "Do not serve my writings as if they were canonical scriptures; but in those things which you did not believe, when you find them there, believe without hesitation. But in these of mine, what you do not hold as certain (unless you understand it as certain) do not hold as firm." And in the letter to Vincentius Victor, book 2 (had in distinction 9, chapter "negare"), he says: "I cannot and ought not to deny that, as it is in the greater writers, so there are many things in my so many little works that can be blamed by a just judgment and with no rashness." From this it is concluded that it is not necessary to adhere without hesitation to the writings of blessed Augustine. Therefore by the same reasoning neither to the writings of any others who are not among the writers of the Bible. |
| Sententiam memoratam etiam rationibus moliuntur ostendere. Quarum prima est haec. Extra illam scripturam nulla catholica veritas invenitur, in qua omnis veritas utilis ad salutem habetur, et omnis falsitas inimica saluti damnatur. Sed secundum Augustinum in scriptura divina quicquid utile est invenitur, et quicquid noxium vel nocivum est damnatur; ergo extra sacram scripturam nulla veritas catholica reperitur. Secunda ratio est haec: Non minus sufficiens pro fidelibus Christianis est novum testamentum una cum veteri, quam fuit solummodo vetus testamentum pro Hebraeis; sed tota fides ad quam astringebantur Hebraei fuit expressa in veteri testamento; ergo et tota fides ad quam de necessitate salutis Christiani tenentur et arcentur in novo testamento et veteri continetur. Ergo Christianus de necessitate salutis non tenetur ad credendum nec credere quod nec in Biblia continetur, nec ex solis contentis in Biblia potest consequentia necessaria et manifesta inferri. Tertio sic. Illud quod eadem facilitate contemnitur qua probatur, ad fidem catholicam minime spectat, esto quod sit verum, non debet inter veritates catholicas numerari; sed secundum Hieronymum de scripturis divinis loquentem, *quod de scripturis auctoritatem non habet, eadem facilitate contemnitur qua probatur*; ergo nulla auctoritas quae ex Scripturis auctoritatem non habet est inter veritates catholicas computanda. | | They endeavour to show the said opinion also by reasons. Of which the first is this. Outside that scripture no catholic truth is found; in it every truth useful for salvation is had, and every falsehood hostile to salvation is condemned. But according to Augustine, in divine scripture whatever is useful is found, and whatever is harmful or hurtful is condemned; therefore outside sacred scripture no catholic truth is found. The second reason is this: the New Testament together with the Old is no less sufficient for Christian faithful than the Old Testament alone was for the Hebrews; but the whole faith to which the Hebrews were bound was expressed in the Old Testament; therefore also the whole faith to which Christians are bound and confined of necessity for salvation is contained in the New Testament and the Old. Therefore a Christian is not bound of necessity for salvation to believe, nor to believe, what is neither contained in the Bible nor can be inferred by a necessary and manifest consequence from what is contained in the Bible alone. Third, thus: that which is despised with the same ease with which it is proved does not pertain to the catholic faith; granted that it be true, it ought not to be numbered among catholic truths. But according to Jerome, speaking of the divine scriptures, "what does not have its authority from the scriptures is despised with the same ease with which it is proved"; therefore no authority which does not have its authority from the Scriptures is to be counted among catholic truths. |

| **Mag.** Sed alii isti sententiae nequaquam consentiunt, dicentes quod multae sunt veritates catholicae et fidem sapientes catholicam, quae nec in divinis scripturis habentur explicite, nec ex solis contentis in eis possunt inferri, quibus tamen fidem indubiam explicitam vel implicitam adhibere est necessarium ad salutem. Ad cuius evidentiam dicunt esse sciendum quod, praeter veritates circa quas licitum est sic vel aliter opinari, veritates quas quilibet catholicus certa credulitate explicite vel implicite tenere astringitur possunt in triplici differentia reperiri. | | Master. But others by no means consent to that opinion, saying that there are many truths catholic and savouring of catholic faith which are neither had explicitly in the divine scriptures nor can be inferred from what is contained in them alone, to which nevertheless it is necessary for salvation to give undoubting faith, explicit or implicit. For the evidence of which they say it must be known that, besides the truths concerning which it is lawful to hold this or that opinion, the truths which every catholic is bound with a certain belief, explicitly or implicitly, to hold can be found in a threefold distinction. |
| Quaedam enim sunt de Deo et Christo secundum humanitatem, ex quibus principaliter salus nostra dependet, sicut quod unus est Deus et tres sunt personae, quod Christus est verus Deus et verus homo, passus et mortuus, et quod resurrexit et ascendit, etc. | | For some are concerning God and Christ according to his humanity, on which our salvation principally depends — as that God is one and there are three persons, that Christ is true God and true man, suffered and died, and that he rose again and ascended, etc. |
| Aliae sunt veritates ex quibus non ita principaliter pendet salus humana, eas tamen oportet firma fide tenere, quia ex revelatione vel approbatione Dei (cui nullus debet catholicus dissentire) ad orthodoxorum notitiam pervenerunt, quibus mediantibus eas fideles posteri susceperunt. Huiusmodi veritates sunt complures in canone confirmato contentae, in quo etiam multae habentur de creaturis, etiam de infidelibus hominibus, quae non directe sed indirecte quodammodo ad salutem humani generis pertinere noscuntur, sicut quod Pharao, Madianitae, Chananaei, et alii infideles quamplurimi multas terras occupaverunt, et filios Israel multipliciter afflixerunt. Nonnullae etiam veritates huiusmodi extra praedictum canonem continentur, quae tamen per revelationem et approbationem divinam mediantibus Apostolis ad catholicos pervenerunt; quia Christus, dum viveret in carne mortali cum Apostolis, multa docuit eos et fecit coram eis quae tamen in Biblia non habentur. Ex revelatione etiam Spiritus sancti (qui secundum promissionem Christi *docturus erat Apostolos omnem veritatem*) multa quae non habentur in sacris litteris didicerunt, quae postea catholicos docuerunt. Omnes veritates praedictas, et quae ex eis consequentia necessaria omni tempore necessitatem habente possunt inferri, dicunt isti catholicas esse tenendas. | | There are other truths on which human salvation does not so principally depend, yet which must be held by firm faith, because by the revelation or approval of God (from whom no catholic ought to dissent) they came to the notice of the orthodox, by whose mediation the faithful who came after received them. Truths of this kind are very many, contained in the confirmed canon, in which also many are had concerning creatures, even concerning unbelieving men, which are known to pertain not directly but somehow indirectly to the salvation of the human race — as that Pharaoh, the Midianites, the Canaanites, and very many other unbelievers occupied many lands and afflicted the children of Israel in many ways. Some truths of this kind are also contained outside the aforesaid canon, which nevertheless came to catholics by revelation and divine approval through the mediation of the Apostles; for Christ, while he lived in mortal flesh with the Apostles, taught them and did before them many things which nevertheless are not had in the Bible. By the revelation also of the Holy Spirit (who, according to Christ's promise, "was to teach the Apostles all truth") they learned many things not had in the sacred writings, which afterward they taught to catholics. All the aforesaid truths, and those which can be inferred from them by a consequence necessary at every time and holding of necessity, these men say are to be held as catholic. |
| Praeter veritates vero praedictas dicunt esse quasdam veritates alias, quae ex solis contentis in scriptura divina et veritatibus quae ad nos per Apostolos pervenerunt concludi non possunt, quae tamen ex praedictis veritatibus, vel aliqua earum, et quibusdam aliis veris quae in facto consistunt, quae vera negari non possunt, manifeste sequuntur. Et de talibus veritatibus, quae ad notitiam pervenerunt, exemplificare nituntur, dicentes quod talis veritas est illa: *Regulae a sanctis Patribus (scilicet Basilio, Benedicto, Augustino, et Francisco) institutae sunt licitae, meritoriae et perfectae*: quia licet ista veritas ex solis contentis in scriptura sacra et veritatibus quas ab Apostolis ecclesia universalis accepit inferri non possit, cum de istis sanctis et gestis ac regulis institutis ab eis nulla in praedictis veritatibus mentio habeatur, illa tamen veritas simul ex scripturis divinis et gestis et actibus praedictorum sanctorum (de quibus gestis et actibus christiano rationali sufficienter potest fieri fides) videtur evidenter. | | But besides the aforesaid truths they say there are certain other truths which cannot be concluded from what is contained in divine scripture alone and the truths which came to us through the Apostles, but which nevertheless follow manifestly from the aforesaid truths, or some of them, together with certain other true things that consist in fact, which true things cannot be denied. And they endeavour to give an example of such truths which have come to notice, saying that such a truth is this: "The rules instituted by the holy Fathers (namely Basil, Benedict, Augustine, and Francis) are lawful, meritorious, and perfect." For although this truth cannot be inferred from what is contained in sacred scripture alone and the truths which the universal church received from the Apostles — since in the aforesaid truths there is no mention of these saints and of the deeds and rules instituted by them — yet that truth is seen evidently from the divine scriptures together with the deeds and acts of the aforesaid saints (concerning which deeds and acts sufficient belief can be produced for a rational Christian). |
| Tales etiam veritates sunt illae: *fides quam tenuit beatus Augustinus fuit et est catholica putanda*; *Symbolum Athanasii est catholicum reputandum et fidele*; *sancta quatuor concilia generalia rite celebrata veritatem catholicam diffinierunt*; et huiusmodi multae, quae ex solis contentis in divina scriptura et doctrina Apostolica inferri non possunt, cum Augustinus et Athanasius tunc non fuerunt in rerum natura, nec tunc illa concilia fuerunt celebrata; ex contentis tamen in illis veris evidenter possunt concludi. Tales veritates dicunt esse quamplurimas, quarum aliquae doctrinis authenticis sunt insertae, nonnullae vero in scripturis non habentur. Istas veritates dicunt non esse catholicas, accipiendo vocabulum *catholicae* stricte, sed dicunt eas sapere catholicam veritatem, etiamsi ex veris aliis inferantur. Et ideo, ut dicunt, large accipiendo *veritates catholicas*, possunt veritates catholicae nuncupari; et illas veritates quas dicunt sapere catholicam veritatem existimant ab omni fideli tenendas, saltem implicite. | | Such truths also are these: "the faith which blessed Augustine held was and is to be reckoned catholic"; "the Athanasian Creed is to be reckoned catholic and faithful"; "the four holy general councils duly celebrated defined catholic truth"; and many of this kind, which cannot be inferred from what is contained in divine scripture and Apostolic doctrine alone, since Augustine and Athanasius were not then in the nature of things, nor were those councils then celebrated; yet from what is contained in those true things they can evidently be concluded. Such truths they say are very many, of which some are inserted in authentic doctrines, while some are not had in the scriptures. These truths they say are not catholic, taking the word "catholic" strictly, but they say they savour of catholic truth, even if they be inferred from other true things. And therefore, as they say, taking "catholic truths" broadly, they can be called catholic truths; and those truths which they say savour of catholic truth they judge to be held by every faithful person, at least implicitly. |
| Adhuc sunt aliae veritates quas dicunt solummodo in facto consistere, cuiusmodi sunt veritates de gestis ecclesiae et sanctorum, quae in gestis, cronicis et historiis fide dignis habentur, quas etiam asserunt a fidelibus minime respuendas. Ex his omnibus isti concludunt quod multae sunt veritates catholicae quae nec in scriptura sacra continentur explicite, nec ex solis contentis in ea possunt inferri. Et multae etiam sunt veritates aliae quas oportet certa fide tenere. | | Still there are other truths which they say consist only in fact, of which kind are the truths concerning the deeds of the church and of the saints, which are had in deeds, chronicles, and trustworthy histories, which also they assert are by no means to be rejected by the faithful. From all this these men conclude that there are many catholic truths which are neither contained explicitly in sacred scripture nor can be inferred from what is contained in it alone. And there are also many other truths which must be held by a certain faith. |

| **Discip.** Licet circa praedicta mihi multae dubitationes occurrant, illis tamen praetermissis ad intentum principale revertor. Mihi autem videtur probabile quod nullus inferior papa potest aliquam assertionem non damnatam explicite tamquam haereticam excommunicare vel damnare explicite; sed adhuc ignoro quo fundamento papa vel concilium generale, in damnando explicite aliquam assertionem non damnatam tamquam haereticam, debet inniti. Unde de hoc quid sentiunt [… *paucae voces evanidae* …] dicere non postponas. **Mag.** Circa interrogationem tuam opiniones contrarias re[cito]. | | Disciple. Although many doubts occur to me concerning the aforesaid, yet, leaving them aside, I return to the principal intent. It seems probable to me that no pope's inferior can excommunicate or condemn explicitly, as heretical, any assertion not condemned explicitly; but I still do not know on what foundation the pope or a general council, in condemning explicitly some assertion not condemned as heretical, ought to rely. Whence do not put off telling [me] what they think about this. Master. Concerning your inquiry I recount contrary opinions. |
| Sunt enim quidam tenentes quod tantae auctoritatis est papa quod ad placitum potest quamcumque assertionem tamquam haereticam damnare, et isti dicuntur esse imitatores papae Sergii, persecutoris papae Formosi, qui (ut in quodam libro de antiquissima litera legi) in tantum volebant papam extollere quod asserebant *papam non posse damnari, sed quod, quicquid faceret, salvaretur*; et isti dicunt quod, quicquid papa diffinit esse tenendum, omnes tenere et credere [debent]. Therefore let us for now pass over this, and show, as you promised, the contrary assertion. |
| **Mag.** Alii asserunt manifeste quod papa et concilium generale ac etiam universalis ecclesia, si recte damnat aliquam assertionem tamquam haereticam (stricte loquendo de assertione haeretica), uni vel pluribus de tribus fundamentis debet inniti et se patenter fundare. Primum est super sacram scripturam, et isti fundamento innitebantur concilia generalia principalia, haereses Arii, Macedonii, Nestorii, Eutichis et Dioscori condemnando. Sicut enim aliqua istorum conciliorum, condendo symbola, in auctoritate sacrae scripturae se fundabant, teste Isidoro, qui habetur *dist. 15 capite 1*, ait: *Sancti patres in concilio Niceno, de omni orbe terrarum convenientes, iuxta fidem catholicam et apostolicam post Apostolos symbolum tradiderunt*: isti haereses condemnando pro fundamento sacras literas posuerunt. Sic etiam Alexander tertius, damnando assertionem dicentem quod *Christus non est aliquid secundum quod homo*, in hac veritate a divina scriptura accepta, *Christus est verus Deus et homo*, se fundavit. Sic etiam sancti Doctores, reprobando ipsas, per scripturas convincere conabantur, sicut in [pluribus] communiter patenter habetur; unde et Isidorus, ut habetur *24 q. 3 c.* "notandum", plures haereses per scripturas sacras arguit evidenter. | | Master. Others assert manifestly that the pope and a general council, and even the universal church, if it rightly condemns some assertion as heretical (strictly speaking of a heretical assertion), ought to rely on one or several of three foundations and plainly ground itself. The first is upon sacred scripture, and on this foundation the principal general councils relied in condemning the heresies of Arius, Macedonius, Nestorius, Eutyches, and Dioscorus. For as some of those councils, in framing creeds, grounded themselves on the authority of sacred scripture — Isidore witnessing, who is had in distinction 15, chapter 1, saying: "The holy fathers in the council of Nicaea, gathering from the whole world, handed down a creed according to the catholic and apostolic faith after the Apostles" — these, in condemning heresies, set the sacred writings as their foundation. So too Alexander III, in condemning the assertion saying that "Christ is not something in that he is man," grounded himself on this truth taken from divine scripture: "Christ is true God and man." So too the holy Doctors, in reproving them, tried to convince by the scriptures, as is commonly and plainly had in many places; whence also Isidore, as is had in 24 q. 3 c. "notandum," evidently argues against many heresies by the sacred scriptures. |

| Tertium fundamentum est revelatio vel inspiratio nova divina. Si enim aliqua veritas aeterna, quae pertinet ad salutem, de novo revelaretur ecclesiae, ista esset tamquam catholica approbanda, et omnem falsitatem ei contrariam posset ecclesia et etiam papa tamquam haereticam condemnare. Et quamvis isti exemplum nesciant invenire (quod umquam ecclesia aliquam haeresim condemnando se in tali revelatione vel inspiratione fundaverit), tamen dicunt quod hoc non est impossibile, quia posset Deus, si sibi placeret, multas veritates catholicas noviter revelare vel inspirare. **Discip.** Quid si papa, vel etiam concilium generale, dicat sibi aliquam veritatem esse revelatam a Deo, vel etiam inspiratam: numquid alii fideles credere astringuntur? **Mag.** Dicunt isti quod absque miraculo manifesto non est eis credendum, quia non sufficit nude asserere quod eis est veritas revelata vel etiam inspirata, sed oportet quod talem revelationem seu inspirationem miraculi operatione confirment apta. | | The third foundation is a new divine revelation or inspiration. For if some eternal truth pertaining to salvation were newly revealed to the church, it would be approvable as catholic, and every falsehood contrary to it the church, and even the pope, could condemn as heretical. And although these men cannot find an example (that the church ever, in condemning some heresy, grounded itself on such a revelation or inspiration), nevertheless they say this is not impossible, for God could, if it pleased him, newly reveal or inspire many catholic truths. Disciple. What if the pope, or even a general council, should say that some truth has been revealed to him by God, or even inspired: are other faithful bound to believe? Master. These men say that without a manifest miracle they are not to be believed, because it does not suffice nakedly to assert that a truth has been revealed or inspired to them, but it is necessary that they confirm such a revelation or inspiration by a fitting working of a miracle. |
| **Discip.** Quid dicerent isti, si omnes Christiani nullo excepto aliquam assertionem tamquam haereticam firmiter acceptarent, quam tamen nec ex scripturis divinis, nec ex aliqua doctrina ab ecclesia procedente possent ostendere? **Mag.** Dicunt quod talis veritas esset tamquam catholica acceptanda, quia talis concors adhaesio omnium Christianorum, nullo excepto, alicui assertioni huiusmodi sine miraculo non potest contingere; cum enim omnes fideles firmissime teneant quod, iuxta promissionem Salvatoris, *Matthaei ultimo*, *vobiscum sum usque ad consummationem seculi*, ecclesia universalis numquam errabit, constat quod sine speciali inspiratione divina numquam ecclesia universalis assertioni quae non dependet ex doctrina ecclesiae praecedenti firmiter adhaerebit; et ideo tunc miraculum fieri esset credendum. | | Disciple. What would these men say if all Christians, none excepted, were firmly to accept some assertion as heretical, which nevertheless they could show neither from the divine scriptures nor from any doctrine proceeding from the church? Master. They say that such a truth would be acceptable as catholic, because such a concordant adherence of all Christians, none excepted, to any assertion of this kind cannot happen without a miracle; for since all the faithful most firmly hold that, according to the Saviour's promise, Matthew last chapter, "I am with you unto the consummation of the age," the universal church will never err, it is established that without a special divine inspiration the universal church will never firmly adhere to an assertion which does not depend on the preceding doctrine of the church; and therefore it would then have to be believed that a miracle was wrought. |
| **Discip.** Quid sentiunt, si omnes Christiani propter paucos vel unum assertioni tali (quae ex doctrina ecclesiae praecedenti probari non posset) adhaererent? **Mag.** Dicunt quod, si unus solus dissentiret, non esset talis veritas acceptanda, quia in uno solo potest stare tota fides ecclesiae, quemadmodum tempore mortis Christi tota fides ecclesiae in sola beata virgine remanebat; nec est etiam credendum quod omni tempore post tempora Apostolorum fuerint aliqui magis accepti Deo quam fuerint Apostoli ante mortem Christi. Si ergo Christus post crucifixionem suam permisit cunctos Apostolos a fide catholica deviare, et solam beatam virginem firmiter permanere in fide, temerarium est asserere quod numquam ante finem mundi Deus permittat totam multitudinem Christianorum praeter unum a fide recedere orthodoxa. | | Disciple. What do they think, if all Christians on account of a few, or of one, were to adhere to such an assertion (which could not be proved from the preceding doctrine of the church)? Master. They say that, if one alone dissented, such a truth would not be acceptable, because in one alone the whole faith of the church can stand — just as at the time of Christ's death the whole faith of the church remained in the blessed Virgin alone; nor is it to be believed that at every time after the times of the Apostles there have been any more accepted by God than the Apostles were before Christ's death. If therefore Christ, after his crucifixion, permitted all the Apostles to deviate from the catholic faith, and the blessed Virgin alone to remain firm in the faith, it is rash to assert that never before the end of the world will God permit the whole multitude of Christians, save one, to fall away from the orthodox faith. |
| **Discip.** Ista nescio improbare, sed videtur quod ad hoc, ut omnes catholici teneantur alicui veritati noviter revelatae firmiter adhaerere, non sufficit eam operatione miraculi confirmare, cum per malos et infideles fiant mirabilia, sicut ex verbis evangelii, *Matthaei 7*, colligitur, et apparet Christo dicente: *multi dicent mihi in illa die, Domine, Domine, nonne in nomine tuo prophetavimus, et in nomine tuo daemonia eiecimus, et in nomine tuo multas virtutes fecimus? confitebor illis quia numquam novi vos* — hoc est, numquam approbavi vos. Quando ergo fit verum miraculum ad assertionem aliquam confirmandam, tenendum est indubie quod talis assertio pro vera debet haberi; et hoc sive illi quibus revelata fuerint inter bonos, sive inter malos computentur. | | Disciple. This I do not know how to disprove; but it seems that for all catholics to be bound to adhere firmly to some newly revealed truth, it does not suffice to confirm it by the working of a miracle, since marvels are done by the wicked and by unbelievers, as is gathered from the words of the gospel, Matthew 7, and appears from Christ's saying: "many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in thy name, and in thy name cast out demons, and in thy name do many mighty works? I will confess to them that I never knew you" — that is, I never approved you. To this objection they respond, saying that, although marvels are done by the wicked, yet a true miracle is never done to confirm a falsehood asserted by the wicked; and therefore when they teach some error, to confirm that error a true miracle is never done; but if they preach the truth, though they be wicked, marvels are sometimes done to confirm the truth — as Judas the traitor, though he was wicked, yet because he taught the truth, worked miracles. When therefore a true miracle is done to confirm some assertion, it must undoubtedly be held that such an assertion ought to be taken as true; and this whether those to whom it was revealed be counted among the good or among the wicked. |
| **Discip.** Ista responsio est apparens, unde quae dixisti de illa secunda sententia sufficiant; sed si nosti aliquos aliter dicere, non differas indicare. **Mag.** Nonnulli putant quod tam concilium generale quam etiam papa, in damnando aliquam assertionem tamquam haereticam, in sacra scriptura divina sola debet se fundare, quia solae veritates in scriptura sacra contentae, et quae sequuntur ex eis, catholicae sunt censendae. De hac sententia tractatus praecedens te poterit informare. | | Disciple. This response is plausible, so that what you have said concerning that second opinion may suffice; but if you know some who say otherwise, do not delay to indicate it. Master. Some think that both a general council and also the pope, in condemning some assertion as heretical, ought to ground itself on divine sacred scripture alone, because only the truths contained in sacred scripture, and those which follow from them, are to be reckoned catholic. | **Prima obiectio.** Et prima quidem obiectio est quod non potest papa haereticari nec contra fidem errare. Sed huic cavillationi leviter potest obviari, primo quidem exemplo in sacra scriptura fundato. Unde beatus Petrus, qui fuit papa longe melior et sanctior Iohanne XXII, erravit in fide, quia contra veritatem evangelii — ut beatus Paulus aperte fatetur ad Galatas 2 capitulo, sic inquiens: Cum autem venisset Cephas Antiochiam, in faciem ei restiti, quia reprehensibilis erat. Et infra: sed cum vidissem quod non recte ambularent [pr. ambulavit] ad veritatem evangelii, dixi Cephae coram omnibus: si tu, cum Iudaeus sis, gentiliter et non Iudaice vivis, quomodo gentes cogis Iudaizare? — et ibi expresse affirmat apostolus quod beatus Petrus non ambulavit ad veritatem evangelii, et per consequens contra fidem erravit. Quare sequitur quod papa potest contra fidem errare. Verumtamen beatus Petrus non fuit haereticus, quia errori suo pertinaciter non adhaesit, immo correptionem beati Pauli benigne suscipiens continuo se correxit; et si suo errori pertinaciter adhaesisset, fuisset haereticus infallibiliter iudicandus. De isto etiam errore Petri habetur in decretis 2 quaestione 7 [?] Volo, ubi sic legitur: Petrus cogebat genus Iudaizare et a veritate evangelii recedere, cum Iudaei gregem faciebant et a cibis gentilium latenter se subtrahebant. Par autem est in se [et] exorbitare et alios verbo et exemplo a fide deicere. | | The first objection is that a pope cannot become a heretic nor err against the faith. But this cavil is easily met, first by an example founded on Holy Scripture. For blessed Peter, who was a pope far better and holier than John XXII, erred in the faith, because [he acted] against the truth of the Gospel — as blessed Paul openly admits in Galatians ch. 2, saying: But when Cephas came to Antioch, I withstood him to his face, because he was blameworthy. And below: but when I saw that they were not walking rightly toward the truth of the Gospel, I said to Cephas before them all: if you, being a Jew, live like a gentile and not like a Jew, how do you compel the gentiles to Judaize? — and there the apostle expressly affirms that blessed Peter did not walk toward the truth of the Gospel, and consequently erred against the faith. Hence it follows that a pope can err against the faith. Nevertheless blessed Peter was not a heretic, because he did not pertinaciously cling to his error; rather, benignly receiving blessed Paul's rebuke, he corrected himself at once. And had he pertinaciously clung to his error, he would infallibly have had to be judged a heretic. Of this error of Peter it is also found in the Decretum, C.2 q.7 [?] "Volo," where it reads: Peter was compelling the [gentiles] to Judaize and to withdraw from the truth of the Gospel, when the Jews made a faction and secretly withdrew from the foods of the gentiles. | **Quarta obiectio.** Quarta obiectio cavillosa est quod ad fratres minores non pertinet de causa fidei se intromittere, sed solummodo ad prelatos, cuiusmodi sunt cardinales, archiepiscopi et episcopi. Sed ista cavillatio faciliter expugnatur, quia quaestio fidei ad omnes pertinet christianos, et non solum ad prelatos, immo etiam ad laicos. Unde etiam in decretis dist. 96 c. Ubinam dicit Nicolaus papa: Ubinam legistis imperatores antecessores vestros synodalibus conventibus interfuisse, nisi forsitan de quibusdam ubi de fide tractandum est, quae causa universalis est, quae omnium communis est, quae non solum ad clericos verum etiam ad laicos et ad omnes omnino pertinet christianos. Igitur ad minores pertinet de causa fidei se intromittere, cum sint de numero christianorum reputandi. Quod etiam ratione patet: nam quod omnes tangit, ab omnibus tractari debet; sed fides christiana omnes tangit christianos; igitur omnes christiani de fide impu[gnanda]... | | The fourth cavilling objection is that it does not pertain to the Friars Minor to involve themselves in a matter of faith, but only to prelates — such as cardinals, archbishops and bishops. But this cavil is easily overthrown, because a question of faith pertains to all Christians, and not only to prelates, indeed even to laymen. Hence in the Decretum, dist. 96 c. Ubinam, Pope Nicholas says: Where have you read that emperors, your predecessors, took part in synodal assemblies, except perhaps in certain ones where a matter of faith was to be treated — which is a universal cause, common to all, pertaining not only to clerics but also to laymen and to absolutely all Christians? Therefore it pertains to the Friars Minor to involve themselves in a matter of faith, since they are to be counted among the number of Christians. This is also plain by reason: for what touches all ought to be treated by all; but the Christian faith touches all Christians; therefore all Christians [have a part] in [defending] the faith [when it is attacked]... |
| ...gnanda habent se intromittere, et ipsam quantum possunt defensare. Praeterea, qui errori cum potest non resistit, ipsum errorem convincitur approbare, ut patet per sacros canones quamplures. Unde Innocentius papa, ut habetur dist. 83 c. Error, dicit sic: error cui non resistitur approbatur, et veritas cum minime defensatur opprimitur; nec caret scrupulo societatis occultae qui manifesto facinori desinit obviare. Facietis, inquit: proculdubio culpam habet qui quod potest corrigere negligit emendare. Ex quibus aperte patet quod si fratres minores nolunt fautorum haeresum supradictorum reputari, debent et tenentur viis eis possibilibus eisdem resistere et fortiter obviare. | | ...to involve themselves in impugning [error against] the faith, and to defend it as far as they can. Moreover, whoever, when he can, does not resist an error, is convicted of approving that error, as is plain from very many sacred canons. Hence Pope Innocent, as is had at dist. 83 c. Error, says thus: an error not resisted is approved, and truth, when it is not defended, is oppressed; nor is he free from the taint of secret collusion who ceases to oppose a manifest crime. And Pope Leo, as is had in the Decretals, "on heretics," c. Qui alios, says: he who, when he can, does not recall others from error, shows himself to be in error. And so a pope, as is had at dist. 86 c. Facietis, says: without doubt he bears guilt who neglects to amend what he can correct. From which it is clearly plain that if the Friars Minor do not wish to be reckoned abettors of the aforesaid heresies, they ought and are bound, by every means possible to them, to resist and boldly oppose them. |
| **Quinta obiectio.** Quinta obiectio cavillosa est quod, quamvis Iohannes XXII errores et hereses dogmatizaverit quamplures, tamen de pertinacia non potest convinci, et ideo praesumendum est quod talia dixit solummodo recitando. Sed ista cavillatio potest multipliciter confutari, eo quod multipliciter eum fuisse pertinacem potest demonstrari. Primo, quia saepius et legitime admonitus, et eos de erroribus resistentes hostiliter verbis et factis est persecutus, ipsos diffamando, excommunicando, quos potuit etiam capiendo, incarcerando; et plures indies [in diris?] sceleratos suis erroribus consentientes ad dignitates ecclesiasticas promovit et exaltavit. Quarto, quia nullum permisit magistrari Parisius in theologica facultate, nisi prius, tactis sacrosanctis, iuraret... | | The fifth cavilling objection is that, although John XXII dogmatized very many errors and heresies, nevertheless he cannot be convicted of pertinacity, and therefore it must be presumed that he said such things only by way of reporting them. But this cavil can be refuted in many ways, since his pertinacity can be shown in many ways. First, because, having been often and lawfully admonished, he persecuted with hostility, in word and deed, those who resisted him concerning his errors, defaming them, excommunicating them, and, whom he could, even seizing and imprisoning them; and he promoted and exalted to ecclesiastical dignities more and more wicked men daily[?] who consented to his errors. Fourth, because he allowed no one to be made a master at Paris in the faculty of theology unless he first swore, upon the holy [Gospels]... |

Sixth, because when those appealing from him and impugning his errors had appealed to a general council duly and lawfully to be convened and held, and offered themselves ready to come to that council set in a safe place, wishing to show the world their innocence, he likewise prevented the council from being held, unwilling that his wickednesses should be discussed and ventilated. | **Sexta obiectio.** Sexta obiectio cavillosa est quod, cum multitudo christianorum praedictum Iohannem XXII usque ad mortem pro papa habuerint, et absque impugnatione dictorum suorum eidem obedierint, fratres minores ipsius impugnatores, eo quod pauci fuerunt, debebant se multitudini per omnia conformasse. Sed huic cavillationi respondet ipse deus, qui Exodi 23 c. praecipit dicens: non sequeris turbam ad faciendum malum, nec in iudicio plurimorum acquiesces sententiae, ut a vero devies. Quae auctoritas sufficiat pro illa obiectione. | | The sixth cavilling objection is that, since the multitude of Christians held the aforesaid John XXII as pope until his death, and obeyed him without impugning his sayings, the Friars Minor who impugned him — because they were few — ought to have conformed themselves in all things to the multitude. But to this cavil God himself replies, who in Exodus ch. 23 commands, saying: you shall not follow the crowd to do evil, nor in judgment shall you acquiesce in the opinion of the many so as to stray from the truth. Let this authority suffice against that objection. |
| **Septima obiectio.** Septima obiectio cavillosa est quod non potest negari quin ipsi fratres minores Iohanni XXII se opponentes sunt excommunicati, cum ipse Iohannes vicesimus [secundus] saepe et saepius per se et per alios sententiam excommunicationis protulit contra eos. Sed ad istam cavillationem leviter potest responderi, eo quod canonicae sanctiones aperte testantur nullum haereticum quic[quam]... ...habere potestatis aut iuris. Unde Ciprianus, ut 24 q. 1 c. Didicimus ait: didicimus omnes omnino haereticos et scismaticos nihil habere potestatis ac iuris. Item eadem causa q. 1 c. Si ergo, sic habetur: si ergo episcopus in haeresi iam damnata lapsus est, antiqua excommunicatione damnatus, alios damnare non potest; excommunicatus enim alios excommunicare non valet. Certum est autem quod omnis haeresis est damnata, et omnis haereticus est excommunicatus per concilium generale, ut habetur extra de haereticis c. Excommunicamus et c. Excommunicamus secundo. Igitur Iohannes vicesimus secundus incidit in hereses damnatas, et ideo fuit excommunicatus, et per consequens alios excommunicare non potuit. Item eadem [causa] q. 1 c. Praedictis, si autem, sic habetur: Si autem ex corde suo novam haeresim confinxerit — ex quo talia praedicare coepit, neminem damnare potuit, quia non potest damnare quicquam iam prostratus. Et eodem c. et 9 [?]. Item [...] sic habetur: liquido constat eum qui ab integritate catholicae fidei recedit maledicendi vel bene[dicendi]... | | ...that no heretic has any power or right whatsoever. Hence Cyprian, at C.24 q.1 c. Didicimus, says: we have learned that all heretics and schismatics whatsoever have no power or right. Likewise the same cause, q.1 c. Si ergo, has it thus: if therefore a bishop has lapsed into a heresy already condemned, being condemned by the ancient excommunication, he cannot condemn others; for an excommunicate cannot excommunicate others. Now it is certain that every heresy is condemned, and every heretic is excommunicated by the general council, as is had in the Decretals, "on heretics," c. Excommunicamus and c. Excommunicamus secundo. Therefore John XXII fell into condemned heresies, and so was excommunicated, and consequently could not excommunicate others. Likewise the same cause, q.1 c. Predictis, "si autem," has it thus: But if from his own heart he has fabricated a new heresy — from the moment he began to preach such things, he could condemn no one, because one already laid low cannot condemn anything. And in the same canon, and [§]9[?]. Likewise [...] it stands thus: it clearly holds that he who departs from the integrity of the catholic faith is known to have no power at all of cursing or bless[ing]... |

| ...benedicendi potestatem minime habere dinoscitur; catholicum namque, utpote se superiorem, maledicere non valet; in alienum a fide tanquam in sibi aequalem sententiam dare non potest. Et eodem c., 2 q. [7?], his auctoritatibus dicitur sic: his auctoritatibus perspicue monstratur quod ex quo aliquis contra fidem temptat aliqua dicere, nec deicere [?] aliquem valet nec damnare. Ex quibus luculenter ostenditur quod praedictus Iohannes vicesimus secundus, haeresi (ut probatum est) multipliciter maculatus, fratres minores ab ipso appellantes et eius hereses improbantes excommunicare non potuit quoquomodo. | | ...of blessing; for a catholic, being his superior, he cannot curse; against one estranged from the faith he cannot pass sentence as against one equal to himself. | Rursum, esto sine praeiudicio quod Iohannes vicesimus secundus non fuisset haereticus (quod falsum est), adhuc sua excommunicationis sententia in praedictos fratres lata omnino fuisset nulla. Nam per appellationem rite interpositam eximitur appellans a potestate et iurisdictione illius a quo appellavit, extra de appellationibus c. Proposuit. Et appellatione pendente non potest nec debet in praeiudicium appellantis aliquid innovari, nec ei gravamen aliquod inferri, 2 q. 7 [?] c. Appellatione, et ff. "nihil innovari appellatione pendente" l. 1 et 16. Ad quem appellatum est habet de tali appellatione cognoscere, et non is a quo appellatum est, extra de appellationibus c. Ut debitus et c. Cum speciali. Si autem ille a quo appellatur posset de tali appellatione cognoscere, in causa propria ius sibi dicere posset; sed nullus debet in causa propria ius sibi dicere, 3 q. 6 [?] c. Inter querelas, et q. [?] de occidendis, et q. [?] c. Ne quis in causa [sua] iudicet vel ius sibi dicat, per totum. Sed Iohannes XXII post et contra appellationem legitime interpositam, et non ante, praefatos fratres minores de facto, licet de iure non posset, excommunicavit; quare, esto quod idem Iohannes tunc temporis fuisset verus papa (quod non est verum), sententia sua contra memoratos fratres lata nulla fuit omnino reputanda. | | Again, grant without prejudice that John XXII had not been a heretic (which is false); even so his sentence of excommunication passed against the aforesaid friars would have been utterly void. For by a duly interposed appeal the appellant is exempted from the power and jurisdiction of him from whom he appealed (Decretals, "on appeals," c. Proposuit). And while an appeal is pending nothing can or ought to be changed to the prejudice of the appellant, nor any burden imposed upon him (C.2 q.7[?] c. Appellatione, and the Digest title "that nothing be changed while an appeal is pending," laws 1 and 16). It belongs to the one to whom appeal is made to take cognizance of such an appeal, and not to the one from whom appeal is made (Decretals, "on appeals," c. Ut debitus and c. Cum speciali). But if the one from whom appeal is made could take cognizance of such an appeal, he could pronounce the law for himself in his own cause; but no one ought to pronounce the law for himself in his own cause (C.3 q.6[?] c. Inter querelas, and q.[?] "de occidendis," and q.[?] c. "Let no one judge or pronounce the law for himself in his own cause," throughout). But John XXII, after and against a lawfully interposed appeal — and not before — excommunicated the aforesaid Friars Minor in fact, though he could not in law; wherefore, grant that the same John had at that time been a true pope (which is not true), his sentence passed against the said friars was to be reckoned altogether void. |
| **Octava obiectio.** Octava obiectio cavillosa est quod, quantumcumque Iohannes XXII erraverit et pertinax haereticus fuerit, successor suus dominus Benedictus XII est catholicus et verus papa, et ideo supranominati fratres, Iohanni XXII resistentes, in hoc quod Benedicto XII non parent, immo (ut dicitur) ipsum realiter impugnant, damnabiliter deliquerunt et inciderunt in poenas rebellibus Romanae ecclesiae a iure taxatas. Sed huic cavillationi brevibus verbis respondetur, quod solo nomine Benedictus duodecimus incidit in laqueum tortuosum, errores et hereses praedecessoris sui defendendo et approbando suos et suas facit esse. Ipse enim doc[trinam]... ...trinam pestiferam praedecessoris sui approbat et potenter defendit, ostendens se cum praedecessore suo praefato haereticum esse manifestum, ut signanter de eo posset dici verbum Ecclesiastici 30 c. sic scriptum, ubi sic legitur: mortuus est pater illius, et quasi non est mortuus, similem enim sibi reliquit post se. Quare non debet dici papa catholicus, sed fautor haeretici et haereticae pravitatis, immo haereticus et peior quam fuerit praedecessor suus, teste Urbano papa qui, ut habetur 24 q. 3 c. Qui aliorum, dicit in haec verba: Qui aliorum errorem defendit multo est damnabilior illis qui errant, quia non solum ille errat sed etiam aliis offendicula erroris praeparat et confirmat. Unde quia magister erroris est, non tantum haereticus sed etiam heresiarcha dicendus est. Praeterea iste, nomine non vere Benedictus XII, ideo in sacra pagina magister, et ideo citius et virilius deberet haeresibus resistere et fidem catholicam defendere quam praedecessor suus, qui in facultate theologica omnino fuit ignarus. Ergo favendo haeresibus et haereticae pravitati, necnon ipsis haereticis, damnabilior est censendus quam praedecessor suus, et per consequens non summus pontifex, sed immo pontifex est merito nominandus. Amplius, isto nomine non vere Benedictus, peior est heresis auctor quam fuerit praedecessor suus; et quod simpliciter conatur totam fidem catholicam ponere in voluntate et dispositione sua et successorum suorum, sed in quadam constitutione — immo verius destitutione — quam ordini fratrum minorum (ut fertur) tenendam imposuit, indicat manifeste. Quae quidem [constitutio], praefatam haeresim retro saeculis inauditam continens, talis est: districtius inhibemus ne, postquam super negotio fidei quaestio seu dubitatio aliqua super qua sunt opiniones ad[verse]... | | The eighth cavilling objection is that, however much John XXII erred and was a pertinacious heretic, his successor the lord Benedict XII is a catholic and true pope, and therefore the above-named friars, in resisting John XXII — in that they do not obey Benedict XII, indeed (as is said) really impugn him — have sinned damnably and have fallen into the penalties fixed by law for rebels against the Roman church. But to this cavil the reply is brief: that "Benedict the Twelfth" only in name has fallen into a tortuous snare, and by defending and approving the errors and heresies of his predecessor makes them his own. For he approves and powerfully defends the pestilent teaching of his predecessor, showing himself, along with his aforesaid predecessor, manifestly a heretic — so that the word of Ecclesiasticus [Sirach] ch. 30 could pointedly be said of him, where it is written: his father is dead, and it is as if he were not dead, for he has left one like himself behind him. Wherefore he ought not to be called a catholic pope, but an abettor of a heretic and of heretical wickedness — indeed a heretic and worse than his predecessor was — as Pope Urban testifies, who (as is had at C.24 q.3 c. Qui aliorum) says in these words: He who defends the error of others is much more damnable than those who err, because not only does he err himself but he also prepares and confirms stumbling-blocks of error for others. Hence, because he is a master of error, he is to be called not only a heretic but even a heresiarch. Moreover this man, "Benedict XII" in name not in truth, is for that reason a master in Sacred Scripture, and therefore ought the more quickly and manfully to resist heresies and defend the catholic faith than his predecessor, who was wholly ignorant in the faculty of theology. Therefore, by favouring heresies and heretical wickedness, and the heretics themselves, he is to be reckoned more damnable than his predecessor, and consequently is deservedly to be named not "supreme pontiff" but rather [merely] "pontifex." Furthermore, this "Benedict" in name not in truth is a worse author of heresy than his predecessor was; and that he simply attempts to place the whole catholic faith in his own will and disposition and that of his successors, he manifestly shows in a certain constitution — indeed, more truly, a destitution — which (as is reported) he imposed on the Order of Friars Minor to be held. This [constitution], containing the aforesaid heresy unheard of in past ages, is as follows: We strictly forbid that, after any question or doubt on a matter of faith concerning which there are opinions op[posed]... |

| ...verse vel diverse deducta fuerit ad apostolicae sedis examen, quisquam extunc alterutram partem declinare, eligere vel approbare praesumat, sed super ea sedis eiusdem iudicium seu declaratio expectetur. Haec verba eius veneno plena, ex quibus expresse sequitur quod nulli revelato a deo (si super eo moveatur quaestio seu dubitatio coram papa, de quo etiam possunt esse opiniones diversae et adversae) debet aliquis frater minor, nec per consequens aliquis christianus, firmiter adhaerere, quia non debet secundum praefatam destitutionem suam alterutram partem eligere, determinare vel approbare, sed voluntatem papae in hoc expectare. Sed de omni revelato a deo potest [quaestio] mota deduci ad examen summi pontificis, et etiam possunt esse opiniones diversae et adversae. Ergo nulli tali revelato a deo debet frater minor, et per consequens nec christianus, ante sententiam summi pontificis firmiter adhaerere, sed voluntatem summi pontificis totaliter expectare. Et ideo potest dici de articulis fidei et de omni contento in scriptura divina, quia super quolibet articulo fidei et super omni contento in scriptura divina potest moveri quaestio, et ad examen deduci summi pontificis, super quo possunt esse etiam opiniones diversae et adversae. Ergo, secundum mandatum istius nomine non vere Benedicti XII, nulli in articulo fidei in scriptura sacra contento (si super eo moveatur quaestio quae ducatur ad examen summi pontificis, et super eo possunt esse opiniones diversae et adversae) debet aliquis frater minor, nec per consequens christianus quicumque, quamcumque partem determinare, eligere vel approbare. Quod est erroneum, iniquum et blasphemum. Nam ex eo sequitur: ergo, si talis quaestio [moveatur]: fuit deus ab aeterno vel non, vel est christiana fides vera vel non — et sic de aliis quibuscumque quaestionibus moveatur coram summo pontifice, cum possunt de huiusmodi quaestione esse inter catholicos et haereticos opiniones diversae et adversae — talis quaestionis nullus frater minor, et eadem ratione nullus christianus, debet veritatem confiteri, donec summus pontifex suam declaraverit voluntatem. Quod non est aliud quam fidem nostram totaliter in arbitrio hominis constituere, et quod credere debeamus ab eo expectare. Sed hoc sapit haeresim manifestam, cum dicat beatus Paulus apostolus 1 Cor. 2: fides vestra non sit in sapientia hominum, sed in virtute dei. | | ...posed or divergent has been brought to the examination of the apostolic see, anyone should thenceforth presume to incline to, choose, or approve either side; but let the judgment or declaration of that same see be awaited upon it. These words of his are full of poison, from which it expressly follows that to nothing revealed by God (if a question or doubt be raised about it before the pope, concerning which there can also be opposed and adverse opinions) ought any Friar Minor — nor consequently any Christian — firmly to adhere, because, according to his aforesaid "destitution," he ought not to choose, determine, or approve either side, but to await the pope's will in the matter. But concerning everything revealed by God, a question once raised can be brought to the examination of the supreme pontiff, and there can also be opposed and adverse opinions. Therefore to no such thing revealed by God ought a Friar Minor — and consequently no Christian — firmly to adhere before the supreme pontiff's sentence, but to await the will of the supreme pontiff entirely. And so the same may be said of the articles of faith and of everything contained in divine Scripture, because about any article of faith and about everything contained in divine Scripture a question can be raised and brought to the examination of the supreme pontiff, concerning which there can also be opposed and adverse opinions. Therefore, according to the mandate of this "Benedict XII" in name not in truth, to no article of faith contained in Sacred Scripture (if a question be raised about it that is brought to the examination of the supreme pontiff, and there can be opposed and adverse opinions about it) ought any Friar Minor — nor consequently any Christian whatever — to determine, choose, or approve any side. Which is erroneous, iniquitous, and blasphemous. For from it follows: therefore, if such a question be raised — was God from eternity or not, or is the Christian faith true or not, and so on for any other questions raised before the supreme pontiff, since about a question of this kind there can be, between catholics and heretics, opposed and adverse opinions — of such a question no Friar Minor, and by the same reasoning no Christian, ought to confess the truth until the supreme pontiff has declared his will. Which is nothing else than to place our faith wholly in the discretion of a man, and [to hold] that we must await from him what we are to believe. But this smacks of manifest heresy, since blessed Paul the Apostle says (1 Cor. 2): let your faith not be in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God. |
| Unde licet ille, nomine non vere Benedictus XII, praedecessori suo in doctrina haeretica nunquam participasset, nec hereses ipsius tanquam proprias defendisset et approbasset, tamen propter istam solam haeresim, cuius ipse est auctor, inventor et institutor, est inter haereticos computandus, et ab omnibus catholicis facta ipsius scientibus merito detestandus; cum, dogmatizans contra fidem catholicam maxime scienter, non solum a iure humano sed etiam a scriptura sacra sit a parte excommunicatus. Quamvis enim in Apocalypsi dicatur quod clamant voce magna dicentes "usquequo, Domine sanctus et verus, non iudicas et vindicas sanguinem nostrum de his qui habitant in terra" — ex quibus verbis sequitur quod desiderant, implorant et sperant vindictam de sanguine proprio, et ideo habent actum sperandi et etiam visionem claram et perfectam — non tamen habetur in Apocalypsi quod animae sanctorum murmurando implorent vindictam praedictam. For although it is said in the Apocalypse that they cry with a loud voice saying "how long, O Lord holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth" — from which words it follows that they desire, implore, and hope for the avenging of their own blood, and therefore have an act of hoping and also the clear and perfect vision — nevertheless it is not held in the Apocalypse that the souls of the saints, murmuring, implore the aforesaid vengeance. For the word "to murmur" in divine scripture is always, or more frequently, taken in a bad sense, and seems to have some kind of sadness annexed to it; but the souls of the saints elicit no such act nor have any sadness, and therefore it is not to be granted that they murmur about the delay of vengeance. It seems, then, that in the aforesaid words, when he says "murmuring they implore," he also hints at a new manifest heresy, namely that the saints in heaven are not wholly freed from every evil of guilt and punishment. |

From which words it is evidently gathered that blessed Paul was caught up to the vision of God's substance; and yet Paul's soul after his death was exalted to a clearer vision. Sic summo nihil est inter illa quae possunt eidem rei competere altius, licet Deus sit altior tali summo. Accipiendo autem summum tertio modo, sic summo nihil est altius in tali specie; unde intensissima nigredine nullus color niger est altior ipsa, tamen est aliquis alius color altior. Sic enim patet quod summum potest capi multipliciter, et ideo ista "summo nihil est altius" habet varios sensus, quorum unus est verus et alius falsus. | | ...and greatest. Taking "highest," then, in the first way, thus nothing is higher than the highest. Taking "highest" in the second way, thus nothing specifically distinct is higher than the highest: in the genus of virtues no virtue distinct from charity is higher than charity, yet one charity is higher than another; so in the genus of the cognitions of God no cognition distinct from the clear vision of God is higher than the clear vision, yet one clear vision of the divine essence is higher than another — for the vision by which the soul of Christ sees God is clearer than the vision by which an angel sees God. And therefore, although the clear vision of God is the highest among the cognitions by which God is known, it is nevertheless not unfitting that souls should first see God less clearly and afterward be exalted to a clearer vision. Thus nothing is higher than the highest among those things that can belong to the same subject, although God is higher than such a "highest." But taking "highest" in the third way, thus nothing is higher than the highest in such a species; hence than the most intense blackness no black color is higher, yet there is some other color higher. For thus it is clear that "highest" can be taken in many ways, and therefore that saying, "nothing is higher than the highest," has various senses, of which one is true and another false. |
| Cum vero accipit tertio quod visio clara est summa exaltatio animarum, patet per praedicta quod ista varios habet sensus falsos, quia ista falsa est accipiendo summum tertio modo; accipiendo etiam summum quarto, est falsa, quia visio clara quam nunc habent animae sanctae non est summa exaltatio animarum, pro eo quod non est clarissima visio quam habebunt — habitura enim sunt post iudicium clariorem. Accipiendo autem summum primo, sic non est ad propositum. Accipiendo autem secundo, sic diversae sunt diversae opiniones, sicut dictum est: quibusdam dicentibus quod clara visio est perfectior dilectione seu fruitione Dei — et illi dicerent quod visio clara est summa exaltatio animarum, sic accipiendo summum; aliis dicentibus quod dilectio seu fruitio est perfectior et altior visione — et illi dicerent quod visio non est summa exaltatio animarum, sed fruitio. | | But when he takes, thirdly, that the clear vision is the highest exaltation of souls, it is clear from what has been said that this has various false senses, because it is false taking "highest" in the third way; and taking "highest" in the fourth way it is also false, because the clear vision which the holy souls now have is not the highest exaltation of souls, for the reason that it is not the clearest vision which they will have — for they are going to have a clearer one after the judgment. But taking "highest" in the first way, thus it is not to the purpose. And taking it in the second way, thus there are diverse opinions, as has been said: some saying that the clear vision is more perfect than the love or fruition of God — and they would say that the clear vision is the highest exaltation of souls, taking "highest" thus; others saying that the love or fruition is more perfect and higher than the vision — and they would say that the vision is not the highest exaltation of souls, but the fruition. |
| Cum vero quarto dicit quod in die iudicii cum Deus sanctos et illorum animas exaltabit, ista conceditur sub isto intellectu, quod animae sanctorum tunc habebunt clariorem visionem Dei, et etiam gaudium de visione corporum gloriosorum et de completione ciuitatis Ierusalem et aliis multis; et ista erit exaltatio animarum. Sanctos vero, id est personas integras compositas ex corpore et anima, exaltabit, quod tunc dabit eis beatitudinem perfectam quam ipsi prius non habuerunt — immo nec ipsi videbunt prius Deum, licet animae sanctorum, et non ipsi, modo videant Deum. Dicendum est quod male infert, si propositiones praecedentes sane intelligantur; si autem intelligantur sicut isti intelligunt, secundum quod dictum est, sic ex propositionibus hereticalibus infert conclusionem hereticam. Intelligit enim quod visio clara sic est summa exaltatio animarum quod anima videns Deum nihil potest acquirere nec aliquo modo proficere, nisi aliquid non habitum desiderare. Et iste sensus est erroneus, quia sequeretur quod anima Christi vidisset Deum ab instanti conceptionis Christi. | | But when he says, fourthly, that on the day of judgment God will exalt the saints and their souls, this is granted under this understanding: that the souls of the saints will then have a clearer vision of God, and also joy at the vision of the glorious bodies and at the completion of the city of Jerusalem and many other things; and this will be an exaltation of souls. And the saints — that is, the whole persons composed of body and soul — he will exalt, in that he will then give them the perfect beatitude which they did not have before; indeed the saints themselves will not see God before, although the souls of the saints, and not the saints themselves, now see God. It must be said that he infers badly, if the preceding propositions are soundly understood; but if they are understood as these people understand them, according to what has been said, thus from heretical propositions he infers a heretical conclusion. For he understands that the clear vision is so the highest exaltation of souls that a soul seeing God can acquire nothing nor in any way advance, except to desire something not yet had. And this meaning is erroneous, because it would follow that the soul of Christ had seen God from the instant of Christ's conception. |
| Cum autem sexto dicit "alias tunc non" [exaltarentur], | | But when he says, sixthly, "otherwise they would then not [be exalted]"... |
| exaltarentur, hoc non sequitur ex praecedentibus, sicut ostensum est; quia, sicut dictum est, quamvis sanctorum animae videant Deum, tamen animae ad clariorem visionem poterunt exaltari, et ipsi sancti, quando resurgent in corpore, ad visionem divinae essentiae et impassibilitatem et alias dotes corporis et animae poterunt exaltari. | | ...they would be exalted — this does not follow from the premises, as has been shown; because, as has been said, although the souls of the saints see God, nevertheless the souls will be able to be exalted to a clearer vision, and the saints themselves, when they rise again in the body, will be able to be exalted to the vision of the divine essence and to impassibility and the other endowments of body and soul. |
| Cum sic conatur probare minorem — scilicet quod Deus in die iudicii sanctos et illorum animas exaltabit — per hoc quod usque ad diem iudicii sunt sub altari, et post diem iudicii videbunt Deum facie ad faciem, dicendum est quod iste textus libri Apocalypsis male intelligit. Non enim ideo animae martyrum dicuntur sub altari usque ad diem iudicii quia non vident deitatem Christi, sed tantum humanitatem — sic iste textus fere exponitur — sed dicuntur sub altari quia, absconse nobis et existentibus in inferno et in purgatorio, saltem multis ab omni malo protectis, clare Deum vident et ipso fruuntur. Porro, quia quantum ad hoc motivum in beato Bernardo nititur se fundare, verba eiusdem beati Bernardi non intelligit, quia non intendit nisi quod sancti secundum animas collocantur sub Christo et Deum vident, donec ipsi sancti integri et perfecti secundum corpus et animam procedant, completo numero fratrum, et participent regnum. | | When he thus tries to prove the minor — namely that God on the day of judgment will exalt the saints and their souls — by the fact that until the day of judgment they are under the altar, and after the day of judgment they will see God face to face, it must be said that he misunderstands that text of the book of the Apocalypse. For the souls of the martyrs are not said to be under the altar until the day of judgment for the reason that they do not see the deity of Christ, but only his humanity — thus is that text generally expounded — but they are said to be under the altar because, hidden from us and from those existing in hell and in purgatory, at least protected by many from every evil, they see God clearly and enjoy him. Moreover, because as to this motive he strives to ground himself in blessed Bernard, he does not understand the words of that same blessed Bernard, because he intends nothing but that the saints as to their souls are placed under Christ and see God, until the saints themselves, whole and perfect as to body and soul, come forth, the number of the brethren being completed, and share in the kingdom. |
| Sed forte diceret aliquis: si sancti tunc participant regnum, et regnum est visio Dei, igitur antea non habebunt visionem Dei. Ad hoc dicendum quod, licet quandoque regnum vocatur clara visio Dei — sicut etiam regnum Dei nonnunquam vocatur ecclesia praesens — tamen interdum regnum Dei appellatur beatitudo consummata, comprehendens animae et corporis beatitudinem; et istud regnum Dei de communi lege sancti ante diem iudicii nequaquam participabunt — immo ipsi, ex anima et corpore constituti, non habebunt de communi lege prius visionem Dei, sed animae ipsorum tantummodo; et de isto regno et gloria animae et corporis loquitur ibi. | | But perhaps someone would say: if the saints then share in the kingdom, and the kingdom is the vision of God, then before that they will not have the vision of God. To this it must be said that, although sometimes the kingdom is called the clear vision of God — as also the kingdom of God is sometimes called the present Church — nevertheless at times the kingdom of God is called the consummated beatitude, comprehending the beatitude of both soul and body; and in this kingdom of God the saints by common law will in no way share before the day of judgment — indeed they themselves, constituted of soul and body, will not by common law have the vision of God beforehand, but their souls only; and it is of this kingdom and glory of soul and body that he speaks there. |
| Sed adhuc quaereret aliquis quomodo sancti erunt super altare post iudicium secundum beatum Bernardum, cum nunquam erunt super Christum. Ad hoc potest responderi dupliciter: uno modo quod in scriptura divina non legitur quod animae sanctorum post iudicium erunt super altare, nec memini me legisse quod hoc Bernardus dicat. Aliter potest dici quod non possunt dici animae super altare post iudicium, quia tunc non erunt post iudicium quibusdam electis absconditae; nunc autem dicuntur sub altari quia, videntes Deum, quasi absconse consolantur et gaudent in Deo — gloria enim eorum latet plures electos et plures existentes in inferno, et ideo possunt dici sub altari, quia ibidem absconditae. | | But someone would still ask how the saints will be above the altar after the judgment, according to blessed Bernard, since they will never be above Christ. To this it can be answered in two ways: in one way, that it is not read in divine scripture that the souls of the saints will be above the altar after the judgment, nor do I recall having read that Bernard says this. In another way it can be said that the souls cannot be said to be above the altar after the judgment, because then, after the judgment, they will not be hidden from certain of the elect; but now they are said to be under the altar because, seeing God, they are as it were hiddenly consoled and rejoice in God — for their glory is hidden from many of the elect and from many who are in hell, and therefore they can be said to be under the altar, because hidden there. |
| Cum vero ultimo allegat beatum Petrum dicentem "ut nos exaltet in salutem" etc., dicendum est quod auctoritas Petri non facit pro isto, quia beatus Petrus loquebatur de hominibus integris ex anima et corpore constitutis; et verum est quod illi exaltabuntur ad visionem claram in die iudicii, et non ante de communi lege, quia, licet animae multorum antea clare videant Deum, tamen homines compositi ex corpore et anima de communi lege antea non videbunt Deum. Et per istum modum omnes fere auctoritates, quas pro heresi supradicta in duobus maximis tractatibus [adducit], | | But when he lastly alleges blessed Peter saying "that he may exalt us unto salvation," etc., it must be said that the authority of Peter does not make for his case, because blessed Peter was speaking of whole men constituted of soul and body; and it is true that they will be exalted to the clear vision on the day of judgment, and not before, by common law, because, although the souls of many see God clearly beforehand, nevertheless men composed of body and soul will by common law not see God beforehand. And in this way almost all the authorities which, on behalf of the aforesaid heresy, he adduces in his two greatest treatises... |
| prolixis adducit, solvuntur, quia loquuntur de electis compositis ex corpore et anima, qui de communi lege Deum ante diem iudicii non videbunt. | | ...in his two greatest prolix treatises, are resolved, because they speak of the elect composed of body and soul, who by common law will not see God before the day of judgment. |
| **Capitulum tertium** | | **Capitulum tertium** |
| Tertia via super qua se fundavit est haec: | | The third way on which he grounded himself is this: |
| > Quia (ut dicit) visio beata non stat cum doctrina, quia quicunque clare videt Deum videt et omnia, secundum Gregorium dicentem: "quid est quod non videt, qui videntem omnia videt?" Sed secundum beatum Augustinum, de cura pro mortuis, animae sanctorum illa quae fiunt hic apud viventes ex se non cognoscunt, sed eis innotescunt aut per animas noviter decedentium seu morientium, aut per revelationes angelorum qui curam habent de viventibus, aut per revelationem immediatam a Deo; cum igitur non videant omnia, et per consequens nec facialiter clare vident Deum. | | > *Because (as he says) the beatific vision does not stand together with instruction, for whoever clearly sees God sees also all things, according to Gregory saying: "what is there that he does not see, who sees Him who sees all things?" But according to blessed Augustine, in the work "On the Care of the Dead," the souls of the saints do not of themselves know the things that happen here among the living, but these become known to them either through the souls of those newly departing or dying, or through the revelations of the angels who have care of the living, or through an immediate revelation from God; since therefore they do not see all things, consequently neither do they see God facially and clearly. |
| Haec ratio eius tertia in una heresi manifesta est fundata, quod scilicet clara visio Dei sine omni scientia esse non potest, vel saltem non est sine omni scientia; quia asserit manifeste quod videntes Deum nulla ignorant, igitur omnia sciunt. Sed haec heresis scripturae divinae aperte repugnat. Ait enim apostolus ad Ephesios 3: "mihi enim omnium sanctorum minimo data est gratia haec, in gentibus evangelizare investigabiles divitias Christi, et illuminare omnes quae sit dispensatio sacramenti absconditi a saeculis in Deo, qui omnia creavit, ut innotescat principibus et potestatibus in caelestibus per ecclesiam multiformis sapientia Dei." | | This third argument of his is founded on one manifest heresy, namely that the clear vision of God cannot exist without all knowledge, or at least is not without all knowledge; because he manifestly asserts that those seeing God are ignorant of nothing, and therefore know all things. But this heresy openly contradicts divine scripture. For the Apostle says to the Ephesians 3: "to me, the least of all the saints, is given this grace, to preach among the nations the unsearchable riches of Christ, and to enlighten all men what is the dispensation of the mystery hidden from ages in God who created all things, that the manifold wisdom of God may be made known to the principalities and powers in the heavenly places through the Church." |
| Ex quibus verbis ultimate colligitur quod angeli sancti ante incarnationem et passionem Christi nonnulla secreta de incarnatione Christi ignorabant; angeli autem tunc viderunt Deum; igitur non omnes videntes Deum omnia sciunt, immo multa ignorant. Quod vero, secundum intentionem apostoli, angeli sancti videntes Deum aliqua de incarnationis mysterio ignorabant, glossa super verba praemissa asserit manifeste, dicens in persona beati Pauli et exponens verba eius praescripta: "quia datum est mihi evangelizare et illuminare et videre — quid hoc est? quia per hoc aliquid accipient qui multa secreta in hoc didicerunt; et hoc quod ait 'evangelizare', dico ita ut multiformis sapientia Dei de reparatione hominum innotescat per ecclesiam, quae dona Dei recipit, id est per apostolos in ecclesia praedicantes principibus et potestatibus (id est per diuersos ordines angelorum qui sunt in caelestibus, id est in caelo ubi et nos erimus)." Dicit namque beatus Hieronymus authenticas dignitates supra memoratum mysterium aliquid pure non intellexisse, donec impleta est passio Christi et apostolorum praedicatio per gentes dilatata. Unde in Isaia angeli admirantes dixerunt "quis est iste qui venit de Edom?", et in psalmo "quis est iste rex gloriae?". Non solum igitur patriarchas et prophetas, sed etiam potestates caelestes multiformis sapientia Dei per ecclesiam est revelata. Et post: "multiformis igitur dicitur Dei sapientia, quia multipliciter multas species et formas habens, principes et potestates per ecclesiam agnoverunt." Ex his omnibus colligitur evidenter quod angelis videntibus Deum aliquid accrevit... | | From which words it is finally gathered that the holy angels before the incarnation and passion of Christ were ignorant of certain secrets concerning the incarnation of Christ; but the angels then already saw God; therefore not all who see God know all things, but rather are ignorant of many. And that, according to the intention of the Apostle, the holy angels who see God were ignorant of certain things concerning the mystery of the incarnation, the gloss on the aforesaid words manifestly asserts, speaking in the person of blessed Paul and expounding his above-written words: "because it is given to me to evangelize and to enlighten and to see — what is this? because through this they will receive something who have learned many secrets in it; and as for what he says, 'to evangelize,' I say this, that the manifold wisdom of God concerning the restoration of men may be made known through the Church, which receives the gifts of God — that is, through the apostles preaching in the Church to the principalities and powers, that is, through the diverse orders of angels who are in the heavenly places, that is, in heaven where we too shall be." For blessed Jerome says that the authentic dignities did not purely understand the above-mentioned mystery until the passion of Christ was accomplished and the preaching of the apostles was spread among the nations. Whence in Isaiah the wondering angels said, "who is this that comes from Edom?", and in the psalm, "who is this king of glory?" Not only, therefore, to the patriarchs and prophets, but also to the heavenly powers has the manifold wisdom of God been revealed through the Church. And afterward: "the wisdom of God, therefore, is called manifold because, having in manifold manner many species and forms, the principalities and powers came to know it through the Church." From all these things it is evidently gathered that to the angels seeing God something accrued... |
