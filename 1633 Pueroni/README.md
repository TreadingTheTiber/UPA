The chapter is preceded by cap. VII, "Vt credatur hic Pontifex Petri successor" (printed 183–226), and prefaced by its own summarium of 66 numbered rubrics (printed 226–229). Printed page = PDF page − 12; the recto foot-line reads "PVERONIS TOM. 2." throughout, which settles the author's name against the garbled census forms.*

*Note on this entry: this entry closes one of the two unidentified names in the census. Diana's affirmative roll-call (the 1655 entry, copied by Murga in 1684) lists "***Puerorum****, de Reg. Aristocratia tom. 2. cap. 8. per tot.*"; Murga prints "Peuronus." Both garble* ***Pueronus*** *— Dom Domenico Pueroni of Cremona, Abbot General of the Olivetan Benedictines — and the citation proves exact: tome II of his* Institutiones *is devoted to the papacy ("in which, concerning its mystical Empyrean Heaven,* ***the Supreme Pontiff***, *the graver controversies are elucidated"), and its* ***caput VIII*** *carries as its running head the thesis itself: "***Hic Pontifex fide divina credendus***." Diana's "per totum" is literal — it is a* ***fifty-four-page chapter on our question***, *among the longest sustained treatments in this collection, by an author the census had lost. The thesis is stated without hedging (n. 36): "with open mouth we pronounce that* ***the assent of divine faith is demanded***." *The ground is the acceptance, and the reigning pope is named per tempora, in capitals, as the worked case (n. 48): "***the concordant acclamation of the faithful, venerating, adoring, and proclaiming URBAN VIII as true Supreme Pontiff, is in evidence of credibility in no way inferior to any other evidences which can be had by us concerning the credibility of the remaining Mysteries of the Catholic faith***" — the acceptance placed on a par with the credibility-evidence of the mysteries themselves; and the pope appears elsewhere in the chapter under his birth-name,* ***MAPHÆUS BARBERINUS***, *in the manner of Torquemada's Nicholas V and Amico's "Cardinal Pamphili." The certification is corporate and universal (n. 45): "***the legitimate election of each and every Roman Pontiff, approved by the judgment of the universal Church***" — with the completeness clause immediately after (n. 50): "***Beware, however, lest you attribute these infallible authorities to a Church that is not universal***," neither in time of schism nor in particular Churches. What distinguishes Pueroni from every other affirmative in the collection is the* ***mechanism*** *he assigns, stated at nn. 51–52. The Holy Spirit's assistance is not given the universal Church to teach infallibly; therefore "***to another infallibility of the Church — the passive, namely, in believing — we reduce the certitude: not of definition, for which there is no need, since the matter has long since been revealed, but of explication, concerning this proposition:*** ***URBAN VIII IS THE UNDOUBTED REVEALED PONTIFF***." *The Church's* ***infallibility in credendo*** *supplies the sufficient proposition of a truth already revealed — the structure the later* fides ecclesiastica *school (this collection's 1b-ii; cf. the 1874 Schrader and 1961 Van Noort entries) would systematize, stated here in 1633 and expressly distinguished from infallibility* in docendo. *Three further features earn the entry its place. First, n. 22 answers the supply-theory on its own ground: to the adversary who takes refuge in divine providence — that God will never fail a pope reputed to hold due power — Pueroni replies, "***why do you not more truly and more fittingly, speaking of that same Divine providence, confess that it wholly concerns Him to provide that the universal Church be never deceived in a matter so grave, by bestowing pontifical honour upon an undeserving man?***" — providence prevents the false pope rather than repairing him, the argument Amico makes at his n. 184 and Haunold makes against Lessius and Tanner. Second, nn. 20–21 dismantle, as "***an egregious fiction; a subtle theorem indeed***," the attempt to split* ***pontifical dignity*** *from* ***certain pontifical power*** *— the very distinction on which the modern sedeprivationist thesis rests, rejected here three centuries before it was proposed. Third, n. 28 supplies the Constance credendum in its full form — heretics being reconciled must believe "the canonically elected Pope, whoever shall exist for the time being (***his name being expressed***)" — the same text Torquemada, Arnaldo Albertini, Arriaga and Córdoba each turn to their own use. And n. 54 records the candour that marks the best authors on this question: no Pontiff has ever pronounced upon it, by his own definition or by conciliar decree, "***not even in time of schism, when it would have seemed most needful, for the establishing of the obedience of the faithful***." Classification is* ***1a*** *(divine faith of the singular, demanded expressly, with the reigning pope named), with the note recording that his proposal-mechanism is the Church's passive infallibility* in credendo *— a 1b-ii-style apparatus put to a 1a conclusion.*

## n. 36 — the thesis (printed 256)

**36.** Coming now to close quarters with the matter, **with open mouth we pronounce that the assent of divine faith is demanded** [...]

## n. 45 — the judgment of the universal Church (printed 263)

**45.** Say therefore most constantly, that **the legitimate election of each and every Roman Pontiff, approved by the judgment of the universal Church**, [...]

## n. 48 — the acclamation of the faithful, with Urban VIII named (printed 264)

**48.** The first is indeed evident; since **the concordant acclamation of the faithful, venerating, adoring, and proclaiming URBAN VIII as true Supreme Pontiff, is in evidence of credibility in no way inferior to any other evidences which can be had by us concerning the credibility of the remaining Mysteries of the Catholic faith.**

## n. 50 — the completeness clause (printed 265)

**50.** **Beware, however, lest you attribute these infallible authorities to a Church that is not universal**: wherefore, since in time of Schism the true and universal Church is not established [...]

## nn. 51–52 — infallibility *in credendo*, not *in docendo* (printed 266–267)

**51.** Nay, neither is the assistance of the Holy Spirit attributed to the universal Church itself, **infallibly to teach** [...] **52.** **To another infallibility of the Church, therefore — the passive, namely, in believing — we reduce the certitude: not of definition, for which there is no need, since the matter has long since been revealed, but of explication, concerning this proposition: URBAN VIII IS THE UNDOUBTED REVEALED PONTIFF.**

## n. 22 — providence prevents rather than repairs (printed 243–244)

**22.** And as for the refuge you have had recourse to in Divine providence — that, for the establishing of His Kingdom of Holy Church, God will never fail a Pontiff reputed to hold the power due to him — **why do you not more truly and more fittingly, speaking of that same Divine providence, confess that it wholly concerns Him to provide that the universal Church be never deceived in a matter so grave, by bestowing pontifical honour upon an undeserving man?**
