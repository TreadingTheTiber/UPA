only, as a certain learned man interprets Thomas, in itself, but also as regards us and on the part

# SIC-NOTES, UNCERTAIN READINGS, IMAGE/OCR DISAGREEMENTS, DAMAGE

## A. Printer's errors and anomalous forms (read from the image, retained in the text)

1. **fol. 4v** — `Romanus Puntifex uicarius esse Christi` — **`Puntifex` sic**, for *Pontifex*.
   Verified at 6× magnification; the *u* is unambiguous, no macron. The OCR layer reads the same.
   (Translated simply as "the Roman Pontiff".)
2. **fol. 4v** — the chapter's opening sentence reads `CIRCA authoritatem uero condentium hoc
   **derretum** aduertendum est…` — **`derretum` sic**, for *decretum*. Verified at 7×; the double
   *r* is unmistakable. (This falls just outside the excerpt translated, but is recorded because it
   bears on the printer's accuracy in this forme.) The OCR layer reads the same.
3. **fol. 5r** — `Cardinalis sante crucis` — **`sante` sic**, for *sanctæ* (Marcello Cervini,
   Cardinal of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme). Verified at 6×; there is no abbreviation mark.
   Italianate form. (Englished as "Cardinal of Santa Croce".)
4. **fol. 5r** — `nempe uniuersalem ecclesiam representans` and `quin & uniuersalem Ecclesiam
   representauerit` are printed **without æ/ę**, while `quod eam repræsentabat` three lines earlier
   is printed **with æ**. All three verified. This is the clearest single instance of the printer's
   mixing; see convention 2. (The distinction is orthographic only and cannot be shown in English.)
5. **fol. 5r** — after `Ecclesia Dei uno,` and before `eóque` there is a small raised ink mark
   resembling an apostrophe or an inverted comma. It is almost certainly a stray ink speck or a
   broken sort, not a mark of punctuation; **not** transcribed, and so not represented in the
   translation. Flagged as uncertain.
6. **fol. 144v** — `Veniens de præsbytero non baptizato` — **`præsbytero` with æ**, whereas the same
   decretal is cited at fol. 216r marginal as `de presbytero` **without**. Both verified at 5–6×.
   Both forms are kept in Latin in the translation, so the alternation remains visible.
7. **fol. 199v** — `Nónne ante approbationem Ecclesię fides in phophetis` — **`phophetis` sic**, for
   *prophetis* (dropped *r*). Verified at 2.2×; the word is unambiguous. Note that `prophetas` is
   spelled correctly nine lines above on the same page. (Translated as "the prophets".)
8. **fol. 199v** — `quam expertis & demostratis` — **`demostratis` sic**, for *demonstratis*.
   Verified at 8×: there is **no** macron over the *o*, so this is a genuine omission and not a
   contraction I have failed to expand. (Translated as "demonstrated".)
9. **fol. 200r** — `Idenque patet de propositionibus` — **`Idenque` sic**, for *Idemque*. Confirmed
   by the OCR layer. The same form recurs at fol. 216r (`Idenque & de quibuscunque alijs`), so it is
   a consistent house form rather than a one-off slip. (Translated as "The same is clear…" and "And
   the same we presume…".)
10. **fol. 200r** — `Christus secundum corpus` (line 6): the **r of `Christus` is badly under-inked
    /broken**, printing as a faint vertical stub, so the word images as `Ch·istus`. Verified at 8×.
    Read as `Christus`; recorded here as damage to the sort, not a variant reading.
11. **fol. 200v** — `& aliunde quam ex pręmissis, habeat aliquos gradus` — the printed spacing reads
    `hab eat`, with a wide space inside the word. Loose spacing only; read as `habeat`.
12. **fol. 200v** — `præmissis` (with æ, line 9), `præmissarum` (with æ, line 18) and `pręmissis`
    (with ę, line 24) all occur **on the same page**. All three verified at 5–6×. (Orthographic only;
    not representable in English.)
13. **fol. 211r** — `ut communiter tradunt doctores thæologi` — **`thæologi` sic**, for *theologi*.
    Verified at 6×; the æ ligature is unmistakable. The OCR layer reads the same. (Translated as
    "the doctors of theology".)
14. **fol. 216r** — `Eadenque certitudine credimus` — **`Eadenque` sic**, for *Eademque*. Verified at
    6×; confirmed by the OCR layer. Cf. sic-note 9 (`Idenque`). (Translated as "And with the same
    certitude…".)
15. **fol. 144r** — `ita magno & firmo consensu, ut certa habentur` — printed `habētur` =
    *habentur*, where the syntax would expect *habeantur*. Rendered as printed ("are… held as
    certain"), not as the expected subjunctive.

## B. Marginal rubrics — verifications and problems

16. **fol. 199v, the rubric requested for verification.** Reported to me as
    "*Certa est ex fide conclusio illata ex una credita et altera evidenti*". The **printed text is**,
    set over six short lines:
    > `Certa ē ex` / `fide, cõclu=` / `sio illata` / `ex una cre` / `dita et alte` / `ra euidēti.`

    i.e. expanded: **`Certa est ex fide, conclusio illata ex una credita et altera euidenti.`**
    Verified at 3.5–4×. Two differences from the reported wording: (a) the printer has a **comma
    after `fide`**, and (b) `euidenti` is spelled with *u*, not *v*. Otherwise the reported wording is
    correct. Translated as "*The conclusion inferred from one premise believed and another evident is
    certain of faith.*"
17. **fol. 200v — `Resp.1. ad argu.3.re-spon.3`.** Printed over three lines as `Reʃp.1. ad` /
    `argu.3.re.` / `ʃpon.3`, i.e. the word *respon.* is broken across lines with a **full stop, not a
    hyphen**, at the break. Verified at 5–10×; the final character is unambiguously a **3**, not a 5
    or a query. The OCR layer independently reads `Resp. 1. ad argu. 3. re. spon. 3`. The sense is
    presumably "*Resp[onsio] 1 ad argu[mentum] 3.æ respon[sionis]*" (Vega's first reply to the
    argument advanced in support of the third *responsio* on fol. 199v), in which case the **final
    `3` is redundant or misplaced**. Reproduced exactly as printed except that `ad` is Englished as
    `to`; the reading is certain, the intended sense is not.
18. **fol. 5r — the Constance-title rubric is apparently corrupt as set.** Printed over eight short
    lines:
    > `Cur nō est` / `adiectum` / `in titulo, l` / `uerbum i` / `lud Eccle=` / `siam uelē` /
    > `re præsen=` / `tans.`

    Read straight through this yields `Cur non est adiectum in titulo, l uerbum ilud Ecclesiam uelem
    repræsentans`, which does not construe. Two defects: (a) the **stray `l` at the end of line 3**,
    which belongs to no adjacent word (the next line begins `uerbum`), and (b) **`uelē`**, which I
    can make no sense of and which most plausibly represents a botched `uniuersalē` (*uniuersalem*)
    — the body text at this exact point reads `nempe uniuersalem ecclesiam representans`.
    The OCR layer, reading the same marginal, produces `Cur non est adiectum in titulo, uerbum illud
    Ecclesiam si am uelere præsentans` — i.e. it silently drops the stray `l`, joins `i-lud` into
    `illud`, and likewise cannot resolve `uelē`. The marginal was transcribed **as printed, with the
    line-breaks marked by `|`**, expanding only `nō`→non and `uelē`→uelem; in the translation the
    frame is Englished and the unconstruable words are left in Latin, with the `|` marks kept. The
    intended sense is certainly "*Why the phrase 'Ecclesiam uniuersalem repraesentans' was not added
    in the title*". **This is the single least certain reading in the whole transcription.**
19. **fol. 200r — `Argum.4` has no final full stop**, whereas `Argum.2.` and `Argum.3.` on the same
    page do. Verified at 6× on an isolated crop of the margin; the space after the 4 is clean. The
    translated rubric `Argument 4` likewise carries no full stop.
20. **fol. 200v — `Euidentia summa alia est a non summa.`** Printed over four lines
    (`Euidentia` / `summa alia` / `est a non` / `summa.`). The OCR layer garbles this, dropping
    `summa alia` from the third line and producing `Euidentia … est a non summa.` The image reading
    is secure at 1.85×. Translated as "*Supreme evidence is other than non-supreme.*"
21. **fol. 211r — `Inno. albritius sum. cōf. li.3. ti.22. q.8.`** The second word is the problem. At
    5× the letters are clearly `a-l-b-r-i` / `t-i-u-s` across the line break, giving **`albritius`**,
    and the OCR layer independently reads `albritius`. I can identify no canonist or summist of that
    name; likely candidates for what was intended (*Albericus*, *Albertus*) do not fit the letters.
    `sum. cōf.` is presumably *Summa Confessorum*, cited by book / title / question. **Left exactly
    as printed; the authority is unidentified.** The companion marginal `Ange. uerbo. Bap.7` is
    certainly Angelus de Clavasio, *Summa Angelica*, s.v. *Baptismus* §7, and is secure.
22. **fol. 216r — `C. Veniens de presbytero non baptizato.`** Secure at 5×; note that the leading `C.`
    is the *capitulum* abbreviation and is distinct from the locus letters A/B/C/D used elsewhere in
    the margins. Left in Latin as a citation formula.
23. **fol. 144r — `Gradus.2. fidei humanæ.`** This is the rubric that marks the beginning of the
    **second grade** of human faith requested in Block B. It stands on the **recto** (fol. 144r =
    PDF 351), not on 144v, at the sentence `Secundum locum inter credibilia humana tenent…`. The
    rubric standing on fol. 144v is `Gradus.3. fidei humanæ.`, which marks the **third** grade, at
    `Eodemque modo licet non æquali iure…`; it therefore also fixes the **end** of the second grade
    at `…donec euidentissimis forsitan argumentis contrarium probaretur.` Both rubrics verified at
    2–2.4×. Translated as "*The 2nd grade of human faith.*" and "*The 3rd grade of human faith.*"

## C. Image ⇄ OCR-layer disagreements (image is authoritative throughout)

24. **PDF 462 — the OCR layer is corrupt, as warned.** It reproduces only the first ten lines of the
    page, then repeats them **five times** with progressively scrambled marginal lists; it never
    reaches `Respon.2.`, `Impugnatio`, `Respon.3.`, `Argum.1.` or any of the body text from
    `Sed hæc satis in præsentia` onward. **The whole of fol. 199v below line 10 is transcribed from
    the image alone, with no OCR cross-check available.** The OCR marginal lists for this page are
    also wrong: they give `Respon. 3.` twice and omit `Certa ē ex fide…` and `Argum.1.` entirely.
25. **PDF 73 — folio number.** The OCR layer prints the running-head number as **`9`**. The image
    shows **`5`** unambiguously at 3×. The OCR is wrong; the rule 2n+63 requires 5.
26. **Systematic long-*s* / *f* confusion in the OCR layer.** Throughout these pages the OCR reads
    *f* as *s*: `suit`/`suerint` for `fuit`/`fuerint`, `facrosancta` for `sacrosancta` (the reverse
    error), `sungi` for `fungi`, `impersecta`/`impersectio` for `imperfecta`/`imperfectio`,
    `mortiserum` for `mortiferum`, `secerint` for `fecerint`. Each of the following was re-verified
    on the image at 6–8×, and the **image reading** is the one translated:
    * fol. 4v `causa **fuit** diuturna quædam ægritudo` (OCR `suit`) — "the cause was a certain long
      illness";
    * fol. 144v `hunc uel illum rite suo **fungi** ministerio` (OCR `sungi`) — the *f* crossbar is
      plainly visible and contrasts with the long-*s* of `suo` two letters earlier — "that this or
      that man duly performs his ministry";
    * fol. 200r `certitudo scientię comparata ad certitudinem fidei **imperfecta** est` and
      `**imperfectio** est, & debilitas fidei` (OCR `impersecta`, `impersectio`) — "is imperfect",
      "is an imperfection";
    * fol. 5r `& expensa **fuerunt** ab omnibus Concilij patribus` (OCR `suerunt`) — "was… weighed by
      all the Fathers of the Council".
27. **fol. 144v line 1** — OCR `& factum de eis dubitare`; image **`& fatuum de eis dubitare`**
    (verified at 1.6×; *fatuum* is also what the sense requires — "they would think it stupid and
    foolish to doubt them"), and that is the reading translated.
28. **fol. 144v** — OCR `certam minimem dubiam fidem`; image **`certam minimeque dubiam fidem`**
    (`minimeꝗ`, verified at 1.6×) — "a certain and in no way doubtful faith".
29. **fol. 144v** — OCR `Tameti enim hunc uel illum`; image **`Tametsi enim…`** — "For although…".
30. **fol. 4v** — OCR `non quoscuius`; image **`non quosuis`** (word divided `quos-|uis` across
    lines) — "not just anyone".
31. **fol. 199v** — OCR (in the surviving fragment) `quique **uere** diligit ipsum` without accent;
    the image has the **grave accent**, `uerè`. Similarly `quàm`, `palàm`, `Nónne` all carry printed
    accents that the OCR drops. Cf. fol. 216r `consecrare uerè Eucharistiam`, accent verified at 6×.
    (The accents are prosodic marks only and have no counterpart in the English.)
32. **fol. 476 (206v)** — the OCR layer scrambles the left-margin authority stack, splitting and
    duplicating `Mag.sent. / Alex.Hal. / Gabr. / Maio.` and mis-transcribing `& alij.` as `Galij.`
    and `5. Bona.` as `Bona.`. None of these fall inside the excerpt translated, but they are
    recorded here as a caution against relying on the OCR for this page's marginalia.
33. **fol. 200v** — OCR `Verū hæc obiectio procedit ex praua intelligentia differē-tiæ`, agreeing
    with the image; but OCR renders the two `q̄` contractions as bare `q̄`, offering no guidance on
    the æ/ę question raised under "Expansion policy" above.

## E. Residual uncertainties, ranked

* **High uncertainty** — the fol. 5r marginal rubric (sic-note 18): the stray `l` and the word
  `uelē`. I cannot resolve either; both are recorded as printed, and are left in Latin inside the
  Englished frame of the rubric.
* **Moderate uncertainty** — `albritius` at fol. 211r (sic-note 21): the letters are legible but the
  authority is unidentified, so I cannot use sense to confirm the reading.
* **Moderate uncertainty** — the trailing `3` of `Resp.1. ad argu.3.respon.3` (sic-note 17): the
  glyph is certain, its function is not.
* **Low uncertainty** — the expansion of `q̄` as `quæ` rather than `quę` at fol. 200v (twice); an
  editorial choice, flagged rather than concealed. It has no effect on the English.
* **Low uncertainty** — sic-note 2 (`derretum`, fol. 4v), taken from outside the excerpt translated
  and checked only at low magnification.
* Everything else translated above rests on readings checked on the page image at between 1.6× and
  10× magnification and reported with confidence.
