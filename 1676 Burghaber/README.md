## Controversia XXVIII: De Electione Summi Pontificis (On the Election of the Supreme Pontiff)

[...]

VI. *Third Doctrine.* **It is of faith that this individual Pontiff existing for the time being — namely, now Innocent XI, whom the whole Church acknowledges and receives as legitimately elected — is the true Pontiff and the Head of the whole Church.** So by name, from our Society, Fr. Alphonsus Salmerón, Valencia, Suárez, Hurtado de Mendoza, Tanner, Amicus, Oviedo, Arriaga, Castro Palao, Martín de Esparza, Cardinal de Lugo, Cardinal Pallavicino, and — lest I be too long in recounting — all the rest of our Fathers today. [...] It is proved, first: It is of faith that everyone canonically elected who enters upon the Apostolic Chair is the true Pontiff and Pastor of the universal Church. But this individual Pontiff, Innocent XI, who has been accepted by the common consent of the Church, has been canonically elected as Head of the Church. Therefore, etc. [...] Whatever the whole Church firmly believes in matters pertaining to Religion is true, since she is the *pillar and ground of truth* (1 Tim. 3:15) and cannot err by acknowledging as true Pontiff one who is not such. **But after the inauguration and promulgation of a Pope, and his universal acceptance and approbation, the Church firmly believes that he was legitimately elected.**

[...]

IX. [...] we shall say with better right, and shall gather from those same passages, **that it pertains to the providence of God not to permit that the whole Church err concerning the Pope by receiving as legitimate one who through a hidden impediment is incapable.** [...]

XII. [...] Whence it is further evident **when it begins to be of faith** that this individual Pontiff, elected for the time being, is the true and legitimate Pontiff; **namely, when the whole or nearly the whole multitude is seen to assent uniformly to the legitimacy of the election, or so many and such have already assented that it cannot prudently be doubted that the rest will come to the same opinion** [...]

XV. [...] **but if anyone were to withdraw with an error of the intellect, whereby he would not believe that he whom the Church has received and retains as supreme Pontiff is the true and legitimately elected supreme Pontiff, he would truly be a Heretic**, supposing that the proposition of which we are treating pertains to the faith. [...]

XVI. [...] Response: the aforesaid Laws neither have, nor can have, place, nor ought they to be understood, except of the first election [...] If therefore that election be supposed void by reason of a hidden impediment, **there succeeds another election, voidable by no positive law, which the Church makes by accepting that man as true Pontiff**; and although this acceptance proceeds from the preceding error, it is nevertheless not conditioned, but absolute; for it is better that the Church should hold for true Pontiff someone who was invalidly elected, than that she should unknowingly lack a true Pontiff [...] **and the Church is deemed to elect, as it were a second time, him whom she accepts.**

[...]

XLII. [...] **When at once and manifestly the validity of an election is established through common acceptance, however much some may afterwards protest, not even all the Electors together can revoke it, once made and accepted.** And so it happened with the Election of Urban VI [...]

XLIII. [...] For in order that the election of a Pope be of faith, and be held as such, **it does not suffice that it be proposed and accepted by the Church in any way whatsoever; but it must be proposed and accepted without probable contradiction — that is, notable contradiction of prudent men, and resting on apparent foundations** [...] From this, however, it is not inferred that it was then just as if all had adhered to a non-true Pope. For although many adhered to him who really was not Pope, **nevertheless the whole Church was not deceived, but only a part** [...]
