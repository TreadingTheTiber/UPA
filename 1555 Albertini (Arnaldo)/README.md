*Note on this entry: Arnaldo Albertini (c. 1480–1544/45), a Majorcan canonist, inquisitor of Valencia and then of Sicily, and from 1534 bishop of Patti, wrote the standard inquisitor's manual on telling Catholic from heretical assertions; it was published a decade after his death (Palermo 1555) and reprinted through the century. His Q. V reproduces, nearly verbatim and with the reigning pope's name updated, the* ***eighth category*** *of Torquemada's Summa de Ecclesia IV.II.9 (the repo's 1453 entry): the grades of Catholic truths end with truths "not absolutely Catholic" but* ***savoring of Catholic truth*** *(*sapiens veritatem catholicam*) — and the worked example is the pope of the day: "because now in the Apostolic See sits **Ioannesmaria de Monte** [Julius III], the proposition* Ioannesmaria de Monte is Pope *is a proposition savoring of Catholic truth" — which,* ***joined with the truth that cannot be denied*** *(that he was duly and canonically elected, e.g. by the whole college or two-thirds), "becomes a Catholic truth: The lord Ioannesmaria de Monte, duly and canonically elected,* ***and received as Pope by the universal Church, is true Pope by determination of the universal Church***" — citing the election law (ca. in nomine domini; de euitanda; vbi periculum) and* ***Constance***: *"the canonically elected Pope for the time being,* ***his proper name being expressed***, *is the successor of blessed Peter, having supreme authority in the Church of God." And the negative — "that he is not true Pope" — "***is an assertion savoring of heresy***" (per Gerson). The transmission chain is now documented inside the collection: Torquemada 1453 names Nicholas V (the pope in whom Basel's divided obedience had just dissolved); Albertini's editors name Julius III; Suárez cites both at their parallel "ad finem" loci — the per-tempora name-update itself exhibiting the doctrine's living application, exactly as Murga's "but I, as now, Innocent XI." Suárez files this school among those who "deny it can be held immediately by divine faith… only by a certain moral certitude" — a post-Tridentine label answering a question the schema predates: for Albertini, as for Torquemada, "Catholic truth" is wider than "formally revealed" (church-determined truths are catholic per se), so the qualified singular attains full catholicity by determination while the bare singular holds the propinquity grade. Q. IV supplies the definition of Catholic truth the grades presuppose, and Q. IX the judicial frame: the supreme authority to define assertions belongs to the Apostolic See and general councils — whose sentences deserve credence "because of the discussion and comparison with the* ***universal multitude of those wise in the faith***, *and because of the undoubted judges,* ***accepted by all***, *greater than every exception" — acceptance-by-all as the ground of judicial certainty, the canonist's analogue of the doctrine. Classification is* ***1b-iv*** *(the propinquity-and-composition grade defined in the root README): the fact-guarantee affirmed through the composition with universal reception and the Constance credendum, with the bare singular held proximate to faith and its denial savoring of heresy.*

## Quaestio V — the eighth grade (fol. 15r)

**16.** [...] the **EIGHTH** and last grade of truths is that which, though it is not, properly speaking, a Catholic truth, is nevertheless one **savoring of Catholic truth**, inasmuch as it is close to Catholic truths [...] For example, because now in the Apostolic See sits Ioannesmaria de Monte, this proposition, *Ioannesmaria de Monte is Pope*, is a proposition savoring of Catholic truth [...] joined with that truth which cannot be denied — that he was duly and canonically elected, as for instance by the whole college of the lord Cardinals, or by two parts thereof — **it becomes a Catholic truth, by saying: The lord Ioannesmaria de Monte, duly and canonically elected, and received as Pope by the universal Church, is true Pope by determination of the universal Church.** *ca. in nomine domini. xxiij. distin. c. de euitanda. de electio. c. vbi periculum, eod. titu. lib. vj.* **And in the Council of Constance it was provided that the canonically elected Pope for the time being, his proper name being expressed, is the successor of blessed Peter, having supreme authority in the Church of God.**

**17.** And **the said assertion conceived negatively — that he is not true Pope — is an assertion savoring of heresy**, as said Ioan. de Gerso. [...]
