- *Selection: the note on dogmatic facts spanning pp. 113–114 (with its body anchor) — the page Mazzella cites as "de Theol. gener., pag. 114 not."; and the censures passage of p. 128 with its third note.*

*Note on this entry: Clemens Schrader, S.J. (1820–1875), professor at the Gregorian and at Vienna, was with Franzelin among the principal theologians of Vatican I; this posthumously thin* Commentarius *(Poitiers 1874) is where the repo's 1879 Mazzella entry sends its readers ("de Theol. gener., pag. 114 not."), and the two notes excerpted here give the* ***fides-ecclesiastica / dogmatic-fact*** *side of the question in its most compressed form — the direct ancestor of the Lennerz–Salaverri line (1b-ii). The long note spanning pp. 113–114 builds the machinery: what is contained in a revealed universal* ***formally*** *is revealed with it (Matthew the Apostle received the Holy Spirit), but singulars contained only* ***confusedly*** *— "this consecrated host is to be adored; this baptized infant is sanctified" — "***cannot be believed by divine faith***," since the singular fact "is neither revealed, nor, as many with J. de Lugo observe, certain beyond fear." Applied to "what the Council of Trent defined must be believed by faith": the singular that Trent was legitimately convoked and celebrated is neither revealed nor self-evident, "and yet, says J. de Lugo, it must be held beyond fear, lest the authority of the Council of Trent be imperiled…* ***it is therefore held only through the authority of the Church, whose infallibility is extended to this fact for the sake of the dogma to be believed, with which that fact is bound up as it were a condition — the fact they call dogmatic***" — with dogmatic* ***facts*** *distinguished as a species from dogmatic* ***texts*** *(the Jansenist controversy), and the machinery then aimed at his own day: "***now the legitimate celebration of the Vatican Council, which by force of dogmatic fact is infallibly certain, is called into doubt***" — the Old-Catholic attack on Vatican I answered by the same apparatus Murga (1684, n. 84) had used for Trent, and the reason Mazzella engages this page when arguing the singular-pope question. The p. 128 note is the entry's second jewel: discussing the manner of theological censures (with Suárez's monition, disp. XIX sec. 6), Schrader reaches for the UPA thesis as his* ***paradigm of a legitimate doctrinal-office assertion***: "*it is one thing* ***to say that it is de fide that the present Pontiff, acknowledged by the universal Church, is the true successor of Peter and Vicar of Christ on earth, and to affirm that the contrary is heretical***; *and another thing to say that the opinion of the theologians denying both is heretical: the former pertains to the* ***doctrinal office of theologians***, *the latter to the* ***judicial office of pastors***" — the whole status quaestionis of 1874 in one sentence: the thesis assertable as de fide with its contrary "heretical" at the doctrinal level, while the deniers' opinion stands uncensured judicially — exactly the three-grade structure the root README's "theological note of the doctrine itself" section documents. (The same note cites "Lorc. disp. 40. n. 25" — Lorca again, in a censures context: a further thread for the pending Lorca attribution.) Classification is* ***1b-ii*** *(fact-guarantee through the Church's infallibility extended to the fact — fides ecclesiastica — beside the 1887 Franzelin entry).*

## The dogmatic-facts note (pp. 113–114)

[...] singulars of this kind [...] **cannot be believed by divine faith**, since that confused comprehension, as they call it, in the revealed universal does not suffice. [...] the singular containing a fact, *this infant is truly baptized*, **is neither revealed, nor, as many with J. de Lugo observe, certain beyond fear [of the contrary]** [...] and yet, says J. de Lugo, it must be held beyond fear, lest the authority of the Council of Trent be imperiled, and that the things defined by it may be believed by divine faith; **it is therefore held only through the authority of the Church, whose infallibility is extended to this fact for the sake of the dogma to be believed, with which that fact is bound up as it were a condition — the fact they call dogmatic** [...] But **now the legitimate celebration of the Vatican Council, which by force of dogmatic fact is infallibly certain, is called into doubt** [...] we have treated of this matter in the work *de Unitate Romana* lib. II., where the infallibility of the Roman Pontiff is discussed.

## The censures passage (p. 128, note 3)

[...] **it is one thing to say that it is *de fide* that the present Pontiff, acknowledged by the universal Church, is the true successor of Peter and Vicar of Christ on earth, and to affirm that the contrary is heretical: and another thing to say that the opinion of the theologians denying both is heretical; the former pertains to the doctrinal office of theologians, the latter to the judicial office of pastors.**
