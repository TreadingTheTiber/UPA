*Note on this entry: Charles-Ghislain Daelman (Mons 1670 – Louvain, 21 December 1731) was doctor-regent and royal professor of theology at Louvain, rector of the university, president of the college of Adrian VI and canon of St. Peter's; his* Theologia seu Observationes theologicae in Summam D. Thomae *was printed after his death (Antwerp, Jouret, 1734–38; this volume 1735, reprinted Louvain 1750–51) and became, with Steyaert, the reference of the Louvain and Mechelen schools. [Dens](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1829%20Dens) cites him by name; the Mechelen course's papal passage is built from his sentences. The satellite pamphlet is a thesis-sheet of the Mechelen Recollects, defended in their convent on 9 August 1768 by Cornelius Van Gompel and Adrian Sengers under Leonard Maes, lector of theology, and printed at Louvain; it names Clement XIII.*

***Why this entry matters: the pivot of the Neesen → Dens chain, at its exact grade.*** *[Neesen](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1678%20Neesen) in 1678 denied that the reigning pope is of faith, on the parity of the baptized child: a conclusion from a revealed universal and a humanly certain singular is not of faith. Daelman grants the parity for everything else and carves out the pope. At printed 14–15 the general rule is stated in the words the Mechelen course would later print — "whether a conclusion deduced from two premises of faith, or from one of faith and one known by natural reason, is of obligating faith? the question seems rather one of name" — and the assent "is borne not so much to the conclusion as to the major premise. And so of the rest;* ***except that there is something special in the following question, whether it is of faith that the modern Pontiff is the true Vicar of Christ and successor of St. Peter****… namely that* ***in the Pontiff one has the judgment of the Church, which on this point does not seem able to be deceived, because it is more shameful to acknowledge a false Head than to have none****; furthermore this man could define badly, and the Church could not work toward another; it is otherwise when there is none. Concerning a baptized child or a consecrated host there is only a particular judgment; indeed it would not matter so much that the Church should err in this particular."*

***The full treatment, and the grade (Q. VI Obs. II, printed 177–178).*** *After proving that the primacy is annexed to the Roman episcopate by divine right, Daelman asks "whether it is also of faith that this particular Roman Bishop has the primacy? and the question is almost one of name, like whether it is of faith that this child who is baptized is justified." His answer, in his own voice: "****It is better said that this is of faith only in general****, that a legitimately baptized child is justified, because only this is revealed;* ***of the particular child it is only a theological conclusion, resting on an evident consequence, and so also of this particular Pontiff. The only disparity is that the judgment of the Church, by which she acknowledges someone as legitimately elected Pontiff, is held to be infallible.*** *There would also be the danger that he might propose something evil to the Church, which she would then be bound to follow, since she holds him for her head." To the objection that God will simply keep the pope from defining heresy: "****Let us rather say that God will prevent such a man from being acknowledged, than that God will prevent the man who is thus to be Pope from erring****; just as he gives signs for distinguishing true miracles from false.* ***And so it is more certain that this man is the legitimately elected Pontiff than that this man is legitimately baptized****." The Church can be without a pope but not with a fictitious one: "while there is none, the Church can work to have one.* ***While there is a fictitious one, and it is unknown to the Church, there is no remedy****." Then the grade, stated for others and for himself: "****Those who hold it to be of faith that this particular Pontiff is Primate cannot say that it is of Catholic or obligating faith, because it is permitted to hold the contrary****," and so "there can be schism without heresy, because if someone were unwilling to acknowledge this particular Pontiff, he would indeed break without cause the union of the members with the head, and the peace, and so offend against charity,* ***yet he would not be a heretic****, because he denies nothing which is of Catholic or obligating faith, although some think it to be of faith or revealed."*

***Classification 1b-iii, with care.*** *Daelman is fact-school: the guarantee is that the Church cannot acknowledge a false head, God "will prevent such a man from being acknowledged," and the acknowledged pope is more certainly pope than the baptized child is baptized. But he does not place the singular under divine faith. In his own voice it is "only a theological conclusion," and what lifts it above the child's case is an infallible judgment of the Church, not a revelation; the de fide position he attributes to "those who hold" it. No faith-habit is assigned to the assent and no* fides ecclesiastica *is named: the Church's judgment functions as an infallible sign. That is the collection's* ***1b-iii*** *— theologically certain, by an infallible sign, with no note of faith assigned — the grade of Franzelin and Hervé, stated at Louvain before 1731. He is not 1a, since he denies that the singular is of faith; not 1b-i, since he does not speak of divine faith* per se secundo*; not 1b-ii, since the Church's judgment is a sign rather than the object of a distinct faith. The denial of the received pope is graded accordingly: schism, not heresy, which sets him below [Carena's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1668%20Carena) "true heretic" and beside [Albizzi's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1683%20Albizzi) canonical ladder.*

***What the Mechelen course did with him.*** *Dens kept Daelman's frame and words — the conclusion-from-a-premise question, "*de fide obligante*," "schism without heresy," the false head worse than none, the baptism disparity — and raised the grade one step: where Daelman has "only a theological conclusion" certified by the Church's judgment, the course printed under Dens's name has "it is plausibly maintained that this proposition is of faith, because it is contained formally in the major premise." The chain therefore runs Neesen (negative, 1678) → Daelman (1b-iii, before 1731) → the Mechelen Recollect theses of 1768, "we believe by divine faith that Clement XIII is the legitimate successor of Peter" (1a) → Dens (1a, from 1777). The school moved by grades, not by a reversal, and the Louvain distinction between divine faith and Catholic faith, which Maes's Thesis I states in its own terms ("an object, though it be revealed, is not of Catholic faith obligating all unless it has been proposed as such by the Catholic Church"), is what let each step be taken without contradicting the last.*

***The acceptance principle in its other application (Q. VII).*** *The same volume carries the principle in the Jansenist controversy. The dispersed Church "is infallible in accepting; there is in her a passive infallibility, but not an active one; she does not judge even in accepting" (printed 227). Of* Unigenitus*: "the whole Church has acceded to that Constitution; for all the Bishops acceded tacitly through so many years… if that Constitution were bad, against faith or morals, they ought to have spoken… Nor does it stand in the way of the reception made by the Church that some have opposed themselves, because this always happened when some were condemned and cut off" (printed 230–231); and "it will not be the law of the Church unless their consent has acceded" (printed 238–239). The vocabulary of reception by the Church that certifies the pope's identity is the vocabulary that certified the constitution; the two applications stand ten pages apart.*

## Q. II Obs. II — the exception for the modern Pontiff (printed 14–15)

Whether a conclusion deduced from two premises of faith, or from one of faith and one known by natural reason, is of obligating faith? **The question seems rather one of name** [...] I am borne by the assent of faith not so much to the conclusion as to the major premise. And so of the rest; **except that there is something special in the following question, whether it is of faith that the modern Pontiff is the true Vicar of Christ and successor of St. Peter** [...] namely that **in the Pontiff one has the judgment of the Church, which on this point does not seem able to be deceived, because it is more shameful to acknowledge a false Head than to have none**; furthermore this man could define badly, and the Church could not work toward another; it is otherwise when there is none.

## Q. VI Obs. II — this particular Pontiff (printed 177–178)

Although it is of faith that, the designation of the Church being posited, even according to the others, the Pontiff or Roman Bishop in general, legitimately elected, has the primacy; yet it is asked whether it is also of faith that this particular Roman Bishop has the primacy? and the question is almost one of name [...] R. **It is better said that this is of faith only in general** [...] of the particular child it is only a theological conclusion, resting on an evident consequence, **and so also of this particular Pontiff. The only disparity is that the judgment of the Church, by which she acknowledges someone as legitimately elected Pontiff, is held to be infallible.** [...]

You will say, God will surely prevent him from defining anything heretical. R. **Let us rather say that God will prevent such a man from being acknowledged, than that God will prevent the man who is thus to be Pope from erring**; just as he gives signs for distinguishing true miracles from false. **And so it is more certain that *this man is the legitimately elected Pontiff* than that *this man is legitimately baptized*.**

Nor say: the Church can well be without a Pontiff, therefore with a fictitious one. R. I deny the consequence, because *no one* will define nothing evil, as a fictitious one would. Next, while there is none, the Church can work to have one. **While there is a fictitious one, and it is unknown to the Church, there is no remedy**; no one will work to have a true one.

**Those who hold it to be of faith that this particular Pontiff is Primate cannot say that it is of Catholic or obligating faith, because it is permitted to hold the contrary.** And so they can solve that objection, namely that there could not be schism without heresy; for they can reply that there can be schism without heresy, because if someone were unwilling to acknowledge this particular Pontiff, he would indeed break without cause the union of the members with the head, and the peace, and so offend against charity, **yet he would not be a heretic**, because he denies nothing which is of Catholic or obligating faith, although some think it to be of faith or revealed; it is otherwise if they deny that the Roman Bishop in general has the primacy. [...]

## Maes, Theses (Mechelen, 9 August 1768), § 5 n. 2

The Roman Pontiff, legitimately elected, according to the irreformable word of Christ receives and possesses the keys of the kingdom of heaven [...] Moreover, **we believe by divine faith that Clement XIII is the legitimate successor of Peter**, for whom Christ prayed that his faith fail not, so that not only is the supreme power of governing the universal Church hereditary, but the infallibility too is hereditary [...]
