*Note on this entry: Antonio de Córdoba, O.F.M. (1485–1578), Observant Franciscan of the province of Castile, is the standing third name of the* ***negative triad*** *— "Cajetan… Bañez… Corduba" — wherever the affirmative authors list the school that denies the de fide certainty of the pope-singular (Suárez de fide disp. X sect. 5; Bonacina n. 9, this collection's 1639 entry; cf. Arriaga). This entry documents what he actually holds, and it is the collection's first witness of a distinct school:* ***School 3a, the reception-guarantee with the general-contradiction requirement*** *(see the root README taxonomy, which this entry occasioned). His position is* ***generated by his papal-infallibility theology***, *a conditional, pre-Tridentine construction (princeps 1569, three centuries before Pastor Aeternus): the Pope as Pope,* ***"doing what is in him"*** *(faciens quod in se est — seeking the truth with due counsel of the Cardinals and Fathers), "***cannot err even for a brief time***, and his definition... is to be stood by under the crime and penalty of heresy" (A); but the Pope* ***through his own fault not doing what is in him*** *"can err in defining of faith" for a time — his worked example is* ***Honorius***, *who "erred in defining of faith because he did not do what was in him, when without the counsel of the Fathers he rashly defined out of his own head" (B). To say the* ***Apostolic See or Roman Church*** *can err defining is* ***heretical***; *to say it simply of the* ***Pope even as Pope*** *"is only a temerarious opinion" — he scolds Cano for collapsing the two (D). Because the act-guarantee is thus conditional, the guarantee must live somewhere else, and Córdoba houses it* ***in the reception***: *a papal definition counts as a definition of the (infallible) See in two cases — made with due counsel,* ***or*** *"made by the Pope alone perseveringly — that is to say, in such wise that it is* ***generally accepted or observed as a rule of faith or morals*** *by the faithful, or by the Pontiffs and Wise of the Church and the Cardinals,* ***without any resistance or general contradiction*** *on their part" (C). The crown text (F) makes the reception the seat of the divine promise: when a definition "as soon as it is issued is* ***not generally contradicted*** *by the faithful, Cardinals, and Pontiffs and wise of the Roman or Catholic Church, then it is to be* ***held firmly by all as true and Catholic, and not only presumed***. *Nor can its error, if it were erroneous, be* ***generally accepted by the Church or her faithful even for a brief time — nor even dissembled***: *but as soon as that erroneous definition of the Pope were issued in public, it would be generally contradicted... And this is a* ***privilege conceded by Christ, or a promise made to the Apostolic See and the Roman or Catholic Church***: *that although the Pope, through his own fault not doing what is in him... can err in defining of faith,* ***yet not in such a way that his error should not immediately be detected by the Church, so that she resists it through her Doctors and Pontiffs generally***." Thus the double indefectibility (E): the promised assistance never fails the See or the Pope doing-what-is-in-him, "***nor the Roman or Catholic Church, so that it should ever follow the error of an erring Pope***... And so it comes about that the magisterium of faith and morals, certain and infallible* ***in this way or in that***, *always exists and never fails." On the same page stands the census's negative placement in his own words: to "there is no certitude whether he is the true Pope, or whether the Pope did what was in him," he answers — "***there is nevertheless moral certitude***," which suffices for holding that he cannot err and that all are bound to his definition under pain of heresy. Structurally, then: in Schools 1 and 2 there is nothing for the Church to* ***do*** *— the fact is certified, or the act is guaranteed before it arrives; in Córdoba the teaching is genuinely evaluable and the Church's response is the divinely protected filter. But the evaluation is* ***corporate, not private***: *the default is presumption and obedience ("it is to be believed that he did what is in him unless the opposite is manifestly established"), the discernment belongs to the* ***Doctors, Pontiffs, and wise generally***, *and in the contested case (G) — a definition "immediately contradicted by the Chief Men of the Church" while the Pope resists, pending examination "whether it is finally to be accepted or refuted" — "that Papal definition is* ***to be obeyed by all, until it be generally defined that it is erroneous***," the obedient being excused by invincible ignorance. Whence the three locks against the sedevacantist appropriation of this school (theirs is the* ***3b*** *variant — minority contradiction — which has no witness): (1) his filter is* ***general*** *contradiction* ***statim*** *— by his own clause, teaching generally accepted and not contradicted at issue is "vera & Catholica firmiter tenenda," which rules against them on 1965 and after; (2) his interim rule is* ***obedience until general judgment***, *the contradictory of standing minority resistance; (3) his school* ***decouples teaching-error from title-loss*** *— the erring pope is an erring* ***true*** *pope (Honorius); the inference error→vacancy exists nowhere in the one school that concedes an error can arrive. The satellites complete the picture. The canonization quaestio (H) runs the same architecture a register lower — it is not heretical to believe the Pope "and the church could err even for a long time" in a canonization, yet the Church canonizing rests "principally on the interior divine illumination and special assistance, which, as is most probably believed, never fails"; and calling a canonized saint damned is "***savoring of heresy***" by composition of a de fide major with a rationally undeniable minor — the Torquemada–Albertini 1b-iv apparatus (the 1453 and 1555 entries) in a second independent sixteenth-century witness. And the Liber I locus the census actually cites (I) — Q. XVII § 2,* ***Sapiens fidem*** *(printed 151) — is where he grades the papal singular itself, using the very apparatus behind this collection's* ***1b-iv*** *class and naming its author. His definition: a proposition "savors of faith" when it can be inferred in a good and necessary consequence "from one [proposition] of faith together with another which,* ***although it is not of faith, nevertheless cannot reasonably be denied***." *The worked papal case follows the Host examples: "***Likewise this proposition savors of faith: Blessed Gregory was Pope***. *For it rightly follows: he was Pope, and only because he was canonically elected; therefore* ***every man canonically elected as Pope is Pope — which is of faith, as Martin V decreed it in the Council of Constance***," *set out in Barbara, in conversion, and enthymematically, and grounded thus: "***both consequences are evident, because he was canonically elected — which cannot reasonably be denied — and no one is Pope for any other reason***." *He then names his sources: "Hæc* ***Gerson. Turecremata, Gabriel***, *& alij vbi sup. & Medina de confessione."* ***How this differs from 1b-iv (Torquemada and Arnaldo Albertini, who quote the same Constance decree) — four differences, each pushing him below the fact-guarantee.*** ***(i) What the decree elevates.*** *Martin V's decree makes the* ***universal*** *de fide ("every man canonically elected as Pope is Pope"); the* ***minor*** *is expressly* ***not*** *of faith, only "rationally undeniable." Since a conclusion follows the weaker part, the singular terminates at* sapiens fidem *— a grade his own § 2 defines as holding "when it is* ***not openly of faith, nor a catholic truth***." *Torquemada and Albertini instead quote the decree in its fuller form — the canonically elected pope* ***"his proper name being expressed"*** *— and reach* ***veritas catholica***. ***(ii) What strengthens the minor.*** *Córdoba's undeniable term is* ***canonical election alone***, *and he closes the door on any other: "no one is Pope for any other reason." There is* ***no reception or acceptance language anywhere in § 2*** *(verified against the page images). The 1b-iv authors compose precisely with* ***universal reception*** *— "duly and canonically elected,* ***and received as Pope by the universal Church***, *is true Pope* ***by determination of the universal Church***" *— which is what converts propinquity into catholic truth. That step is absent here.* ***(iii) Which pope is in the example.*** *His subject is* ***Gregory the Great***, *a long-dead pontiff whose election is undeniable as a matter of historical record; the 1b-iv authors name the* ***reigning*** *pope (Nicholas V, Julius III), where reception must do the work because the election-record cannot. Ask Córdoba about the reigning pope and the answer is not* sapiens fidem *but the moral certitude of Quaestio III (passage E), defeasible "so long as the opposite is not manifestly established."* ***(iv) The resulting counterfactual.*** *1b-iv affirms with 1a that the Church's acceptance* ***cannot be wrong***; *Córdoba affirms no such thing of the acceptance of a* ***man***. *What he does hold divinely secured is that an erroneous* ***definition*** *can never be generally received "nor even dissembled" — a guarantee on the teaching, not on the title. He therefore shares 1b-iv's machinery without its conclusion, and the shortfall is not incidental but generative: because the Constance decree leaves the singular at propinquity (and the reigning pope at moral certitude with falsehood possible under it), the guarantee has to be relocated downstream into reception — which is exactly the 3a structure of Quaestio III. He is best read as the* ***negative-school counterpart of Torquemada***: *same syllogistic tool, same cited masters, one step shorter, and a reception-guarantee supplied afterwards to carry the weight the fact-guarantee will not bear. (The Immaculate-Conception passage printed here in v. 1.0 under the census reference belongs to a different question — forward-referenced from this very page as "infra q. 44" — and has been replaced; its anti-inference principle, that universal practice does not of itself generate a de fide obligation, remains genuine and consistent with § 2, but it is not the locus the census cites.) Classification is* ***3a*** *(reception-guarantee, general-contradiction requirement: the fact held with moral certainty only; the teaching protected in its reception by Christ's privilege; the school's structure closed for ex-cathedra definitions by Pastor Aeternus cap. 4 — "ex sese, non autem ex consensu Ecclesiae" — while its substance survives as the Church's infallibility in credendo).*

*Footnote — the mainline closure of Córdoba's fallibility-window. The due-counsel objection on which his system rests ("the Pope can define without counsel and mature deliberation, therefore he can err") was put and answered from the act-guarantee side by Diego Granado, S.J. (In 2am 2ae, 1629, Tract. VII disp. II — evaluated for this collection but not entered, since he never poses the singular-pope question). Granado rejects Valencia's solution (providence protects the definition whether or not diligence is applied — it would make carelessness sinless, and providence "disposes all things sweetly... does not exclude, but rather demands counsel and due diligence") and answers with Cano and Báñez:* ***"by the very fact that God assists the Pontiff lest he be deceived, He assists also that he apply due diligence"*** *— providence covers the process itself, so the case on which the reception-guarantee is built (a pope defining out of his own head, as Córdoba reads Honorius) is divinely prevented from arising; and if per impossibile he so defined, "he could err, because he defines immaturely; and he could not, because God always assists him." The 3a structure thus stands or falls with the conditional papal-infallibility theology that generates it.*

## Passage F — the privilege-clause (pp. 268–269)

[...] where it is asked, **Whether in doubts of faith the Papal definition is to be stood by, or not** — I respond: when its error is not manifestly established, or **when that definition, as soon as it is issued, is not generally contradicted** by the faithful, Cardinals, and Pontiffs and wise of the Roman or Catholic Church, **then it is to be held firmly by all as true and Catholic, and not only presumed**; nor then can its error, if it were erroneous, **be generally accepted by the Church or her faithful even for a brief time — nor even dissembled**: but as soon as that erroneous definition of the Pope were issued in public, it would, as has been said, be generally contradicted by the faithful. **And this is a privilege conceded by Christ, or a promise made to the Apostolic See and the Roman or Catholic Church: that although the Pope, through his own fault not doing what is in him, or without the due counsel of the Cardinals and Wise, can err in defining of faith, yet not in such a way that his error should not immediately be detected by the Church, so that she resists it through her Doctors and Pontiffs generally.**

## Passage E — the moral-certainty answer (pp. 267–268)

[...] And so it comes about that **the magisterium of faith and morals, certain and infallible in this way or in that, always exists in the Apostolic See and the Roman or Catholic Church, and never fails.** But as to what is said at the end of the confirmation, that **there is no certitude whether he is the true Pope, or whether the Pope did what was in him** — I respond that although there is not the certitude of Catholic faith, or infallible certitude, **there is nevertheless moral certitude**, or there can be, so long as the opposite is not manifestly established [...]

## Passage G — the interim-obedience doubt (pp. 269–270)

[...] it is doubted, I say, what the faithful are bound to do in that intermediate time: are they to obey that Papal definition, or not? [...] I respond that in that intermediate time **that Papal definition is to be obeyed by all, until it be generally defined that it is erroneous** [...] and meanwhile the subjects who obey are excused from the fault of error by invincible ignorance [...]

## Passage B — Honorius (p. 264)

To the other [argument], from the Third Council of Constantinople, I say that Honorius **erred in defining of faith for this reason: because he did not do what was in him, when without the counsel of the Fathers he rashly defined out of his own head.**

## Passage I — Liber I, Q. XVII § 2: the census locus (printed 151)

{marg. *§. 2. Savoring of faith.*} [...] In the second way, according to Gerson, and Gabriel and Simancas [...] and commonly the doctors — and this seems the better definition — **that proposition savors of faith which can be inferred, in a good and necessary consequence, from one [proposition] of faith together with another which, although it is not of faith, nevertheless cannot reasonably be denied** [...]

**Likewise this proposition savors of faith: Blessed Gregory was Pope.** For it rightly follows: he was Pope, and only because he was canonically elected; therefore **every man canonically elected as Pope is Pope — which is of faith, as Martin V decreed it in the Council of Constance** [...] Every man canonically elected is Pope; Blessed Gregory was canonically elected; therefore he was Pope. [...] **Both consequences are evident, because he was canonically elected — which cannot reasonably be denied — and no one is Pope for any other reason.**

And such a proposition savoring of faith is also, and is called, **approaching and pertaining to faith**. Thus **Gerson, Turrecremata, Gabriel**, and others in the place cited, and Medina, *de confessione. q. 4.*
