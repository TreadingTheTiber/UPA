*Note on this entry: Peter Dens (Boom, 12 September 1690 – Mechelen, 15 February 1775) studied with the Oratorians at Mechelen and at Louvain (master of arts 1711, licentiate in theology 1723), lectured at Affligem 1717–1723, held the chair of theology at the Mechelen seminary until 1729 and again 1741–1747, and was its President from 1735 until his death — the chair that [Laurent Neesen](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1678%20Neesen) had held, and whose* Theologia moralis *Dens supplemented in his first publication (1758). The* Theologia ad usum seminariorum *was printed under his name posthumously (Mechelen 1777, eight volumes) and continued by the seminary's professors with revisions; the Catholic Encyclopedia cautions that Dens "is not the author of the complete course," so that "Dens" names the* ***Mechelen house course*** *rather than one hand. The papal passage cites Steyaert (Louvain, †1701) and descends from the Louvain lectures of [Daelman](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1735%20Daelman) (†1731, printed 1735), whose wording it keeps — the conclusion-from-a-premise question, "de fide obligante," "schism without heresy," the false head worse than none — while raising his grade: Daelman called the singular "only a theological conclusion" certified by the Church's infallible acknowledgment; the Mechelen course calls it "plausibly of faith." This entry uses the Hanicq "new corrected edition" of 1829, which names* ***Pius VIII*** *(elected 31 March 1829); the same passage is attested naming* ***Pius VI*** *in the Liège edition of 1790 (Google Books* om49AQAAMAAJ*, printed 125–126) and* ***Pius VII*** *in the Mechelen edition of 1819 (*XZlQAAAAcAAJ*, printed 24–25), the pope's name being updated at each printing. Those two earlier strata read "de fide* ***obligante***" *where 1829 reads "de fide* ***catholica***"; the 1829 reviser changed the label, not the doctrine. The Dublin edition of 1832 (Coyne) omits the De Fide tract altogether.*

***Why this entry matters: the Mechelen line reverses itself.*** *Neesen in 1678 put the question in the sharpest form, naming Innocent XI, and answered no, "certain only by human prudence, under which falsehood can speculatively lie," on the ground that canonical election "depends on several conditions which we are not bound to believe by divine faith," with the baptism parallel as his argument. His text was reprinted unchanged at Lille in 1687 and 1693 (the name updated to Innocent XII), at Antwerp in 1715 (Innocent XII still, now a fossil) and 1730. The course taught in the same seminary two generations later states Neesen's baptism argument as the objection and denies it. The reversal therefore falls between 1730 and 1777, inside Dens's own tenure.*

***The frame (N. 14).*** *The papal case is the worked example of a general question: is a syllogistic conclusion from a revealed premise of faith? Answer 1: as inferred, no, "it will be an assent of theological science." Answer 2: it "can nevertheless be called of faith, insofar as it is considered as contained in the premise of faith," though, "as Steyaert says, such faith does not properly and immediately tend toward this conclusion taken singly, but rather falls upon the premise which contains it." The NOTE then distinguishes* ***formal*** *containment ("something identical with it, or a part of it, or an inseparable property") from* ***virtual*** *(Tobias's dog had a tail). What is formally contained "will be directly of faith: for God, in whom there is no confused knowledge, knows distinctly all the singulars contained under the universal, and where he reveals this universal, he speaks of all the things which are formally contained in it, and for all of them interposes his authority." The example is the one Neesen used against the thesis: "since it is of faith that every duly baptized infant has been justified, if I am certain that I have rightly baptized this infant, I can believe by divine faith that he has been justified."*

***The resolution.*** *"****It is plausibly maintained*** *that this proposition,* Pius VIII is the true successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ, *is of faith; because it is contained formally in the major premise as a part in the whole … which is of faith according to all. Wherefore Martin V, in the bull against the errors of Wyclif and John Hus … orders that one suspected of heresy be asked whether he believes that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being,* ***his name being expressed***, *is the successor of Blessed Peter." The margin reads "Est probabiliter de fide": the affirmative is held as the more plausible opinion, not as certain.*

***The acceptance argument, against Neesen (the Obj. and its reply).*** *The objection is Neesen's own: it is not of faith that this infant is justified if it is not certain he was baptized, "but neither is it certainly established that the modern Pontiff has been legitimately elected." The reply: "I deny the minor: the reason for the disparity is that whether a child has been legitimately baptized* ***depends on the act of a private man***, *of which we often have no certain knowledge: whereas* ***the legitimate election of the Pontiff rests upon the proposition of the universal Church, acknowledging the modern Pontiff as legitimate; in which matter it cannot err, lest otherwise it acknowledge as head one who is not head, and that without remedy****." That is the acceptance guarantee in its strongest form: the Church's acknowledgment of the man is what certifies the minor, and the Church cannot be wrong about her own head because there would be no remedy for it.*

***The limitation: divine faith, not catholic faith.*** *"It is to be noted, however, that it is not of* ***catholic*** *faith that the modern Pontiff is the successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ: for that has not been sufficiently proposed to the whole Church with the obligation of believing. From which it is clear that there can be* ***schism****, that is, separation from subjection to the Pontiff,* ***without heresy****." The closing rule fixes the sense: where the Church herself makes the deduction and proposes it, the proposition is of catholic faith and its denier a heretic; where she does not, "he who sees it clearly will be a heretic in the forum of God, not in the forum of the Church," which is why such propositions are censured "as erroneous, or proximate to heresy" rather than heretical. This is exactly [Salmerón's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1602%20Salmeron) position, and it takes the same code: the withheld predicate "catholic" is a claim about universal proposition, not a lesser assent. Compare [Bartis](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1731%20Bartis) § 67 two years before the first Mechelen printing, where the denier is a material but not a formal heretic for the same reason.*

***The De Ecclesia excerpts.*** *N. 92: "Who is this successor of Peter? It is of faith that he is the Roman Pontiff … This succession is continued down to the present day in a series of 255 Pontiffs." N. 93: the Pope "receives it immediately from Christ as his Vicar … Nor does it stand in the way that the Pope is elected by the Cardinals: for their election is only a* conditio sine qua non*, and once it is posited, he receives power and jurisdiction immediately from Christ" — the same election-as-condition doctrine as Bartis § 61 and [Sendín](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1699%20Sendin) § 91. N. 97, on whether the Pope even as a private person can be a heretic: "the negative opinion is more probable, so that the privilege of Peter, of which Luke 22:32,* I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not, *passes also to Peter's successors," proved by the fact that "of no Pontiff has it hitherto been possible to prove that he was a formal heretic," with the Marcellinus, Liberius and Honorius objections answered (Honorius "condemned as a favourer of heresy, not as a heretic"), and the canon* Si Papa *read as stating only "what could be done, in the case that a Pontiff should fall from the faith: but we think this case has never happened, nor will happen."*

***Classification 1a*** *(fact-guarantee, divine faith of the singular by formal containment, the minor certified by the universal Church's acknowledgment, the note "catholic" withheld for want of proposition). It is filed under 1829 as the edition transcribed; the doctrine is in print by 1790 and belongs to the Mechelen course of 1735–1777.*

## De Virtute Fidei N. 14 — the resolution, the objection, and the limitation (printed 285–287)

*Is it of faith that the modern Pontiff Pius VIII is the legitimate successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ? since it is inferred from these premises: Every Pontiff legitimately elected is the true successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ: but the modern Pontiff Pius VIII is legitimately elected; therefore he is the true successor of St. Peter, and Vicar of Christ.*

R. **It is plausibly maintained that this proposition, Pius VIII is the true successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ, is of faith; because it is contained formally in the major premise as a part in the whole**, namely in this: Every Pontiff legitimately elected is the true successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ; which is of faith according to all. Wherefore Martin V, in the bull against the errors of Wyclif and John Hus, published in the Council of Constance, orders that one suspected of heresy be asked, among other things, « whether he believes that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being, **his name being expressed**, is the successor of Blessed Peter, having supreme authority in the Church of God. » Coll. Conc. Hard. Tom. 8, col. 915.

*Obj.* Although it is of faith that every legitimately baptized infant has been justified, it is nevertheless not of faith that this infant has been justified, if it is not certainly established that he has been legitimately baptized: but neither is it certainly established that the modern Pontiff has been legitimately elected; therefore etc.

R. I deny the minor: the reason for the disparity is that whether a child has been legitimately baptized **depends on the act of a private man**, of which we often have no certain knowledge: whereas **the legitimate election of the Pontiff rests upon the proposition of the universal Church, acknowledging the modern Pontiff as legitimate; in which matter it cannot err, lest otherwise it acknowledge as head one who is not head, and that without remedy.**

It is to be noted, however, that **it is not of catholic faith** that the modern Pontiff is the successor of St. Peter and Vicar of Christ: for that has not been sufficiently proposed to the whole Church with the obligation of believing. From which it is clear that **there can be schism, that is, separation from subjection to the Pontiff, without heresy.**

Gather when one who denies what is implicitly revealed is to be called a heretic, and when not. If the Church herself makes the deduction and proposes it, the proposition is of catholic faith, and one who denies it is a heretic; but if the Church does not make the deduction, he who does not see the consequence will not be a heretic: he, however, who sees it clearly will, conformably to what has been said, be **a heretic in the forum of God, not in the forum of the Church**. This is also the reason why the Church, in proscribing propositions of this kind, does not condemn them as heretical, but only as erroneous, or proximate to heresy. [...]

## De Ecclesia N. 93 — election as condition sine qua non (printed 438)

*From whom does the supreme Pontiff, legitimately elected, receive his power and jurisdiction?* R. He receives it immediately from Christ as his Vicar, in the manner in which Peter received it. Nor does it stand in the way that the Pope is elected by the Cardinals: for **their election is only a *conditio sine qua non*, and once it is posited, he receives power and jurisdiction immediately from Christ.**
