*Note on this entry: a Benedictine of the Spanish Congregation, professed at Irache and attached to its university. The Quaestio's stated subject is the certitude owed to the propositions condemned by Alexander VII (24 Sept. 1665 and 18 March 1666) — but Murga cannot settle it without first settling whether it is of faith that the condemning pope is pope, and the resulting digression (roughly nn. 77–90) is one of the richest **census** passages of the whole disputed question. Two things make it valuable beyond its own argument. First, it preserves **Suárez's first-person testimony in direct speech** — his own account of what he taught at Rome in 1585 — which is not in the repository's 1583 Suárez entry. Second, it reproduces **Diana's affirmative census**, naming some twenty authors with precise loci, a majority of whom are not yet in this collection (Arriaga, Salmerón, Esparza, Amico, Comitoli, Verricelli, Poncius, the Walenburch brothers, Bordoni, Bonacina, Albertini, Lorca, Granado). Murga's own position is **1a**: after relaying the authorities he argues the point in his own voice through five proofs (nn. 82–86) and concludes that "it must be said that it is of faith that Innocent XI is the true Pontiff." He also updates Diana's text from Innocent X to the pope of his own day — "**but I, as now, Innocent XI**" — a small but telling instance of the doctrine being applied to the reigning pontiff by each successive writer.*

## Quaestio IV — *Qua certitudine tenendum sit, propositiones damnatas ab Alexandro Septimo... vere tales esse*

[...]

**78.** [Suárez, quoted in the first person:] [...] **"I, however, nearly thirty years ago, that is, in the year of the Lord 1585, taught at Rome, *that it is of faith that this Pontiff is the true Pontiff, after he has been received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church, and so approved that all are bound to obey him, even in the definitions of faith;* because this obligation necessarily supposes that faith to be certain"** [...] and that particular truth **"is sufficiently contained in the universal dogma of faith, that the successor of Peter is the true Pontiff, and then it is sufficiently applied and proposed to the faithful."** [...] *Thus far Suárez.*

**79.** Antoninus Diana, in the sixth resolution cited, inquired **whether it be altogether of faith, e.g., that this individual Innocent X is the true Pontiff and the true successor of Peter**; and having recounted, for the negative side, Bañez, Cajetan, and Castro *adversus haereses*, he holds the affirmative, with **Cardinal de Lugo, Suárez, Oviedo, Hurtado, Arriaga, Tanner, Castro Palao, Amico, Comitoli, Verricelli, Poncius, John of St. Thomas, Adrian and Peter de Walenburch, Bordoni, and Bonacina** [...]

**81.** [...] **"as now (he says) our Lord Innocent X"** — *but I, as now, Innocent XI* — **"holds, without any vestige of controversy, the helm of the Christian Republic and the keys of Peter."** [...] The question, therefore, is in the second consideration: **whether it is to be believed with Divine Faith that Innocent the Eleventh, and other Pontiffs peacefully received, are the legitimate successors of Peter. And Diana answers that it is to be believed with Divine faith that all and each of the Pontiffs of this kind are the legitimate successors of Peter.** And indeed in this sense Suárez holds this opinion: **"After he has been received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church"** [...]

**82.** *It is proved first*, from the Bull of Martin V which is had in the Council of Constance, in which he lays down that heretics returning to the Church should believe **that the Pope canonically elected, who shall be for the time being (his name being expressly stated), is the successor of Peter** [...]

**84.** *Thirdly*, because **if it is not established of faith that Pius IV was the true Pontiff, neither will it be established that the Council of Trent is valid, since it received all its force from his approbation. But that is well-nigh heretical.**

**85.** *Fourthly*, because if it were not of faith that this individual Pontiff, e.g., Innocent XI, is the true Pontiff and successor of St. Peter, **whatever is believed as certain of faith concerning the Judge of controversies in the Church would beyond doubt be ridiculous; for how am I bound to believe of faith that a Judge of controversies is given in the Church, if I cannot, from faith, say who he is?** [...] For anyone could say: faith teaches only that I am bound to go to the Judge of controversies; but it does not tell me that he is Innocent XI. [...] **Therefore, that what is said of the Judge of controversies may not be ridiculous, it must be said that it is of faith that Innocent XI is the true Pontiff, and the successor of St. Peter.**

**86.** *Fifthly* it is proved, because every proposition of faith can be inferred from two propositions of faith [...] But this proposition, *The Pope defining cannot err*, is certain of faith. [...] **For in whatever syllogism that conclusion be inferred, *The Pope defining cannot err*, there must be assumed as the minor, *This Pontiff defining is the true Pope*, which, since it is not known by the natural light, must necessarily be of faith; otherwise the conclusion would not be of faith.**
