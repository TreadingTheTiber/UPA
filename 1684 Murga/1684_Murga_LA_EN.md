| **QUÆSTIO IV.** | | **QUÆSTIO IV.** |
| *Qua certitudine tenendum sit, propositiones damnatas ab Alexandro Septimo, die 24. Septembris 1665. & 18. Martii 1666. ad minus tanquam scandalosas, vere tales esse, prohibitas, & damnatas, & omni probabilitate carentes, & ab omni disputatione, tam publica, quam privata, nisi forsan impugnando, exulandas, & ad quid obligent hac Decreta?* | | *With what certainty is it to be held that the propositions condemned by Alexander VII on the 24th day of September 1665 and the 18th of March 1666, at least as scandalous, are truly such, prohibited and condemned, and devoid of all probability, and to be banished from all disputation, public as well as private, unless perhaps by way of impugning them; and to what do these Decrees oblige?* |
| 1. SUppono, prædictas opiniones ac Alexandro Septimo damnatas, esse quinque supra quadraginta, quas exscripsi in fine Tractatus *de Appellationibus* cum integris Decretis, à sua Sanctitate, hac de re editis, cum stricta prohibitione, sub præcepto, & excommunicatione lata, Summo Pontifici, pro tempore existenti reservata; contra quoscumque, quibuscumque etiam dignitatibus fulgentes, ne quis illas, aut conjunctim, aut divissim doceat, defendat, edat, aut de eis, etiam disputative, publice, aut privatim tractet, nisi forte impugnando: & ne prædictas opiniones, aut aliquam ipsarum ad praxim deducant, districte prohibet in virtute sanctæ obedientiæ, & sub interminatione divini judicii, omnibus Christi fidelibus cujuscumque conditionis, dignitatis, ac status, etiam specialissima nota dignis. Quæ omnia statuit, decrevit, ac prohibuit præfatus Alexander Papa Septimus, pro Tribunali sedendo in Sacra Congregatione Eminentissimorum Sanctæ Romanæ Eccldsiæ Cardinalium, adversus hæreticam pravitatem Inquisitorum Generalium, pro universa Christiana Republica, eorum suffragiis sigillatim super unaquaque ipsarum opinionum auditis, & post sedulam, & accuratam dictarum opinionum discussionem, & examen præhabitum, nedum à pluribus in Sacra Theologia Magistris, sed ab eisdem postea Eminentissimis Dominis Cardinalibus. Quod omne, ex dictis patet Decretis, quæ adamussim reddo in fine mei quinti Voluminis, *de Appellationibus*, inscripti. | | 1. I SUppose that the aforesaid opinions condemned by Alexander VII are five above forty, which I have transcribed at the end of the Treatise *de Appellationibus*, together with the entire Decrees published by His Holiness upon this matter, with strict prohibition, under precept and excommunication *latæ sententiæ* reserved to the Supreme Pontiff for the time being; against any persons whatsoever, though resplendent with whatsoever dignities, that no one teach, defend, or publish them, either jointly or severally, or treat of them, even disputatively, publicly or privately, unless perhaps by way of impugning them: and lest they reduce the aforesaid opinions, or any one of them, to practice, he strictly forbids it, in virtue of holy obedience and under the threat of the divine judgment, to all Christ's faithful of whatsoever condition, dignity, and state, even such as are worthy of most special mention. All which the aforesaid Pope Alexander VII established, decreed, and prohibited, sitting for tribunal in the Sacred Congregation of the Most Eminent Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, Inquisitors General against heretical pravity for the universal Christian commonwealth, their votes having been heard one by one upon each single one of these opinions, and after a sedulous and accurate discussion and examination of the said opinions had beforehand, not only by very many Masters in Sacred Theology, but afterwards by the same Most Eminent Lords Cardinals as well. All which is plain from the said Decrees, which I render exactly at the end of my fifth Volume, entitled *de Appellationibus*. |
| 2. *Secundo* suppono, Summos Pontifices aliquando suas facere declarationes, qua tales, & illæ ab eis, tanquam in Sede Apostolica Divi Petri sedentibus dimanant, & prodeunt; aliquando vero ab eis, non qua Vicariis Christi Domini, & successoribus Principis Apostolorum dependent, sed qua Doctoribus particularibus. De quo duplici procedendi modo in Declarationibus Summorum Pontificum, videndi veniunt Franciscus Suarez, *tomo 4. de Religione, lib. 3. cap. 4. num. 8. in fine, & num. 11. pag. mihi 460.* Cardinalis Bellarminus, *tom. 1. lib. 4. de Romano Pontifice, cap. 2. in principio, & cap. 6.* Petrus Hurtado de Mendoza, *tract. de Fide, disputat. 37. §. 2.* Franciscus Amicus, *tom. 4. dicto tract. disp. 2. sect. 4. num. 145. & 239. disp. 7. sect. 1. n. 37.* Joannes de Cardenas, *in Crisi Theologica, par 1. tract. 1. disp. 9. c. 20. art. 3.* | | 2. *Secondly* I suppose that the Supreme Pontiffs sometimes make their declarations as such, and these issue and go forth from them as from men sitting in the Apostolic See of St. Peter; but sometimes they proceed from them, not as from Vicars of Christ the Lord and successors of the Prince of the Apostles, but as from particular Doctors. Concerning which twofold manner of proceeding in the Declarations of the Supreme Pontiffs, there are to be seen Francisco Suárez, *tomo 4. de Religione, lib. 3. cap. 4. num. 8. in fine, & num. 11. pag. mihi 460.* Cardinal Bellarmine, *tom. 1. lib. 4. de Romano Pontifice, cap. 2. in principio, & cap. 6.* Pedro Hurtado de Mendoza, *tract. de Fide, disputat. 37. §. 2.* Francisco Amico, *tom. 4. dicto tract. disp. 2. sect. 4. num. 145. & 239. disp. 7. sect. 1. n. 37.* Juan de Cárdenas, *in Crisi Theologica, par 1. tract. 1. disp. 9. c. 20. art. 3.* |
| 3. *Tertio* suppono, Pontificem Summum, qua talem, aliquando declarare, decernere, & definire in Conciliis, illis interessendo, vel illa jam celebrata confirmando, aliquando adhibitis præviis consultationibus Sacrarum Congregationum S. Romanæ Ecclesiæ Cardinalium, & etiam ut in plurimum, plurimorum in Sacra Theologia, & in Ecclesiastica Disciplina Doctorum peritissimorum, nedum in publicum cœtum advocatorum, sed etiam privatim consultorum; & aliquando denique, præmissis informationibus, seu probationibus, per depositiones virorum fide dignorum receptis. De quibus Bellarminus, *dicto cap. 2.* Amicus, *disp. 7. sect. 1. citat. à num. 23. & signanter num. 36.* | | 3. *Thirdly* I suppose that the Supreme Pontiff, as such, sometimes declares, decrees, and defines in Councils, either by being present at them or by confirming them when already celebrated; sometimes with previous consultations of the Sacred Congregations of the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church employed, and also, as for the most part, of very many Doctors most skilled in Sacred Theology and in Ecclesiastical Discipline, not only summoned into a public assembly, but also consulted privately; and sometimes, finally, upon informations or proofs received through the depositions of men worthy of credence. Concerning which Bellarmine, *dicto cap. 2.* Amicus, *disp. 7. sect. 1. citat. à num. 23. & signanter num. 36.* |

| 6. Licet præter tot authoritates pro meo supposito firmando citatas, facile possem plures alias adducere, & cumulare, finem illis imponere duxi, recensendo Conc. Florentinum, sic definientem: *Diffinimus Sanctam Apostolicam Sedem, & Romanum Pontificem in universum orbem tenere Primatum, & ipsum Romanum successorem esse B. Petri Principis Apostolorum, & verum Vicarium Christi, totiusque Ecclesiæ caput, & omnium Christianorum Patrem, ac Doctorem existere: & ipsi in B. Petro pascendi, regendi, & gubernandi universalem Ecclesiam à Dno. Nro. Jesu Christo plenam potestatem traditam esse, quemadmodum etiam in gestis Oecumenicorum Conciliorum, & in sacris Canonibus continetur:* Hæc Florent. Concil. *in Decreto Unionis Græcorum,* &c. & pariter reddendo Cajetanum, *dicto cap. 14. à n. 30.* in sequentia prorumpentem: Prætereo (inquit) turbam Canonum, & Decretorum (quam dinumerare nemo potest) idem sentientium: Tam evidenter siquidem ex his, quæ attulimus, clarum est, *Romanum Pontificem Petri successorem à Christo in Petro institutum esse totius Ecclesiæ Pastorem, ut locus dubitationi nullus relictus sit.* Siquis autem post hæc hæsitans invenitur, aut rudis, aut ineptus, aut cum hæreticis, seu schismaticis sapit, aut certe Christianus non est, ineruditus quippe valde, & indocilis est quisquis certitudinem mathematicam in omnibus quærit, ut Philosophi tradunt. Hæreticorum, & schismaticorum proprium est sacra uti scriptura, secundum sensum tamen, quo ipsi faciunt illam sonare. Christianorum autem, qui vere Catholici sunt, sapientia sacram Scripturam intelligit secundum sensum, quem Sancti Doctores, quem Sacra Concilia, Apostolica authoritate roborata interpretantur. Hunc sciendi Christianam veritatem modum, quisquis repellit, Christianus non est: ab hac via, qui recedit, infidelis invenitur. *Nam licentia exorbitandi ab hac via, si admittitur, Trinitatis unam substantiam, revocare in dubium cum Ario fas est: Spiritum quoque Sanctum procedere à Filio, ambiguum adhuc cum priscis erit: panem in Sacramento Altaris converti in Corpus Christi dubitatur. Hæc enim omnia nulla alia via, aut authoritate sunt Christianis reddita certa, & indubia fide tenenda, nisi interpretatione Sacræ Scripturæ, juxta Sanctorum documenta, & Sacrorum Conciliorum, authoritate Apostolica confirmatorum, diffinitionem.* Quo circa, quum textus Evangelici ad propositum adsint, & Sanctorum doctrina conformis in proposito pateat, nullo penitus Sanctorum reluctante, Sacrorumque Conciliorum cum Apostolica authoritate inveniatur diffinitio, omnisque Christianorum ætas à Petro hucusque in Sancta Ecclesia Catholica hoc confessa inveniatur, reliquorumque Doctorum testimonia innumera sint: *certum ita redditur Romanum Pontificem Petri successorem, in Petro à Christo institutum, totius Ecclesiæ Pastorem, ut certior fides haberi non possit. Amen.* | | 6. Although, besides so many authorities cited for the establishing of my supposition, I could easily adduce and heap up many others, I have thought to put an end to them by recounting the Council of Florence, thus defining: *We define that the Holy Apostolic See and the Roman Pontiff hold the Primacy over the whole world, and that the Roman Pontiff himself is the successor of Blessed Peter the Prince of the Apostles, and the true Vicar of Christ, and the head of the whole Church, and the Father and Teacher of all Christians; and that to him, in Blessed Peter, full power of feeding, ruling, and governing the universal Church was delivered by Our Lord Jesus Christ, even as is also contained in the acts of the Ecumenical Councils and in the sacred Canons:* Thus the Council of Florence, *in Decreto Unionis Græcorum,* &c. And likewise by rendering Cajetan, *dicto cap. 14. à n. 30.*, breaking forth into what follows: I pass over (says he) the throng of Canons and Decrees (which no one can number) that hold the same: for from the things which we have brought forward it is so evidently clear *that the Roman Pontiff, Peter's successor, was instituted by Christ in Peter as Pastor of the whole Church, that no place is left for doubt.* But if anyone after these things is found hesitating, he is either untaught, or inept, or savors with the heretics or schismatics, or certainly is not a Christian; for whoever seeks mathematical certitude in all things is exceedingly unlearned and indocile, as the Philosophers teach. It is proper to heretics and schismatics to use the sacred Scripture, but according to that sense in which they themselves make it sound. But the wisdom of Christians who are truly Catholic understands the sacred Scripture according to that sense which the Holy Doctors, which the Sacred Councils strengthened by Apostolic authority, interpret. Whoever rejects this manner of knowing Christian truth is not a Christian: he who withdraws from this way is found an unbeliever. *For if license to stray from this way be admitted, it is lawful to call into doubt with Arius the one substance of the Trinity; that the Holy Spirit also proceeds from the Son will still be ambiguous with the ancients; it is doubted that the bread in the Sacrament of the Altar is converted into the Body of Christ. For all these things have been rendered certain to Christians, and to be held with undoubted faith, by no other way or authority than by the interpretation of Sacred Scripture according to the documents of the Saints, and by the definition of the Sacred Councils confirmed by Apostolic authority.* Wherefore, since the texts of the Gospel are at hand to the purpose, and the doctrine of the Saints in agreement upon the point lies open, with not a single one of the Saints resisting, and since the definition of the Sacred Councils with Apostolic authority is found, and every age of Christians from Peter down to this day is found to have confessed this in the Holy Catholic Church, and the testimonies of the remaining Doctors are innumerable: *it is thus rendered certain that the Roman Pontiff, Peter's successor, instituted in Peter by Christ, is the Pastor of the whole Church, so that a more certain faith cannot be had. Amen.* |
| 7. Hactenus Eminentissimus Cajetanus, quem sequitur illustrissimus Canus, Primarius Sacræ Theologiæ olim interpres in Salmanticensi Universitate, & postea Episcopus Canariensis, in suo doctissimo volumine, *de Locis Theologicis, lib. 6. cap. 3. Proposit. 3. pag. mihi 215.* sequentia proferens: Petro defuncto, divino jure esse, qui illi succedat in eadem authoritate, & potestate. Nam, cum oves Christi essent usque ad consummationem sæculi futuræ, desipiet profecto, qui Christum existimabit, ovili perpetuo Pastorem temporarium suffecisse. Quod igitur Dominus instituendo pastorale Ecclesiæ munus primo Pastori contulit, id ordinarie cuilibet successori intelligitur contulisse, ut Cajetanus, *De Primatu Ecclesiæ Romanæ, c. 12.* eruditissime comprobavit. | | 7. Thus far the Most Eminent Cajetan, whom the most illustrious Cano follows — formerly Primary interpreter of Sacred Theology in the University of Salamanca, and afterwards Bishop of the Canaries — in his most learned volume *de Locis Theologicis, lib. 6. cap. 3. Proposit. 3. pag. mihi 215.*, putting forth what follows: that, Peter being dead, there is by divine right one who succeeds him in the same authority and power. For, since the sheep of Christ were to be until the consummation of the world, he will surely be a fool who shall think that Christ substituted a temporary Pastor for a perpetual sheepfold. What therefore the Lord, in instituting the pastoral office of the Church, conferred upon the first Pastor, that he is understood to have conferred ordinarily upon every successor whatsoever, as Cajetan, *De Primatu Ecclesiæ Romanæ, c. 12.*, most eruditely proved. |
| 8. Præterea, Regnum Christi futurum erat æternum: ergo & Ecclesiæ principatus non fuit temporalis. Alioqui, si Petri potestas ad posteros non est translata, nec cœterorum quidem Apostolorum potestatem ad succedentes Episcopos transmissam agnoscemus. Ita nunc in Ecclesia, nulla aut Episcoporum, aut Sacerdotum potestas erit. Qui est error in fide manifestus. Et inferius, post plura adducta testimonia, ex veteri Testamento desumpta, ad suam probandam propositionem addit: Non itaque, ut ad propositum revertamur, domus una sine patre familias, una civitas sine rectore, ovile unum sine pastore uno, mortuo Petro, relictum est, sed est aliquis, qui illi in regendi, ac pascendi oves authoritate succedat. Et rursus inferius prosequitur: *Tantam ergo habet Summus à Christo institutus Pontifex in Ecclesia authoritatem, quanta ad continendos Christianæ reipublicæ cives in fide, Religione, officio est necessaria:* Suboriri autem inter nos necessum est de fide, ac Religione controversias, quibus componendis, si nullus est à Christo datus Judex ad quem referretur tandem hujusmodi quæstio omnis sine fine esset, perpetuaque dissensio, totque, tamque dissonantes de fide, ac de Religione sententiæ, quot sunt in Ecclesia, & quam sunt diversa capita. Quas ob res, si Ecclesiæ unitatem, charitatem, consociationem conservare oportet: necesse est profecto, ut unus sit relictus à Christo Princeps, qui non aliter, ac Petrus dissidentium lites dirimere, solvereque quæstiones possit. Hæc omnia Doctor ille, cui consonat Suarez, *tract. de Fide, disputat. 5. sect. 8. n. 1.* | | 8. Moreover, the kingdom of Christ was to be eternal: therefore neither was the principate of the Church temporary. Otherwise, if Peter's power has not been transferred to those who came after, neither shall we acknowledge that the power of the other Apostles was transmitted to the succeeding Bishops. Thus there will now be in the Church no power either of Bishops or of Priests. Which is a manifest error in the faith. And below, after very many testimonies drawn from the Old Testament have been adduced to prove his proposition, he adds: Not therefore, that we may return to our purpose, was one house left without a father of the family, one city without a ruler, one sheepfold without one shepherd, when Peter was dead; but there is someone who succeeds him in the authority of ruling and feeding the sheep. And again below he continues: *Therefore the Supreme Pontiff instituted by Christ has as much authority in the Church as is necessary for containing the citizens of the Christian commonwealth in faith, in religion, in duty:* But it must needs be that controversies concerning faith and religion arise among us, for the composing of which, if there be no Judge given by Christ to whom every such question might at length be referred, it would be without end, and there would be perpetual dissension, and as many and as dissonant opinions concerning faith and concerning religion as there are heads in the Church, and as diverse as those heads are. For which reasons, if the unity, charity, and fellowship of the Church must be preserved, it is surely necessary that one Prince be left by Christ, who, no otherwise than Peter, may be able to settle the disputes of those who disagree and to resolve questions. All this that Doctor, with whom Suárez agrees, *tract. de Fide, disputat. 5. sect. 8. n. 1.* |

| 13. *Nono suppono*, omnes Catholicos inter se convenire, sed non cum hæreticis, in hoc, quod Summus Pontifex solus, vel cum suo particulari consilio aliquid in re dubia statuens, sive errare possit, sive non, sit ab omnibus fidelibus obedienter audiendus. But the third exposition, which he himself calls the true one, is that the Lord obtained two privileges for Peter by means of that prayer: one, that he himself could never lose the Faith, however much he were tempted by the devil — which is something more than the gift of perseverance: for he is said to persevere unto the end who, although he sometimes fall, yet rises again, and at the end is found faithful; but for Peter the Lord obtained that he could never fall, as regards the Faith. | 18. Alterum privilegium est, ut ipse tanquam Pontifex, non posset unquam docere aliquid contra Fidem; sive ut in Sede ejus nunquam inveniretur, qui doceret contra veram Fidem. | 32. *Secundo* probat Bellarminus, Summum Pontificem, cum totam Ecclesiam docet, in his, quæ ad Fidem pertinent, nullo casu errare posse, ex promissione à à Christo Domino D. Petro facta, *Mathæi 16.* ibi: *Super hanc petram ædificabo Ecclesiam meam, & porta inferi non prævalebunt adversus eam.* Quia ad litteram petra, & fundamentum Ecclesiæ dicitur Petrus, ut Summus Rector Ecclesiæ, uti demonstrat idem Cardinalis, *tom. 1. lib. 1. cap. 10.* & proinde ait, quilibet ejus successor, similiter est petra, & fundamentum Ecclesiæ. | | 32. *Secondly* Bellarmine proves that the Supreme Pontiff, when he teaches the whole Church in those things which pertain to Faith, can in no case err, from the promise made by Christ the Lord to St. Peter, *Matt. 16.*, there: *Upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.* Because to the letter Peter is called the rock and foundation of the Church, as Supreme Ruler of the Church, as the same Cardinal demonstrates, *tom. 1. lib. 1. cap. 10.*; and therefore, he says, any successor of his whatsoever is likewise the rock and foundation of the Church. |
| 33. *Tertio* idem probat, ex illo *Joannis ultimo: Pasce oves meas:* sub quibus verbis demonstrat, *dicto lib. 1. cap. 14. 15. & 16.* Pontificem fuisse institutum Pastorem, & Doctorem totius Ecclesiæ. Ex quo infert: *Ergo tota Ecclesia illum audire, & sequi tenetur. Ergo si ille erret, tota Ecclesia errabit.* | | 33. *Thirdly* he proves the same from that of *the last chapter of John: Feed my sheep:* under which words he demonstrates, *dicto lib. 1. cap. 14. 15. & 16.*, that the Pontiff was instituted Pastor and Doctor of the whole Church. From which he infers: *Therefore the whole Church is bound to hear and follow him. Therefore if he err, the whole Church will err.* |
| 34. *Quarto*, & ultimo probat errare non posse Pontificem in fide, cum totam docet Ecclesiam, ex duplici experimento. *Primum* est, quod omnes Patriarchales Sedes à Fide defecerunt, ita ut hæretici, & hæresim profitentes in eis sederint, excepta Romana Sede, ut habetur in forma reconciliationis Ecclesiarum, quæ à Romana defecerant, perscripta per Bonifacium Papam Secundum, *in epist. ad eulalium Episcopum Cartaginensem;* per Ormisdam Papam, *in epist. ad Episcopos Hispaniæ missa;* & data per Adrianum Papam, ad Octavam Synodum Generalem, & à Cano recensita, *de Locis Theologicis, lib. 6. cap. 4. in fine,* in qua habetur: *Non potest Domini Nostri Jesu Christi prætermitti sententia dicentis: Tu es petrus, & super hanc petram ædificabo Ecclesiam meam: & hæc, quæ dicta sunt, rerum probantur effectibus; quia in Sede Apostolica extra maculam semper est Catholica servata religio.* Idem Canus *cap. 6.* recencet Sextam Synodum Generalem, *in actione 4.* continere professionem Fidei, quam Agatho Papa ad Concilium ipsum missit, sub tenore isto: *Beati Apostoli adnitente præsidio, hæc Apostolica ejus Ecclesia nunquam à via veritatis in qualibet erroris parte deflexa est.* Et infra. *Hæc enim Apostolica Christi Ecclesia per Dei gratiam à tramite Apostolicæ traditionis nunquam errasse probabitur; nec hæreticis novitatibus depravata suetubuit: sed ut ab exordio Christianæ Fidei percepit ab auctoribus suis Apostolorum Christi Principibus illibata finetenus permanet secundum ipsius Domini Salvatoris divinam pollicitationem: Petre, Petre, ecce Sathan expetivit vos, &c. Ego autem pro te rogavi, ut non deficiat Fides tua: Et tu confirma fratres tuos. Quod Apostolicos meos prædecessores confidenter fecisse semper est cognitum.* Hactenus S. Agatho Papa, Siculus, ex Benedictinis assumptus, ut testantur Arnoldus Uvion, *tom. 1. p. 131.* Azorius, *part. 1. lib. 12. cap. 21. quæst. 2.* Tritensius, *de Viris Illustribus lib. 1. c. 5.* San Vitores, *en el Sol del Occidente, to. 1. prelud. 8.* Yepes, *en la Coronica General, tom. 2. fol. 327. 329. 331. 332. y 289.* | | 34. *Fourthly* and lastly he proves that the Pontiff cannot err in faith when he teaches the whole Church, from a twofold experiment. *The first* is that all the Patriarchal Sees fell away from the Faith, so that heretics, and men professing heresy, have sat in them, the Roman See excepted, as is had in the form of reconciliation of the Churches which had fallen away from the Roman, written out by Pope Boniface II, *in epist. ad eulalium Episcopum Cartaginensem;* by Pope Hormisdas, *in epist. ad Episcopos Hispaniæ missa;* and given by Pope Adrian to the Eighth General Synod, and recounted by Cano, *de Locis Theologicis, lib. 6. cap. 4. in fine*, in which is had: *The sentence of Our Lord Jesus Christ cannot be passed over, who says: Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church: and these things which have been said are proved by the effects of events; because in the Apostolic See the Catholic religion has always been preserved without stain.* The same Cano, *cap. 6.*, recounts that the Sixth General Synod, *in actione 4.*, contains the profession of Faith which Pope Agatho sent to that same Council, under this tenor: *By the aid of the blessed Apostle assisting, this his Apostolic Church has never been turned aside from the way of truth into any part whatever of error.* And below: *For this Apostolic Church of Christ, by the grace of God, shall be proved never to have erred from the path of Apostolic tradition; nor has it succumbed, depraved by heretical novelties: but as from the beginning of the Christian Faith it received from its authors, the Princes of Christ's Apostles, so it remains unimpaired unto the end, according to the divine promise of the Lord Savior himself: Peter, Peter, behold Satan hath desired to have you, &c. But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: And do thou confirm thy brethren. Which it has always been known that my Apostolic predecessors have confidently done.* Thus far St. Agatho, Pope, a Sicilian, taken from among the Benedictines, as Arnold Wion attests, *tom. 1. p. 131.*, Azor, *part. 1. lib. 12. cap. 21. quæst. 2.*, Trithemius, *de Viris Illustribus lib. 1. c. 5.*, San Vítores, *en el Sol del Occidente, to. 1. prelud. 8.*, Yepes, *en la Coronica General, tom. 2. fol. 327. 329. 331. 332. y 289.* |
| 35. Non tamen ignoro, secundum aliquorum sententiam, plures Summos Pontifices, à Fide Catholica defecisse; quam refert Franciscus Amicus, & eos, qui hac afficiuntur nota, *in Cursu Theologico, tom. 4. disput. 7. sect. 1.* Ad quam respondet, *num. 37.* primo, in genere, & universaliter, nullum ex dictis Pontificibus, in rebus Fidei errasse, ut Pontificem, hoc est, ut docentem Ecclesiam ex Cathedra: cujus signum (inquit) est, quia nullus affertur, qui suum errorem, in quem, ut privata persona lapsus fuit, Ecclesiæ, sequendum proponit; ad eumque fide divina credendum illam adstringit, & obligat. Quæ certe responsio comprobatur authoritatibus decem, modo redditis, quarum prima est Sancti Lucii Primi, Papæ, & Martyris, & ultima Sancti Bernardi. Et hoc quidem, & nihil amplius intendimus probare, cum Cardinali Bellarmino concesisse Christum Dominum D. Petro, & successoribus ejus, in secundo privilegio, de quo supra, *num. 18.* | | 35. I am not, however, ignorant that, according to the opinion of some, several Supreme Pontiffs have fallen away from the Catholic Faith; which opinion Francisco Amico relates, together with those who are branded with this mark, *in Cursu Theologico, tom. 4. disput. 7. sect. 1.* To which he replies, *num. 37.*, first, in general and universally, that none of the said Pontiffs erred in matters of Faith as Pontiff, that is, as teaching the Church *ex Cathedra*: the sign of which (says he) is that not one is adduced who proposed to the Church, to be followed, the error into which he had fallen as a private person, and who bound and obliged her to believe it with divine faith. Which reply is certainly confirmed by the ten authorities just now rendered, of which the first is that of Saint Lucius I, Pope and Martyr, and the last that of Saint Bernard. And this indeed, and nothing more, do we intend to prove, together with Cardinal Bellarmine: that Christ the Lord granted to St. Peter and to his successors the second privilege, concerning which above, *num. 18.* |
| 36. Deinde sigillatim respondet pro quolibet dictorum Pontificum, quos recensui, *n. 16.* pro ut sequitur. Pro Zephirino, rem esse suspectam, an etiam ut personam privatam, hæresim approbaverit. Quoad Liberium, ait, rem videri certam, quod tædio exilii Arianorum hæresi subscripserit, ut privatam personam, ad evitandam exilii pœnam, non vero ut Pontificem ex Cathedra docentem, & extrinsice hæresi consensisse, sicut Marcellinus idololatriæ. Quoad Felicem dicit, quod licet Arianis communicaverit antequam verus esset Pontifex, quando scilicet Liberius in exilio vivevat, ubi autem verus fuit Pontifex factus, nedum ab Arianorum abstinuit communione, sed Constantinum Imperatorem, ut hæreticum damnavit. Quoad Leonem Primum inquit Amicus, falso ab adversariis interpretari. Quoad Gelasium vero, quod liber contra Euthicem, illius non sit, sed Genadii, vel Gelasii Episcopi Cæsariensis. Item quoad Anastasium dicit, quod esto ille cum Photino hæretico communicaverit, potuisse, ut privata persona cum illo communicare, sine hæresis approbatione; & esto approbaverit, num propterea ille ut Pontifex ex Cathedra docens erravit? Quoad vigilium ait: scripsisse epistolam confirmantem hæresim Monothelitarum, non tamen obligando fideles ad illam amplectendam, & quando non erat verus Pontifex, sed Antipapa in schisma contra Silverium. Quoad Honorium Primum dicit, quod quidquid sit, an inciderit in dictam Monothelitarum hæresim, certum est non in eam consensisse, ut Pontifex illam ex Cathedra docendo, & ad eam Ecclesiam obligando, sed ut privatus homo. Et quoad Gregorium Tertium, falsum esse, concesisse propter quancumque infirmitatem impedientem usum matrimonii, virum aliam ducere uxorem, sed propter infirmitatem reddentem naturaliter impotentem ad coitum, ut propter nimiam arctitudinem, | | 36. Next he replies severally for each of the said Pontiffs whom I recounted, *n. 16.*, as follows. For Zephyrinus, that the matter is doubtful whether even as a private person he approved heresy. As to Liberius, he says that the matter seems certain, that from weariness of exile he subscribed to the heresy of the Arians, as a private person, to avoid the penalty of exile, but not as Pontiff teaching *ex Cathedra*, and that he consented to the heresy extrinsically, as Marcellinus to idolatry. As to Felix he says that, although he communicated with the Arians before he was true Pontiff, namely while Liberius was living in exile, yet when he had been made true Pontiff, he not only abstained from the communion of the Arians, but condemned the Emperor Constantine as a heretic. As to Leo I, Amicus says that he is falsely interpreted by the adversaries. But as to Gelasius, that the book against Eutyches is not his, but Gennadius's, or that of Gelasius Bishop of Caesarea. Likewise as to Anastasius he says that, granted that he communicated with Photinus the heretic, he could have communicated with him as a private person, without approbation of the heresy; and granted that he approved it, did he therefore err as Pontiff teaching *ex Cathedra*? As to Vigilius he says: that he wrote a letter confirming the heresy of the Monothelites, yet without obliging the faithful to embrace it, and at a time when he was not true Pontiff, but Antipope in the schism against Silverius. As to Honorius I he says that, whatever be the case whether he fell into the said heresy of the Monothelites, it is certain that he did not consent to it as Pontiff teaching it *ex Cathedra* and obliging the Church to it, but as a private man. And as to Gregory III, that it is false that he granted that a man might take another wife on account of any infirmity whatsoever impeding the use of matrimony, but on account of an infirmity rendering him naturally impotent for intercourse, as on account of excessive narrowness, |
| 37. In aliis autem Patriarchalibus Sedibus, certum est sedisse hæreticos, & hæresim publice profitentes, & non solum qua privatas personas, sed qua illarum Pontificos. Nam in Constantinopolitana, teste Cardinali Bellarmino *tom. 1. libro 4. de Potestate spirituali Summi Pontificis, cap. 2.* sederunt Macedonius, Nestorius, & Sergius hæresiarchæ. In Alexandrina, Georgius, & Lucius Ariani, Dioscorus, Euthychianus, Cyrus Monothelita, & alii. In Hierosolymitana, Joannes Origenista, & ante eum, Euthychius, Irenæus, Hilariusque Ariani sederunt. Et in Antiochena, Paulus Samosetanus hæresiarcha, Petrus Gnaphæus, Euthychianus, Macharius, Monothelita, & alii. Nihil tamen tale de Romana Sede ostendi potest, juxta supra dicta, & juxta dictum Ruffini, *in Expositione Symboli,* videlicet: *In Ecclesia Urbis Romæ, neque hæresis ulla sumpsit exordium, & mos ibi servatur antiquus.* Ex quo apparet Christum pro illa rogasse, ut non deficeret Fides ejus. | | 37. But in the other Patriarchal Sees it is certain that heretics have sat, and men publicly professing heresy, and not only as private persons, but as Pontiffs of those Sees. For in the Constantinopolitan, as Cardinal Bellarmine attests, *tom. 1. libro 4. de Potestate spirituali Summi Pontificis, cap. 2.*, there sat Macedonius, Nestorius, and Sergius, heresiarchs. In the Alexandrian, George and Lucius the Arians, Dioscorus the Eutychian, Cyrus the Monothelite, and others. In the Jerusalemite, John the Origenist, and before him Eutychius, Irenaeus, and Hilary, Arians, sat. And in the Antiochene, Paul of Samosata the heresiarch, Peter Gnapheus the Eutychian, Macarius the Monothelite, and others. Yet nothing of the kind can be shown concerning the Roman See, according to what has been said above, and according to the saying of Rufinus, *in Expositione Symboli*, namely: *In the Church of the City of Rome no heresy has ever taken its beginning, and there the ancient custom is preserved.* From which it appears that Christ prayed for it, that its Faith should not fail. |
| 38. Secundum experimentum ex quo probat Bellarminus *dicto libro 4. cap. 3.* Summum Pontificem, cum totam Ecclesiam docet in his, quæ ad Fidem pertinent, errare non posse, etiam si ad id Concilio non utatur Generali est, quoniam plurimas hæreses damnavit Papa, sine Concilio Generali. Jovinianum enim Siricius Papa, primo Romæ damnavit, & secundo à Synodo Mediolanensi de ipsius authoritate celebrata, ut constat ex Sircii epistola ad Ecclesiam Mediolanensem, quæ habetur *la 2 tomo 1. Epistola Romanorum Pontificum* & ex Henrico Spondano, *in Compend. Baronii, Anno Christi 390. & 6. Siricii, num. 7.* | | 38. The second experiment from which Bellarmine proves, *dicto libro 4. cap. 3.*, that the Supreme Pontiff, when he teaches the whole Church in those things which pertain to Faith, cannot err, even if he employ no General Council for it, is that the Pope has condemned very many heresies without a General Council. For Pope Siricius first condemned Jovinian at Rome, and secondly he was condemned by the Synod of Milan celebrated upon his authority, as is evident from Siricius's letter to the Church of Milan, which is had *la 2 tomo 1. Epistola Romanorum Pontificum*, and from Henri de Sponde, *in Compend. Baronii, Anno Christi 390. & 6. Siricii, num. 7.* |
| 39. Pelagium etiam damnavit primo, dictus Siricius, in dicto Joviniano, à quo hæresim hausit, & sumpsit ipse Pelagius, uti utrumque testatur D. Hieronymus, *lib. 2. contra Pelagium, & in Jeremiam, Præfat. libro. 4.* Postea vero fuit Pelagius in Concilio Carthaginensi damnatus. Sic enim ait Spondanus, Anno Christi 412. *num. 5.* Quod vero audis de Cælestii Carthagine primo judicio; haud velim existimes, ipsam Pelagianam hæresim in Synodo Carthaginiensi primum fuisse damnatam. Et enim S. Prosper, *Carm. de Ingrat.* hæc spectans oculis, constanter affirmat, primitus contigisse Romæ damnari: *more nimirum majorum, ut nova emergens hæresis primum omnium, à prima omnium Sede examinaretur, & damnaretur.* | | 39. Pelagius also the said Siricius first condemned, in the said Jovinian, from whom Pelagius himself drew and took his heresy, as St. Jerome attests of both, *lib. 2. contra Pelagium, & in Jeremiam, Præfat. libro. 4.* But afterwards Pelagius was condemned in the Council of Carthage. For thus says Spondanus, in the year of Christ 412, *num. 5.* But as to what you hear concerning the first judgment of Caelestius at Carthage, I would by no means have you think that the Pelagian heresy itself was first condemned in the Synod of Carthage. For St. Prosper, *Carm. de Ingrat.*, beholding these things with his own eyes, constantly affirms that it first befell that it was condemned at Rome: *after the manner, namely, of our forefathers, that a newly emerging heresy should first of all be examined and condemned by the first of all Sees.* |
| 40. Cœlestii etiam hæresis, damnata etiam fuit à Siricio Papa, priusquam à Concilio damnaretur Carthaginensi: quia uti ait Spondanus, ubi proxime, *numero 4.* Cœlestius, primarius Pelagii discipulus fuit. Unde si Pelagiana hæresis à Siricio Papa, qui præcessit dictum Carthaginense Concilium, fuit in Joviniano damnata, damnata etiam ab eodem Siricio fuit; Cœlestii, Pelagii primarii discipuli, hæresis. | | 40. The heresy of Caelestius also was condemned by Pope Siricius before it was condemned by the Council of Carthage: because, as Spondanus says in the place just cited, *numero 4.*, Caelestius was the foremost disciple of Pelagius. Whence, if the Pelagian heresy was condemned in Jovinian by Pope Siricius, who preceded the said Council of Carthage, the heresy of Caelestius, Pelagius's foremost disciple, was also condemned by the same Siricius. |
| 41. | 44. Prima, hujusmodi est. Summus Pontifex eam habet in definiendis rebus Fidei authoritatem, quam ipse D. Petrus primus Pontifex, à Deo institutus. Sed D. Petrus infalibilem authoritatem in definiendis rebus Fidei dependenter à Concilio Generali non habuit. Ergo nec Summus Pontifex D. Petri successor eam habet dependenter à Concilio Generali. Major constat, quia Petrus infalibilem authoritatem definiendi res Fidei non habuit ut privata persona, sed ut publicus Pastor, & universalis Ecclesiæ Rector: quippe qui illam à Christo accepit in bonum, & utilitatem Ecclesiæ, adeoque continuandam in suis successoribus. The Supreme Pontiff has that authority in defining matters of Faith which St. Peter himself, the first Pontiff instituted by God, had. But St. Peter did not have infallible authority in defining matters of Faith dependently upon a General Council. Therefore neither has the Supreme Pontiff, St. Peter's successor, that authority dependently upon a General Council. The major is evident, because Peter had infallible authority of defining matters of Faith not as a private person, but as public Pastor and Ruler of the universal Church: inasmuch as he received it from Christ for the good and utility of the Church, and therefore to be continued in his successors. The minor is evident from all those promises which Christ made to Peter as the future Ruler of the Church, all of which were made to the single individual person of Peter alone, with no mention made of the Church: *Matt. 16. To thee will I give the keys of the kingdom of heaven: whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth, it shall be bound also in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose upon earth, it shall be loosed also in heaven.* And likewise *Luke 22. I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and do thou at length confirm thy brethren.* And also, *John 21. Feed my sheep.* Since therefore Christ does not limit in Peter the power of confirming the brethren in Faith, of binding and loosing, and of feeding the sheep, by dependence upon the Church, but confers it absolute and free from all dependence, the adversaries falsely limit it by dependence upon a General Council. For which reason certainly there make the authorities which I recounted above, *numero 4*, taken from Cardinal Cajetan, *tom. 1. Opusculorum. tract. 2. cap. 14.* |
| 45. Nec valet hinc inferri Summum Pontificem. non debere in rebus Fidei definiendis, ullos adhibere consultores. Non inquam valet, quia licet dictis in locis, potestas definiendi, absolute, & independenter sit Petro, & successoribus ejus collata, aliunde constat, debere Pontificem adhibere Doctores, qui res examinent, & discutiant ante quam res ad Fidem spectantes definiat, nempe ex modo, quo Christus assistentiam promissit Pontifici, scilicet modo humano se habenti. | | 45. Nor is it valid to infer from this that the Supreme Pontiff ought not to employ any consultors in defining matters of Faith. It is not valid, I say, because although in the said passages the power of defining is conferred absolutely and independently upon Peter and his successors, it is established from another source that the Pontiff ought to employ Doctors who may examine and discuss the matters before he defines things pertaining to Faith — namely, from the manner in which Christ promised assistance to the Pontiff, that is, in a manner conformable to human ways. |
| 46. Quod si petas, quid si Pontifex vellet nullis consultis res Fidei, aut morum definire, habebit ne infalibilem Dei assistentiam, ne in definitione erret? But if you ask, what if the Pontiff should wish to define matters of Faith or of morals with none consulted, will he have the infallible assistance of God, that he err not in the definition? Amicus replies, *loco citato, n. 3.*, after Cano, *lib. 5. de Locis Theologicis, c. 5. quæst. 3.*, that the Holy Spirit will never permit the Pontiff to approach the deciding of controversies of Faith with the Doctors unconsulted: because he who promised the end promised also the means necessary to the end; but Christ promised to Peter and to his successors that the gates of hell should never prevail against his judgment, and for pronouncing a true judgment of Faith the previous consultation of Doctors and discussion of the truth is necessary. | 47. Secunda ratio, pro nostra sententia est: quia quotiescunque in aliqua propositione expresse revelata alia implicite continetur, hæc implicite contenta, pertinet ad eandem fidem, non quidem ut deducta; sed ut explicata. At in hac propositione expresse revelata: *Petrus etiam extra Concilium Generale, res Fidei definiens, habet infalibilem authoritatem à Christo,* virtute continetur hæc, non ut deducta, sed ut explicata: *Quivis legitimus Petri successor, etiam extra Concilium Generale res Fidei definiens, habet infalibilem authoritatem.* Ergo etiam hæc, non ut deducta, sed ut explicata pertinebit ad eandem Fidem, ad quam pertinet major expressa revelata. The second reason for our opinion is this: because as often as in some expressly revealed proposition another is implicitly contained, this implicitly contained one pertains to the same faith, not indeed as deduced, but as explicated. But in this expressly revealed proposition: *Peter, even outside a General Council, defining matters of Faith, has infallible authority from Christ*, this is virtually contained, not as deduced, but as explicated: *Any legitimate successor of Peter whatsoever, even outside a General Council, defining matters of Faith, has infallible authority.* Therefore this also, not as deduced but as explicated, will pertain to the same Faith to which the expressly revealed major pertains. The major is expressly had in the Sacred Oracles rendered a little before in the first reason, in which all the promises made by Christ to Peter, as Pastor of the universal Church, were made to his singular and individual person. The minor is evidently deduced from the end for which Christ conferred such authority upon Peter, namely that he might perpetuate it in the Church, according to that: *Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.* Which reason Diana relates and follows, *dicto tractatu 5. resolutione 4. numero 3.* |

And here he brings forth what follows: As concerns the doctrine of morals, it is no less certain that that which is a sin cannot be defined by the Pontiff to be good, than it is certain that he cannot err in the doctrine of faith, since it can also pertain to faith to believe whether this be a sin or not: whence he cannot define that usury is not a sin, etc. Thus Father Granado. |
| 53. Diana autem, *dicto n. 4.* istam firmat assertionem: Dicendum est, de fide esse, non posse Pontificem statuere aliquid ab universali Ecclesia observandum, quod legi divinæ, vel naturali contrarium sit. | | 53. But Diana, *dicto n. 4.*, affirms this assertion: It must be said that it is of faith that the Pontiff cannot establish anything to be observed by the universal Church which is contrary to the divine or to the natural law. |
| 54. Illam probat I. quia de fide est Pontificem errare non posse in pascendis ovibus; sed oves non solum pascuntur doctrina fidei, sed morum institutis. Ergo de fide est, in his institutis errare non posse, sicut errare non potest in doctrina fidei. | | 54. He proves it I. because it is of faith that the Pontiff cannot err in feeding the sheep; but the sheep are fed not only by the doctrine of faith, but by the institutes of morals. Therefore it is of faith that he cannot err in these institutes, just as he cannot err in the doctrine of faith. |
| 55. II. De fide est, in Ecclesia deficere non posse spiritum veritatis; sed si Pontifex observandum præciperet legi divinæ, aut naturali contrarium, jam deficeret in Ecclesia spiritus veritatis. Ergo non posse illud præcipere de fide est. | | 55. II. It is of faith that the spirit of truth cannot fail in the Church; but if the Pontiff should command something contrary to the divine or natural law to be observed, the spirit of truth would already fail in the Church. Therefore that he cannot command this is of faith. |
| 56. III. De fide est, Pontificem in rebus fidei non posse errorem docere; sed ex eo, quod præciperet aliquid observandum legi divinæ, aut naturali contrarium, errorem doceret, quia doceret credere, hoc esse observandum, quod est error in fide. Ergo de fide est illud non posse præcipere. | | 56. III. It is of faith that the Pontiff cannot teach error in matters of faith; but from the fact that he should command something to be observed contrary to the divine or natural law, he would teach error, because he would teach men to believe that this is to be observed, which is an error in faith. Therefore it is of faith that he cannot command that. |
| 57. Quarto probat ab inconvenienti; quia si posset Pontifex in ferendis legibus de rebus ad salutem necessariis errare, sequeretur, quod tota Ecclesia errare posset in negotio salutis; sed in salutis negotio tota Ecclesia errare non potest. Ergo nec potest Pontifex errare in ferendis legibus de rebus ad salutem necessariis. Minor patet ex Christi promissione: *& portæ inferi non prævalebunt adversus eam:* quia errare si posset, sive falsitate doctrinæ, sive malitia, & morum iniquitate, vere contra eam prævalere possent Inferorum portæ. | | 57. Fourthly he proves it from an inconvenience: because if the Pontiff could err in enacting laws about things necessary to salvation, it would follow that the whole Church could err in the business of salvation; but in the business of salvation the whole Church cannot err. Therefore neither can the Pontiff err in enacting laws about things necessary to salvation. The minor is plain from the promise of Christ: *and the gates of hell shall not prevail against her:* because, if she could err, whether by falsity of doctrine, or by malice and iniquity of morals, the gates of Hell could truly prevail against her. |
| 58. Sequela probatur: quia ex Christi præcepto tota tenetur Ecclesia Summo obedire Pontifici præcipienti, vel prohibenti necessaria ad salutem. Ergo si posset Summus Pontifex errare in hujusmodi præceptis, & prohibitionibus, posset errare tota Ecclesia in necessariis ad salutem. | | 58. The consequence is proved: because by the precept of Christ the whole Church is bound to obey the Supreme Pontiff commanding or prohibiting things necessary to salvation. Therefore, if the Supreme Pontiff could err in precepts and prohibitions of this kind, the whole Church could err in things necessary to salvation. |
| 59. Et confirmatur. Si posset Pontifex Summus errare in præcipiendis rebus ad salutem necessariis, posset etiam errare in rebus fidei; quia eadem Sacra scriptura docet, vitia esse turpia, & vitanda; è contra vero virtutes, honestas esse, & amplectendas: Si autem Summus Pontifex, aut vitium aliquod præciperet, aut virtutem prohiberet, contra ipsam sacram doceret Scripturam, vitium esse honestum, & amplectendum; & è contra, virtutem turpem esse, & vitandam, cum non possit vitium præcipere, nisi sub ratione boni, & honesti; nec virtutem prohibere, nisi sub ratione mali, & turpis. Ergo si Papa errare posset in præcipiendis rebus ad salutem necessariis, errare etiam posset in rebus ad fidem spectantibus. | | 59. And it is confirmed. If the Supreme Pontiff could err in commanding things necessary to salvation, he could also err in matters of faith; because the same Sacred Scripture teaches that vices are base and to be shunned, and, on the contrary, that virtues are honest and to be embraced. But if the Supreme Pontiff should either command some vice, or prohibit a virtue, he would teach against that same sacred Scripture that vice is honest and to be embraced, and, on the contrary, that virtue is base and to be shunned; since he cannot command a vice save under the aspect of the good and the honest, nor prohibit a virtue save under the aspect of the evil and the base. Therefore, if the Pope could err in commanding things necessary to salvation, he could also err in things pertaining to the faith. |
| 60. Suppositum nostrum probat Bellarminus, *loco citato*, dicendo. Non solum in Decretis fidei errare non potest Summus Pontifex, sed neque in præceptis morum, quæ toti Ecclesiæ præscribuntur, & quæ in rebus necessariis ad salutem, vel in iis, quæ per se bona, vel mala sunt versantur. | | 60. Bellarmine proves our supposition, *loco citato*, saying: Not only in the Decrees of faith can the Supreme Pontiff not err, but neither in the precepts of morals which are prescribed to the whole Church, and which are concerned with things necessary to salvation, or with those things which are *per se* good or evil. |
| 61. Dicimus *primum*, non posse errare Pontificem in his præceptis, quæ toti Ecclesiæ præscribuntur, quia ut supra diximus, in præceptis, & judiciis particularibus, non est absurdum Pontificem errare. Addidimus *secundo*, quæ in rebus necessariis ad salutem, vel per se bonis, aut malis versantur, quia non est erroneum dicere Pontificem, in aliis legibus posse errare, nimirum superfluam legem condendo, vel minus discretam, &c. | | 61. We say *first*, that the Pontiff cannot err in those precepts which are prescribed to the whole Church, because, as we said above, in particular precepts and judgments it is not absurd that the Pontiff should err. We added *secondly*, which are concerned with things necessary to salvation, or *per se* good or evil, because it is not erroneous to say that the Pontiff can err in other laws, namely by enacting a superfluous law, or a less discreet one, etc. |
| 62. Ac ut rem totam exemplis declaremus; Non potest fieri, ut Pontifex erret, præcipiendo aliquod vitium, ut usuram, vel prohibendo virtutem, ut restitutionem; quia hæc sunt per se bona, vel mala. Nec potest fieri, ut erret præcipiendo aliquid contra salutem, ut circuncisionem, vel Sabbathum: vel prohibendo aliquid necessarium ad salutem, ut Baptismum, aut Eucharistiam, licet hæc non sint per se bona, vel mala: ut autem jubeat aliquid, quod non sit bonum, neq; malum ex se, neq; cnntra salutem, sed tamen est inutile, vel sub pœna nimis gravi illud præcipiat, non est absurdum dicere posse fieri, quanquam non est subditorum, de hac re dubitare, sed simpliciter obedire. | | 62. And that we may declare the whole matter by examples: It cannot come about that the Pontiff should err by commanding some vice, as usury, or by prohibiting a virtue, as restitution; because these are *per se* good or evil. Nor can it come about that he should err by commanding something against salvation, as circumcision, or the Sabbath; or by prohibiting something necessary to salvation, as Baptism, or the Eucharist, although these are not *per se* good or evil. But that he should enjoin something which is neither good nor evil of itself, nor against salvation, but is nevertheless useless, or should command it under too grave a penalty, it is not absurd to say that this can come about; although it is not for subjects to doubt about this matter, but simply to obey. |
| 63. Probatur jam propositio, & *primo*, quod non possit Papa errare in præceptis morum ad salutem necessariorum; quia tunc tota Ecclesia graviter læderetur, & erraret in rebus necessariis: quod est contra promissionem Domini, *Joann. 16. Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem.* Quod intelligitur (ut minimum) de veritate necessaria ad salutem. | | 63. The proposition is now proved, and *first*, that the Pope cannot err in the precepts of morals necessary to salvation; because then the whole Church would be gravely injured, and would err in things necessary: which is against the promise of the Lord, *Joann. 16. When that Spirit of truth shall come, he will teach you all truth.* Which is understood (at the least) of the truth necessary to salvation. |
| 64. *Secundo*, quia Deus tunc deesset Ecclesiæ in necessariis, quandoquidem præcipit illi, ut sequatur Pontificem, & Pontificem permittit errare in necessariis. At certe, si Deus nulli rei deest in necessariis, quanto minus Ecclesiæ suæ? | | 64. *Secondly*, because God would then be wanting to the Church in things necessary, since he commands her to follow the Pontiff, and permits the Pontiff to err in things necessary. But surely, if God is wanting to no thing in what is necessary, how much less to his own Church? |
| 65. Quod autem non possit Pontifex errare in moribus per se bonis, vel malis, probatur. Nam tunc Ecclesia, non posset vere dici sancta, ut in Symbola Apostolorum vocatur. Nam sancta dicitur potissimum ob sanctam professionem, ut alibi ostendimus, quia nimirum legem, & professionem sanctam profitetur, quæ nihil docet falsum, nihil præcipit malum. | | 65. But that the Pontiff cannot err in morals *per se* good or evil is proved. For then the Church could not truly be called holy, as she is called in the Symbol of the Apostles. For she is called holy chiefly on account of her holy profession, as we have shown elsewhere, because, namely, she professes a holy law and profession, which teaches nothing false, commands nothing evil. |
| 66. *Secundo*, quia tunc necessario erraret etiam circa fidem. Nam fides Catholica docet, omnem virtutem esse bonam, omne vitium esse malum: si autem Papa erraret præcipiendo vitia, vel prohibendo virtutes; teneretur Ecclesia credere vitia esse bona, & virtutes malas, nisi vellet contra conscientiam peccare. Hactenus Cardinalis Bellarminus. | | 66. *Secondly*, because then she would necessarily err also concerning the faith. For the Catholic faith teaches that every virtue is good, every vice evil: but if the Pope should err by commanding vices, or by prohibiting virtues, the Church would be bound to believe that vices are good and virtues evil, unless she were willing to sin against conscience. Thus far Cardinal Bellarmine. |

Et ita sentit etiam Innocentius Tertius, *in capite, Per venerabilem, Qui filii sint legitimi.* Denique, omnia adducta in assertione, hoc corollarium confirmant; præsertim, quia ut supra dicebam, *Decreta morum includunt doctrinam moralem, non minus necessariam ad salutem, quàm sint aliæ veritates fidei.* | | 67. Father Francis Suarez, *tract. de Fide, disp. 5. sect. 8. n. 7.*, after he had affirmed, *n. 4.*, that the Pontiff defining *ex Cathedra* is a rule of faith which cannot err, when he authentically proposes something to the universal Church as to be believed with divine faith, speaks thus: From this general assertion it follows *first*, that the Pontiff cannot err in the precepts, or moral matters, which he hands on or approves for the universal Church. This is to be understood as to the substance, or as to the honesty of morals: for as to the circumstances, whether of multiplying precepts, or of rigor, or of excessive penalties, it is not inconvenient that some human defect be sometimes committed, because this is not against the sanctity of the Church; but to approve base things as honest, or conversely to condemn honest things as iniquitous, is repugnant to the truth and sanctity of the Church: and therefore in these also the Pontiff cannot err. Which Antoninus said to be of faith, 3. *part. tit. 12. c. 8. §. 2.*; but Cano, *l. 5. de Locis, c. 5.*, said that it is little removed from it. Other more recent writers, however, press this matter somewhat less: Molina, *de Justitia, disp. 325.*; Bellarmine, *l. 4. de Romano Pontifice, c. 5.* It is declared by an example. For the Church approved the communion of the laity under one species as sufficient for salvation; and therefore this is held as certain of faith, according to the Council of Trent, *sess. 22. c. 1.*: but in this and like matter, in order that the Church may hold it, the approbation of the Pontiff suffices, nay rather the Church is bound to follow it: therefore, that the Church may not err, it is necessary that the Pontiff cannot err in such matter. And so thinks also Innocent the Third, *in capite, Per venerabilem, Qui filii sint legitimi.* Finally, all the things adduced in the assertion confirm this corollary; especially because, as I was saying above, *the Decrees of morals include a moral doctrine no less necessary to salvation than are the other truths of faith.* |

Pasce oves meas.* Quæ testimonia ita sunt ab Ecclesia intellecta, & perpetua traditione, ac Patrum, Conciliorum, & ipsorum Pontificum definitione firmata, ac declarata, ut suppono ex propria materia (de Fide scilicet) & videri possunt, quæ Gratianus congerit, *distinct. 12. sui Decreti, & 24. q. 1.* per plura capita, & late Bellarminus, *l. 4. de Summo Pontifice*, à principio per aliquot capita (quorum non pauca testimonia ego ipse supra cumulavi *à n. 17. ad n. 34.*) & Turrecremata, *in Summa, l. 2. c. 10.* Et ob hanc rem observatum semper in Ecclesia fuit, ut graviores fidei quæstiones, & causæ, ad Romanam Sedem, tanquam ad fidei Cathedram, & magistram deferrentur, ut dicitur *in cap. Majores, de Baptismo*, & sumitur, *ex cap. Damnamus, de Summa Trinitate, & Fide Catholica, & cap. Fideli, eod. tit. in 6. cap. Ad abolendam, de Hæreticis, & cap. Præceptis, & cap. Non decet, dist. 12. & cap. Nulli fas, dist. 19.* ac tandem ex Conc. Florentino, *in Decreto Unionis*, & ex Conc. Trident. *sess. 7. can. 3. de Baptismo*, & *ex sess. 25. circa finem*, decreto de *Delectu ciborum*, & plura referunt citati Authores, Simancas, *de Catholicis Institutionibus, cap. 45.* Valde autem diminuta fuisset Christi Domini providentia circa suam Ecclesiam, si in decidendis talibus quæstionibus ux Cathedra, Vicarium suum labi permitteret; Neque ipse juste posset, sub anathematis vinculo, subditos compellere ad standum his declarationibus, seu definitionibus, si in doctrina tradenda errare posset. Cum ergo ostensum sit prædictam Constitutionem, continere declarationem cujusdam veritatis, valde necessariæ ad mores Ecclesiæ, & ad quietem, & pacem animarum, plane talis declaratio, infalibilem continet certitudinem. | | 71. Thus far Suarez, *loco citato*, who, *in tom. 4. de Relig. l. 3. c. 4. corollario 2.*, after he had asserted, *c. 3. n. 3.*, that the admission of Scholastics into the Society of Jesus constitutes them in a true and proper religious state, his assertion being proved from the Bull of Gregory XIII, *Quanto fructuosus*, of the year 1582, and from the Constitution *Ascendente Domino*, of the year 1584, says as follows. *Secondly*, it is inferred from what has been said how certain is the truth declared by the Pontiff. For I judge it to be altogether infallible, *so that it cannot be denied without error in faith.* The reason is, because the sentence of the Pontiff, in those things which pertain to doctrine, contains an infallible certitude, from the institution and promise of Christ: *I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not; and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren. Lucæ 32. & Mathæi 16. Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against her.* And the concession was made, *Joannes 21. Feed my sheep.* Which testimonies have been so understood by the Church, and so confirmed and declared by perpetual tradition, and by the definition of the Fathers, of the Councils, and of the Pontiffs themselves, that I suppose it from its proper matter (namely, that on Faith); and there may be seen the things which Gratian heaps up, *distinct. 12. sui Decreti, & 24. q. 1.*, through several chapters, and at large Bellarmine, *l. 4. de Summo Pontifice*, from the beginning through some chapters (not a few of which testimonies I myself have accumulated above, *à n. 17. ad n. 34.*), and Turrecremata, *in Summa, l. 2. c. 10.* And for this reason it was always observed in the Church that the graver questions and causes of faith should be referred to the Roman See, as to the Chair and mistress of faith, as is said *in cap. Majores, de Baptismo*, and is taken *ex cap. Damnamus, de Summa Trinitate, & Fide Catholica, & cap. Fideli, eod. tit. in 6. cap. Ad abolendam, de Hæreticis, & cap. Præceptis, & cap. Non decet, dist. 12. & cap. Nulli fas, dist. 19.*, and at length from the Council of Florence, *in Decreto Unionis*, and from the Council of Trent, *sess. 7. can. 3. de Baptismo*, and *ex sess. 25. circa finem*, the decree *de Delectu ciborum*; and the cited Authors report more, [as does] Simancas, *de Catholicis Institutionibus, cap. 45.* But greatly diminished would the providence of Christ the Lord concerning his Church have been, if in deciding such questions *ex Cathedra* he should permit his Vicar to fall; nor could he himself justly compel his subjects, under the bond of anathema, to stand by these declarations or definitions, if he could err in handing on doctrine. Since therefore it has been shown that the aforesaid Constitution contains the declaration of a certain truth very necessary to the morals of the Church, and to the quiet and peace of souls, plainly such a declaration contains an infallible certitude. |

| 73. Quod si petas, an dicta procedant in Legibus, aut Decretis morum, quæ nec sunt de Juris naturæ, aut juris Divini rebus, sed de indiferentibus, qualia essent præcepta de jejunio in Adventu, & de communione Eucharistiæ, in tribus anni temporibus? Respondeo cum Diana Coordinato, *tom. 9. tr. 9. resol. 13. n. 7.* procedere etiam in indifferentibus, in quorum legibus, & Decretis non potest Summus Pontifex ex Cathedra, seu Papa, qua talis decernens errare: quia Ecclesia tenetur Papæ obedire tanquam Pastori suo, & quia non præcipit rem inhonestam, aut bonam, & honestam prohibet: Unde, eo ipso, quod statuit hujusmodi legem, sive de re, quæ antea erat sub consilio, ut in exemplis adductis; sive de alia re, quovis alio modo indifferenti, nihil aliud facit (verba Dianæ reddo) quam constituere in materia virtutis actum, quem præcipit. | | 73. But if you ask whether the things said hold in Laws or Decrees of morals which are not about matters of the law of nature, or of divine law, but about indifferent things — such as would be the precepts concerning the fast in Advent, and concerning the communion of the Eucharist at the three seasons of the year? Whence, by the very fact that he establishes a law of this kind, whether about a matter which was previously under counsel, as in the examples adduced, or about another matter indifferent in any other way whatsoever, he does nothing else (I render Diana's words) than constitute in the matter of virtue the act which he commands. |
| 74. Neque obstat, quod unus Pontifex, ex his Decretis alterare valeat, & mutare, quæ alius statuerat. Neuter enim erravit, sed pro temporum diversitate; quod modo Pontifex conveniens judicat, postea successor, aut ipsemet statuens, prudenter, propter circumstantiam aliquam, inconveniens poterit judicare, & ita alterare, & mutare. Quod bene, & concludenter probatur ex *capite: Priscis, 55. distantia, ex capit. Sententiam, 35. quæstione 9.* ex Nicolao I. desumpto, ubi lego: *Ergo de judicio Romani Præsulis non retractando, quia nec mos exigit, quod diximus, comprobato, non negamus ejusdem Sedis sententiam posse in melius commutari, cum aut sibi surreptum aliquid fuerit, aut ipsa pro consideratione ætatum, vel temporum, seu gravium necessitatum dispensatione quædam ordinare decrevit.* Et *in cap. Senonensi, *in Decreto de unitate Ecclesiæ, & damnatione hæreticorum*, ubi legitur: *Ecclesia, quæ errare, non potest, diversa, & nonnunquam ... pugnantia licet proposuerit Decreta: nihil tamen est in tanta Decretornm multitudine, quod fidei Orthodoxæ adversetur.* Ex Concilio denique Tridentino, *sessione 25. capite 18.* dicente: *Quapropter sciant universi, Sacratissimos Canones exacte ab omnibus, & quoad ejus fieri poterit, indistincte observandos. Nor does it stand in the way, that one Pontiff can alter and change, out of these Decrees, the things which another had established. For neither erred, but [acted] according to the diversity of times: what the Pontiff now judges suitable, afterwards his successor, or he himself establishing, will be able prudently, on account of some circumstance, to judge unsuitable, and so to alter and change it. Which is well and conclusively proved from *capite: Priscis, 55. distantia, ex capit. Sententiam, 35. quæstione 9.*, taken from Nicholas I, where I read: *Therefore, the judgment of the Roman Prelate not being to be retracted — since neither does custom require it — what we have said being approved, we do not deny that the sentence of the same See can be changed for the better, when either something has been obtained from her by subreption, or when she herself, out of consideration of ages, or of times, or of grave necessities, has decreed to ordain certain things by dispensation.* And *in cap. Scit sancta Romana Ecclesia, that diverse constitutions or customs, according to place and time, in no wise hinder the salvation of believers, if Canonical authority does not withstand them.* It is proved also from *capit. Necessaria, 1. quæst. 7.*, taken *ex Epistol. 1. Gelasii I.*, and from many Councils: from that great Lateran, of 1215 Fathers, under Innocent III, saying *in capite 50. It ought not to be judged reprehensible, if according to the variety of times human statutes are sometimes varied, especially when urgent necessity, or evident utility, demands it: since God himself changed in the New some of the things which he had established in the Old Testament.* From the Council of Sens, *in Decreto de unitate Ecclesiæ, & damnatione hæreticorum*, where it is read: *The Church, which cannot err, although she has proposed diverse and sometimes [?] conflicting Decrees, yet there is nothing in so great a multitude of Decrees which is opposed to the Orthodox faith.* Finally, from the Council of Trent, *sessione 25. capite 18.*, saying: *Wherefore let all know that the most sacred Canons are to be exactly observed by all, and, so far as it can be done, without distinction. But if an urgent and just reason, and sometimes a greater utility, shall demand it, dispensation is to be made with some.* For the same argument, see Diana Coordinatus, *tomo 9. tractatu 13. numero 8.*, and many in him, and Peter Crespet *in Summa Ecclesiastica Disciplina, verbo Canones Patrum, aliquando necessitate variantur, & immutantur, & verbo Traditiones Patrum, quandoque variantur.* |
| 75. Quod si iterum petas, An Summus Pontifex errare possit in Constitutione, aut Decreto facto in rebus fidei, aut morum pro una Provincia, aut Diœcesi? Respondet Diana, *de resolutione 13. numero 6.* quod quamvis aliqui sentiant errare posse; quia tunc munus Pontificis gerere non videatur; eo quod Ecclesiam Universalem illo non adstringat Decreto, ipse tamen credit; cum Bannez 2. 2. *quæstione 1. articulo 10. dubio 6. conclusione 4.* de fide esse; nec tunc errare posse Pontificem. Quia Pontifex; quatenus supremus est Pastor; legem; aut Decretum statuit obligantem aliquam Provinciam, vel Diœcesim: quia secundum hanc rationem, non solum Regimen totius Ecclesiæ illi commissum est, sed etiam cujuslibet Provinciæ, & Parochiæ. Si ergo respectu totius Ecclesiæ errare non potest, quia illius est suprema regula veritatis; cum etiam cujuslibet Provinciæ, & Parochiæ respectu, sit eadem regula, sane efficitur errare non posse respectu Provinciæ, aut Parochiæ. | | 75. But if you ask again, Whether the Supreme Pontiff can err in a Constitution, or Decree, made in matters of faith or of morals for one Province or Diocese? Diana answers, *de resolutione 13. numero 6.*, that although some think he can err — because then he does not seem to bear the office of Pontiff, in that he does not bind the Universal Church by that Decree — he himself nevertheless believes, with Bañes 2. 2. *quæstione 1. articulo 10. dubio 6. conclusione 4.*, that it is of faith that neither then can the Pontiff err. Because the Pontiff, inasmuch as he is supreme Pastor, establishes a law or Decree obliging some Province or Diocese: because according to this account, not only the Government of the whole Church has been committed to him, but also that of any Province and Parish whatsoever. If, therefore, in respect of the whole Church he cannot err, because he is her supreme rule of truth, since in respect also of any Province and Parish whatsoever he is the same rule, it is soundly brought about that he cannot err in respect of a Province or Parish. |
| 76. Secundo, si admittatur errare posse Pontificem in statuto speciali, præcipiendo observari in aliqua Provincia, vel Parochia; quæ contraria sunt legi divinæ, aut naturali, videtur admitti errare posse circa totam Ecclesiam: quia Ecclesia Universalis sufficiens habet fundamentum ad credendum sic ordinatum, & præceptum bonum esse, & servandum ab illa Provincia, seu Parochia: Sed tale judicium est erroneum: Ergo ex errore speciali Pontificis, cogitur Ecclesia universalis errare, quod dici non potest. | | 76. Secondly, if it be admitted that the Pontiff can err in a special statute, commanding things contrary to the divine or natural law to be observed in some Province or Parish, it seems to be admitted that he can err concerning the whole Church: because the Universal Church has a sufficient foundation for believing that what is so ordained and commanded is good, and to be observed by that Province or Parish. But such a judgment is erroneous. Therefore, from a special error of the Pontiff, the universal Church is forced to err, which cannot be said. |

Et *in capite de Libellis dist. 20.* tanquam veri, & legitimi Pontifices Silverius, Sozimus, Innocentius, Syricius, Cœlestinus, Leo, Hilarius, Gelasius, Ormisda, & Gregorius approbantur, à Leone Papa Quarto. Et huic sententiæ magnum robur (inquit ipse Diana) cedit Clemens Octavus, qui teste Puente Hurtado, *ubi supra* carceravit nonnullos Doctores, qui tunc oppositum censuerunt, eosque jubens adire Romam, rationem reddituros illius sententiæ. | | 80. And Diana adds: Our opinion seems to be the opinion of Augustine, *tom. 6. in Epistol. contra potest. Donat. & tom. 2. epist. 115.*, of Irenæus, *lib. 3. capite 3.*, of Basil, Ambrose, Jerome, whom Bañes reports, *d. art. 10. de Authoitate Papæ.* And the Councils favor it, he says: for the Sixth General confessed Agatho to be the true Pontiff, as that of Chalcedon confessed Leo, and the Milevitan, Innocent III. And *in capite de Libellis dist. 20.*, Silverius, Zosimus, Innocent, Siricius, Celestine, Leo, Hilary, Gelasius, Hormisdas, and Gregory are approved, by Pope Leo the Fourth, as true and legitimate Pontiffs. And to this opinion great strength (says Diana himself) is yielded by Clement the Eighth, who, on the testimony of Puente Hurtado, *ubi supra*, imprisoned certain Doctors who then judged the opposite, ordering them to go to Rome, to render an account of that opinion. |
| 81. Pro hac sententia fundanda, supponit Diana Summum Pontificem, posse considerari dupliciter. *Primo*, quando eligitur, & adoratur illegitime, vel cum suspicione illegitimitatis. *Secundo*, quando tam legitime eligitur, & adoratur, ut tota Ecclesia illum suscipiat, ut nunc (inquit) Dominus noster Innocentius X. (ego autem ut nunc Innocentius XI.) sine ullo controversiæ vestigio, tenet Christianæ Reipublicæ clavum, clavesque Petri. Si loquamur de Pontifice in prima consideratione, (ait Diana) non est quæstio: quia dum est probabile fundamentum, de illegitimitate Pontificis, non potest fide divina credi eum esse successorem Petri: quia fides divina non potest exerceri, quando est locus hæsitationi prudenti: probabile autem fundamentum, occasionem præbet hæsitationi prudenti. Quapropter non quiescit Ecclesia, antequam erecto omni dubitandi fundamento, tollantur schismatum occasiones. Est igitur quæstio in secunda consideratione, Utrum sit Fide Divina credendum, Innocentium Undecimum, & alios Pontifices pacifice susceptos, esse legitimos successores Petri. Et respondet Diana, credendum esse fide Divina, omnes, & singulos ejusmodi Pontifices, esse legitimos successores Petri. Et quidem in hoc sensu, hanc tenet sententiam Suarez, ibi: *Postquam sufficienti consensu totius Ecclesiæ receptus est*, & alii tenere debent. | | 81. For the establishing of this opinion, Diana supposes that the Supreme Pontiff can be considered in two ways. *First*, when he is elected and adored illegitimately, or with a suspicion of illegitimacy. *Secondly*, when he is so legitimately elected and adored that the whole Church receives him, as now (he says) our Lord Innocent X — but I: as now Innocent XI — without any vestige of controversy holds the helm of the Christian Commonwealth, and the keys of Peter. If we speak of the Pontiff in the first consideration (says Diana), there is no question: because so long as there is a probable foundation concerning the illegitimacy of the Pontiff, it cannot be believed with divine faith that he is the successor of Peter: because divine faith cannot be exercised when there is room for prudent hesitation; but a probable foundation affords occasion for prudent hesitation. Wherefore the Church does not rest until, every foundation for doubting being removed [?], the occasions of schisms are taken away. The question, therefore, is in the second consideration: Whether it is to be believed with Divine Faith that Innocent the Eleventh, and other Pontiffs peacefully received, are the legitimate successors of Peter. And Diana answers that it is to be believed with Divine faith that all and each of the Pontiffs of this kind are the legitimate successors of Peter. And indeed in this sense Suarez holds this opinion, there: *After he has been received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church*; and others ought to hold it. |
| 82. Probatur *primo* hæc sententia, ex Bulla Martini V. quæ habetur in Concilio Constantiensi, in qua disponit, ut hæretici ad Ecclesiam redeuntes credant Papam canonice electum, qui pro tempore fuerit (expresso ejus nomine) esse successorem Petri, &c. Ergo snpponit Concilium nunc determinate de moderno, v. gr. Innocentio XI. expresso ejus nomine, debere credi esse Petri successorem. | | 82. This opinion is proved *first* from the Bull of Martin V, which is had in the Council of Constance, in which he disposes that heretics returning to the Church should believe that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being (his name being expressed), is the successor of Peter, etc. Therefore the Council supposes that now, determinately concerning the present Pope, e.g., Innocent XI, his name being expressed, it must be believed that he is the successor of Peter. |
| 83. *Secundo*, quia multa Concilia determinate agnoscunt pro tali Petri successore, eum Pontificem, qui tunc præerat: quod etiam fuerunt plures Ecclesiæ Patres, quorum non pauci recensiti sunt *supra, numero 4. & 80.* At non est major ratio de uno, quam de alio. Ergo idem de omnibus, canonice electis, & à tota recepti Ecclesia, dicendum est. | | 83. *Secondly*, because many Councils determinately acknowledge as such a successor of Peter that Pontiff who then presided: which many Fathers of the Church also did [?], not a few of whom have been recounted *supra, numero 4. & 80.* But there is no greater reason concerning one than concerning another. Therefore the same is to be said of all who are canonically elected and received by the whole Church. |
| 84. *Tertio*, quia si non constat de fide Pium IV. fuisse verum Pontificem, neque constabit Concilium Tridentinum esse validum, cum ab illius approbatione totam suam acceperit vim. Id autem fere hæreticum est. | | 84. *Thirdly*, because if it is not established of faith that Pius IV was the true Pontiff, neither will it be established that the Council of Trent is valid, since it received all its force from his approbation. But that is well-nigh heretical. |
| 85. *Quarto*, quia si non esset de fide hunc numero Pontificem v. gr. Innocentium XI. esse verum Pontificem, & successorem D. Petri, quidquid de Judice controversiarum in Ecclesia, ut certum de fide, creditur, esset dubio procul ridiculum; nam quomodo de fide debeo credere, Judicem dari controversiarum in Ecclesia, si non possum, ex fide, quis ille sit, dicere? Ad quid enim deserviret pro praxi, assensus de hujusmodi Judicis quasi vaga existentia? Non utique teneremur ex fide, in dictis controversiis, Papam adire. Quilibet enim dicere posset fides solum docet; me teneri Judicem adire controversiarum; sed non mihi dicit, illum esse Innocentium XI. Ergo, ex fide, non teneor illum adire, aut illi decidenti credere. Ergo, ut ridiculum non sit, quod de Judice dicitur controversiarum, dici debet, de fide esse Innocentium XI. esse verum Pontificem, & successorem D. Petri. | | 85. *Fourthly*, because if it were not of faith that this individual Pontiff, e.g., Innocent XI, is the true Pontiff and successor of St. Peter, whatever is believed as certain of faith concerning the Judge of controversies in the Church would beyond doubt be ridiculous; for how am I bound to believe of faith that a Judge of controversies is given in the Church, if I cannot, from faith, say who he is? For to what would an assent concerning the, as it were, vague existence of such a Judge serve for practice? We should assuredly not be bound from faith, in the said controversies, to go to the Pope. For anyone could say: faith teaches only that I am bound to go to the Judge of controversies; but it does not tell me that he is Innocent XI. Therefore, from faith, I am not bound to go to him, or to believe him deciding. Therefore, that what is said of the Judge of controversies may not be ridiculous, it must be said that it is of faith that Innocent XI is the true Pontiff and successor of St. Peter. |
| 86. *Quinto* probatur, quia omnis propositio de fide, potest inferri ex duplici propositione de fide, & etiam, ut aliis placet, ex una de fide, & altera lumine naturali contenta. Sed ista propositio, *Papa definiens non potest errare*, est certa de fide. Ergo inferri potest ex duplici præmissa, modo dicto; quod tamen nullo modo fieri potest, ut patebit discurrenti, nisi propositio ista sit de fide: *Hic Pontifex definiens, est verus Papa.* Nam in quocumque syllogismo illa inferatur conclusio, *Papa definiens non potest errare*, debet assumi ut minor, *Hic Pontifex definiens, est verus Papa*, quæ cum non sit nota lumine naturali, necessario debet esse de fide, aliàs conclusio non esset de fide. | | 86. *Fifthly* it is proved, because every proposition of faith can be inferred from two propositions of faith, and also, as others hold, from one of faith and another contained in the natural light. But this proposition, *The Pope defining cannot err*, is certain of faith. Therefore it can be inferred from two premises, in the manner stated; which nevertheless can in no way be done, as will be plain to one who runs through it, unless this proposition be of faith: *This Pontiff defining is the true Pope.* For in whatever syllogism that conclusion be inferred, *The Pope defining cannot err*, there must be assumed as the minor, *This Pontiff defining is the true Pope*, which, since it is not known by the natural light, must necessarily be of faith; otherwise the conclusion would not be of faith. |
| 87. Sed huic argumento respondet Bannez, *ubi supra*, non requiri, ut aliqua conclusio de fide inferatur, utramque præmissam esse de fide, vel unam de fide, & alteram evidentem; sed sufficere, unam esse de fide, & alteram moraliter certam: quia tunc conclusio non sequitur ex illa propositione moraliter certa, tanquam ex causa, vel tanquam ex conditione sine qua non, sive ut applicante. Et adducit Exemplum iu prædicatore, qui si tibi rite proponit Evangelicam doctrinam, fide divina teneris veritatibus ab eo propositis assentiri: cum tamen non sit fide divina certum, hunc prædicatorem esse infalibilis veritatis. | | 87. But to this argument Bañes answers, *ubi supra*, that it is not required, in order that some conclusion of faith be inferred, that both premises be of faith, or one of faith and the other evident; but that it suffices that one be of faith and the other morally certain: because then the conclusion follows from that morally certain proposition not as from a cause, but as from a condition without which not, or as applying it. And he adduces the Example of a preacher, who, if he duly proposes to you the Evangelical doctrine, you are bound with divine faith to assent to the truths proposed by him: although nevertheless it is not certain with divine faith that this preacher is of infallible truth. |
| 88. At replicat Diana, & solutionem præcludere intendit, argumento ducto ab inconvenienti: quia si vera illa esset, sequeretur hanc propositionem esse de fide: *sub hac numero Hostia consecrata, est Corpus Christi*: quia revera infertur per evidentem consequentiam ex una de fide, qualis est ista: *In omni Hostia debite consecrata, est Corpus Christi*, & insuper infertur tanquam ex conditione, & ut applicante ex altera certa moraliter, scilicet: *Hæc Hostia est debite consecrata*: Ergo *sub hac Hostia est Corpus Christi*: erit propositio de fide. | | 88. But Diana replies, and intends to preclude the solution, by an argument drawn from an inconvenience: because if that were true, it would follow that this proposition is of faith: *under this individual consecrated Host is the Body of Christ*: because in truth it is inferred by an evident consequence from one [proposition] of faith, such as is this: *In every duly consecrated Host is the Body of Christ*, and moreover it is inferred, as from a condition and as applying, from another morally certain one, namely: *This Host is duly consecrated*: Therefore *under this Host is the Body of Christ* will be a proposition of faith. |
| Idem argumentum potest de his formari propositionibus. *Hic parvulus baptizatus recipit gratiam: Iste qui ordinatur est Sacerdos:* & de aliis similibus: quas compertum est apud omnes, non esse de fide, cum possit illis subesse falsum, ex defectu intentionis baptizantis, ordinantis, & consecrantis. Neque exemplum de prædicatore obstat, quia licet prædicator, aliis in rebus, non sit infalibilis veritatis: at prout hic, & nunc prædicat, credere debes infalibilis veritatis esse: siquidem obligaris ejus dictis fide divina assentiri. | | The same argument can be formed of these propositions: *This baptized infant receives grace: This man who is ordained is a Priest:* and of others like them: which it is ascertained among all are not of faith, since falsity can underlie them, from a defect of intention in the one baptizing, ordaining, and consecrating. Nor does the example of the preacher stand in the way, because although the preacher, in other matters, is not of infallible truth, yet as he here and now preaches, you must believe him to be of infallible truth; since you are obliged to assent to his words with divine faith. |
| 89. Neque contra nostram sententiam obstat, si dicatur: Non est de fide certum, Innocentium XI. esse baptizatum, aut nullum defectum substantialem in ejus electione intervenisse. Ergo non est de fide Innocentium XI. esse verum, & legitimum Pontificem, Petri successorem, & infalibilis veritatis. Non enim obstat, quia licet antecedens non sit de fide; est tamen consequens, postquam communi Ecclesiæ consensu habitus ille est pro vero Pontifice: & successore Petri: quia illa, cum columna sit veritatis, errare non potest in illo consensu. Quibus suppositis, sit. | | 89. Nor does it stand against our opinion, if it be said: It is not certain of faith that Innocent XI is baptized, or that no substantial defect intervened in his election. Therefore it is not of faith that Innocent XI is the true and legitimate Pontiff, successor of Peter, and of infallible truth. For it does not stand in the way, because although the antecedent is not of faith, the consequent nevertheless is, after he has by the common consent of the Church been held for the true Pontiff and successor of Peter: because she, since she is the pillar of truth, cannot err in that consent. These things being supposed, be it as follows. |
| **CONCLUSIO I.** | | **CONCLUSION I.** |
| 90. Omni certitudine tenendum est; prædictas propositiones esse vere damnatas scandalosas, ita ut neque actu interno, neque externo, possit aliquis dubium formare, de hac veritate. | | 90. It is to be held with all certitude that the aforesaid propositions are truly condemned as scandalous, so that neither by an internal nor by an external act can anyone form a doubt about this truth. |
| Conclusionem istam probo *primo*. Omnino certum, & verum est, Pontificem, & legitimum Petri, in Sede Romana successorem, ex Cathedra, seu qua Summum Pontificem, & non qua Doctorem particularem, doctrinam nedum Fidei, sed etiam morum universis Christi fidelibus tradendo, aut prohibendo, errare non posse, tradendo scilicet falsam, & animarum saluti noxiam, & præjudicialem, & veram, & proficuam dictæ saluti prohibendo. Ergo etiam omnino certum est, & verum, ita ut nullus, aut actu interno, aut externo propositiones prædictas ex Cathedra damnatas ab Alexandro Septimo, vere tales esse, ut tales habendas, in dubium revocare possit. | | I prove this conclusion *first*. It is altogether certain and true that the Pontiff, and legitimate successor of Peter in the Roman See, *ex Cathedra*, or as Supreme Pontiff, and not as a particular Doctor, in handing on or in prohibiting to all the faithful of Christ a doctrine not only of Faith but also of morals, cannot err — namely, by handing on a false doctrine, noxious and prejudicial to the salvation of souls, and by prohibiting a true one profitable to the said salvation. Therefore it is also altogether certain and true that no one can, either by an internal or by an external act, call into doubt that the aforesaid propositions condemned *ex Cathedra* by Alexander the Seventh are truly such, and are to be held as such. |
| 91. Antecedens, est notum, id enim, quod continet in primis, est, verum, & legitimum, Romanum Pontificem, succedere Apostolo Petro in primatu Ecclesiæ; & in potestate illi à Christo Domino tradita *Joannis 21.* dum dixit, *Pasce oves meas.* Hæc Catholica veritas supra firmata est in 4. Notabili, seu supposito *à numero 4. ad numer. 8. inclusive*, ex quamplurimis Sanctis, Ecclesiæ Patribus, Conciliis, & Pontificibus Summis: & ita quidem efficaciter, ut à nullo Catholico negari possit, aut in dubium revocari. | | 91. The antecedent is known; for that which it contains in the first place is that the true and legitimate Roman Pontiff succeeds the Apostle Peter in the primacy of the Church, and in the power delivered to him by Christ the Lord, *Joannis 21.*, when he said, *Feed my sheep.* This Catholic truth was established above in the 4th Notable, or supposition, *à numero 4. ad numer. 8. inclusive*, out of very many Saints, Fathers of the Church, Councils, and Supreme Pontiffs; and indeed so efficaciously that it cannot be denied by any Catholic, or called into doubt. |
| 92. Secundo continet, Summum Pontificem, esto errare possit quatenus persona particularis, seu privata, & qua Doctor particularis, & non solum in doctrina morum sed etiam fidei, juxta ea, quæ dicta sunt *numero 12. 61. 62. 67. & 70.* Et esto errare possit, etiam, ut Ecclesiæ caput in controversis facti, ad tradita *numero 11.* tamen in neutra Doctrina Fidei, & morum errare potest, in quantum Ecclesiæ Caput, Doctor, & Pastor, ut eam ut talis doceat, quasi ex Cathedra, seu in quantum utitur Magisterio, & potestate pascendi Christi fideles, sibi in Petro ab eodem Christo tradita, & omnibus in Apostolica Sede legitime sedentibus, ut dictum est *numero 17. & sequentibus*, quoad res Fidei, & quoad res morum, *à numero 52. ad 56.* & signanter, *num. 74. 75. & 76.* | | 92. Secondly, it contains that the Supreme Pontiff — granted that he can err inasmuch as he is a particular or private person, and as a particular Doctor, and not only in the doctrine of morals but also of faith, according to those things which have been said *numero 12. 61. 62. 67. & 70.*; and granted that he can err, even as head of the Church, in controversies of fact, according to what was handed down *numero 11.* — nevertheless in neither Doctrine, of Faith and of morals, can he err, inasmuch as he is Head, Doctor, and Pastor of the Church, so as to teach her as such, as it were *ex Cathedra*, or inasmuch as he uses the Magisterium and the power of feeding the faithful of Christ delivered to him in Peter by the same Christ, and to all who legitimately sit in the Apostolic See, as was said *numero 17. & sequentibus*, as regards matters of Faith, and as regards matters of morals, *à numero 52. ad 56.*, and signally, *num. 74. 75. & 76.* |
| 93. Consequentia ostenditur, quia omnino constat in Ecclesia id, quod affirmat, scilicet Alexandrum Septimum, dictas damnavisse, & prohibuisse propositiones, non uti Doctorem particularem, & ut personam privatam, sed uti Doctorem, & Pastorem universalem Ecclesiæ, & ex Petri Cathedra. Quod certe ex ipsorum Decretorum inspectione evidentissimum fit. In primo enim sic lego. | | 93. The consequence is shown, because that which it affirms is altogether established in the Church, namely, that Alexander the Seventh condemned and prohibited the said propositions, not as a particular Doctor and as a private person, but as universal Doctor and Pastor of the Church, and from the Chair of Peter. Which indeed becomes most evident from the inspection of the Decrees themselves. For in the first I read thus. |
| 94. *Sanctissimus Dominus noster audivit, non sine magno animi sui mœrore, complures opiniones Christianæ disciplinæ relaxativas, & animarum perniciem inferentes, partim antiquatas, iterum suscitari, partim noviter prodire, & summam illam luxuriantium ingeniorum licentiam in dies magis excrescere, per quam in rebus ad conscientiam pertinentibus, modus opinandi irrepsit, alienus omnino ab Evangelica simplicitate, Sanctorumq; Patrum doctrina, & quem, si pro forte: sequerentur regula fideles in praxi sequuntur, ingens irruptura esset, Christiana vita corruptela. Quare ne ullo unquam tempore viam salutis, quam suprema veritas Deus, cujus verba, in æternum permanent, strictam esse definivit, in animarum perniciem dilatari, seu verius perverti contingeret, idem Sanctissimus Dominus Noster, ut Oves sibi creditas, ab hujusmodi spatiosa, lataque, per quam itur ad perditionem viam, pro Pastorali solicitudine, in rectam semitam evocaret, earumdem opinionum examen, pluribus in Sacra Theologia Magistris, & deinde Eminentissimis, & Reverendissimis Dominis Cardinalibus, contra hæreticam pravitatem Generalibus Inquisitoribus serio commisit, qui tantum negotium strenue aggressi, ei que sedulo incumbentes, & mature discussis, usque ad hanc diem, infra scriptis propositionibus, super unaquaque ipsarum, sua suffragia, Sanctitati suæ sigillatim exposuerunt.* | | 94. *Our Most Holy Lord has heard, not without great grief of his mind, that very many opinions relaxing Christian discipline and bringing on the ruin of souls, partly antiquated, are again being raised up, partly are newly coming forth, and that that utmost license of luxuriating wits is daily growing greater, through which, in matters pertaining to conscience, a manner of opining has crept in altogether alien from Evangelical simplicity and from the doctrine of the holy Fathers; and which, if the faithful should follow it as a rule in practice [?], an immense corruption of Christian life would burst in. Wherefore, lest at any time it should happen that the way of salvation — which the supreme truth, God, whose words remain for ever, has defined to be strait — should be widened, or rather perverted, to the ruin of souls, the same our Most Holy Lord, that he might, out of his Pastoral solicitude, call back the Sheep committed to him from this sort of spacious and broad way, by which one goes to perdition, into the right path, seriously committed the examination of the same opinions to several Masters in Sacred Theology, and then to the Most Eminent and Most Reverend Lord Cardinals, General Inquisitors against heretical pravity; who, vigorously undertaking so great a business, and diligently applying themselves to it, and the below-written propositions having been maturely discussed down to this day, set forth their suffrages upon each one of them singly to His Holiness.* |
| 95. In quo quidem Decreti exordio plura ad rem veniunt ponderanda. *Primum* est, usum fuisse Alexandrum consiliis, & suffragiis, nedum plurimorum in Sacra Theologia Magistrorum, sed etiam Sacræ Congregationis Eminentissimorum Cardinalium, Generalium Inquisitorum contra hæreticam pravitatem, post maturum uniuscujusque propositionis examen, & & sedulam discussionem: quibus usus fuit, ut humano modo so haberet in dijudicandis prædictis propositionibus. Licet enim assistentia Spiritus Sancti non fuerit ei promissa in Petro pro definiendis rebus Fidei, & morum, dependenter ab alicujus consilio, aut assensu, ut licite tamen, & prudenter operetur, consilio indiget alieno, ut dictum est *numero 45. & 46.* quod certe non fuisset ab Alexandro petitum, & multo minus à tot, & talibus Consiliariis, & Consultoribus, si ut privatus Doctor fuisset processurus. | | 95. In which exordium of the Decree there are indeed several things that come to be weighed as pertinent to the matter. The *first* is that Alexander used the counsels and suffrages, not only of very many Masters in Sacred Theology, but also of the Sacred Congregation of the Most Eminent Cardinals, General Inquisitors against heretical pravity, after a mature examination and diligent discussion of each single proposition: which he used, that he might bear himself in a human manner in adjudging the aforesaid propositions. For although the assistance of the Holy Spirit was not promised to him in Peter for defining matters of Faith and of morals dependently upon anyone's counsel or assent, yet, that he may act lawfully and prudently, he needs another's counsel, as was said *numero 45. & 46.*: which assuredly would not have been sought by Alexander, and much less from so many and such Counsellors and Consultors, if he had been about to proceed as a private Doctor. |
| 96. Secundum, quod venit ponderandnm est, eo animo Alexandrum, Decretum edidisse, *ut Oves sibi creditas ab spatiosa, lataque per quam itur ad perditionem via, pro Pastorali solicitudine, in rectam semitam evocaret.* Quæ, certe Decreti clausula, evidentissime demonstrat processisse illius Conditorem, non ut personam privatam, & ut Doctorem particularem, sed uti Universalem Ecclesiæ Doctorem, & Pastorem: qua tali enim creditæ sunt à Christo Oves ejus Petro, & in Petro, Alexandro, & non qua Fabio Ghisio. | | 96. The second thing that comes to be weighed is that Alexander published the Decree with this mind, *that he might, out of his Pastoral solicitude, call back the Sheep committed to him from the spacious and broad way, by which one goes to perdition, into the right path.* Which clause of the Decree most evidently demonstrates that its Framer proceeded, not as a private person and as a particular Doctor, but as universal Doctor and Pastor of the Church: for as such were his Sheep committed by Christ to Peter, and in Peter to Alexander, and not as to Fabio Chigi. |
| 97. Et denique eadem veritas demonstratur, ex utriusque Decreti fine: *Statuit* (utrobique dicitur,) *& decrevit, prædictas propositiones, & unamquamque ipsarum, ut minimum, tanquam scandalosas esse damnandas, & prohibendas, sicut eas damnat, & prohibet, ita, ut quicumque illas, aut conjunctim, aut divisim docuerit, defenderit, ediderit, aut de eis, etiam disputative, publice, aut privatim tractaverit, nisi forsan impugnando, ipso facto incidat in Excommunicationem, à qua non possit (præterquam in articulo mortis) ab alio quacumque etiam dignitate fulgente, nisi a pro tempore existente Romano Pontifice, absolvi. Insuper districte in virtute Sanctæ Obedientiæ, & sub interminatione divini Judicii prohibet omnibus Christi fidelibus, cujuscumque conditionis, dignitatis, ac status, etiam specialissima nota dignis, ne prædictas opiniones, aut aliquam ipsarum ad praxim deducant.* Demonstratur itaque ex his Decretorum finibus, Alexandrum Septimum, qua Summum Pontificem processisse, quia qua persona particularis non posset damnare, & prohibere opiniones illas, maxime omnibus Christi fidelibus cujuscumque conditionis, dignitatis, ac status, & multo minus, illos præcepto formali, & excommunicationi Sedi Apostolicæ reservatæ, subjicere. | | 97. And finally the same truth is demonstrated from the ending of each Decree: *He has established* (it is said in both places) *and decreed that the aforesaid propositions, and each one of them, are, at the least, to be condemned and prohibited as scandalous, even as he does condemn and prohibit them; so that whosoever shall have taught, defended, or published them, either conjointly or severally, or shall have treated of them, even disputatively, publicly or privately, unless perchance by way of impugning them, shall ipso facto incur Excommunication, from which he cannot (except at the point of death) be absolved by any other, however resplendent with dignity, save by the Roman Pontiff existing for the time being. Moreover he strictly, in virtue of Holy Obedience, and under the threat of the divine Judgment, forbids all the faithful of Christ, of whatsoever condition, dignity, and state, even such as are worthy of most special note, to reduce the aforesaid opinions, or any of them, to practice.* It is demonstrated, therefore, from these endings of the Decrees, that Alexander the Seventh proceeded as Supreme Pontiff, because as a particular person he could not condemn and prohibit those opinions, especially [not] to all the faithful of Christ of whatsoever condition, dignity, and state, and much less subject them to a formal precept, and to an excommunication reserved to the Apostolic See. |
| 98. Pro nobis est Joannes de Cardenas, *in Crisi Theologica, parte 1. tractatu 1. disputat. 9. capit. 20. articulo 3.* ubi dicto, Romanum Pontificem loqui ex Cathedra, docere Ecclesiam, loqui Ecclesiæ, & loqui ut Caput Ecclesiæ, esse quatuor omnino synonima, & ejusdem prorsus significationis; ait: Sed quid est Pontificem loqui ex Cathedra, & ut Caput Ecclesiæ? Optime explicuit P. Valentia, 2. 2. *puncto 7. §. 39.* inquiens: Tunc vere definire Summum Pontificem, ut Ecclesiæ Caput, quando Decretum ederet pro universa Ecclesia, quodque vellet à fidelibus indubitanter admitti, quod idem tradit P. Suarez, *contra Regem Angliæ, libro 1. capite 6. numero 15. & 16.* & P. Granado, *2. 2. controvers. 1. tractat. 7. disputat. 2. sect. 1. numero 1.* & communiter Doctores. | | 98. For us is John de Cardenas, *in Crisi Theologica, parte 1. tractatu 1. disputat. 9. capit. 20. articulo 3.*, where, having said that for the Roman Pontiff to speak *ex Cathedra*, to teach the Church, to speak to the Church, and to speak as Head of the Church, are altogether four synonyms, and of entirely the same signification, he says: But what is it for the Pontiff to speak *ex Cathedra*, and as Head of the Church? Fr. Valentia excellently explained it, 2. 2. *puncto 7. §. 39.*, saying: That the Supreme Pontiff then truly defines, as Head of the Church, when he should issue a Decree for the universal Church, and one which he should will to be by the faithful indubitably admitted; which the same is handed down by Fr. Suarez, *contra Regem Angliæ, libro 1. capite 6. numero 15. & 16.*, and Fr. Granado, *2. 2. controvers. 1. tractat. 7. disputat. 2. sect. 1. numero 1.*, and commonly the Doctors. |
| 99. Et statim prosequitur Cardenas, dicendo: Hinc manifeste patet hoc Decretum, esse Alexandri VII. loquentis ex Cathedra, docentis Ecclesiam, & ut capitis Ecclesiæ. *Primo.* Quia præcepit hoc Decretum publicari suo Nomine, sicut cœteræ leges universales promulgantur. | | 99. And Cardenas at once pursues, saying: Hence it manifestly appears that this Decree is of Alexander VII speaking *ex Cathedra*, teaching the Church, and as Head of the Church. *First.* Because he commanded this Decree to be published in his own Name, just as the other universal laws are promulgated. |
| 100. *Secundo.* Tunc enim decernit ut caput Ecclesiæ; quando Decretum edit, pro universa Ecclesia, illudque vult ab omnibus fidelibus indubitanter admitti: atqui sic edidit Decretum hoc: Ergo in eo loqnitur ex cathedra, & ut caput Ecclesiæ. Minor patet, nam in ultima periodo dicitur: *In Virtute sanctæ obedientiæ, & sub interminatione divini Judicii prohibet omnibus Christi Fidelibus, cujuscumque conditionis, dignitatis, ac status, etiam specialissima nota dignis, &c.* Item constat ex proœmio, ubi dicitur perniciem ex prædictis propositionibus esse universalem, ibi: *ingens irruptura esset Christianæ vitæ corruptela.* Et requiri etiam remedium universale pro omnibus ovibus sibi commissis, illis verbis: *Ut oves sibi creditas ab ejusmodi spatiosa, lataque, per quam itur ad perditionem, via, pro Pastorali solicitudine, in rectam semitam evocaret.* | | 100. *Secondly.* For he then decrees as Head of the Church, when he issues a Decree for the universal Church, and wills it to be indubitably admitted by all the faithful: but thus he issued this Decree: Therefore in it he speaks *ex Cathedra*, and as Head of the Church. The minor is plain, for in the last period it is said: *In virtue of holy obedience, and under the threat of the divine Judgment, he forbids all the faithful of Christ, of whatsoever condition, dignity, and state, even such as are worthy of most special note, &c.* Likewise it is established from the proem, where the ruin arising from the aforesaid propositions is said to be universal, there: *an immense corruption of Christian life would burst in.* And that a universal remedy also is required for all the sheep committed to him, in those words: *That he might, out of his Pastoral solicitude, call back the sheep entrusted to him from this sort of spacious and broad way, by which one goes to perdition, into the right path.* |
| 101. Quando autem obiter, & per transennam Pontifex aliquid decernit, etiam in Materia Fidei, & morum, non tunc loquitur ex Cathedra, neque decernit ut Caput Ecclesiæ. Patet. Quia id quia ita loquitur Pontifex, non intendit à fidelibus indubitanter admitti. Ideo, cum Nicolaus I. *cap. A quodam Judæo, de Consecration. distantia 4.* dixit Baptismum in nomine Christi collatum valere; id quidem non dixit ut Caput Ecclesiæ, quia id dixit obiter, & pe transennam, cum solum assumpsisset definiendum, An Baptismus à Judæo collatus, sit validus. Omnia prædicta P. Cardenas. | | 101. But when the Pontiff decrees something incidentally, and in passing, even in matter of Faith and of morals, he does not then speak *ex Cathedra*, nor decree as Head of the Church. It is plain. Because that which the Pontiff so speaks, he does not intend to be indubitably admitted by the faithful. Therefore, when Nicholas I, *cap. A quodam Judæo, de Consecration. distantia 4.*, said that Baptism conferred in the name of Christ is valid, he did not indeed say this as Head of the Church, because he said it incidentally and in passing, since he had taken up for defining only whether Baptism conferred by a Jew is valid. All the aforesaid, Fr. Cardenas. |
| 102. *Secundo* probatur nostra principalis conclusio. Absque errore in Fide, negari non potest, quod verus, & legitimus Romanus Pontifex Petri successor, in Petro fuerit institutus à Christo, Pastor, & Doctor totius Ecclesiæ Catholicæ, *Joannis ultimo* dicente: *Pasce oves meas:* nec quod tota Ecclesia illum audire, & sequi teneatur, ut bene ex illo infert Bellarminus, *lib. 1. supra cit. numero 33. & lib. 4. capite 2. versic. Deinde, & capite 5. vers. Ac ut rem,* & Suarez, *de Fide, disputat. 5. sect. 8. numero 9. supra etiam, numero 13. & 67.* recensiti. Si autem Pontifex erraret, falsam doctrinam tradendo, aut veram prohibendo, etiam in rebus moralibus, erraret etiam Ecclesia tota, illum audiendo, & sequendo. Sed hoc non est dicendum, cum Fidei adversetur, & promissioni Christi, *Joannis 16.* ibi: *Cum venerit ille Spiritus veritatis, docebit vos omnem veritatem:* Et cum Ecclesia sit columna veritatis. Ergo cum omnino certum debeat esse Pontificem Alexandrum Septimum errare non potuisse in damnatione, & prohibitione dictarum propositionum moralium, tenendum est tanquam omnino certum, quin remaneat locus interno, aut externo dubio, tales propositiones, vere esse scandalosas, & prohibitas. | | 102. *Secondly* our principal conclusion is proved. Without error in Faith it cannot be denied that the true and legitimate Roman Pontiff, successor of Peter, was instituted in Peter by Christ as Pastor and Doctor of the whole Catholic Church, *Joannis ultimo* saying: *Feed my sheep;* nor that the whole Church is bound to hear and to follow him, as Bellarmine well infers from that place, *lib. 1. supra cit. numero 33. & lib. 4. capite 2. versic. Deinde, & capite 5. vers. Ac ut rem*, and Suarez, *de Fide, disputat. 5. sect. 8. numero 9.*, recounted also above, *numero 13. & 67.* But if the Pontiff should err, by handing on a false doctrine, or by prohibiting a true one, even in moral matters, the whole Church too would err, in hearing and following him. But this is not to be said, since it is opposed to Faith, and to the promise of Christ, *Joannis 16.*, there: *When that Spirit of truth shall come, he will teach you all truth;* and since the Church is the pillar of truth. Therefore, since it must be altogether certain that Pope Alexander the Seventh could not err in the condemnation and prohibition of the said moral propositions, it is to be held as altogether certain — so that no room remains for internal or external doubt — that such propositions are truly scandalous and prohibited. |
| 103. *Tertio* probatur ratione, qua utitur Suarez, *tomo 4. de Relig. lib. 3. capite 4. Corollar. 2.* ad probandum admissionem Scholasticorum in Societatem Jesu, constituere illos, in vero, & proprio Religioso statu, supposita duplici Constitutione Gregorii XIII. *Quanto fructuosius, & Ascendente Domino,* sic ait: Secundo infertur ex dictis, quam sit certa veritas à Pontifice declarata. (In duplici recensita Constitutione.) Existimo enim esse omnino infalibilem, ita ut sine errore in Fide, negari non possit. Ratio est, (hanc pro mea adduco conclusione) quia sententia Pontificis in his, quæ ad doctrinam pertinent, infalibilem continet certitudinem ex Christi institutione, & promissione: *Ego rogavi pro te: ut non deficiat fides tua, & tu aliquando conversus confirma fratres tuos. Lucæ 22. & Mathæi 16. Tu es Petrus, & super hanc petram ædificabo Ecclesiam meam, & portæ inferi non prævalebunt adversus eam.* Et concessio facta est, *Joannis 21. Pasce oves meas.* Quæ testimonia ita sunt ab Ecclesia intellecta, & perpetua traditione, ac Patrum, Conciliorum, & ipsorum Pontificum definitione firmata, ac declarata. (Reliqua vide *supra numero 71.*) Valde autem diminuta fuisset Christi Domini providentia circa suam Ecclesiam, si in decidendis talibus quæstionibus ex Cathedra, Vicarium labi permitteret. Hæc P. Suarez. Ex quibus bene sequitur, omnino certum debere esse propositiones ab Alexandro Septimo damnatas, & prohibitas tanquam scandalosas, vere tales esse. Alias sententia Pontificis, in his, quæ ad mores, quæ ad doctrinam pertinent, infalibilem certitudinem non haberet, nec contineret; quod esset contra institutionem, promissionem, & concessionem Christi, & contra traditionem Ecclesiæ, & Definitiones Patrum, Conciliorum, & Summorum Pontificum. Ergo omnino certum debet esse, propositiones damnatas tanquam scandalosas, ab Alexandro Septimo, vere tales esse. | | 103. *Thirdly* it is proved by the reason which Suarez uses, *tomo 4. de Relig. lib. 3. capite 4. Corollar. 2.*, for proving that the admission of Scholastics into the Society of Jesus constitutes them in a true and proper Religious state, the twofold Constitution of Gregory XIII, *Quanto fructuosius* and *Ascendente Domino*, being supposed; thus he says: Secondly it is inferred from what has been said how certain is the truth declared by the Pontiff. (In the twofold Constitution recounted.) For I judge it to be altogether infallible, so that it cannot be denied without error in Faith. The reason is (this I adduce for my own conclusion) because the sentence of the Pontiff, in those things which pertain to doctrine, contains an infallible certitude from the institution and promise of Christ: *I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not; and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren. Lucæ 22. & Mathæi 16. Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against her.* And the concession was made, *Joannis 21. Feed my sheep.* Which testimonies have been so understood by the Church, and confirmed and declared by perpetual tradition, and by the definition of the Fathers, of the Councils, and of the Pontiffs themselves. (For the rest see *supra numero 71.*) But greatly diminished would the providence of Christ the Lord concerning his Church have been, if in deciding such questions *ex Cathedra* he should permit his Vicar to fall. Thus Fr. Suarez. From which it well follows that it must be altogether certain that the propositions condemned and prohibited by Alexander the Seventh as scandalous are truly such. Otherwise the sentence of the Pontiff, in those things which pertain to morals, which pertain to doctrine, would neither have nor contain an infallible certitude; which would be against the institution, the promise, and the concession of Christ, and against the tradition of the Church, and the Definitions of the Fathers, of the Councils, and of the Supreme Pontiffs. Therefore it must be altogether certain that the propositions condemned as scandalous by Alexander the Seventh are truly such. |
| 104. Confirmat Suarez assumptum suum, de eo, quod Scholares Societatis suæ, sint veri Religiosi, & quod id, *sine errore in Fide negari non possit,* ex doctrina Theologorum omnium, omniumque Catholicorum, qui pro certo habent, Summum Pontificem, non posse errare in doctrina morum Ecclesiæ tradenda (verba ejus reddo:) nam hæc doctrina multum pertinet ad bonos mores, & ex opposito, multa inferri possunt absurda, & scandalosa in Ecclesia, & è converso, si hæc declaratio Pontificis falsa esse posset, ex illa multa absurda: & contra bonos mores sequerentur; ut quod status Religiosus commutaretur in non Religiosum, & quod votum Religionis impleretur sine statu Religioso. Et similia. Ita Suarez. Si ergo omnes Theologi, & Catholici, pro certo habent Summum Pontificem errare non posse, in doctrina morum Ecclesiæ tradenda, & doctrina tradita à Gregorio XIII. de eo, quod Scholares Societatis, veri sint Religiosi, multum pertinet ad bonos mores, & ex opposita multa sequerentur contra bonos mores absurda, si doctrina Gregorii falsa esse posset, quare idem dici non debebit, si doctrina Alexandri in duplici Decreto nostro tradita, falsa esse posset? Nonne multum pertinet ad bonos mores, quod homo aliquo tempore vitæ suæ actus Fidei, Spei, & Charitatis eliciat? Quod vir Equestris provocatus, duellum non acceptet, ne notam apud alios incurrat timiditatis? & sic de aliis propositionibus damnatis discurrendo: Nonne ex oppositis sequentur multa contra bonos mores absurda? Ergo omnino certum debet esse, propositiones damnatas ab Alexandro Septimo tanquam scandalosas, vere scandalosas esse, damnatas, & prohibitas. | | 104. Suarez confirms his assumption, concerning the fact that the Scholars of his Society are true Religious, and that this *cannot be denied without error in Faith*, from the doctrine of all Theologians, and of all Catholics, who hold it for certain that the Supreme Pontiff cannot err in handing on to the Church the doctrine of morals (I render his words): for this doctrine pertains much to good morals, and from the opposite many absurd and scandalous things could be inferred in the Church; and conversely, if this declaration of the Pontiff could be false, from it many absurdities, and things against good morals, would follow: as that the Religious state would be changed into a non-Religious one, and that the vow of Religion would be fulfilled without the Religious state. And the like. Thus Suarez. If therefore all Theologians and Catholics hold it for certain that the Supreme Pontiff cannot err in handing on to the Church the doctrine of morals, and the doctrine handed on by Gregory XIII, concerning the fact that the Scholars of the Society are true Religious, pertains much to good morals, and from the opposite many absurdities against good morals would follow if the doctrine of Gregory could be false — why ought not the same to be said, if the doctrine handed on by Alexander in our twofold Decree could be false? Does it not pertain much to good morals that a man should at some time of his life elicit acts of Faith, Hope, and Charity? That a Knight, being challenged, should not accept a duel, lest he incur among others the mark of timidity? And so running through the other condemned propositions: Would not many absurdities against good morals follow from the opposites? Therefore it must be altogether certain that the propositions condemned by Alexander the Seventh as scandalous are truly scandalous, condemned, and prohibited. |
| 105. Pro nostra conclusione adsunt plures alii gravissimi Authores, quorum aliquos, & eorum doctrinas libet pro illius corroboratione, & majore dilucidatione producere; & tradere. In primis enim Joannes Ægidius Trullench. *tomo 1. libro 1. in Decalog. capite 2. dubio 4. numero 18.* post alia, scripsit: Ex quo fit, si Ecclesia definit aliquam propositionem hæreticam, aut temerariam, aut scandalosam esse, *credo de Fide, tali censura esse dignam:* quia alias obligaremur fateri Ecclesiam errare posse, in his, quæ ad Fidem pertinent, contra Paulum *ad Thimot. 3.* dicentem, Ecclesiam esse columnam, & firmamentum veritatis. | | 105. For our conclusion there are present several other most grave Authors, some of whom, and their doctrines, it is well to produce and hand on for its corroboration and greater elucidation. For in the first place John Giles Trullench, *tomo 1. libro 1. in Decalog. capite 2. dubio 4. numero 18.*, after other things, wrote: From which it comes about that, if the Church defines some proposition to be heretical, or rash, or scandalous, *I believe it to be of Faith that it is worthy of such a censure:* because otherwise we should be obliged to confess that the Church can err in those things which pertain to Faith, against Paul *ad Thimot. 3.*, saying that the Church is the pillar and ground of truth. |
| 106. Manuel Ambrosio de Filguera, Clerigo Menor, *en la Casos de Consciencia, tratado 13. de la Descommunion, cap. 1.* despues de haver puesto en el articulo septimo los dichos Decretos de Alexandro Septimo, en el articulo octavo propone, y resuelve algunas dudas acerca de ellos. La ultima es tel tenor siguiente. | | 106. Manuel Ambrose de Filguera, Clerk Minor, *en la Casos de Consciencia, tratado 13. de la Descommunion, cap. 1.*, after having set down in the seventh article the said Decrees of Alexander the Seventh, in the eighth article proposes and resolves certain doubts concerning them. The last is of the following tenor. |
| La sexta duda es, si pudo errar Alexandro Septimo, condenando por escandalosas, las dichas quarenta, y cinco opiniones morales, o mandando, que no se ponganen practica; que es le mismo, que preguntar, si puede el Summo Pontifice errar, poniendo leyes perteneçientes alas costumbres. Respondo, que no puede errar. Enseñan esta resolucion *como de Fe,* San Antonino, *3. par tit. 12. cap. 2. §. 2.* Sylvestro, *Fides, numero 2.* Castro Palao, *tomo 1. tractatu 4. disputatione 1. pun. 5. §. 3. numero 4.* y otros, que defienden lo mismo, diziendo, *que dista poco de la Fe,* como Cano, *cap. citato* (esto es, *libro 5. de Locis Theologicis, cap. 5.*) y otros que refiere el Mo. Joan de Santo Thoma, *2. 2. disputatione 9. artic. 3.* Porque es de fe, que no puede errar el Summo Pontifice en apacentar alos Fides, que son sus ovezas. Pues los Fieles, no solo son apacentados con la doctrina de la Fe, sino tambien con la honestidad de costumbres saludables, que es loque Christo mando à San Pedro, y sus successores; *Luego es de fe, o dista poco de ella nuestra resolucion.* Y assi el Pontifice no puede errar mandando alguna cosa contraria a la ley Divina, o natural. De que se infiere consiguiente mente, que Alexandro Septimo, no pudo errar, mandando que no se pusiessen en practica, dichas quarenta, y cinco opiniones morales. | | The sixth doubt is whether Alexander the Seventh could err in condemning as scandalous the said forty-five moral opinions, or in commanding that they be not reduced to practice; which is the same as to ask whether the Supreme Pontiff can err in laying down laws pertaining to morals. I answer that he cannot err. This resolution is taught *as of Faith* by St. Antoninus, *3. par tit. 12. cap. 2. §. 2.*, Sylvester, *Fides, numero 2.*, Castro Palao, *tomo 1. tractatu 4. disputatione 1. pun. 5. §. 3. numero 4.*, and others who defend the same, saying *that it is little removed from Faith*, as Cano, *cap. citato* (that is, *libro 5. de Locis Theologicis, cap. 5.*), and others whom Master John of St. Thomas reports, *2. 2. disputatione 9. artic. 3.* For it is of faith that the Supreme Pontiff cannot err in feeding the Faithful, who are his sheep. Now the Faithful are fed not only with the doctrine of Faith, but also with the honesty of wholesome morals, which is what Christ commanded to St. Peter and his successors; *therefore our resolution is of faith, or is little removed from it.* And thus the Pontiff cannot err in commanding anything contrary to the Divine or natural law. From which it is consequently inferred that Alexander the Seventh could not err in commanding that the said forty-five moral opinions should not be reduced to practice. |
| 107. Pater Joannes de Cardenas, *in Crisi Theologica, tomo 1. disputatione 9. cap. 20. articulo 6.* in specie etiam nostri casus, Decretorum Alexandri Septimi, inquisito, An hæc Decreta sint Definitiones Pontificiæ? supponit, vocem *Definitio,* dupliciter usurpari. *Primo,* pro decisione, seu declaratione articuli Fidei. *Secundo,* pro qualibet decisione, seu declaratione Pontificis, quam tradit ut omnino certam, & indubitabilem. In qua acceptione ait, *numer. 351.* cum Pontifex summus aliquid in materia doctrinæ decidit, ut certum, & absque periculo errandi, dicitur id definire. *Nam definire, & pro certo decidere, idem sunt.* Quo supposito, *numero 353.* ait: Itaque dum Romanus Pontifex docet Ecclesiam aliquid in quo errare non potest, id quidem docet, tanquam omnino certum, ut ex terminis notum est. Quod autem docet, ut omnino certum, id expresse definit, & si non definiat tanquam articulum Fidei: Definire enim, vel decidere, est rem tanquam omnino certam declarare. Sunt enim multa omnino certa, in quibus Pontifex errare non possit, quamvis non perveniant ad certitudinem Fidei. Hæc ibi dictus Author, & statim prosequitur, in numeris sequentibus. | | 107. Father John de Cardenas, *in Crisi Theologica, tomo 1. disputatione 9. cap. 20. articulo 6.*, in the very kind of our case also, that of the Decrees of Alexander the Seventh, having inquired whether these Decrees are Pontifical Definitions, supposes that the word *Definition* is used in two ways. *First*, for the decision or declaration of an article of Faith. *Secondly*, for any decision or declaration whatsoever of the Pontiff which he hands on as altogether certain and indubitable. In which acceptation he says, *numer. 351.*, that when the Supreme Pontiff decides something in matter of doctrine as certain and without danger of erring, he is said to define it. *For to define, and to decide for certain, are the same.* Which being supposed, *numero 353.*, he says: And so, while the Roman Pontiff teaches the Church something in which he cannot err, he teaches it indeed as altogether certain, as is known from the terms. But what he teaches as altogether certain, that he expressly defines, even if he does not define it as an article of Faith: for to define, or to decide, is to declare a thing as altogether certain. For there are many things altogether certain in which the Pontiff cannot err, although they do not attain to the certitude of Faith. Thus there the said Author, and he at once pursues, in the following numbers. |
| 108. His positis, dicendum est, hæc Decreta, esse definitiones Pontificis, secundo modo. Constat. Illud enim Pontifex definit secundo modo, quando aliquid declarat, vel affirmat pro certo in materia, in qua errare non potest: Sed Alexander Septimus affirmat pro certo, eas propositiones esse scandalosas in materia in qua errare non potest. Ergo in illo Decreto Pontifex vere definit. Consequentia est legitima. Major constat ex dictis *numer. 351.* Minor etiam patet, quia affirmat eas propositiones esse scandalosas, cum tamen illæ versentur in materia morum: in qua Romanum Pontificem Ecclesiæ loquentem, errare non posse, constat ex capite sexto. | | 108. These things being laid down, it must be said that these Decrees are definitions of the Pontiff in the second mode. It is evident. For the Pontiff defines in the second mode when he declares or affirms something for certain in a matter in which he cannot err: But Alexander the Seventh affirms for certain that those propositions are scandalous, in a matter in which he cannot err. Therefore in that Decree the Pontiff truly defines. The consequence is legitimate. The major is established from the things said *numer. 351.* The minor also is plain, because he affirms those propositions to be scandalous, whereas they are nevertheless engaged in matter of morals: in which that the Roman Pontiff, speaking to the Church, cannot err, is established from the sixth chapter. |
| 109. Confirmatur. Nam ut constat ex *numero 352.* Decretum Clementis Octavi, quo affirmavit, opinionem de absolutione inter Absentes, esse falsam, temerariam, & scandalosam, fuit Definitio Romanæ Cathedræ, quia illud pro certo decidit: Ergo cum Alexander Septimus pro certo decidat eas propositiones à se damnatas, esse scandalosas, dicendum est, eam esse definitionem. | | 109. It is confirmed. For, as is established from *numero 352.*, the Decree of Clement the Eighth, by which he affirmed the opinion concerning absolution between those Absent to be false, rash, and scandalous, was a Definition of the Roman Chair, because he decided it for certain: Therefore, since Alexander the Seventh decides for certain that those propositions condemned by him are scandalous, it must be said that this is a definition. |
| 110. Nec valet si dicas, ad definitionem formalem, requiri, quid Pontifex dicat, *Definimus, decidimus, declaramus,* vel quid simile. Nam verbum *Damnamus,* in materia doctrinæ, in qua Pontifex errare non potest, est verbum simile, & æquivalens. Nam, hoc, quod est damnare doctrinam tanquam certo scandalosam, idem est quod decidere, & declarare, eam scandalosam esse. | | 110. Nor is it of avail if you say that for a formal definition it is required that the Pontiff say, *We define, we decide, we declare*, or something similar. For the word *We condemn*, in matter of doctrine in which the Pontiff cannot err, is a similar and equivalent word. For this — to condemn a doctrine as certainly scandalous — is the same as to decide and declare that it is scandalous. |
| 111. Hactenus Pater Cardenas, *dicto cap. 20. art. 6.* postquam *c. 6. art. 1. ejusdem Disputat. 9.* dixerat, & latissima probaverat ibi, & in sequentibus articulis, demonstrationibus esse, certissimum inter Catholicos Doctores, Romanum Pontificem errare non posse in materia morum. | | 111. Thus far Father Cardenas, *dicto cap. 20. art. 6.*, after he had said, *c. 6. art. 1. ejusdem Disputat. 9.*, and had there most amply proved, and in the following articles by demonstrations, that it is most certain among Catholic Doctors that the Roman Pontiff cannot err in matter of morals. |
| 112. Ad meum intentum, & meæ conclusionis probationem adducit præfatus Pater Cardenas, eodem articulo 1. capit. 6. Sylvestrum, *in Summa, verbo Fides, numero 2.* dicentem: *Ad fidem pertinet inhærere determinationi Pontificis Summi, in his, quæ sunt Fidei; imo etiam in his, quæ spectant ad bonos mores:* quia in hujusmodi etiam Ecclesia errare non potest, & consequenter nec Caput ejus, in quantum caput, sive in quantum Pontifex. | | 112. For my purpose, and for the proof of my conclusion, the aforesaid Father Cardenas adduces, in the same article 1, chapter 6, Sylvester, *in Summa, verbo Fides, numero 2.*, saying: *It pertains to faith to adhere to the determination of the Supreme Pontiff in those things which are of Faith; nay, even in those which regard good morals:* because in matters of this kind also the Church cannot err, and consequently neither can her Head, inasmuch as he is head, or inasmuch as he is Pontiff. |
| 113. Adducit etiam Ferdinandum Castrum Palaum, *tomo 1. tract. 4. disp. 1. pun. 5. §. 3. n. 4.* docentem esse de Fide, non posse Romanum Pontificem errare, statuendo aliquid à tota Ecclesia observandum, quod legi divinæ, vel naturali contrarium sit. Et tenet etiam (addit) *id esse de Fide, loquendo de lege, seu Constitutione lata pro una Provincia, cum Magistro Bannez, 2. 2. quæst. 1. artic. 16. dubio 6. conclus. 4.* | | 113. He also adduces Ferdinand Castro Palao, *tomo 1. tract. 4. disp. 1. pun. 5. §. 3. n. 4.*, teaching that it is of Faith that the Roman Pontiff cannot err in establishing something to be observed by the whole Church which is contrary to the divine or natural law. And he holds also (he adds) *that this is of Faith, speaking of a law or Constitution enacted for one Province, with Master Bannez, 2. 2. quæst. 1. artic. 16. dubio 6. conclus. 4.* |
| 114. Ob hanc rationem, certum omnino esse (inquit Pater Cardenas) quod Romanus Pontifex errare non possit in approbatione Religionum, quantum ad Constitutiones, & leges earum, ad honestatem, & bonitatem moralem earum, affirmat Sanctus Thomas, *Opusculo 19. capite 4.* & Bannez, *2. 2. quæstione prima, articulo 10. dubio octavo, conclusione quarta,* & oppositam conclusionem dicit esse hæreticam. Et pro eo, quod sit de Fide, refert Palaum, *loco citato, §. 6. numero secundo.* Dianam, *tractatu de Infalibilitate Decretorum Pontificis parte undecima, resolutione 25.* & apud eum, Amicum, *tomo 4. disputatione 7. sectione 3. numero 79.* & Franciscum del Castillo, *tomo 2. in 3. Sententiarum, disputatione 16. quæstione nona, numero 8.* qui tres dicunt, id esse de Fide. | | 114. For this reason, that it is altogether certain (says Father Cardenas) that the Roman Pontiff cannot err in the approbation of Religious Orders, as regards their Constitutions and laws, as regards their moral honesty and goodness, St. Thomas affirms, *Opusculo 19. capite 4.*, and Bannez, *2. 2. quæstione prima, articulo 10. dubio octavo, conclusione quarta*, and he says the opposite conclusion to be heretical. And for the fact that it is of Faith he reports Palao, *loco citato, §. 6. numero secundo*, Diana, *tractatu de Infalibilitate Decretorum Pontificis parte undecima, resolutione 25.*, and in him, Amico, *tomo 4. disputatione 7. sectione 3. numero 79.*, and Francis del Castillo, *tomo 2. in 3. Sententiarum, disputatione 16. quæstione nona, numero 8.*, which three say that it is of Faith. |
| 115. Ubi notabis *primo,* dum Authores dicunt Pontificem errare non posse in materia Fidei, & morum, condistinguere materiam morum à materia Fidei: & ideo non solum errare non posse in materia morum: quando hanc materiam tradit, definiendo esse de Fide, sed etiam quando materiam morum docet, etiam absque definitione articuli Fidei. | | 115. Where you will note *first*, that while the Authors say that the Pontiff cannot err in matter of Faith and of morals, they distinguish matter of morals from matter of Faith: and therefore he cannot err in matter of morals, not only when he hands on this matter by defining it to be of Faith, but also when he teaches matter of morals even without the definition of an article of Faith. |
| 116. Hinc patet, pro hac assertione stare Caramuelem, *in Theologia Fundamentali, numero 161. fundamento tertio.* Nam *numero 160.* pro fundamento, cum statuisset hanc propositionem: *Vera Ecclesia in substantialibus articulis errare non potest:* prædicto *numero 161.* declarat, quinam sint substantiales articuli: aitque, accidentales eos esse, qui *nec mores, nec Fidem concernerent.* Ex quo patet substantiales, in quibus errare non potest, esse, non solum articulos Fidei, sed etiam morum, absque definitione Fidei traditos. (Ergo debet certum omnino esse; Summum Pontificem in substantialibus, qualia sunt, quæ continenter in prædicta Alexandri Septimi damnatione, errare non posse: concernunt enim omnia illa ad mores.) | | 116. Hence it is plain that Caramuel stands for this assertion, *in Theologia Fundamentali, numero 161. fundamento tertio.* For *numero 160.*, for a foundation, when he had laid down this proposition: *The true Church cannot err in substantial articles*, in the aforesaid *numero 161.* he declares which are the substantial articles: and he says that those are accidental which *concern neither morals nor Faith.* From which it is plain that the substantial ones, in which she cannot err, are not only the articles of Faith, but also those of morals, handed on without a definition of Faith. (Therefore it must be altogether certain that the Supreme Pontiff cannot err in substantial matters, such as are those which are contained in the aforesaid condemnation of Alexander the Seventh: for all those concern morals.) |
| 117. Notabis *secundo,* prædictos Authores non solum pro certo affirmare, non posse errare Romanum Pontificem in iis, quæ præcipit, nempe præcipiendo turpia, vel prohibendo honesta, sed etiam nec errare posse, dum expresso judicio approbat, vel reprobat mores, ita ut approbet vitia, vel actus virtutis condemnet. Omnia prædicta dicit Pater Cardenas. *in prædicto articulo 1.* | | 117. You will note *secondly*, that the aforesaid Authors not only affirm for certain that the Roman Pontiff cannot err in those things which he commands, namely by commanding base things, or by prohibiting honest ones, but also that he cannot err while by an express judgment he approves or reprobates morals, so as to approve vices, or condemn acts of virtue. All the aforesaid Father Cardenas says, *in prædicto articulo 1.* |
| 118. Antoninus Diana Coordinatus, *tomo nono, tractatu quinto de Infalibilitate Decretorum Romani Pontificis extra Concilium, resolutione decima,* & parte ii quærit; An quando Pontifex definit aliquam propositionem esse temerariam, erroneam, scandalosam, &c. sit hæreticus, qui pertinaciter negaret illam esse talem? An vero, solum sit temerarium, erroneum, scandalosum, &c? Et respondet. *Aliqui affirmant esse hæreticum, id negare: quia Pontifici revelatur id, quod definit; ac proinde, qui pertinaciter negat, quod Pontifex definit, negat objectum revelatum, & in hæresim incidit.* | | 118. Antoninus Diana Coordinatus, *tomo nono, tractatu quinto de Infalibilitate Decretorum Romani Pontificis extra Concilium, resolutione decima*, and in part 11, asks whether, when the Pontiff defines some proposition to be rash, erroneous, scandalous, etc., he is a heretic who should pertinaciously deny it to be such? Or whether it is only rash, erroneous, scandalous, etc.? And he answers. *Some affirm it to be heretical to deny this: because that which he defines is revealed to the Pontiff; and accordingly, he who pertinaciously denies what the Pontiff defines, denies a revealed object, and falls into heresy.* |
| 119. Sed ad propositam quæstionem (prosequitur Doctor ille,) sic respondet Turrianus, *in 2. 2. tomo primo, disputatione 55. dublo quinto:* Constat sæpe Pontificem Summum damnare aliquam propositionem ut hæreticam, aut tanquam erroneam, vel temerariam; aliquando declarat propositionem esse de Fide; interdum probabilem. Vocamus ergo in quæstionem, An in his censuris assignandis, possit Ecclesia errare; constat autem ex dictis, ad quæstionem primam Pontificem non posse errare in definitionibus Fidei; quærimus ergo, an possit errare assignando alias censuras? | | 119. But to the proposed question (that Doctor pursues) Turrianus thus answers, *in 2. 2. tomo primo, disputatione 55. dubio quinto:* It is evident that the Supreme Pontiff often condemns some proposition as heretical, or as erroneous, or rash; sometimes he declares a proposition to be of Faith; sometimes probable. We call in question, therefore, whether in assigning these censures the Church can err; but it is established from the things said to the first question that the Pontiff cannot err in definitions of Faith; we ask, therefore, whether he can err in assigning other censures? |
| 120. Mihi sane valde certum est, Pontificem non posse errare in his censuris; nam certum est in definiendis articulis Fidei, veritatibus, non posse errare, ut dixi; assignare vero has censuras, pertinet ad veritates Fidei. Ergo nullo modo admittendum est posse errare. Et explicatur, quia cum declarat aliquam propositionem esse erroneam, plane definitio pertinet proxime ad Fidei veritates, quia propositio erronea est contraria conclusioni deductæ evidenter ex principiis Fidei: Ergo non posset Pontifex errare in hac censura, nisi errans aliquo modo circa Fidei veritates. Et idem judicium est, si declaret aliquam propositionem, esse proximam errori, vel sapientem hæresim. | | 120. To me indeed it is very certain that the Pontiff cannot err in these censures; for it is certain that in defining the articles of Faith, [and] truths, he cannot err, as I have said; but to assign these censures pertains to the truths of Faith. Therefore it is in no way to be admitted that he can err. And it is explained: because when he declares some proposition to be erroneous, plainly the definition pertains proximately to the truths of Faith, since an erroneous proposition is contrary to a conclusion evidently deduced from the principles of Faith: Therefore the Pontiff could not err in this censure, unless erring in some way concerning the truths of Faith. And the same judgment holds if he should declare some proposition to be next to error, or savoring of heresy. |
| 121. Deinde in aliis censuris propositionum, quæ non ita proxime videntur pertinere ad doctrinam Fidei, ut cum dicitur aliqua propositio temeraria, etiam dicendum est, errare non posse Pontificem: quia pertinet ad doctrinam, & veritatem rerum Religionis Christianæ: quod si in hoc posset errare, non esset omnino certa definitio circa veritatem Fidei, quia posset contingere, ut damnaret aliquam propositionem temerariam, quæ esset magis consentanea principiis Fidei, quam contraria, quod nullo modo est admittendum. Et ita existimo esse errorem, asserere in his Censuris posse Pontificem errare. Ita Turrianus, *qui in censura Theologica, quæ adest in tomo Selectarum Disputationum, in Centuria prima, dubio 56.* quæsito, An mereatur censuram asserere, Pontificem posse errare circa censuras propositionum: Respondet, certum esse Pontificem, non posse errare in assignando censuram alicujus propositionis, quia in rebus pertinentibus ad doctrinam, non potest errare, & oppositum asserere, est impium, scandalosum, temerarium, & erroneum. | | 121. Then, in the other censures of propositions, which do not seem to pertain so proximately to the doctrine of Faith — as when some proposition is called rash — it must also be said that the Pontiff cannot err: because it pertains to the doctrine and truth of the matters of the Christian Religion: for if he could err in this, the definition concerning the truth of Faith would not be altogether certain, because it could happen that he should condemn as rash some proposition which was more consonant to the principles of Faith than contrary to them, which is in no way to be admitted. And so I judge it to be an error to assert that the Pontiff can err in these Censures. Thus Turrianus, who, *in censura Theologica, quæ adest in tomo Selectarum Disputationum, in Centuria prima, dubio 56.*, having asked whether it deserves a censure to assert that the Pontiff can err concerning the censures of propositions, answers that it is certain that the Pontiff cannot err in assigning the censure of any proposition, because in things pertaining to doctrine he cannot err, and to assert the opposite is impious, scandalous, rash, and erroneous. |
| 122. Et quidem Coninch, *de Actibus supernat. disputatione octava, dubio octavo, numero 135.* (prosequitur Diana) docet, eum, *qui negat prædictas censuras, asserere aliquid, ex quo per evidentem consequentiam deducitur hæresis,* & Eminentissimus Dominus meus Cardinalis Lugo, *de Fide, disputatione vigesima, numero 109.* asserit, tale assertum fore erroneum, & errori proximum quod antea dixerat Bannes, *in 2. 2. D. Thomæ, quæstione 10. articulo 2.* | | 122. And indeed Coninck, *de Actibus supernat. disputatione octava, dubio octavo, numero 135.* (Diana pursues) teaches that he *who denies the aforesaid censures asserts something from which by an evident consequence heresy is deduced*; and my Most Eminent Lord Cardinal Lugo, *de Fide, disputatione vigesima, numero 109.*, asserts that such an assertion would be erroneous, and next to error; which Bañes had said before, *in 2. 2. D. Thomæ, quæstione 10. articulo 2.* |
| 123. Verum Malderus, *in 2. 2. D. Thomæ, quæstione undecima, articulo secundo, membro quinto,* distinguendum putat; dicit enim revelationem quidem esse à Deo, Ecclesiam non posse errare in definitionibus circa doctrinam, adeoque, qui hoc negaret cum pertinacia, fore hæreticum; si autem ipse affirmaret propositionem, quam Ecclesia censuit esse temerariam, sed veram; nisi simul dicat Ecclesiam errare in hujusmodi censura, non erit hæreticus, sed incurret in errorem hæresi proximum. Si enim hæc propositio non sit temeraria, sequitur aliquid contra Fidem, nimirum, Ecclesiam errare in definitionibus circa doctrinam. Itaque distinguendum est: Vel enim, qui negat hanc propositionem esse temerariam, solum intendit dicere eam esse veram, & sic non erit hæreticus, etiam si noverit Ecclesiam determinasse quod sit temeraria: potest enim fieri, ut non nisi probabile putet Ecclesiam in talibus non posse errare, aut ut noverit quidem Ecclesiam hoc damnasse tanquam temerarium, & credat quidquid ut temerarium damnat Ecclesia esse revera tale; sed non pertractet illa simul ad concludendum hoc esse vere temerarium, vel intendit sustinere pertinaciter Ecclesiam errare in censura istius propositionis, & erit hæreticus. | | 123. But Malderus, *in 2. 2. D. Thomæ, quæstione undecima, articulo secundo, membro quinto*, thinks a distinction must be made; for he says that it is indeed by revelation from God that the Church cannot err in definitions concerning doctrine, and thus that he who should deny this with pertinacity would be a heretic; but if he himself should affirm a proposition which the Church has judged to be rash, but true — unless he at the same time say that the Church errs in a censure of this kind — he will not be a heretic, but will incur an error next to heresy. For if this proposition be not rash, something follows against Faith, namely, that the Church errs in definitions concerning doctrine. And so a distinction must be made: For either he who denies this proposition to be rash intends only to say that it is true, and thus he will not be a heretic, even if he knows that the Church has determined that it is rash — for it can come about that he thinks it only probable that the Church cannot err in such matters, or that he knows indeed that the Church has condemned this as rash, and believes that whatever the Church condemns as rash is really such, but does not put these together so as to conclude that this is truly rash — or he intends pertinaciously to maintain that the Church errs in the censure of that proposition, and he will be a heretic. |
| 124. Et hanc Malderi sententiam (addit Diana) docet etiam Pater Oviedo, *de Fide, controversia 4. puncto 9. numero 124.* asserit enim posse pertinaciter negare censuram ab Ecclesia illatam; quin judicet Ecclesiam posse errare, ac proinde ex hoc capite fore hæreticum, qui illam negaret, non præcise, quia negaret censuram, quia hanc non definit Pontifex ut revelatam, sed quia negaret specialem assistentiam Spiritus Sancti, vi cujus Pontifici nunquam errare permittet, & contra hanc assistentiam assereret, Pontificem de facto errasse. Hæc Doctor ille, quæ quoad mores, parum relevare videntur, pertinaciter negantem propositionem à Pontifice damnatam; sive enim illam neget, quia judicet, vel non, Pontificem posse errare, vel quia illi neget assistentiam Spiritus Sancti, semper erit hæreticus propositionem negans. | | 124. And this opinion of Malderus (Diana adds) Father Oviedo also teaches, *de Fide, controversia 4. puncto 9. numero 124.*; for he asserts that one can pertinaciously deny a censure inflicted by the Church without judging that the Church can err, and accordingly that on this head he would be a heretic who should deny it — not precisely because he would deny the censure, since the Pontiff does not define this as revealed, but because he would deny the special assistance of the Holy Spirit, in virtue of which He will never permit the Pontiff to err, and against this assistance would assert that the Pontiff had in fact erred. Thus that Doctor; which things, as regards morals, seem to relieve little the one pertinaciously denying a proposition condemned by the Pontiff; for whether he denies it because he judges, or does not judge, that the Pontiff can err, or because he denies to him the assistance of the Holy Spirit, he will always be a heretic in denying the proposition. |
| **CONCLUSIO II.** | | **CONCLUSION II.** |
| 125. OPiniones ab Alexandro Septimo damnatæ, & contentæ in Decretis, de quibus in titulo Quæstionis, omni carent probabilitate, & sunt omnino falsæ post Decreta dicta: adeo, ut sint, non solum non probabiles, id est gravi destitutæ fundamento, sed etiam improbabiles positive, id est certo falsæ. | | 125. The opinions condemned by Alexander the Seventh, and contained in the Decrees of which the title of the Question treats, lack all probability, and are altogether false after the said Decrees: so much so that they are not only not probable, that is, destitute of a grave foundation, but also positively improbable, that is, certainly false. |
| 126. Hanc conclusionem tenet Pater Joannes de Cardenas *in Crisi Theologica, tractatu 1. disputatione nona, capite 20. articulo 8.* Et probatur quoad primam partem, de non probabilitate opinionum damnatarum: quia implicat contradictionem, doctrinam esse probabilem, & prudenti fundamento innitentem; etiam simul in ratione doctrinæ moralis scandalum generare, & animarum perniciem. Quia ex quo in materia morali doctrina sit scandalizans, prudens non est. Quia prudens doctrina in materia morum illa est, quæ mores incorruptos servat; quæ autem scandalizat, & animabus perniciem infert, non aliter eam infert, nisi quatenus mores corrumpit, & ad peccatum inclinat, & provocat. Ergo eo ipso quod propositiones sint scandalosæ, necessario sunt non probabiles. | | 126. This conclusion Father John de Cardenas holds, *in Crisi Theologica, tractatu 1. disputatione nona, capite 20. articulo 8.* And it is proved as to the first part, concerning the non-probability of the condemned opinions: because it implies a contradiction that a doctrine be probable, and resting on a prudent foundation, and at the same time, in the character of a moral doctrine, generate scandal and the ruin of souls. Because from the fact that in moral matter a doctrine is scandalizing, it is not prudent. For a prudent doctrine in matter of morals is that which preserves morals uncorrupted; but that which scandalizes, and brings ruin to souls, does not bring it otherwise than inasmuch as it corrupts morals, and inclines and provokes to sin. Therefore by the very fact that the propositions are scandalous, they are necessarily not probable. |
| 127. Quoad secundam vero partem conclusionis, ut illam probemus, aliqua supponi debent. *Primum* est, magnum intercedere discrimen inter eam propositionem, quæ in materia morum scandalosæ censetur judicio privati cujuslibet Doctoris, & inter eam, quæ in simili, materia expresso judicio Pontificis Summi qua talis, & loquentis ex Cathedra scandalosa asseritur. Hoc enim expressum Papæ judicium, duo habet, quibus caret cujuslibet privati Doctoris judicium: certitudinem nempe, & perpetuitatem. Certitudinem quidem: quia Papa ex Cathedra loquens Ecclesiæ, in materia morum, errare non potest, uti late probat P. Cardenas *dicta disp. 9. capite 6.* & nos supra, *num. 99. & seqq.* | | 127. But as to the second part of the conclusion, in order that we may prove it, some things must be supposed. The *first* is that there is a great difference between that proposition which in matter of morals is judged scandalous by the judgment of any private Doctor, and that which in like matter is asserted to be scandalous by the express judgment of the Supreme Pontiff as such, and speaking *ex Cathedra*. For this express judgment of the Pope has two things which the judgment of any private Doctor lacks: namely, certitude and perpetuity. Certitude indeed: because the Pope speaking *ex Cathedra* to the Church, in matter of morals, cannot err, as Fr. Cardenas amply proves, *dicta disp. 9. capite 6.*, and we above, *num. 99. & seqq.* |
| 128. Perpetuitatem etiam habet Papæ judicium loquentis ex Cathedra: quia reliqui Doctores privati per hanc propositionem; *Hæc assertio non est probabilis;* significant, non adesse de præsenti fundamentum grave pro ea assertione; & ideo apud illos æquivalet illi; *Hæc est assertio non habens fundamentum grave, dum non apparet aliud fundamentum, præter ea, quæ de præsenti afferuntur pro ea.* Et ideo relinquitur locus, ut si postea apparuerit fundamentum grave, transeat assertio de non probabili, in probabilem. Quod ita de facto contigisse, certum est; cum multæ propositiones, quæ olim non erant probabiles, jam apparente denuo fundamento gravi sint probabiles. Papa vero, cum è Petri loquitur Sede Ecclesiæ, & opinionis negat probabilitatem, aut affirmat scandalosam eam esse, id docet, Ecclesiæque declarat in perpetuum, tanquam omni tempore sustinendam. Et ideo non dicit solum, quod de præsenti nullum pro ea fundamentum apparet grave, sed quod nec unquam apparebit, & quod semper pro scandalosa reputanda est. Unde omnimodam relinquit desperationem de ejus probabilitate, aut de ablatione scandali, quod propositio ipsa secum importat. | | 128. The judgment of the Pope speaking *ex Cathedra* has perpetuity also: because the other private Doctors, by this proposition, *This assertion is not probable*, signify that there is not at present a grave foundation for that assertion; and therefore among them it is equivalent to this: *This is an assertion not having a grave foundation, so long as no other foundation appears beyond those which are at present brought forward for it.* And therefore room is left that, if afterwards a grave foundation should appear, the assertion may pass from non-probable into probable. That this has in fact so happened is certain, since many propositions which formerly were not probable are now probable, a grave foundation having appeared anew. But the Pope, when he speaks to the Church from the See of Peter, and denies the probability of an opinion, or affirms it to be scandalous, teaches this and declares it to the Church in perpetuity, as to be maintained at all times. And therefore he does not say only that at present no grave foundation appears for it, but that none ever will appear, and that it is always to be reputed scandalous. Whence he leaves an utter despair of its probability, or of the removal of the scandal which the proposition itself carries with it. |
| 129. *Secundum,* quod supponimus est, quod ubi aliquis, absque errandi periculo, tradit propositionem esse falsam, illa est certo falsa. Et ratio est, quia immunitas à periculo errandi, certitudinem involvit: ubi autem est certitudo, non est errandi contingentia; & ubi est contingentia errandi, non est certitudo. Item, ubi excluditur contingentia errandi, adest certitudo; & ubi excluditur certitudo, contingentia errandi astruitur, affirmatur. | | 129. The *second* thing which we suppose is that, where someone, without danger of erring, hands on that a proposition is false, that proposition is certainly false. And the reason is, because immunity from the danger of erring involves certitude: but where there is certitude, there is no contingency of erring; and where there is contingency of erring, there is no certitude. Likewise, where the contingency of erring is excluded, certitude is present; and where certitude is excluded, the contingency of erring is built up and affirmed. |
| 130. *Tertium* est, propositionem scandalosam dupliciter considerari posse, vel qua scandalosa à parte rei, & ante quamlibet hominum existimationem; vel qua scandalosam, præsuposita hominum existimatione. contigit enim aliquando, opinionem veram esse à parte rei, sed quia homines ignorant fundament. grave ejus veritatis, fit, quod ea propositio probabilis non sit, cum absque fundamentum gravi, probabilis esse non possit. In hoc igitur casu, à parte rei scandalosa non est, cum contineat doctrinam veram à parte rei, & licitam, & inoxiam; supposita tamen existimatione hominum, quæ ad fundamentum non pertingit veritatis, est scandalosa, cum dirigere conscientiam suadeat, absque gravi fundamento apparente. | | 130. The *third* is that a scandalous proposition can be considered in two ways: either as scandalous *a parte rei*, and before any estimation of men whatsoever; or as scandalous, the estimation of men being presupposed. For it happens sometimes that an opinion is true *a parte rei*, but, because men are ignorant of the grave foundation of its truth, it comes about that that proposition is not probable, since without a grave foundation it cannot be probable. In this case, therefore, it is not scandalous *a parte rei*, since it contains a doctrine true *a parte rei*, and lawful, and harmless; yet, the estimation of men being supposed, which does not reach to the foundation of the truth, it is scandalous, since it persuades one to direct conscience without an apparent grave foundation. |
| 131. Quibus suppositis secunda pars nostræ conclusionis probatur, quod scil. propositiones ab Alexand. Papa VII. damnatæ, ut scandalosæ, sint positive improbabiles, & certo falsæ. *Primo,* Quia quando Papa ex Cathedra loquens damnat expresso judicio, in materia morum, propositionem aliquam, ut scandalosa, illam damnat ut falsam: sed id judicium Papæ in materia morum est certum: ergo talis propositio evadit certo falsa. Consequentia evidens est, quia quotiescumque judicium de falsitate propositionis est certum, propositio evadit certo falsa. | | 131. These things being supposed, the second part of our conclusion is proved, namely, that the propositions condemned by Pope Alexander VII as scandalous are positively improbable and certainly false. *First*, because when the Pope, speaking *ex Cathedra*, condemns by an express judgment, in matter of morals, some proposition as scandalous, he condemns it as false: but that judgment of the Pope in matter of morals is certain: therefore such a proposition turns out certainly false. The consequence is evident, because as often as the judgment concerning the falsity of a proposition is certain, the proposition turns out certainly false. |
| 132. Major clara constat, & ostenditur. Quia propositio, quam Romanus Pontifex ex Cathedra in materia morum damnat ut scandalosam à parte rei, non potest esse vera. Nam si à parte rei esset vera, à parte rei esset licitum ejus objectum, & solum ob ignorantiam esset illicitum: ergo Rom. Pontif. erraret, si ex Cathedra in materia morum damnaret ut illicitum, objectum à parte rei licitum. Sed Romanus Pontifex ex Cathedra loquens errare non potest in materia morum: ergo objectum à parte rei licitum, non potest damnare ut illicitum. Ergo Rom. Pontif. eo ipso, quod ex Cathedra damnet propositionem in materia morum ut scandalosam, illa vera esse non potest à parte rei: ergo eo ipso, quod damnet illam, ut scandalosam, damnat eam, ut falsam. | | 132. The major is clearly established, and is shown. Because a proposition which the Roman Pontiff *ex Cathedra* in matter of morals condemns as scandalous *a parte rei* cannot be true. For if *a parte rei* it were true, its object would *a parte rei* be lawful, and only through ignorance would it be unlawful: therefore the Roman Pontiff would err, if *ex Cathedra* in matter of morals he should condemn as unlawful an object lawful *a parte rei*. But the Roman Pontiff speaking *ex Cathedra* cannot err in matter of morals: therefore he cannot condemn as unlawful an object lawful *a parte rei*. Therefore the Roman Pontiff, by the very fact that *ex Cathedra* he condemns a proposition in matter of morals as scandalous, [establishes that] it cannot be true *a parte rei*: therefore by the very fact that he condemns it as scandalous, he condemns it as false. |
| 133. Aliud autem est dicendum, quando Doctor privatus (& idem dico de Papa non loquente ex Cathedra) censet propositionem aliquam scandalosam: quia ad hoc privatum judicium sufficit, quod pro illa propositione, ut scandalosa notata, non appareat ullum grave fundamentum: quia privatus Doctor capax est ignorantiæ, (pro ut ignorantia dicit quodlibet scientiæ defectum) de eo, quod est à parte rei. Cum autem Summus Pontifex ex Cathedra loquens, non sit capax ignorantiæ in judicio expresso, quod in materia morum format, ob infalibilem St. Spiritus assistentiam in re tam gravi; Inde est, quod dum propositionem in materia morum affirmat scandalosam, illa à parte rei vera esse non posset. | | 133. But another thing must be said when a private Doctor (and I say the same of the Pope not speaking *ex Cathedra*) judges some proposition scandalous: because for this private judgment it suffices that for that proposition, noted as scandalous, no grave foundation appear: because a private Doctor is capable of ignorance (inasmuch as ignorance means any defect of knowledge whatsoever) concerning that which is *a parte rei*. But since the Supreme Pontiff speaking *ex Cathedra* is not capable of ignorance in the express judgment which he forms in matter of morals, on account of the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit in a matter so grave, hence it is that, while he affirms a proposition in matter of morals to be scandalous, it could not be true *a parte rei*. |
| 134. Confirmatur. Cum Rom. Pontif. damnat ex Cathedra propositionem, ut scandalosam in materia morum, non damnat illam tanquam scandalosam ex præsuposita ignorantia hominum, sed tanquam vere, & à parte rei scandalosam; alias erraret in materia morum, si objectum à parte rei licitum, & honestum, reprobaret ut scandalosum: sed doctrina quæ à parte rei est scandalosa, non potest à parte rei esse vera: ergo à parte rei ea doctrina damnata non potest esse vera. Et cum id judicium Papæ ex Cathedra sit certum: ea propositio manet certo falsa. | | 134. It is confirmed. When the Roman Pontiff condemns *ex Cathedra* a proposition as scandalous in matter of morals, he does not condemn it as scandalous from a presupposed ignorance of men, but as truly and *a parte rei* scandalous; otherwise he would err in matter of morals, if he should reprobate as scandalous an object lawful and honest *a parte rei*: but a doctrine which is *a parte rei* scandalous cannot *a parte rei* be true: therefore *a parte rei* that condemned doctrine cannot be true. And since that judgment of the Pope *ex Cathedra* is certain, that proposition remains certainly false. |
| 135. *Secundo* probatur secunda conclusionis nostræ pars. Dum Summus Pontifex docens è Cathedra Ecclesiam in materia morum, definit propositionem esse scandalosam, vi ejus oraculi manet certo, & in perpetuum destituta fundamento gravi, & cum certa, & omnimoda desperatione de probabilitate ejus: sed propositio, quæ manet certo, & in perpetuum non probabilis, & cum certa, & omnimoda desperatione de probabilitate ejus, manet certo falsa, atque adeo improbabilis positivè: ergo propositio, quam Summus Pontifex docens Ecclesiam ex Cathedra in materia m[o]rum, definit esse scandalosam, manet certo falsa, & improbabilis positive. Consequentia patet. Major autem constat ex dictis *supra numero 128.* | | 135. *Secondly* the second part of our conclusion is proved. While the Supreme Pontiff, teaching the Church from the Chair in matter of morals, defines a proposition to be scandalous, by force of that oracle it remains certainly and in perpetuity destitute of a grave foundation, and with a certain and utter despair of its probability: but a proposition which remains certainly and in perpetuity not probable, and with a certain and utter despair of its probability, remains certainly false, and thus positively improbable: therefore a proposition which the Supreme Pontiff, teaching the Church *ex Cathedra* in matter of morals, defines to be scandalous, remains certainly false and positively improbable. The consequence is plain. But the major is established from the things said *supra numero 128.* |
| 136. Et minor probatur. Quando propositio est vera à parte rei, nullum principium certum obstat quo minus aliquando aliquod fundamentum grave appareat ad prædictam veritatem promovendam: nam contra veritatem, aut contra fundamentum ejus nullum principium certum existere potest, quod re vera certum sit: ergo quando est principium certum contra fundamentum alicujus propositionis, talis propositio vera esse non potest: ergo est certo falsa. Sed cum Summus Pontifex in materia morum expresso judicio affirmat in perpetuum, propositionem non esse probabilem, id principium certum excludit in perpetuum quodlibet fundamentum grave ejus propositionis: ergo in eo casu propositio sic damnata, vera esse non potest: ergo necessario, & certissime est falsa. | | 136. And the minor is proved. When a proposition is true *a parte rei*, no certain principle stands in the way but that at some time some grave foundation may appear for promoting the aforesaid truth: for against truth, or against its foundation, no certain principle can exist which is really certain: therefore when there is a certain principle against the foundation of some proposition, such a proposition cannot be true: therefore it is certainly false. But when the Supreme Pontiff in matter of morals by an express judgment affirms in perpetuity that a proposition is not probable, that certain principle excludes in perpetuity any grave foundation whatsoever of that proposition: therefore in that case the proposition so condemned cannot be true: therefore necessarily and most certainly it is false. |
| 137. Ubi notandum est, quod quando Doctor privatus dicit propositionem aliquam esse positive improbabilem, si verum dicit, omnino tollit spem, aut formidinem futuræ probabilitatis; atque de improbabili non posse propositionem in probabilem transire. Id enim asserere non potest, nisi ex vero principio, cum improbabile positive, idem sit, quod certo falsum: principium autem certum, quod cogit ad asserendam falsitatem propositionis, nullam de opposito formidinem relinquit. | | 137. Where it must be noted that, when a private Doctor says some proposition to be positively improbable, if he says the truth, he takes away entirely the hope, or the fear, of a future probability; and that the proposition cannot pass from improbable into probable. For he cannot assert this save from a true principle, since to be positively improbable is the same as to be certainly false: but a certain principle, which compels one to assert the falsity of a proposition, leaves no fear concerning the opposite. |
| 138. Concludo dicendo, quod ut propositio in materia morum scandalosa sit, salvatur, in hoc quod non sit probabilis, sive in eo, quod gravi careat fundamento in hominum existimatione, cum conscientias ad operandum dirigat in morum, sine gravi fundamento, quamvis ea, à parte rei vera forte sit. Cum autem id contingat ex defectu scientiæ in materia morum quæ cadere neutiquam potest in Romano Pontifice, ex Cathedra docente Ecclesiam, inde est, non posse dictum Pontificem, qua talem, & qua ex Cathedra docentem, docere aliquam opinionem esse scandalosam, si à parte rei illa vera sit. Unde quoties Papa ex Cathedra in materia morum affirmat, esse, improbabilem positive. Et sic quamvis dicens scandalosam, simul dicat, eam non esse probabilem, eo ipso affirmat esse positive improbabilem. I conclude by saying that, for a proposition in matter of morals to be scandalous, it is verified in this, that it be not probable, or in this, that it lack a grave foundation in the estimation of men, since it directs consciences to act in morals without a grave foundation, although *a parte rei* it be perhaps true. But since this happens from a defect of knowledge in matter of morals, which can by no means fall upon the Roman Pontiff teaching the Church *ex Cathedra*, hence it is that the said Pontiff, as such, and as teaching *ex Cathedra*, cannot teach some opinion to be scandalous, if *a parte rei* it be true. Whence, as often as the Pope *ex Cathedra* in matter of morals affirms [a proposition to be scandalous, he affirms it] to be positively improbable [?]. And thus, although in saying it scandalous he at the same time says that it is not probable, by that very fact he affirms it to be positively improbable. | Propositio *scandalosa,* intelligi debet, quæ præstat occasionem ruinæ spiritualis, inclinando auditores ad peccata, vel avocando eos ab exercitio virtutum. Tales propositiones detrahentium Religioso statui, pericula item piorum exercitiorum exaggerantium, unde tepescunt homines, & timent, ne in his periculum sibi lateat. Item si quis populo ad venerationem Eucharistiæ, vel alicujus Sancti concurrenti, dicat, *quis scit, an hæc Hostia sit consecrata? quis scit, an hoc sit corpus illius Sancti, & non sit corpus alicujus malefactoris, loco illius suppositum?* Quæ omnia, in aliquo vero sensu, possunt non esse contra fidem; generant tamen scandalum, & removent homines ab exercitio pietatis. Aliud exemplum affert Suarez, *numero 19.* nempe si quis absolute dicat imagines non esse adorandas. Hæc tamen propositio plus est, quam scandalosa, cum erret etiam circa ipsum dogma fidei, ut constat ex Concilio Tridentino, *sessione 15.* & ex formula professionis fidei per Pium IV. edita, & quam omnes Episcopi, & aliæ personæ emittere tenentur. Hactenus Cardinalis de Lugo. | | A *scandalous* proposition must be understood as one which affords occasion of spiritual ruin, by inclining hearers to sins, or by calling them away from the exercise of virtues. Such are the propositions of those who detract from the Religious state, and likewise of those who exaggerate the dangers of pious exercises, whence men grow lukewarm, and fear lest in these a danger lie hidden for them. Likewise, if anyone should say to a people gathering for the veneration of the Eucharist, or of some Saint: *who knows whether this Host be consecrated? who knows whether this be the body of that Saint, and not the body of some malefactor, substituted in his place?* All which things, in some true sense, can be not against faith; yet they generate scandal, and remove men from the exercise of piety. Another example Suarez brings forward, *numero 19.*, namely, if anyone should say absolutely that images are not to be adored. This proposition, however, is more than scandalous, since it errs also concerning the very dogma of faith, as is established from the Council of Trent, *sessione 15.*, and from the formula of the profession of faith published by Pius IV, and which all Bishops and other persons are bound to emit. Thus far Cardinal de Lugo. |
