## De orationibus inthronizationis (On the Prayers of Enthronement)

[...]

Concerning their antiquity, the manuscript codex of the Vatican Library Barb. lat. 631 (formerly XIV 4) provides us with precious testimony. [...] nevertheless we believe those prayers to be very ancient and in their substance to ascend to the time when the election of a bishop to the pontificate was still an exception, namely to the tenth or even ninth century. For it is said in the first prayer: *O God, who do not disdain to be present wherever you are invoked with devout mind, be present, we beseech you, to our invocations, and to this your servant N., **whom the common judgment of your people has chosen to reach the apostolic summit**, may he have attained the highest point. Through...*

Certainly, it is not probable that the prayer was composed after the year 1059, since after the decree of Nicholas II, there can scarcely be proper discussion of the *"common judgment of the people,"* indeed, *judgment* was reserved to the cardinal bishops. On the other hand, we believe that the relationships between the texts of the prayers and the practice of papal elections must be recognized. These elections of bishops, as we have already seen, were considered to have been conducted contrary to the canons. **Nevertheless, they were always justified by the fact of a common and universal election by the people.** Thus concerning Marinus it is said that he was elected *"by the advocacy and election of the Roman people"*; concerning Formosus it is reported: *"Your leading phalanxes and satraps, your common people and your schools, from the greatest to the least, elected him, acclaimed him, praised him, and adored him..."*. In a similar manner concerning John XIII: *"Then by all the Roman people... he is elected"*. We can recognize the same intention in the cited prayer; **the common judgment of the people justifies an election conducted contrary to the canons; moreover, the providence of God is emphasized, by which the elected one has attained the apostolic summit.** [...]

[...]

*Note: Wasner — a canonist writing under the 1917 Code — here documents, from the liturgical books themselves, the pre-systematic acceptance-idiom of the ninth and tenth centuries: elections defective by the letter of the canons (a bishop translated to the Roman See) were healed and justified in the Church's own prayers by the "commune iudicium plebis," the common and universal adhesion of the people, under the providence of God. His three witnesses — Marinus, Formosus, John XIII — are the same dossier printed in this repository's 0914 Auxilius and 0968 Adalbertus entries.*
