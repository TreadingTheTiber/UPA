- *Selection: Quaestio XXIX entire — "Vtrùm Fides sit discursiua?" — printed pp. 547–549 (objections 1–3, Arts. 1–14, Ad 1–3), from Tomus I of the Cursus theologicus, editio tertia "recognita ab auctore et aucta" (Lyon: Borde, Arnaud, Borde & Barbier, 1666), dedicated to Alexander VII. The dedication records that Esparza's first theological opuscula appeared "a decade ago," at the dawn of Alexander VII's pontificate, under the borrowed name of his student Count Eusebius Truchses — which dates the composition of this material under Innocent X († 1655), who is named as the reigning pope in the text.*

*Note on this entry: Martín de Esparza Artieda, S.J. (1606–1689), of Navarre ("Navarrus" on the title page), long professor of theology at the Roman College, treats the pope-singular at the precise point the schools had made its home: the* ***theological-conclusion problem***. *Quaestio XXIX asks whether faith is discursive, and resolves it with a distinction (Arts. 7–8): a cognition* ***inferred*** *from premises "is not an act of faith, but of another species" — but "a truth evidently inferible from two premises of faith, or from one of faith and another naturally metaphysically evident, is* ***certain from faith, and can thereby be believed by divine faith, nor can it be denied, per se speaking, except by heresy***." Then the worked application (Arts. 10–13), and it is built as a deliberate* ***asymmetry***. *The negative side (Art. 10) is the entire occult-conditions catalogue that the objectors of this collection press — and Esparza concedes it wholesale: "it is* ***not*** *of faith that under* ***these-in-number*** *accidents of a consecrated host the Body of Christ the Lord is present, that* ***this-in-number man*** *baptized or ordained has received baptismal grace or Priestly power, and so of other things of this kind." The positive side is the single exception, with the reigning pope named per tempora: "***But that it is of faith — and indeed public and Catholic faith — that this-in-number man, e.g. the present Innocent X, is the Supreme Pastor of the Church, and Pontiff, and universal Vicar of Christ, and legitimate successor of St. Peter***; that the things defined and declared by him as of faith, and proposed by him as such, are truly of faith; and so of the other things pertaining to the supreme office and power of the Supreme Pontiff." The reasons make the asymmetry principled. For the host and the private singulars (Art. 11): no sufficient indicia of credibility exist, "and — what is the chief thing — the revelation made to the Church... is not rendered false or useless,* ***nor is the intention of God who reveals it frustrated***, *even if one or another Host which appears duly consecrated" is not. For the pope (Art. 12): God revealed that the visible Church will endure perpetually, that the gates of hell shall not prevail, that she is built on Peter with the office of supreme pastor — "and these things cannot subsist unless Peter's successors endure perpetually with the same power of feeding and ruling, as the Church endures perpetually — which* ***can neither endure visible without a likewise visible head, nor be sustained, fed, or ruled visibly by a man removed from the living***" — the perpetuity of the succession without intermission, wielded expressly against a Church headed by the dead. Art. 13 then answers the question this collection has traced through Bordoni's coronation-terminus and John of St. Thomas:* ***when*** *does the singular become de fide? "***Namely, when the whole, or nearly the whole, multitude of the faithful is seen to assent uniformly to the legitimacy of the election; or when already so many, and such, have assented, that it cannot prudently be doubted that the rest will come to the same opinion***, the circumstances known near the place of the election being considered. For no more suitable proposition of this article is given in fact, or can easily be given or devised, acting conformably to the human condition,* ***to which divine Providence accommodates itself***." Note the two refinements unique to him: the* ***"nearly the whole" clause*** *(tota, aut ferè tota — a built-in denial that a dissenting residue can veto the completion; cf. Arriaga's "some few who may be discontented" and the completeness-clause dossier), and the* ***projective completion*** *(assent already so weighty that the remainder's concurrence is beyond prudent doubt — the certification need not wait upon the last diocese). The article concludes in the terms that settle his classification: "It is concluded therefore... that in the present matter* ***the divine faith of the Supreme Pontificate, conferred on this determinate man***, *is preceded by the supernatural apprehension..." — fides divina of the singular, its denial heresy per Art. 8. Classification is* ***1a***. *His idiom "de fide* ***publica, & Catholica***" *deserves its own note: it is the same formula as Ripalda's "fide non solum privata, sed etiam publica et catholica," and Esparza is among the seventeenth-century authors whom the later fides-ecclesiastica school (this collection's 1b-ii; cf. the 1874 Schrader entry) claims as forerunners — but in this text the "public and Catholic" qualifies* ***divine*** *faith under the Church's public proposition, not a distinct lesser habit; the singular is expressly credible "per fidem diuinam." He is thus a proposal-side 1a witness whose vocabulary the 1b-ii school would later inherit and re-grade.*

## Art. 10 — the asymmetry, with Innocent X named (pp. 548–549)

**Art. 10.** From the distinction given it is again inferred in particular that **it is not of faith that under these-in-number accidents of a consecrated host the Body of Christ the Lord is present, that this-in-number man baptized or ordained has received baptismal grace or Priestly power, and so of other things of this kind: But that it is of faith — and indeed public and Catholic faith — that this-in-number man, e.g. the present Innocent X, is the Supreme Pastor of the Church, and Pontiff, and universal Vicar of Christ, and legitimate successor of St. Peter**; that the things defined and declared by him as of faith, and proposed by him as such, are truly of faith; and so of the other things pertaining to the supreme office and power of the Supreme Pontiff.

## Art. 12 — the perpetual visible head (p. 549)

**Art. 12.** [...] these things cannot subsist unless Peter's successors endure perpetually with the same power of feeding and ruling, as the Church endures perpetually — **which can neither endure visible without a likewise visible head, nor be sustained, fed, or ruled visibly by a man removed from the living** [...]

## Art. 13 — when it begins to be de fide (p. 549)

**Art. 13.** From which reason it is clear, moreover, **when it begins to be of faith that this-in-number man elected for the time is the true and legitimate Supreme Pontiff of the Church. Namely, when the whole, or nearly the whole, multitude of the faithful is seen to assent uniformly to the legitimacy of the election; or when already so many, and such, have assented, that it cannot prudently be doubted that the rest will come to the same opinion**, the circumstances known near the place of the election being considered.
