*Note on this entry: Juan Sendín Calderón (d. before 1699; L. Fabre,* Archivo Ibero-Americano *33 [1930] 271–303) was a Scotist of the Alcalá school, Franciscan Provincial of Castile, and — like Torres before him and Carena beside him —* ***an officer of doctrinal qualification for the Inquisition***. *His tracts were published after his death by his "most faithful disciple" Bernique, at Alcalá, "adorned with some additions." The volume names three popes: Controv. IV names* ***Alexander VII*** *(1655–67), Controv. VI names* ***Clement IX*** *(1667–69), and the* Additio *to Dub. II names* ***Innocent XII*** *(1691–1700) four times. The first two strata are Sendín's own, written across the 1660s; the third is Bernique's, written for the 1699 press — and it is more affirmative than its author. This is the fifth Alcalá witness in the collection, and the third, after Ojea and Arbiol, to be cited with Ojea by a contemporary ("*Lumbier, Sendín, & Oxea*," the [1702 Arbiol](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1702%20Arbiol) entry). Classification is* ***1a***, *with a precise limitation that the note sets out.*

***The dubium is general, and Sendín says the papal case is its application.*** *Dub. II asks whether the revelation of a universal is the immediate revelation of its singulars, "whether the descent be made under the same terms or under different terms." § I settles the first (the concordant opinion, with Pedro Hurtado and Lugo). § II turns to the second against* ***Arriaga***, *and there the pope appears as the standing example: the objections "hold for that celebrated question, whether it is of faith that Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff, since it rests on the same foundations and is assailed by the same arguments as ours." He retorts Arriaga's own argument: "***it stands well that this proposition, Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff, is of faith, although it is not of faith, nor certain with metaphysical certitude, that he was validly elected***." And § 94 closes the body: "***From what has been said that celebrated controversy is resolved: whether it is of faith that Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff?*** *… since it is of faith that everyone validly elected is Supreme Pontiff,* ***whenever it is established by moral evidence that this man was validly elected, by the same evidence it is established that he is contained under the universal revelation***; *and therefore this will be of faith:* Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff, *as we asserted of that:* Peter contracted original sin."*

***The theory — acceptance as a previous condition, not a motive (§§ 91–93, and Block A).*** *This is what earns Sendín his place beside the other three. In the syllogism the assent to the conclusion "is moved* ***adequately by the revealed major***; *the minor stands as* ***a previous condition applying the revelation***, *in that it manifests its object to be contained under the revealed universal: since therefore that which has only the character of a prudent application of the revelation* ***requires only moral certitude***, *as is clear in the judgment of prudent credibility, hence it is that the conclusion is more certain than one of the premises." Then the decisive sentence: "***the conclusion which we call of faith is not a formal conclusion, nor do we assent to it with an assent of faith insofar as it is inferred from a twofold premise*** *… but … it is moved immediately by the divine authority revealing." Block A states the same of the pope by name: the assent of divine faith "by which Alexander VII is believed to be true Pontiff essentially supposes another assent, by which he is believed to have been validly elected;* ***this last assent to the valid election is moved adequately by the human authority of the Church accepting him as true Pope; yet the assent of divine faith, by which he is believed to be true Pope, is not moved even inadequately by human authority, but purely and precisely by divine authority***." *The acceptance, in short, is the* ***conditio praevia*** *that applies the revelation; the faith rests on the revelation alone.*

***The limitation — what the acceptance does not do (§ 79).*** *Here is the sentence that divides him from [Platel](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1680%20Platel): "that Clement IX was validly elected is nowhere revealed; otherwise it would be revealed that the electors are legitimate electors, and so it would be of faith that they are true Cardinals and truly baptized;* ***but no one will say that, even after the peaceful acceptance of the Supreme Pontiff, it is heretical to assert that several of the electors were not baptized. Nor does the acceptance infer anything beyond a moral certitude, consisting in the common consensus of many, whom it is so exceedingly difficult to deceive or to be deceived, that no one can prudently doubt their truth***." *Acceptance certifies the* ***conclusion*** *by faith — because the conclusion rests on the revelation, not on the minor — but carries the underlying* ***facts*** *only to moral certainty. Platel, in the same decade, holds that the acceptance is "an exercised and virtual revelation" by which the essentials themselves — man, baptized — are "implicitly revealed and consequently of faith." Sendín denies exactly that. And he is the structural inverse of [Neesen](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1678%20Neesen): the same premise about the facts, the opposite conclusion — because Neesen holds that the conclusion follows the weaker premise, and Sendín holds that a premise which is only a condition does not limit the conclusion at all.*

***The Additio — the Alcalá school in 1699, more affirmative than its author.*** *After § 94 the text continues without a number: "***Since this question is most celebrated in our time, and frequently agitated in the schools, it seemed good to add some arguments here for the benefit of students***." *Three objections follow with replies, and the name in every example is* ***Innocent XII***, *who came to the see twenty-two years after Clement IX died. With the title page's* "aliquibus additionibus ornatum," *that settles its authorship: it is Bernique's, and it records what was being taught at Alcalá at the press-date. Its final response is the strongest statement of the acceptance-doctrine in the volume: "***It is answered finally that such a proposition is of faith directly and reflexly, supposing however the peaceful acceptance of the Church***." *Two reasons — first, that for reflex faith it suffices that the existence of the revelation be morally established, "and of the revelation of this particular,* Innocent XII is Supreme Pontiff, *it is likewise morally established, supposing the peaceful acceptance"; and second: "***as many theologians, especially of this University, maintain: given the peaceful acceptance of the whole Church, the valid election is not only morally certain, but is also of faith*** *… and the reason is that the truth of this valid election is founded on that promise of Christ (Matt. 28) 'Behold I am with you… even to the consummation of the world,' and (Matt. 16) 'the gates of hell shall not prevail against it': whence, supposing the peaceful acceptance, it will be of faith that this man was validly elected,* ***otherwise the said revelations would be falsified, since the Church would be without a true Pastor, against Christ's promise***." *That is Platel's position, and Ojea's — attached, by his own pupil, to a book whose author had denied it in § 79. It adds the obligation "in all the faithful of believing such a particular proposition," from Martin V's Constance bull, "his proper name being expressed." And it ends with a sentence in the tradition of Torres, Ojea and Neesen on the censure question: whether the opposite opinion, that the proposition is not immediately of faith, "is or is not probable —* ***that pertains to the Church***; *and I only assert that the assent terminated at such a proposition does not rest merely on a probable knowledge of the existence of the revelation."*

***Where he sits.*** *Four late-century authors fix the axis of what the acceptance certifies. Neesen (1678): the facts uncertain, therefore the conclusion not of faith. Sendín (1660s): the facts morally certain only, the conclusion nonetheless of faith, the acceptance a previous condition. Ojea (1661): the acceptance a second election, in which God prevents the divine-law defects. Platel (1680) and Sendín's own editor (1699): the acceptance a virtual revelation, so that the valid election and its essentials are themselves of faith. Sendín's is the most carefully limited of the affirmative positions, and the one that answers Neesen most directly, since it grants his premise and denies his inference. Classification* ***1a***.*

## Controv. IV sect. 6 § 2 — the acceptance as a previous condition (printed 50–51)

[...] In the manner in which this assent of divine faith, by which Alexander VII is believed to be true Pontiff, essentially supposes another assent, by which he is believed to have been validly elected: **this last assent to the valid election is moved adequately by the human authority of the Church accepting him as true Pope; yet the assent of divine faith, by which he is believed to be true Pope, is not moved even inadequately by human authority, but purely and precisely by divine authority**: the human authority of the Church, therefore, moves to the previous assent by which this man is believed validly elected [...] It is not therefore universally true that whatever is supposed for the assent of revelation is required as its motive; but only that it is required either as a motive, or as a **previous condition**.

## Controv. VI Dub. II, §§ 78–79 — the retort to Arriaga, and the limitation (printed 103)

**78** But if, as Arriaga must concede, this is of faith, *Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff*, behold his argument retorted: it stands well that this proposition, *Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff*, is of faith, although it is not of faith, nor certain with metaphysical certitude, that he was validly elected [...]

**79** The antecedent is proved, because that Clement IX was validly elected is nowhere revealed; otherwise it would be revealed that the electors are legitimate electors, and so it would be of faith that they are true Cardinals, and truly baptized; **but no one will say that, even after the peaceful acceptance of the Supreme Pontiff, it is heretical to assert that several of the electors were not baptized. Nor does the acceptance infer anything beyond a moral certitude, consisting in the common consensus of many, whom it is so exceedingly difficult to deceive or to be deceived, that no one can prudently doubt their truth.**

## Controv. VI Dub. II, §§ 92–94 — the theory, and the resolution (printed 106)

**92** And so in this syllogism — *Every man descending from Adam contracted original sin; Peter is a man descending from Adam; therefore Peter contracted original sin* — the assent to the conclusion is moved adequately by the revealed major proposition; but the minor stands as **a previous condition applying the revelation**, in that it manifests its object to be contained under the revealed universal proposition: since therefore that which has only the character of a prudent application of the revelation **requires only moral certitude**, as is clear in the judgment of prudent credibility, hence it is that the conclusion is more certain than one of the premises [...]

**93** Therefore **the conclusion which we call of faith is not a formal conclusion, nor do we assent to it with an assent of faith insofar as it is inferred from a twofold premise** [...] but if the assent to the aforesaid conclusion is of faith, **it is moved immediately by the divine authority revealing** [...]

**94** **From what has been said that celebrated controversy is resolved: whether it is of faith that Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff?** For from what has been said it is sufficiently clear that this doubt is to be resolved affirmatively: since it is of faith that everyone validly elected is Supreme Pontiff, **whenever it is established by moral evidence that this man was validly elected, by the same evidence it is established that he is contained under the universal revelation**; and therefore this will be of faith: *Clement IX is Supreme Pontiff*, as we asserted of that: *Peter contracted original sin*.

## The Additio (Bernique, 1699) — the Alcalá school's position at the press-date (printed 106–108)

*Addition.* **Since this question is most celebrated in our time, and frequently agitated in the schools, it seemed good to add some arguments here for the benefit of students.** [...]

**It is answered finally that such a proposition is of faith directly and reflexly, supposing however the peaceful acceptance of the Church.** [...] 2. because, **as many theologians, especially of this University, maintain: given the peaceful acceptance of the whole Church, the valid election is not only morally certain, but is also of faith**, and consequently, since this is the condition under which the particular proposition is contained under the universal, the particular proposition will turn out not only directly but also reflexly of faith. And the reason is that the truth of this valid election is founded on that promise of Christ [...] *Behold I am with you even to the consummation of the world*, and [...] *the gates of hell shall not prevail against it*. Whence, supposing the peaceful acceptance, it will be of faith that this man was validly elected, **otherwise the said revelations would be falsified, since the Church would be without a true Pastor, against Christ's promise**. Finally, without doubt, supposing the peaceful acceptance, there is in all the faithful an obligation of believing such a particular proposition, as is clear from the Bull of Martin V in the Council of Constance [...] *whether he believes that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being, **his proper name being expressed**, is the successor of Blessed Peter, having supreme authority in the Church*. But whether the opposite opinion, asserting that such a proposition is not immediately of faith, is or is not probable — **that pertains to the Church**; and I only assert that the assent terminated at such a proposition does not rest merely on a probable knowledge of the existence of the revelation.
