*Note on this entry: Martino Bonacina (c. 1585–1631), Milanese moral theologian ("Mediolanensis, Comes Palatinus" on the title page), wrote both a complete conclave-law treatise and, within his censures volume, a de fide treatment of the pope's identity — and the pair makes him the collection's* ***controlled experiment on its own method***. *The core text sits where divine law forces itself into a canonical book: to apply the Bulla Cœnæ's first excommunication (heretics), the punctum* Quid sit hæresis *must say what is de fide, and the seventh worked assertion (n. 9) is the pope-singular, with the reigning pope named per tempora: "it can with probability be maintained that he is a heretic, and consequently excommunicated in the Bulla Cœnæ,* ***who denies this Supreme Pontiff Urban VIII to be true Pope and head of the Church, after he is sufficiently proposed***" — for "in the opinion of many Doctors it is de fide that this man is true Pope"; because reconciled heretics must, by* ***Martin V's constitution***, *be asked whether they believe the Pontiff to be Peter's successor; because "***the whole Church believes and holds this man to be true Pope; therefore she believes it by divine faith****, not human; otherwise she could be deceived, since falsehood can underlie human faith"; because what he defines is believed with divine faith, "otherwise we should be obliged to believe by divine faith the judgment of this Pontiff, whom by merely human faith we believed to be Pope"; and because the pseudo-pope has no assistance of the Holy Spirit (Matth. 24). The* Dixi *then supplies the acceptance-clause: sufficient proposal obtains "when it is established that he was duly and truly elected;* ***or, as Suárez says (num. 6), while it is morally established that he has been accepted by the whole Church, and peacefully possesses the primacy, and hence can oblige all the faithful to believe whatever he shall have defined***." Seven objections follow — a catalogue, in 1627, of the modern controversy: the Eucharist-parity objection; the* ***occult-conditions objection*** *(not de fide that he is baptized, priest, canonically elected), answered three ways — "from this argument it only follows that* ***the Holy Spirit, who assists the Church, will not permit that one be elected Pope, and held as such in the whole Church, who is not baptized, a priest, and legitimately elected***"; the connected-articles answer; and Suárez's, that God "by his infallible providence is judged to have so disposed that he never err in the election of him who is chosen for this dignity" — ; the preacher-parity objection (answered: the Pontiff is judge and interpreter of God); the* ***Pope Joan*** *objection (answered: a fable "fabricated by heretics," per Bellarmine, Onuphrius, and Scherer's Vienna treatise); the never-revealed objection (answered from revelation* in genere *of Peter's successors, with Suárez); the* ***antipapacy objection***, *drawing the entry's central guarantee: "***it never happened that the whole Church esteemed someone to be true Pope who in very fact was not such****, though sometimes for a time she did not recognize the true and certain Pastor;* ***but it is one thing not to recognize the true Pastor, another to conspire and consent in someone as Pastor who really is not***" — and while the Church lacks a recognized head she is not exposed to false dogmas, since what is done then is not esteemed certain "***until it be decreed by an undoubted Pope***"; and the not-punished objection, with the candid dogmatic-status note: the denier is not punished as a heretic because "the opinion which I follow* ***is not explicitly defined by the Church***, *and has many contrary Doctors" — yet "in the opinion of all" the denier who withholds obedience is a schismatic. The appendix then prints the* ***canonical register*** *of the same author on the same doubt-cases, and the contrast teaches two lessons. First:* ***writing as a canonist, Bonacina is not answering questions of divine protection at all*** *— the election treatise resolves the doubtful Cardinals of a schism jurisdictionally (Appendix A: they fall under a General Council, "since the right of such Cardinals is not established, while it is established that a General Council has power in the whole Church" — "tota Ecclesia" measuring a council's reach, where the core text uses the same words to name the subject whose acceptance cannot fail); and where the divine-law question does surface, the treatise* ***hands it to his own theological locus by name*** *(Appendix B: whether a legitimately elected pope can fall into heresy and be deprived "is established from what was said in the Bulla Cœnæ, disp. 1, quæst. 2, punct. 1, num. 12 and following" — the very punctum of the core text). Second, the* ***two-tier defect doctrine*** *(Appendix C, on the lex Barbarius): for a defect* ***voided by human law only*** *(simony, form), the acts of the putative pope stand, "because the Church would then be said to delegate jurisdiction to him and to supply" — "for the Church in such an event supplies what it can supply" (Appendix E) — and the simony exception is admissible only "***while the Pope has not yet been received***" (Appendix D); but for a defect of* ***divine or natural law*** *(a woman elected by error), the acts are void against Iason's reading of Barbarius, for "***the Church cannot supply for a defect of Divine or natural law***" — though "the Church is not above the Pontiff,* ***it would nevertheless be above this man, who would be not a true but a presumed Pontiff***," who (with Alterius) "***cannot infallibly define matters of faith***." Ecclesiastical-law defects, then, are repaired by the Church herself; divine-law defects have no canonical repair at all — for these the only safeguard is the one the core text asserts: God does not permit the whole Church to accept a false head, the guarantee known through universal peaceful acceptance (in Bonacina stated as the historical-theological fact, on Suárez's revelation-in-genere; the providential mechanics are explicit in his sources and in this collection's Amico and Arriaga entries). The sedevacantist hypothesis — a divine-law defect persisting for decades beneath universal reception — is precisely the configuration for which his canon law admits no remedy and his theology denies the possibility. Classification is* ***1a*** *(de fide of the accepted singular per the many-Doctors sentence Bonacina follows, Suárez-line, with the acceptance-clause in the* Dixi *and the not-explicitly-defined candor documented).*

## Core text, n. 9 — the thesis with Urban VIII named, and the Suárez acceptance-clause (p. 7)

**9** Seventhly, that it can with probability be maintained that he is a heretic, and consequently excommunicated in the Bulla Cœnæ, **who denies this Supreme Pontiff Urban VIII to be true Pope and head of the Church, after he is sufficiently proposed**, or after it is established that he was duly and canonically elected. [...] Then because **the whole Church believes and holds this man to be true Pope; therefore she believes it by divine faith**, not human; otherwise she could be deceived, since falsehood can underlie human faith. [...] I said, *after he is sufficiently proposed* [...] **or, as Suarez says num. 6, while it is morally established that he has been accepted by the whole Church, and peacefully possesses the primacy, and hence can oblige all the faithful to believe whatever he shall have defined.**

## Core text, n. 10 — the occult-conditions objection and the providence response (p. 7)

You will object secondly, we cannot believe by divine faith this man to be true Pope, unless we also believe him to be baptized, a priest, and canonically elected; but it is not established for us [...] I respond first, that from this argument it only follows that **the Holy Spirit, who assists the Church, will not permit that one be elected Pope, and held as such in the whole Church, who is not baptized, a priest, and legitimately elected.**

## Core text — the antipapacy objection answered (p. 8)

I respond that **it never happened that the whole Church esteemed someone to be true Pope who in very fact was not such**, though sometimes for a time she did not recognize the true and certain Pastor; **but it is one thing not to recognize the true Pastor, another to conspire and consent in someone as Pastor who really is not.** For while the Church does not recognize the true Pastor and head, she is not exposed to the danger of false dogmas (since the things which are done at that time are not esteemed certain, **until it be decreed by an undoubted Pope**;) as she would be exposed, if she recognized and received a false Pastor as true and certain.

## Appendix C — the two-tier Barbarius rule (pp. 78–79)

You will say, if a woman were by error elected Pontiff, would her acts be valid? **3** I respond in the negative, against Iasonem *in l. Barbarius, num. 64. ff. de offic. Prætor.* The reason is, because a woman is by Divine law incapable of the Pontifical dignity, and hence lacks jurisdiction, **nor can the Church supply this defect, because it cannot supply for a defect of Divine or natural law** [...] **But if some man be invalidly raised to the Pontificate with some defect voiding the election by human law only, his acts done with putative title and common error would be valid, because the Church would then be said to delegate jurisdiction to him and to supply**; for although the Church is not above the Pontiff, **it would nevertheless be above this man, who would be not a true but a presumed Pontiff** [...] who [with Alterius] **cannot infallibly define matters of faith, because the power of infallibly defining matters of faith belongs only to the true Pontiff and a General Council.**
