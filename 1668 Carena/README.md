*Note on this entry: Cesare Carena (c. 1597–1659) was not a speculative theologian but the* ***Fiscal Advocate and Consultor of the Holy Office*** *— the prosecutor of an inquisitorial tribunal — and his* Tractatus, *first printed at Cremona in 1636 and reissued through the century, was the standard working handbook "for all Bishops, Inquisitors, their ministers, and* ***confessors***," *its author "specially deputed by Our Most Holy Lord Urban VIII and the Most Eminent Universal Inquisition" (title page). The passage below names* ***Innocent X*** *(1644–1655) as the reigning pope, so it belongs to a revision made in those years; this text follows the Bologna 1668 printing. Its interest is that it gives the doctrine* ***at the level of practice***: *not what a theologian concludes, but what a confessor is directed to do. The context is § XVI, on hearing the confessions of schismatics. N. 70 distinguishes the censures — heresy requires error in the intellect together with its outward manifestation, schism only the external act, "even though there be no intention of withdrawing from obedience to the Pontiff as head of the Church." N. 72 supplies the* ***completeness clause in directive form***: *"when there exist two Supreme Pontiffs at the same time," if one election is established canonical and the other not, the confessor must "advise the penitent,* ***and command***, *that he acknowledge for true Pontiff the one who is established to have been canonically elected"; but "if the matter is doubtful, and the canonical election of neither is established," he must advise penitents "to hold themselves as members of the one Catholic Church, and to refer themselves to its Supreme head, Christ the Lord, and to acknowledge those Pontiffs* ***not as certain, but as doubtful***, *and to show themselves ready to acknowledge for true Pontiff the one whom the* ***General Council*** *shall have approved as such" — with the addition, from Naldus and Pueroni, that in such doubt obedience may be withheld from either "without danger of mortal sin or excommunication." Then n. 73, which is the strongest censure-verdict in this collection: "***Fourthly, the confessor must observe that one who should say that it is not of faith that this-in-number Pope — for example, Our Most Holy Lord INNOCENT X — is the true Pontiff, is not to be held for a Schismatic, but for a true HERETIC***," *as Suárez proved at length (de fide disp. 10 sect. 5 per totam) "by many Councils, Constitutions of the Supreme Pontiffs, and authorities of the Fathers." The ratio carries the acceptance-condition in its own words: "***For just as it is of faith that this-in-number is the visible Church of God, so also must it be of faith that her visible head — to whom she is subject and is bound to obey — is this visible man, held for Supreme Pontiff by the common consensus of the Church; and if this is of faith, then one asserting the contrary is a heretic***," *citing Turrianus (Selectae disputationes part. 1, disp. 35, dub. 5) "where [he notes] that* ***this is now more certain than it was before the times of Clement VIII***"; *then Bonacina at the exact locus of this collection's 1639 entry (de censuris in specie, tom. 3, disp. 1, quaest. 2, punct. 1,* ***n. 9***), *"seen after these things were written"; and "Father* ***Pueroni***, *seen most recently, ad Bullam Coenae cap. 1, num. 142" — the 1633 entry's author, whom Carena elsewhere on the same page calls "***my fellow-citizen***," *both being Cremonese. The verdict is thus a step beyond Bonacina, who held the denier a schismatic "in the opinion of all" and a heretic only probably, and the direct contradictory of Castro and Lensaeus (the 1534 and 1588 entries), for whom such a denier "must be called a schismatic, nevertheless he ought not to be reckoned a heretic." The entry's second value is what it shows about* ***Cum ex apostolatus officio***. *Carena cites Paul IV's bull twice, and only twice in the whole treatise: at printed 65 as the* ***third*** *reserved case, "concerning any Magistrates whatsoever, and Lords both Ecclesiastical and Temporal,* ***falling into schism***" *(the second case, on those denying the Trinity, Christ's divinity and the Virgin's virginity, belongs to Paul IV's other bull,* Cum quorundam hominum malitia); *and at n. 45 (printed 76), where the fautors and defenders of schismatics, though untouched by the Bull* Coenae, *"are bound by the excommunication of which in the Bull of Paul IV beginning* Cum ex Apostolatus officio." *A full-text sweep of the treatise for the bull's election-nullity material — a heretic's election void, privation or deposition for heresy, the "even if accepted by all with universal obedience" clause, papal election, conclave — returns* ***nothing***. *The Holy Office's own prosecutor, writing the manual by which inquisitors and confessors worked, knew the bull (he even notes at printed 105 that* ***Gregory XV's constitution of 30 August 1622 confirmed it*** *and extended it beyond Spain "to any parts of the Christian world"), used it exclusively for its* ***censures against rulers who fall into schism and their abettors***, *and in the same title told confessors that denying the reigning pope's title is heresy. Classification is* ***1a*** *(of faith that this-in-number man, held for Supreme Pontiff by the common consensus of the Church, is the true Pontiff; the denial censured as heresy).*

## § XVI, n. 73 — the censure-verdict (printed 78)

**73.** Fourthly, the confessor must observe **that one who should say that it is not of faith that this-in-number Pope — for example, Our Most Holy Lord INNOCENT X — is the true Pontiff, is not to be held for a Schismatic, but for a true HERETIC**, as *Suar.* most amply proved, *de fide disput. 10. sect. 5. per totam*, where he confirmed this by many Councils, Constitutions of the Supreme Pontiffs, and authorities of the Fathers. **For just as it is of faith that this-in-number is the visible Church of God, so also must it be of faith that her visible head — to whom she is subject and is bound to obey — is this visible man, held for Supreme Pontiff by the common consensus of the Church; and if this is of faith, then one asserting the contrary is a heretic**, well [says] *Turrian. in suis selectis disput. part. 1. disp. 35. dub. 5.*, where [he notes] that **this is now more certain than it was before the times of Clement VIII**; at length *Bonacin.*, seen after these things were written, *de censuris in specie tom. 3. eius operum disp. 1. quæst. 2. punct. 1. n. 9.*; and Father *Pueronus*, seen most recently, *ad Bullam Cœnæ cap. 1. num. 142.*

## § XVI, n. 72 — the two-claimant rule (printed 78)

**72.** Thirdly, when there exist two Supreme Pontiffs at the same time, if the canonical election of the one is established and the non-canonical election of the other, **the confessor must advise the penitent, and command, that he acknowledge for true Pontiff the one who is established to have been canonically elected**: But if the matter is doubtful, and the canonical election of neither is established, then the confessor must advise the penitents to hold themselves as members of the one Catholic Church, and to refer themselves to its Supreme head, Christ the Lord, and **to acknowledge those Pontiffs not as certain, but as doubtful**, and to show themselves ready to acknowledge for true Pontiff **the one whom the General Council shall have approved as such** [...]

## Titulus III, n. 45 — *Cum ex apostolatus* used for its censures (printed 76)

**45.** But this excommunication does not comprehend merely mental Schismatics [...] nor the fautors and d[efenders] of Schismatics [...] **although these are bound by the excommunication of which in the Bull of Paul IV beginning *Cum ex Apostolatus officio*,**

## Titulus II, § II — the third reserved case (printed 65)

The third case [...] as is had in the Constitution of Paul IV beginning ***Cum ex Apostolatus officio***, **concerning any Magistrates whatsoever, and Lords both Ecclesiastical and Temporal, falling into schism**, of which I have spoken in the title on schism.
