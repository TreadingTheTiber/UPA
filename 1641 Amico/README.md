- *Selection: Disputatio VI (on the remote proposer, the supreme Pontiff), Sectio V entire — "An possit esse fide Diuina certum, hunc numero Romanum Pontificem esse verum Petri successorem, & Christi in terris Vicarium?" — printed pp. 117–122, marginal nn. 147–184 (the printer's numeral anomalies are kept as printed: "257." for 157, and "181." printed twice, the second standing for 182).*

*Note on this entry: Francesco Amico, S.J. (1578–1651), of Cosenza (the title page's "Consentinus"), taught theology at Naples and Aquila and then at Graz, where his nine-volume* Cursus theologici *was gathered for the Douai press of the widow of Balthazar Bellère. Tomus IV (De Fide, 1641) reaches the singular pope inside the treatise on the* ***proposer*** *of the faith: Disputatio VI treats the remote proposer who is the supreme Pontiff, and its Sectio V asks the question in the classic singular form — whether it can be certain* ***by divine faith*** *that* ***this-in-number*** *Roman Pontiff is the true successor of Peter. He first gives the negative school five full arguments (nn. 147–151: Cajetan, Bañez, Alphonsus de Castro — the unknowable election-conditions, the absence of Scripture/tradition/definition for the singular, the non-heretical denier, the Joan legend, the possible incapax), then rules for the affirmative at n. 152: Valentia, Suárez (de fide disp. 10 sect. 5), "Albertinus" (the census locus of this collection's 1610 Francesco Albertini entry, cited to the very dubitatio), and Sylvius — "***Quæ verior est & sequenda***" ["which opinion is truer and to be followed"] — adding that "the greatest difficulty is in assigning an efficacious reason." Nn. 153–167 then test and reject his colleagues' reasons, before his own two. The* ***vera ratio*** *(n. 168): the universal — every Pontiff canonically raised to the Chair of Peter is Peter's true successor — is revealed; the singular minor — "this singular man,* ***for example Urban VIII, who now holds the helm of the Church***, *has been canonically raised" — is explicated by signs that render it morally certain; and the electors "promulgate and propose him* ***to the whole Church*** *to be adored as legitimate Pontiff": "***this moral certitude being in place, the faithful are bound by divine faith to accept him as the legitimate successor of Peter***" — the acceptance-obligation stated as the conclusion, with the reigning pope named per tempora (the collection's running motif: Torquemada's Nicholas V, the 1555 Albertini's Julius III, the 1610 Albertini's Clement VIII/Paul V margin, Diana's Innocent X, Murga's Innocent XI). The confirmation (n. 169) argues from parity with the legitimate council and answers the "merely private Cardinals" objection: the college "was instituted by the whole Church," so "the testimony of the Cardinals is* ***the testimony of the whole Church***, *whose place they bear" — and even a provincial council declared legitimate by the Pontiff "obliges that it be accepted by divine faith." N. 174 closes the first reason with the completeness clause: "it is required that his Canonical election* ***be publicly established, and that there be no doubt***." The second reason (n. 175) is the determinate-head argument: whoever believes by divine faith that* ***this*** *Church and* ***this*** *council (Trent) are legitimate cannot but believe that* ***this determinate head*** *is true Pontiff — as a determinate physical body includes its determinate physical head. N. 178 then draws the striking extension* ***against Valencia's exercise-condition***: *the singular is de fide "not only when he defines something... or establishes some law... or through a Jubilee dispenses the treasury of the Church, as Valentia teaches; but also* ***even before any juridical act proper to the Pontiff***, *provided that his Canonical election be morally certain and undoubted among the faithful." The replies (nn. 179–184) close with providence (n. 184): the infallible assistance is promised only to the legitimately elected, and God, "since He can always provide His Church with a legitimate Pastor... will* ***never permit that one incapable be elected to the Chair of Peter***" — the more connatural course, and "more congruent with the power and wisdom of God," than legitimating an intruder's acts after the fact. Classification is* ***1a*** *(fact-guarantee de fide divina of the singular, held prior to any papal act, with the acceptance of the whole Church carrying the electors' testimony and providence securing the election-premise).*

## Sectio V, n. 152 — the resolution (p. 118)

**152.** {marg. *Valentia, Suarez, Albertinus, Sylvius more truly affirm it.*} THE SECOND opinion, the affirmative, is that of Valentia [...], of Suarez [...], of Albertinus [...], of Sylvius [...], and of others. ***Which [opinion] is truer and to be followed.*** The greatest difficulty is in assigning an efficacious reason.

## Sectio V, n. 168 — the *vera ratio*, with the reigning pope named (pp. 120–121)

**168.** {marg. *The true reason.*} [...] But this proposition — Every Pontiff canonically raised to the Chair of Peter is the true successor of Peter, and Vicar of Christ — is revealed: and from it, by moral evidence, this other can be deduced: this singular man, **for example Urban VIII, who now holds the helm of the Church**, has been canonically raised to the Chair of Peter [...] Then, when the electors promulgate and propose him **to the whole Church** to be adored as the legitimate Pontiff and true Vicar of Christ [...] **this moral certitude being in place, the faithful are bound by divine faith to accept him as the legitimate successor of Peter.**

## Sectio V, n. 178 — before any juridical act (p. 122)

**178.** {marg. *Valentia.*} From what has been said it is deduced that it is established by Divine faith that this Roman Pontiff is the legitimate successor of Peter, and the true vicar of Christ, not only when he either defines something to be believed by Divine faith, or establishes some law, obliging the whole Church, concerning things necessary to salvation, or when through a Jubilee he exercises the juridical act of dispensing the treasury of the Church, as Valentia teaches; **but also even before any juridical act proper to the Pontiff, provided that his Canonical election be morally certain and undoubted among the faithful.**

## Sectio V, n. 184 — the providence reply (p. 122)

**184.** [...] But God, since He can always provide His Church with a legitimate Pastor, it must rather be said that **He will never permit that one incapable be elected to the Chair of Peter**, than that, this being permitted, He will legitimate all his acts: since the former is more connatural, and more congruent with the power and wisdom of God [...]
