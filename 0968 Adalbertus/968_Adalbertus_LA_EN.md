| **CONTINUATIO REGINONIS. A. 965.** | | **CONTINUATION OF REGINO. A.D. 965.** |
| DCCCCLXV. imperator Papie natale Domini celebravit et peracta festivitate statim in patriam dispositis in Italia regni negotiis commeavit. Cui filii sui Otto rex et Willihelmus archiepiscopus in confinio Franciae et Alamanniae in villa Heimbodesheim[^1] occurrerunt et cum magna ibi eum alacritate susceperunt. Inde Wormatiam progressus fratrem suum Brun archiepiscopum in purificatione sanctae Mariae sibi obvium habuit; sicque totam quadragesimam in Francia commorans in Inglinheim pascha cum magno gaudio celebravit. Indeque navigio Coloniam attingens matrem suam domnam Mahtildam et sororem suam Gerbirgam reginam filiumque eius regem Lotharium sibi obvios condigno ibi amore et honore tractavit; sicque in Saxoniam iter direxit. Benedictum etiam illum depositum ab urbe Romana secum in Franciam advexit, quem Adaldago archiepiscopo custodiendum commisit. Duas quoque filias Berengarii in palatio cum domna imperatrice decenti sibi honore detinuit. | | 965. The emperor celebrated the Nativity of the Lord at Pavia, and when the festivities were completed, having arranged the affairs of the kingdom in Italy, he immediately returned to his homeland. His sons King Otto and Archbishop William met him at the border of Francia and Alemannia in the town of Heimbodesheim[^1] and received him there with great joy. Proceeding thence to Worms, he had his brother Bruno the archbishop come to meet him at the Purification of Saint Mary; and thus remaining throughout all of Lent in Francia, he celebrated Easter with great rejoicing at Ingelheim. And proceeding thence by ship to Cologne, he received his mother Lady Matilda and his sister Gerberga the queen and her son King Lothair, who came to meet him, with fitting love and honor; and thus he directed his journey into Saxony. He also brought with him into Francia that Benedict who had been deposed from the city of Rome, whom he committed to Archbishop Adaldag for safekeeping. He also kept at his palace, with the Lady Empress, the two daughters of Berengar, treating them with due honor. |
| Eodem anno quidam ex Langobardis more solito ab imperatore deficiunt et Adalbertum in Italiam reducunt. Tunc imperator Burchardum ducem Alamannorum in Italiam misit; qui ad congrediendum ei, ubicumque inveniretur, cum Langobardis imperatoris fidelibus et Alamannis iusum per Padum navigavit et illis, ubi eum audierat esse, partibus navim applicuit. Quos in prima fluminis egressione Adalbertus insiluit; ubi et frater eius Wido cum aliis quam (Iun. 25.) pluribus occubuit. | | In the same year, certain of the Lombards, according to their custom, defected from the emperor and brought Adalbert back into Italy. Then the emperor sent Burchard, Duke of the Alemanni, into Italy; who, in order to engage him wherever he might be found, sailed down the Po with the Lombards loyal to the emperor and with the Alemanni. And he brought his ship to those regions where he had heard that Adalbert was. Upon their first disembarkation from the river, Adalbert attacked them; and there his brother Wido, along with very many others, fell (June 25). |
| Ipse vero Adalbertus vix fuga lapsus evasit et montana quaedam, ubi imperatorem lateret, intravit. Hac igitur dux laetus victoria in patriam repedavit et, quae sibi acciderant, imperatori significavit. Interim Wido Mutinensis episcopus vulpina calliditate imperatori se simulans fidelem ipsique infideles se proditurum iactitans legatione Adalberti fungens in Saxonia imperatorem aggreditur; nec tamen visu aut allocutione ipsius participatur, sed cum dedecore redire permissus infra Alpes ultra Curiam comprehenditur et in Saxoniam remissus in Sclavis custodiae mancipatur. (Aug. 26.) Uodo Strazburgensis episcopus obiit; cui Erchanboldus successit. | | But Adalbert himself barely escaped by flight and entered certain mountainous regions where he might hide from the emperor. Therefore the duke, rejoicing in this victory, returned to his homeland and reported to the emperor what had befallen him. Meanwhile Wido, Bishop of Modena, with vulpine cunning feigning himself faithful to the emperor and boasting that he would betray those who were disloyal to him, acting as Adalbert's legate, approached the emperor in Saxony; yet he was not granted the privilege of seeing or speaking with him, but being permitted to return in disgrace, he was apprehended on the far side of the Alps beyond Chur and sent back to Saxony to be committed to custody among the Slavs. (August 26) Uodo, Bishop of Strasbourg, died; Erchanbold succeeded him. |
| Eodem etiam anno domnus Leo papa obiit. Tunc legati Romanorum, Azo videlicet protoscriniarius et Marinus Sutriensis ecclesiae episcopus, imperatorem pro instituendo, quem vellet, Romano pontifice in Saxonia adeuntes honorifice suscipiuntur et remittuntur. Et Otgerus Spirensis episcopus et Liuzo Cremonensis episcopus[^2] cum eisdem Romam ab imperatore diriguntur. Tunc ab omni plebe Romana Iohannes, Narniensis ecclesiae episcopus, eligitur et sedi (Oct. 1.) apostolicae pontifex intronizatur. Qui statim maiores Romanorum elatiore animo, quam oporteret, insequitur, quos (Dec. 16.) in brevi inimicissimos et infestos patitur. Nam ab urbis prefecto[^3] et quodam Rotfredo[^4] comprehenditur et urbe expulsus in Campania custodiae mancipatur. | | In that same year, Lord Pope Leo died. Then the legates of the Romans, namely Azo the protoscrinarius and Marinus, bishop of the church of Sutri, traveling to Saxony to seek the emperor *for the purpose of appointing* whomever he might wish as Roman Pontiff, were received honorably and sent back. And Otgerus, bishop of Speyer, and Liuzo, bishop of Cremona[^2] were sent to Rome by the emperor along with them. Then John, bishop of the church of Narni, was elected by all the Roman people and enthroned as pontiff on the (Oct. 1.) apostolic see. He immediately pursued the leading men of the Romans with a more haughty spirit than was fitting, and these he (Dec. 16.) soon found to be most hostile and dangerous enemies. For he was seized by the prefect of the city[^3] and a certain Rotfredo[^4] and, expelled from the city, was consigned to custody in Campania. |
| Hoc demum anno imperatore ab Italia redeunte, Heinrico archiepiscopo Treverensi Thiedericus eiusdem ecclesiae diaconus et Adalberoni Metensi episcopo[^5] Thiedericus consobrinus imperatoris[^6] successores instituuntur. (Mart. 5.) Brun quoque archiepiscopus, germanus imperatoris, vir (Oct. 11.) ducatu pariter et episcopatu dignissimus, V. Idus Octobris obiit; cui Folcmarus in episcopatu successit. Eodem anno Gero marchionum nostri temporis optimus (Mai. 20.) et precipuus obiit. Lotharius[^7] rex domnam Hemmam[^8] sibi coniugio copulavit. | | Finally, in this year, as the emperor was returning from Italy, Thiedericus, deacon of the same church, was appointed successor to Henry, archbishop of Trier, and Thiedericus, a cousin of the emperor,[^6] was appointed successor to Adalbero, bishop of Metz.[^5] (Mart. 5.) Also Bruno the archbishop, brother of the emperor, a man (Oct. 11.) most worthy of both the duchy and the episcopate, died on the 5th of the Ides of October; Folcmarus succeeded him in the episcopate. In the same year, Gero, the best (Mai. 20.) and foremost of the margraves of our time, died. Lothair[^7] the king united in marriage to himself Lady Hemma.[^8] |
