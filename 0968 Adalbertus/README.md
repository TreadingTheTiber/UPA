## A.D. 965: The Election of Pope John XIII

[...]

In that same year, Lord Pope Leo died. Then the legates of the Romans, namely Azo the protoscrinarius and Marinus, bishop of the church of Sutri, traveling to Saxony to seek the emperor *for the purpose of appointing* whomever he might wish as Roman Pontiff, were received honorably and sent back. And Otgerus, bishop of Speyer, and Liuzo, bishop of Cremona were sent to Rome by the emperor along with them. **Then John, bishop of the church of Narni, was elected by all the Roman people and enthroned as pontiff on the apostolic see.** He immediately pursued the leading men of the Romans with a more haughty spirit than was fitting, and these he soon found to be most hostile and dangerous enemies. For he was seized by the prefect of the city and a certain Rotfredo and, expelled from the city, was consigned to custody in Campania.

[...]

*Note: This contemporary notice — "Tunc ab omni plebe Romana Iohannes, Narniensis ecclesiae episcopus, eligitur et sedi apostolicae pontifex intronizatur" — is the same acceptance-idiom the Formosan dossier employs (see the 0914 Auxilius entry: "the entire world celebrated his renowned name"), and it is cited as such by Franciscus Wasner (see the 1936 Wasner entry), who groups Marinus, Formosus, and John XIII as elections "always justified by the fact of a common and universal election by the people."*
