The transcription follows the Lyon 1651 printing (Universi cursus theologici tomus V, "nunc primum in Galliis prodit," Laurent Anisson & Soc.); the linked Internet Archive copy is the Antwerp 1649 Plantin-Moretus printing of the same tomus, whose pagination differs. Printer's anomalies kept as printed and documented (e.g. "STatura" for "Statutâ"; the running heads of printed 107/109 lag one section behind the "SECTIO VIII"/"SECTIO IX" headings).*

*Note on this entry: Rodrigo de Arriaga, S.J. (1592–1667), of Logroño ("Lucroniensis Hispanus"), taught at Valladolid and Salamanca before his long career at Prague, where the title page styles him sometime professor and now* ***chancellor of the imperial university***. *His de fide volume reaches the singular pope in Disputatio VII on the Church and its Head, whose Sectio VIII asks — naming the reigning pope in the question itself — "whether it is of Faith that* ***this individual — Innocent X, for example*** *— is the true Pope and successor of Peter" (n. 49; the elected man appears as "Cardinal Pamphili," and later by full name, "Ioan. Bapt. Pamphilium, seu Innocentium X."). N. 49 maps three schools: the theological-conclusion-only school; Tanner's affirmative; and a* ***media sententia*** *holding it de Fide only "that he who has been received by the Church* ***validly exercises all things****, as if he were true Pontiff — yet* ***not*** *of Faith that he* ***is*** *the true Pontiff," resting on Tanner's conditional "Even if the Pontiff were not in very fact the true Pontiff, whatever he defines will truly be of Faith,* ***God supplying such a defect for the good of the Church***" — the collection's fact-or-effect disjunction (see the 1632 Turnbull entry) stated as an actual seventeenth-century school, effect-horn only. Arriaga rejects it as involving contradiction: valid exercise of all the offices* ***is*** *the papacy, "for the Pontiff is not properly constituted such by the manner in which he receives the power, but by the power which he has." Then n. 50: "***the common opinion is more pleasing, which says that it is altogether of Faith that he who without contradiction and universally has been received by the Church as true Pontiff is the true Pope and successor of Peter***" — the acceptance-clause inside the thesis itself — proved six ways: (1)* ***Martin V's bull at Constance*** *(the returning heretic must believe the canonically elected pope for the time being, "his name being expressed," to be Peter's successor) applied in terms to "the modern pope, e.g.* ***Innocent X, his name being expressed***" — the Constance credendum per-tempora, as in Torquemada and the 1555 Albertini; (2) councils have determinately recognized the popes of their day; (3) if it were not of Faith that* ***Pius IV*** *was true pope, "neither will it be established that the* ***Council of Trent*** *is valid, since the Council receives its whole force from the Pope. And that is nearly heretical"; (4) the Church is known by its head — though Arriaga candidly demurs ("Leuis probatio": the true Church is also known sede vacante, "even if the interregnum should last two, three, or more years"); (5) otherwise the Pontiff's definitions and* ***canonizations*** *would not be of Faith; (6) the Judge-of-Controversies would be "a Judge with a quasi-vague existence": "faith only teaches me that I am bound to approach the Judge of Controversies; but it does not tell me that he is Ioan. Bapt. Pamphili, or Innocent X" — reducing the de Fide judge to a practical absurdity. The mechanism (nn. 52–53): God reveals "in actu exercito — by* ***not permitting that anyone be proposed to the whole Church indubitably as true Pope except him who really is such***"; and against the valid-election answer Arriaga presses the occult-conditions objection (baptism of elect and electors, simony) and concludes: "***It is better answered that this truth, or the valid election, is established for us by the fact that the Cardinals, and the Bishops, and the Clergy, and almost the whole body of the Church accept that election without contradiction***" — acceptance expressly replacing election as the epistemic ground. N. 54 works the Great Schism: once the election of* ***Urban VI*** *was "promulgated and accepted through the world," the Electors "***cannot revoke it, even all of them together***" (the margin: "In Urban VI* ***the heretics lie that the true series of Pontiffs failed***"); the distant faithful may lack sufficient proposition "and will have to await fuller information"; and the completeness clause is built into the conclusion: the contradiction that suspends the de Fide status is "not of some few who may be discontented... but notable, of prudent men, and resting on apparent foundations — in a word,* ***probable***" — only then "was it not of Faith who was the true Pope" among Gregory XII, Benedict XIII, and John XXIII. N. 55 adds the sanatio coda: those invincibly deceived under a schism afterwards unite to the true Pontiff and seek confirmation of the graces received from the apparent one, and popes "found afterwards indubitably and without contradiction" validate or invalidate what was done, "so that in the Church every reason for doubting may cease — as did* ***Martin V***." N. 56 supplies the proposition-by-report application from Arriaga's own desk: "we here* ***at Prague***, for example, cannot know what was done at Rome in the election except from letters and report" — when it is written so indubitably that we are prudently bound to believe in a human way, "then we shall also be bound to believe* ***by divine Faith*** *that this individual is Pope." And the closing dubium presses the sanction with a pedigree argument: he who denies a pope "universally and without contradiction received... as* ***now Innocent X***" "will assuredly be a* ***heretic***: otherwise in the same way one could without heresy deny his predecessor* ***Urban VIII***, and* ***Gregory XV***, and so of the others — in a word,* ***of all up to Peter exclusively***: whereby that principle certain of Faith would be destroyed, that there have been in the Church successors of Peter, Vicars of Christ, visible Heads of the Church." Classification is* ***1a*** *(fact-guarantee de Fide of the universally received singular, acceptance-side in the Suárez line he expressly defends, with the effect-only media sententia examined and rejected).*

## Sectio VIII, n. 50 — the thesis (p. 107)

**50.** {marg. *The common opinion is pleasing.*} **The common opinion is therefore more pleasing, which says that it is altogether of Faith that he who without contradiction and universally has been received by the Church as true Pontiff is the true Pope and successor of Peter**: which Suárez defends in that sect. 5, and Tanner. {marg. *It is proved First:*} And it is easily urged, First, because in the Bull of Martin V at the Council of Constance it is disposed that heretics returning to the Church believe that the canonically elected Pope for the time being (**his name being expressed**) is the successor of Peter, etc.: therefore the Council supposes that now, determinately, concerning the modern pope — e.g. **Innocent X, his name being expressed** — it must be believed that he is Peter's successor. [...]

## Sectio VIII, n. 53 — acceptance, not bare election, as the ground (p. 108)

**53.** {marg. *Through whom God reveals this man to be the true Pontiff.*} [...] This reason however does not satisfy; for the whole difficulty will press upon the election itself: for whence shall we know that it is a true and valid election, especially since many things are required for its validity **which are purely occult**? — namely, that the elect be baptized; that the Electors be baptized; that the election be not simoniacal, etc. **It is better answered that this truth, or the valid election, is established for us by the fact that the Cardinals, and the Bishops, and the Clergy, and almost the whole body of the Church accept that election without contradiction.** [...]

## Sectio VIII, n. 54 — Urban VI and the completeness clause (p. 108)

**54.** [...] the revelation was already made through the first manifestation and acceptance of the election: **and once made and promulgated, the Electors cannot revoke it, even all of them together**; whence in that case he without doubt remains true Pontiff. [...] {marg. *In Urban VI the heretics lie that the series of Pontiffs failed.*} [...] for in the conclusion it is laid down that he must be proposed and accepted **without probable contradiction** in order that his election be of Faith. And I understand a contradiction **not of some few who may be discontented** — for these do not take away the certitude of the election — **but a notable one, of prudent men, and resting on apparent foundations: in a word, probable** [...]

## Sectio VIII, closing dubium — denying the received pope is heresy (p. 108)

{marg. *Doubt.*} [...] but if, when a Pope has been universally and without contradiction received as true Pontiff, **as now Innocent X**, someone should deny that he is true Pope, **he will assuredly be a heretic**: otherwise in the same way one could without heresy deny also his predecessor **Urban VIII**, and **Gregory XV**, and so of the others — in a word, **of all up to Peter exclusively: whereby that principle certain of Faith would be destroyed, that there have been in the Church successors of Peter, Vicars of Christ, visible Heads of the Church**, etc.
