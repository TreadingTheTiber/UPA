Quale schisma fuisse memorię proditum est per quadraginta circiter annos, cùm se tres pro Pontificibus gererent, quod in Concilio Constanciensi Sigismundo Romanorũ rege, tandem aliquãdo finitum est. Tametsi enim fide credere teneamur legitimum Petri successorem supremũ esse totius Ecclesiæ in terris caput, nõ tamen eadem fide recipere tenemur Leonem aut Clemẽtem vel Gregorium verũ esse Petri successorem: neque enim ad fidẽ catholicã pertinet quàm quisque Pontifex rectè aut canonicè eligatur aut electus sit: quo fit vt qui obedientiã Clementi aut Leoni denegaret, hoc solũ prætextu quòd eũ diceret nõ canonicè fuisse electũ, quãuis schismaticus dicẽdus esset, nõ tamẽ hęreticus censeri deberet. Alius enim est error in his quæ sunt facti, alius in his quę sunt iuris atq̃ doctrinę. | | […] So also on other occasions it has come about that a schism was born for this reason alone, that the Pontiff who was held [to be such] was thought not to have been rightly and lawfully elected. Such a schism has been handed down to memory as having lasted about forty years, when three men bore themselves as Pontiffs, which at long last was brought to an end in the Council of Constance, Sigismund being king of the Romans. There were therefore hereupon many schismatics, and yet not heretics. For who would dare to fasten the charge of heresy upon any one of those who called themselves pontiffs, or upon those who obeyed them — a charge which the general Council laid against none of them? For although we are bound to believe by faith that the legitimate successor of Peter is the supreme head of the whole Church on earth, **we are nevertheless not bound by the same faith to receive Leo or Clement or Gregory as being the true successor of Peter**: for it does not pertain to Catholic faith how any Pontiff is rightly or canonically elected, or has been elected: whence it comes about that one who should deny obedience to Clement or Leo, on this pretext alone, that he says he was not canonically elected — although he must be called a schismatic, nevertheless he ought not to be reckoned a heretic. For error in things which are of fact is one thing, error in things which are of right and of doctrine another. |

| Ad similem modum, si Ecclesia diceretur definiendo quippiam errasse atq̃ defecisse, hic defectus ratione partis, hoc est, Ecclesiastici magistratus accidisse deberet intelligi. neq̃ enim omnium est definire de his quæ fidei sunt, sed eorũ id munus, qui Ecclesię pręsunt: quos si decernendo labi contigerit, iure dicitur Ecclesia errasse atq̃ à veri tramite defecisse. nam cœcum hominem aut non videntem dicimus, non quia in pedibus visum non habet, sed quia non habet in oculis. Sin ad fidei absolutum defectum respicitur, non definitionis erratum, istóque sensu dicitur Ecclesia defecisse: id manifestum est ex toto non ex parte sola dijudicandum relinqui. sic enim Ecclesia defecerit, cùm nullus remanserit cœtus, vera præditus fide: possetque hoc modo non defecisse Ecclesia etiamsi priore defecisset. Neque enim totam mox communitatem necesse esset vera fide vacuam inueniri, propterea quòd eos qui præsunt definiendo falli contigisset. Ideóq̃ accidit interdum vt particularis Ecclesia, verbi gratia Carthaginensis, in eo cui Cyprianus præfuit concilio, iuxta priorem modum deficeret, non item necessariò iuxta posteriorem. Ecclesiæ verò Dei vniuersali, cælesti benignitate certissimisque promissis prouisum est, | | In like manner, if the Church were said to have erred in defining something and to have failed, this failure would have to be understood as having happened in respect of a part, that is, of the ecclesiastical magistracy. For it does not belong to all to define concerning those things which are of faith, but that charge is theirs who preside over the Church: and if it should befall these to slip in decreeing, the Church is rightly said to have erred and to have fallen away from the path of the true. For we call a man blind, or not seeing, not because he has no sight in his feet, but because he has none in his eyes. But if regard is had to an absolute failure of faith, and not to an error of definition, and in that sense the Church is said to have failed: it is manifest that this is left to be judged from the whole and not from a part alone. For in this way the Church will have failed when no assembly shall have remained endowed with the true faith; and in this manner the Church could have not failed, even if she had failed in the former way. For it would not be necessary that the whole community should straightway be found void of the true faith, for the reason that it had befallen those who preside to be deceived in defining. And therefore it happens at times that a particular Church — for example that of Carthage, in that council over which Cyprian presided — should fail according to the former mode, and not likewise of necessity according to the latter. But for the universal Church of God it has been provided, by heavenly kindness and by most certain promises, |
| vt neutro modo deficeret vsque ad seculi consummationem. Quia reuera si definiendo per errorem deficeret, in iis qui præsunt: facilè pronaq̃ via sensim error in totum Ecclesiæ corpus grassaretur. | | that she should fail in neither mode, even to the consummation of the world. Because in truth, if she were to fail by error in defining, in those who preside, easily and by a ready path the error would gradually spread into the whole body of the Church. |

But this pastoral authority again, and this consent in handing down doctrine, can be regarded in two ways: once in the whole body itself of pastors and doctors who, throughout the Christian world, at diverse times and places, have flourished in the praise of doctrine and have left to posterity some monuments of their faith and erudition; once in the congregation and assembly of pastors coming together at one and the same time into one place, either for dispelling the mists of errors which new men spread abroad not without the ruin of the flock, or for the correction of morals and the restoration of collapsed discipline. |

| Nam quod addunt, *iudicium de doctrina ad omnes spirituales Dei filios pertinere: ideoq̃, authoritatem Ecclesiæ ex ea quidem parte, non ad solos eius pastores reuocandam, neque in iis solis constituendam esse*: in eo, hoc errore labuntur, quod iudicandi de doctrina munus atque officium, à pastoratu diuellunt: vt cùm fateri non audeant omnes spirituales Dei filios, esse Ecclesiæ pastores & doctores; ne perturbent ordinem quem Apostolus insinuat, dicẽs; Nunquid omnes Doctores? {marg. *1. Cor. 12.*} tamen omnibus spiritualibus iudicãdi authoritatem asscribant. Atqui eorum propriè est de doctrina iudicandi munus, quorum est doctrinã subditis sibi populis tradere docendóq̃ pascere: vt si nõ omnes sint pastores & doctores, sequatur illud quoq̃, ex officio & munere propriáq̃ functione nõ omnes esse de doctrina iudices. | | For as to what they add, *that judgment concerning doctrine pertains to all the spiritual sons of God: and therefore that the authority of the Church, on that side at least, is not to be referred back to her pastors alone, nor to be established in them alone*: in this they fall into this error, that they tear the charge and office of judging concerning doctrine away from the pastorate: so that, although they dare not confess that all the spiritual sons of God are pastors and doctors of the Church — lest they disturb the order which the Apostle intimates, saying, Are all doctors? {marg. *1. Cor. 12.*} — they nevertheless ascribe to all the spiritual the authority of judging. But the charge of judging concerning doctrine belongs properly to those whose office it is to hand down doctrine to the peoples subject to them and to feed them by teaching: so that, if not all are pastors and doctors, this also follows, that by office and charge and their proper function not all are judges of doctrine. |
