*Note on this entry: not St. Vincent Ferrer (†1419), but a later Dominican of the same religious name — a son of San Esteban at Salamanca who, as the title page records, was for fifteen years regent of studies at the Roman College of St. Thomas (the Minerva). The question is posed of the reigning pope by name — "**whether it is to be held de fide that Clement IX, who now reigns, is the true successor of Peter in the papacy**" — which dates the composition to 1667–69. Ferré answers **1a**, but by an argument that is his own and that no other entry in this collection reproduces: the Church's universal peaceful acceptance is **equivalently or virtually a definition** that this man is pope. Three features are worth the reader's attention. (1) The **animate-rule argument** (n. 31): a definition cannot be a true definition unless it issues from a true pope "as from the living rule of faith," exactly as Scripture's authority presupposes believing the books canonical — the inanimate rule; so whoever must believe the definitions de fide must believe de fide that he defines. (2) The **acceptance-as-definition** doctrine (n. 33 and the reply that follows): the Church proposes this man to me as the rule of faith — awaiting his definitions, seeking his canonizations, asking his indulgences — and cannot err in a matter pertaining to faith; the acceptance therefore functions "as through a definition," from which the right election and the legitimacy of the electors are then inferred as theological consequences, not presupposed. (3) The **providential disjunction** (n. 34), which states both schools of this repository's taxonomy in a single sentence and refuses to rest on the weaker one alone. Ferré also answers the acephalous-Church objection (an accepting body without a head cannot define) by distinguishing acts that presuppose the head from acts constitutive of him, citing Constance.*

## §. VII. — *Utrum sit de fide tenendum, quod Clemens IX, qui modò regnat, sit verus Petri in Papatu successor*

**30.** We suppose that that is properly to be held *de fide* to which faith gives immediate assent by force of divine revelation alone, immediately proposed to us by the Church. [...] We inquire therefore whether, concerning **this Clement IX, who is agreed to have been duly elected pope on account of the common acceptance of the whole Church**, it is to be believed by divine faith that he is the true pope and the true successor of Peter in the papacy; or whether this is only a theological conclusion, deduced from one premise *de fide*, namely from this: *every person duly elected and accepted as pope is the supreme Pontiff*, and another acquired by the natural light, namely, *Clement IX has been duly elected pope*. [...] The first [opinion] is that it in no way pertains to faith [...] For this opinion there are cited here by Suárez many Thomists among the ancients, and others not Thomists, namely **Castro, Albertinus, Corduba**, and others [...]

**31.** **I say therefore: it is immediately to be held *de fide* that Clement IX, who now reigns, is the true pope and the legitimate successor of Peter.** It is proved first: for it is immediately to be held *de fide* that the definitions, if any be issued by Clement IX, are true definitions [...]; therefore it will be to be held *de fide* that he is the true Pope. [...] **the aforesaid definitions cannot be true definitions unless they arise from a true pope as from the living rule of faith** [...] This is explained: the things which are set forth in the sacred letters, **which are the inanimate rules of faith**, are not believed *de fide* immediately, except after it is believed that the aforesaid sacred letters are canonical [...]; **therefore likewise we shall not be able to believe that the definitions made by Clement are to be believed *de fide* immediately, unless by the same faith we immediately believe that he himself is the true pope.**

[...]

**33.** The principal conclusion is proved secondly: **the Church here and now proposes to me Clement IX as the rule of faith, for from him it awaits definitions, it desires the rule of morals, it seeks the canonizations of saints, it asks for indulgences, and it looks for solutions of doubts in matters of faith; but the Church cannot err in a matter pertaining to faith; therefore by force of this proposal it is rendered *de fide* that he is the supreme pontiff.** [...]

[...] And so, **since the peaceful acceptance itself of this man elected as true pope is equivalently or virtually a definition about this, that he is pope**, before that acceptance it cannot be established *de fide* that this man is duly elected [...]; **but through the acceptance itself, as through a definition, it is declared that he who is peacefully accepted as pope is true pope, and then by theological consequences it is inferred that he is duly elected and that the electors were legitimate**, and the other things which are desired for a right election.

**34.** You will say that, since from the defect that can be committed in a definition there would follow a common error, by this very fact that the Pope defines God supplies all his defects [...] §. But on the contrary: **from the peaceful acceptance also, by which the Church accepts this man in particular as supreme pontiff, there follows a common error, if he who is accepted has not been duly elected; for the whole Church would be deceived in holding as the living rule of faith one who in reality was not such; therefore likewise it will have to be said either that God will not allow one to be peacefully accepted by the Church as pope who has not been duly elected, or that, if He allow him to be peacefully accepted as pope, He will supply the defects committed in his election, and thus the faith by which we believe him to be pope will not suffer shipwreck.**
