*Note on this entry: a Portuguese canonist's book-length refutation of **Ludovico Antonio Muratori** — the author of this repository's 1714 entry, and the leading name of School 2. Moraes writes at Lisbon in 1748, a doctor of canon law formed at Coimbra, in a work licensed by the Portuguese Inquisition and dedicated to the Prince of Brazil; the "Flashes" are aimed at Muratori's* De ingeniorum moderatione in religionis negotio *(published under the pseudonym "Lamindus Pritanius"), the same work the 1714 entry excerpts. This first Ray is therefore the fullest **direct, contemporary rebuttal of the School 2 position** in the collection, and it is printed here entire — all 160 numbered paragraphs, with no elisions — since the argument against Muratori runs continuously through it. Moraes holds **1a**: the singular ("this man is the true Pontiff") is of divine faith immediately, the universal entering the act of faith; he censures its denial in the strongest terms in the tradition (n. 15); and he reads* Cum ex apostolatus *by the same route as the repository's Passerinus (1670) and Kirsch (1767) entries, but with his own argument from contradiction with Martin V, Lateran, Florence and Alexander III (nn. 18–19).*

## Coruscatio V — Radius Primus: *Aliquas doctrinas, quas Muratorius proponit in praedicto libro, omnino destruit*

[...]

**5.** And in the first place, at *lib. 1. cap. 18. pag. 147.* he undertakes to prove this, namely, that this proposition does not pertain to the faith: *This or that man is the true Roman Pontiff* — a question stirred up by more recent Theologians. And in this he is himself already deceived; for in that opinion **St. Augustine** seems to have been [...]

[...]

**12.** **That the Church receives this infallibility from her true head the Roman Pontiff is beyond doubt certain.** For although a very few Doctors, as Duvallius says, doubt of this truth; there is nevertheless none who denies this infallibility of the Church, **provided she adhere to her legitimate head**: from which it follows that she receives it from the head itself, beyond controversy. {marg. Tract. de Roman. Pontif. potest. p. 4. q. 7.}

[...]

**15.** Moreover, we ought to believe that Christ promised His Church that He would not fail her by His peculiar providence [...] But since from certain principles of certain faith conclusions of faith are deduced, **it follows infallibly that it pertains to the faith that this or that Pontiff is a true Pontiff: otherwise He would not rightly provide for His Church in so grave a matter; since He would permit her, deceived perhaps for many years, to remain joined to an illegitimate spouse, a false Teacher to be reputed a true one, one to be believed His Vicar who in truth was not His** [...] — and therefore the contrary proposition **"is temerarious, scandalous, schismatic, ill-sounding, and suspect of heresy, and he who utters it is to be punished as a heretic, as Bañez and Castro Palao teach."**

[...]

**18.** What seems to be of greater weight, and in which Muratori most trusts [...] is **Constitution 19 of Paul IV** [*Cum ex apostolatus officio*], in which the Pontiff says: *If at any time it shall appear that anyone, before his assumption to the Roman Pontificate, had deviated from the Catholic faith... the promotion, or assumption, made of him, even in concord, and even with the unanimous consent of the Cardinals, shall be null, invalid, and void; nor can it be said to have gained force... through the enthronization of the same Roman Pontiff, or his adoration, or through the obedience rendered to him by all, and the course of whatever length of time in the premises.*

**19.** Then, to the Constitution of Paul IV **we oppose the Constitution of Martin V** [...] in which the heretics there in question are not otherwise reconciled to the Church, unless they have professed that they believe the present Pontiff to be the true Pontiff. Therefore Martin *judges the Church immune from that error in this affair.* **We oppose the Lateran Council, the Synod of Florence, Alexander III, and the others cited above**, defining that all duly elected according to the form there established are to be held as true Pontiffs. Therefore all these *judge the Church immune from that error.* What then? Shall we say that Paul IV stood contrary to all these, or defined contrary things? Far be it. Paul IV, therefore, must be explained. **Certainly he in no way defined that the whole Church can err in the acceptance of a Pope, as Muratori wishes to deduce; but he was wholly occupied in setting forth the form which, being observed, the election would beyond doubt turn out certain** [...]

**20.** *Wherever two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I* [...] The Cardinal Electors are gathered in the name of the Lord for a most arduous business, on which the whole government, security, and doctrine of the Church and of the faithful depends. **Shall the Spirit of the Lord fail in a business so arduous and grave, directing their wills, kindling their hearts, and illuminating their minds? Let heretics think so, let Muratori judge it possible. For us Catholics to doubt it would assuredly be the greatest sacrilege.**

**21.** It is the concordant opinion of all Theologians and Canonists, that **the Pontiff duly elected receives confirmation, power, and jurisdiction over the universal Church not from men, but immediately from God.** Either, then, Christ the Lord bestows this supreme power upon the Pontiff duly and canonically elected, or He does not? **If He does not bestow it, He deceives the Church, and permits her to be governed by one who is not a true Shepherd; which to affirm, or to think, of His infallible providence, would be temerarious, scandalous, erroneous, nay even heretical.** But if He does bestow it, as is certain, **how will it not be to be believed of faith, that he is the true Pontiff, to whom God commits His own place over the universal Church** [...]
