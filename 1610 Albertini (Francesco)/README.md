*Note on this entry: Francesco Albertini, S.J. (1552–1619), of Catanzaro, taught theology at Naples; his* Corollaria *(t. 1, 1610) proceed by philosophical principles, each generating theological corollaries. The third principle is the syllogism rule — "the conclusion follows the weaker part" — and this quaestio asks whether, contrary to that very rule, a theological conclusion explicated through a* ***natural*** *premise can be certain with divine faith. His resolution makes the pope's identity the paradigm case, at nn. 10–13, and settles a census knot in the process: Suárez cites him ("Albertinus, tom. 1, coroll. 3 Theologico, ex tertio principio philosophico, quæst. 3, a num. 10") on the* ***affirmative*** *side with Salmerón and Valencia, in the same disputation where he cites Arnaldo Albertini among the negative-side authors — two different men, both now in this collection (see the 1555 entry). Three passages carry the doctrine. First, the thesis, with the reigning pope named in the text and updated in the margin: "that* ***this Clement VIII*** *is the true supreme Pontiff, duly and canonically elected,* ***is of divine faith, especially when he proposes something to be believed***" — margin: "***Clement VIII — or, as it now is, Paul V*** *— is the true and legitimate Pontiff, and the Council of Trent legitimate: in what way this is of divine faith" (composed under Clement VIII, printed 1610 under Paul V with both names standing — the doctrine's per-tempora application visible on the page, as in Torquemada's Nicholas V, the 1555 Albertini's Julius III, Diana's Innocent X, Murga's "but I, as now, Innocent XI"). Second, the mechanism that reconciles the de fide conclusion with the naturally fallible premise: the canonical election and Trent's legitimate congregation rest on "preceding human faith, which of its nature can admit falsehood;* ***but it pertains to divine providence not to permit that falsehood underlie it, but to concur by a certain peculiar help, so that the human faith concerning the canonical election and the sufficient proposal may not deceive the Church; for otherwise we could even doubt the articles of faith***" — with the margin rubric stating it as an obligation: "***Deus tenetur assistere peculiari cōcursu, ne falsum subsit veri Pontificis electioni*** *[God is bound to assist with a peculiar concursus, lest falsehood underlie the election of the true Pontiff]." Third, the* ***proposal*** *doctrine (n. 13): that this council is legitimately congregated "is proposed by the Pontiff himself;* ***but that this man is the supreme Pontiff is proposed by the Church, and also by the Pontiff himself, who is the head, who even proposes himself as true Pontiff, especially when he defines something of faith***" — followed by the express restriction that "this reasoning, so explicated,* ***does not seem to have place in other Theological conclusions*** *explained through a premise that is only morally certain": the pope-singular is unique among theological conclusions precisely because the Church immediately proposes it to all the faithful. This makes Albertini the collection's* ***earliest proposal-side author*** *— before Castro Palao (1631) and Ferré (1669) — and the only one with the* ***self-proposal*** *argument (the pope, as head, proposing his own title in the act of defining); cf. the proposal memo. He is the "Albertinus" of Verricelli's n. 7 chain-citation (1653 entry) and of Suárez's affirmative list (1583 entry). Classification is* ***1a*** *(de fide divina of the singular, with the exercise-accent "praesertim cùm aliquid proponit credendum" recalling Gregory of Valencia's 2→1a, and providence securing the human premise).*
