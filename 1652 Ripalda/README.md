The Vivès text carries printing faults, all kept and documented in sic-notes (one clause of n. 71 appears corrupt and is flagged for collation against a seventeenth-century edition).*

*Note on this entry: Juan Martínez de Ripalda, S.J. (1594–1648), of Pamplona, professor primarius at Salamanca and censor of the Supreme Inquisition (so the title page styles him), left his* De virtutibus theologicis *to be published after his death. His treatment of the pope-singular is the most explicitly* ***graded*** *account in this collection of how acceptance changes what the believer can do. The frame (Sect. IV, nn. 65–67) is the conditional-revelation analysis: universal revelations about subjects whose qualifying condition cannot be verified in the singular are* ***conditionatae ex parte objecti***; *but "when the condition necessary for the revealed predicate to belong to the object exists absolutely, such a predicate is absolutely credible by faith in the singular subject" — the principle* ***Mazzella adopts by name*** *(the 1879 entry: "the absolute truth is altogether indistinct from the conditioned"). Sectio V then asks what evidence of the singular's subsumption suffices, and builds a* ***ladder***. *N. 70, Assero primo: moral evidence of the grade "which excludes not only doubt but even prudent misgiving" suffices* ***for absolute divine faith of singulars under a common revelation*** *— with Lugo, Pedro Hurtado (disp. XI), Suárez, and Bellarmine in the margin, and the collection's most quotable analogy: "***if God revealed that all the cities of France were to be destroyed within a year, we could believe by divine faith that Paris is to be destroyed, though we know only by moral evidence that it is a French city***." Nn. 71–77 grade the lower rungs (Trent sess. VI c. 9 on the state of grace answered by the intrinsic/extrinsic certitude distinction; merely probable subsumption yields an assent honest and prudent but not obligatory). N. 78: on probable arguments one can believe "by a faith elicited from the infused habit, though* ***imperfect and infirm***, *that this host contains Christ, that this infant is justified, that this man has been stripped of the habit of grace, and that that man is among the blessed." Then the pope-singular climbs both rungs (n. 79): even "***though his legitimate election be doubted***," this man can be believed true Pope by that imperfect faith, if probable and prudent arguments exist — "***because God revealed that every man legitimately elected, and accepted by the Church as vicar of Christ and successor of Peter, is the true Pontiff of the Church and vicar of Christ***" — the acceptance-clause standing inside the revealed universal itself. "***But if there can be no doubt, nor prudent misgiving, concerning the legitimate election, then, with the common consensus of the whole Church, the singular man can be believed to be true Pope and vicar of Christ with a faith even most firm — and not only private, but also public and catholic*** *— because this singular subject's being contained under the common revelation is proposed as evidently credible by moral evidence banishing prudent misgiving." Two conditions jointly lift the singular to the top grade: election beyond prudent misgiving,* ***and the common consensus of the whole Church*** *— acceptance as the evidence-completer. N. 80 closes with a census-in-miniature that cross-confirms this collection's own identifications: "So think* ***Suarez*** *(disp. v sect. 8 n. 12; disp. x sect. 5),* ***Hurtadus disp. xxvii per totam*** *[Pedro Hurtado, the 1631 entry],* ***Lugo*** *(disp. i sect. 13 § 5), with* ***Salmerone, Valentia, Albertino*** *[the 1610 Francesco Albertini entry] and other writers of our Society —* ***against Turrecrematum, Cajetanum, Bannez, Canum, Vegam, Cordubam, Castrum*** *[the negative school: the 1453, 1517, 1586, 1563, 1569, 1534 entries] and others cited by them." His idiom "***fide... non solum privata, sed etiam publica et catholica***" *is the same formula as Esparza's "de fide publica & Catholica" (the 1666 entry) — in both authors it grades* ***divine*** *faith under the Church's public proposition, not a distinct lesser habit; the later fides-ecclesiastica school (1b-ii) inherited the vocabulary and re-graded it, and Mazzella (1879) builds his immediate-de-fide case expressly on Ripalda. Classification is* ***1a*** *(fides divina absoluta, firmissima, publica et catholica of the singular in the Suárez line, with the graded ladder showing precisely what the acceptance adds: it converts a non-obligatory, infirm assent into the firmest public faith).*

## Sectio V, n. 70 — the first assertion, with the Paris example (pp. 153–154)

**70.** I assert first: **the aforesaid moral evidence suffices for absolute faith of singulars under a common revelation** — so Lugo, disputation I, num. 313, and Hurtadus, disputation XI, section 3 § 25, with Suárez, Bellarmine and others. First, because **if God revealed that all the cities of France were to be destroyed within a year, we could believe by divine faith that Paris is to be destroyed, though we know only by moral evidence that it is a French city.** [...]

## Sectio V, n. 79 — the ladder applied to the pope, and the crown text (pp. 155–156)

**79.** Finally, you will find that it can be believed by imperfect and infirm faith that this man is the true Pope and Vicar of Christ, **even though his legitimate election be doubted**, if there are probable and prudent arguments for the legitimate election — **because God revealed that every man legitimately elected, and accepted by the Church as vicar of Christ and successor of Peter, is the true Pontiff of the Church and vicar of Christ. But if there can be no doubt, nor prudent misgiving, concerning the legitimate election, then, with the common consensus of the whole Church, the singular man can be believed to be true Pope and vicar of Christ with a faith even most firm — and not only private, but also public and catholic** — because this singular subject's being contained under the common revelation is proposed as evidently credible by moral evidence banishing prudent misgiving.

## Sectio V, n. 80 — the roll-call (p. 156)

**80.** So think Suarez, disp. v, sect. 8, num. xii, and disp. x, sect. 5; Hurtadus, disp. xxvii, per totam; and Lugo, disp. i, sect. 13, § 5, **with Salmeron, Valentia, Albertino and other writers of our Society — against Turrecrematum, Cajetanum, Bannez, Canum, Vegam, Cordubam, Castrum and others cited by them.** [...]
