- *Selection: Controversia VII, "De Successione Primatus S. Petri" (printed pp. 393–413) — the heading and the status quaestionis (n. I, printed 393), and the closing run nn. XLVII–L (printed 412–413), ending where Controversia VIII begins. The intervening sections are a catalogue of some sixty scriptural, patristic and conciliar testimonies to the perpetuity of Peter's primacy. Printed anomalies kept and documented, including a compositor's omission at n. L ("…in re tam graui. **re** autem per Ecclesiam propositâ" for "**De re** autem…"), verified at high magnification.*

*Note on this entry: Adrian (1609–1669) and Peter (1610–1675) van Walenburch, brothers of Rotterdam, both auxiliary bishops of Cologne, wrote their controversial works jointly. They are named in* ***Diana's affirmative census*** *— "Adrian and Peter van Walenburch,* in examine princip. fidei p. 2. contr. 7. n. 50" *— and Murga copies the citation (the 1655 and 1684 entries). That reference is exact, and this entry documents it:* ***Controversia VII closes at n. L***, *immediately before Controversia VIII, precisely as cited. The location matters, because the passage exists* ***only*** *in this standalone octavo: the brothers' great folios,* Tractatus generales de controversiis fidei *(Cologne 1669–70), reprint the* Examen *material reorganized into "Examina" against particular adversaries, and the singular-pope argument does not survive the reorganization — a sweep of all 2,388 folio pages finds no tract on papal election and no such passage; nor does the shorter 1664 recension of the* Examen *contain it (the word* primatus *does not occur in it once). The controversia asks whether Peter's primacy "passed to his successor — that is, whether his power was given by Christ so as to remain* ***perpetually in the Church by way of ordinary jurisdiction***" (n. I), answers it through the testimonies, and then closes with two sections of doctrine. N. XLIX, a* ***Notatio*** *on papal election, states the distinction the modern controversy turns on: "the* ***designation of the person*** *who is appointed to succeed the predecessor is* ***not determined by Christ, but left to the Church***… *but the* ***collation of the power is from Christ***, *who by one act of will granted it to Peter and to all his legitimate successors, that is, to those* ***legitimately designated***: *which will being posited, by a certain hypothetical necessity the dignity is communicated to the one duly elected" (Suárez cited in the margin) — so "the collation of power made to St. Peter perseveres, just as his dignity perseveres morally the same, though in different succeeding persons," sealed with a Roman-law analogy: "there is* ***one proconsulate***, *though successively there be many proconsuls: for so also* ***one primacy*** *was given to St. Peter,* ***which remains one***, *though successively many hold the primacy." Then n. L, the census locus: "***Next, we believe by faith that this man, Innocent X, is the true Pontiff***: *for Christ revealed immediately that St. Peter and his successors are truly Pontiffs.* ***And this revealed truth — that Innocent X is the true Pontiff — is sufficiently proposed to us when it is morally established that he has been accepted by the whole Church, and peacefully possesses his primacy: for the universal Church cannot err in a matter so grave***. *[But] concerning a matter proposed by the Church, there is in this* ***no greater difficulty than when the Church proposes that these books are Canonical***. *But if anyone should wish to maintain that this is not to be believed by faith, but that moral certitude suffices:* ***we are not contentious***" (citing Jansenius, Spongia cap. 44). Three things deserve notice. First, the* ***formula is Suárez's own***: "acceptatum à totâ Ecclesiâ, & pacificè possidere suum primatum" is the clause Bonacina introduces expressly as "vt ait Suarez num. 6" (the 1639 entry) — both authors are quoting* De fide *disp. 10 sect. 5 n. 6, so the parallel is common dependence, not borrowing. Second, the* ***canon-of-Scripture analogy*** *ties the papal-identity case to the rule-of-faith question the rest of the* Examen *is about: the Church proposing* which man is pope *is no harder than the Church proposing* which books are canonical *— the same argument Turnbull's Baron would attack from the Protestant side (the 1632 entry). Third, the* ***"non sumus contentiosi"*** *is the most irenic statement of the doctrine in the collection: they hold the fact de fide and decline to quarrel with those who hold only moral certitude — a School-1 author expressly refusing to anathematize School 2. The reigning-pope naming (Innocent X) places them in the per-tempora chain with Torquemada's Nicholas V, the 1555 Albertini's Julius III, the 1610 Albertini's Clement VIII/Paul V, Amico's Urban VIII, Arriaga's and Esparza's and Bonacina's Innocent X, Ripalda's contemporaries, and Murga's Innocent XI. Classification is* ***1a*** *(fide credimus of the singular: the revealed universal, sufficiently proposed through the whole Church's acceptance and peaceful possession, guaranteed by the universal Church's inability to err in so grave a matter).*

## Controversia VII, n. I — the state of the question (printed 393)

**CONTROVERSIA VII.** *On the Succession of the Primacy of St. Peter.*

**I.** It is asked. {marg. *The state of the question.*} **Whether the Primacy of St. Peter passed to his successor? — that is, whether his power was given by Christ so as to remain perpetually in the Church by way of ordinary jurisdiction.**

## Controversia VII, n. XLIX — the Notatio on papal election (printed 412–413)

**NOTE.** **XLIX.** In the election of the Pontiff it is to be observed that **the designation of the person who is appointed to succeed the predecessor is not determined by Christ, but left to the Church** — and therefore now this, now that mode has been observed in the Church. **But the collation of the power is from Christ, who by one act of will granted it to Peter and to all his legitimate successors, that is, to those legitimately designated: which will being posited, by a certain hypothetical necessity the dignity is communicated to the one duly elected**; and thus the collation of power made to St. Peter perseveres, just as his dignity perseveres morally the same, though in different succeeding persons. {marg. *b Suar. de tripl. virt. Theol. disp. 10. sect. 4. n. 3.*} [...] where it is said **that there is one proconsulate, though successively there be many proconsuls: for so also one primacy was given to St. Peter, which remains one, though successively many hold the primacy.**

## Controversia VII, n. L — the census locus (printed 413)

**L.** **Next, we believe by faith that this man, Innocent X, is the true Pontiff**: for Christ revealed immediately that St. Peter and his successors are truly Pontiffs. **And this revealed truth — that Innocent X is the true Pontiff — is sufficiently proposed to us when it is morally established that he has been accepted by the whole Church, and peacefully possesses his primacy: for the universal Church cannot err in a matter so grave.** [But] concerning a matter proposed by the Church [the print reads simply *re*; see the sic-notes], **there is in this no greater difficulty than when the Church proposes that these books are Canonical.** But if anyone should wish to maintain that this is not to be believed by faith, but that moral certitude suffices: **we are not contentious.** See Jansenius, *Spongiam. cap. 44. pag. 169.*
