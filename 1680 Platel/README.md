The volume is a duodecimo with printed* ***marginal summaries*** *in the outer margin, fourteen of which fall in the selection and are transcribed. The linked Google Books copy is a Cologne 1688 printing of Pars III; the transcription follows the Cologne 1700 reprint held here, whose approbations are dated Douai 1680 and note that the impression was largely made before Innocent XI's condemnation of the sixty-five propositions in 1679.*

*Note on this entry: Jacques Platel, born at Bersée in 1608, taught theology at Douai for his whole career and served as rector of the university; his* Synopsis *appeared in five parts at Douai between 1678 and 1683 and was reprinted through the next generation. Pars III is dated by its approbations to 1680 and names* ***Innocent XI*** *as reigning. The section entered here is the affirmative thesis in its* ***strongest late-century form***: *not only is it of faith that the reigning pope is Peter's successor, but the universal acceptance is itself* ***"an exercised and virtual revelation of God,"*** *so that it reaches even the divine-law essentials of the pontificate — and a man may be true Pope though his conclave election was, through a hidden defect, no election at all. Classification is* ***1a***.*

***The thesis and its four proofs (§§ 153–154).*** *"***It is of faith that any Roman Pontiff in particular, legitimately elected and received as such by the universal Church, is the true Pontiff, the successor of Peter and the Vicar of Christ***" — Suárez, Vázquez, Valencia, Tanner, Lugo, "and others most commonly." The proofs: (1) Martin V's Constance decree, that suspects of heresy be asked whether they believe "the canonically elected Pope, whoever he shall be for the time being,* ***his name being expressed***, *is the successor of St Peter"; (2)* ***indefectibility*** in credendo — *"this object,* Innoc. XI est verus Pontifex, *is contained in the universal propositions revealed by God," namely that the Church "which is the pillar of truth" cannot be universally deceived in what pertains to her common religion, "and that this Roman Pontiff now reigning is the living rule of faith is something pertaining to the religion of the whole Church, nay to the very foundation of faith,* ***so that it would be more pernicious for the Church to err in this than in other things to be believed***": *hence "in the general infallibility of the universal Church promised and revealed by God is contained* ***infallibility in believing, or in acknowledging this determinate man, Innocent XI, to be the living rule of faith***"; (3) Albertini's argument — the universal "*omnis Pontifex Romanus legitime electus est verus Petri successor*" is revealed "***precisely for the sake of this contingent particular***… *since it is revealed for no other end than that one may without doubt have recourse to this man as to the rule of faith; for it would plainly help nothing to know vaguely and indeterminately that there is some judge of controversies in the Church, if who he is could not be certainly determined*"; (4) that the visible Church over which this Pontiff presides must be believed to be simply the true Church, "which does not stand without this true and legitimate Head."*

***The objection that decides the axis, and two replies (§ 155).*** *"You will say: it is not of faith that this man is legitimately elected, but it is only certain* ***with a great moral certitude***." *This is Neesen's objection of 1678 and the point on which Sendín, in the same decade, draws his line. Platel gives two answers.* ***R. 1: moral certitude suffices***, *because being legitimately elected "is only an argument, or* ***motive of credibility***, *by which the revelation of this man's pontificate is previously proposed" — the pontificate consisting not in the election but "in having universal jurisdiction over the whole Church, delivered by Christ to Peter and his successors"; and motives of credibility "do not require greater certitude than a great moral one." He adds that the legitimate election is "a* conditio sine qua non, *and with which, by force of the present providence, the pontificate infallibly is," so that from the revealed major and a morally certain minor the conclusion "can be believed by divine faith."* ***R. 2, from Arriaga***: *"***the universal acceptance itself, by which this man in particular is admitted by the whole Church without any contradiction as legitimately elected and as true Pontiff, is an exercised and virtual revelation of God***; for by the very fact that God, according to His promises, directs the Church, lest in accepting this man as legitimate Pontiff she err…" — with the printed margin stating it as a thesis: "*Acceptatio Ecclesiae hanc agnoscentis ut verum Pontificem est virtualis revelatio ejus Pontificatus, sicut virilis [sexus], Baptismi & similium Pontificatui essentialium.*" The consequence follows at once: "***Hence a man can be the true Pontiff, even though on account of a hidden defect his election was not legitimate — if, namely, he has been accepted by the whole Church as true Pontiff; in which case the acceptance itself is a virtual election, and a revelation of the true pontificate.***"*

***The divine-law essentials (§ 156).*** *"You will say: it is not of faith that Innocent XI is a man, is baptized, and the like things essential to the pontificate." Platel denies the assumption: "I say these are* ***implicitly revealed (and consequently of faith) by the very fact that his legitimate pontificate is revealed through the universal acceptance of the Church***." *This is the exact point [Sendín](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1699%20Sendin) denies — "nor does the acceptance infer anything beyond a moral certitude" as to whether the electors were baptized — and the exact point on which [Ojea](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1661%20Ojea) had already said that God "will never permit" a divine-law defect "in this second election." Platel goes furthest of the three: the essentials are not merely prevented but* ***revealed***. *He adds a characteristic aside: the same holds of the Pontiff's priesthood "if it is essential to the pontificate, which Arriaga rightly doubts; because the whole jurisdiction in which the pontificate consists is independent of order, since a Pontiff legitimately elected before his ordination is for some time true Pontiff without orders."*

***What acceptance does to definitions — a clean anti-3a sentence (§ 152).*** *"The acceptance by which the universal Church admits the propositions defined by the Pontiff as to be believed by divine faith* ***confirms them indeed infallibly, yet does not give them their first infallibility***, *which they have from the assistance promised by Christ to the Roman Pontiff as head of the Church, distinct from her other members" — the margin: "*Acceptatio Ecclesiae decretis Pontificiis infallibilitatem non dat sed infallibiliter confirmat.*" Reception adds "an infallible confirmation, or a new infallibility founded in the special promise made to the Church," but the definition was infallible before it. And the* ***completeness clause*** *is given in its* maior pars *form: "here by the universal Church is to be understood* ***by far its greatest and principal part***; *for morally the whole Church is reckoned to acquiesce in a Pontifical definition even though some bishops are unwilling, or hedge at subscribing" — as the four bishops did at Nicaea, "alleging that the formula… contained a question of fact, of Arius's doctrine and its sense."*

***The negative school reported — and reported as School 2 (§ 157).*** *After disposing of Pope Joan as "a fable most mendaciously fabricated" (Maimbourg's* Schism of the Greeks, *1677, traces it to a jest about John VIII's weakness toward Photius), Platel names the other side: "Torquemata, Cajetan, Cano, Báñez, Castro, Vega, Córdoba hold that it is not of faith that this man in particular is the true Pontiff, but only that it is certain with a great moral certitude, beneath which falsehood cannot morally lie, so that consequently it cannot prudently be doubted." Then the sentence that matters for how the negative school is to be read: "***Most of them, however, hold that all things which he proposes to the whole Church as to be believed are infallibly true; because by the very fact that he is commonly held legitimate, even if through a hidden defect he is illegitimate, he has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, lest the whole Church be bound by his decrees to err***, which is repugnant to the infallibility promised her by God." A contemporary of the tradition reporting that the negatives generally held the* ***effect-guarantee*** *— which is precisely how this collection has read [Neesen](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1678%20Neesen), and why he is filed as School 2.*

***Loss of office (§ 158).*** *A Pontiff is deprived of the pontificate (1) by voluntary abdication, as Celestine V, "even without the consent of the Cardinals"; (2) "also unwillingly, by* ***external notorious heresy***, *and indeed* ***more probably* ipso facto*, without any declaration of the Church*** *by those whose office it is to elect a Pontiff, which is required when the heresy is not notorious" (*cap. Si Papa*, dist. 40) — "***He cannot be deposed for other crimes***, as Cajetan, Suárez and the Scholastics more commonly teach, against Panormitanus and various canonists"; and (3) "on account of insanity prudently judged perpetual,* ***and a reasonable doubt concerning his election***" — the contested case, here stated as a ground of loss rather than as an exemption from the guarantee. § 151 should also be noted: the due-counsel condition is affirmed, but with the Granado answer rather than Neesen's — "the divine providence never has permitted and never will permit that he proceed rashly in defining; for by the very fact that God promised him special assistance to infallibility in defining, He also promised assistance to a prudent and diligent deliberation."*

***Where he sits.*** *With Sendín, Ojea and Neesen, Platel fixes the late-century map of what the acceptance certifies. Neesen: the facts are uncertain, so the conclusion is not of faith. Sendín: the facts are morally certain only, but the conclusion is of faith, the acceptance being a previous condition applying the revelation. Ojea: the acceptance is a second election, in which God prevents divine-law defects. Platel: the acceptance is a virtual revelation, so that the facts themselves — man, baptized — are of faith. The doctrine Platel attributes to Arriaga is the one this collection holds from him at n. 54 of the [1651 Arriaga](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Arriaga) entry ("the revelation has already been made through the first manifestation and acceptance of the election"), though the locus Platel and Sendín cite — the* De fide *prologue, disp. 1 sect. 7 subsect. 3 — is not one this collection holds.*

## § 153 — the thesis, and its second proof: infallibility *in credendo* (printed 106–107)

**153** **It is of faith that any Roman Pontiff in particular, legitimately elected and received as such by the universal Church, is the true Pontiff, the successor of Peter and the Vicar of Christ** — so Suárez, Vázquez, Valencia, Tanner, Lugo, and others most commonly. It is proved, 1, from the decree of Martin [V] in the Council of Constance, in which it is commanded that those suspected of heresy be asked whether they believe that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being, **his name being expressed**, is the successor of St Peter, having supreme authority in the Church of God. 2. Because this object, *Innocent XI is the true Pontiff*, is contained in the universal propositions revealed by God [...] by which it is revealed that the Church, which *is the pillar of truth* (1 Tim. 3), cannot be universally deceived in things pertaining to the common religion of the whole Church, or in believing some error; but that this Roman Pontiff now reigning is the living rule of faith is something pertaining to the religion of the whole Church, nay to the very foundation of faith, **so that it would accordingly be more pernicious for the Church to err in this than in other things to be believed**. Therefore in the general infallibility of the universal Church promised and revealed by God is contained **infallibility in believing, or in acknowledging this determinate man, Innocent XI, to be the living rule of faith**.

## § 154 — the universal is revealed for the sake of the particular (printed 107)

3. Because when a universal proposition is revealed **for the sake of the contingent particular**, of which no greater certitude can be had that it is contained in that universal than a moral one, that suffices for believing it by divine faith [...] but that universal proposition, *every Roman Pontiff legitimately elected is the true successor of Peter and Vicar of Christ*, is revealed to the Church [...] and indeed precisely for the sake of this contingent one: *this man, who is held by the Church as legitimately elected, is the true successor of Peter* — since it is revealed for no other end than **that one may without doubt have recourse to this man as to the rule of faith**; for otherwise it would plainly help nothing to know vaguely and indeterminately that there is in the Church some judge of controversies, if who he is could not be certainly determined.

## § 155 — moral certitude suffices; the acceptance is a virtual revelation (printed 108)

**155** You will say, 1: it is not of faith that this man is legitimately elected, but it is only certain **with a great moral certitude**. I answer, 1, that this suffices; because being legitimately elected is only an argument, or **motive of credibility**, by which the revelation of this man's pontificate is previously proposed — which pontificate does not consist in being legitimately elected, but in having universal jurisdiction over the whole Church, delivered by Christ to Peter and his successors. [...] I answer, 2, from Arriaga: **the universal acceptance itself, by which this man in particular is admitted by the whole Church without any contradiction as legitimately elected and as true Pontiff, is an exercised and virtual revelation of God**; for by the very fact that God, according to His promises, directs the Church lest in accepting this man as legitimate Pontiff she err [...]

## § 156 — a true Pope despite an invalid election; the essentials revealed (printed 108–109)

**Hence a man can be the true Pontiff, even though on account of a hidden defect his election was not legitimate — if, namely, he has been accepted by the whole Church as true Pontiff; in which case the acceptance itself is a virtual election, and a revelation of the true pontificate.** You will say, 2: it is not of faith that Innocent XI is **a man**, is **baptized**, and the like things essential to the pontificate. Therefore, etc. I deny the assumption, and I say that these are **implicitly revealed (and consequently of faith) by the very fact that his legitimate pontificate is revealed through the universal acceptance of the Church**.

## § 152 — acceptance confirms definitions but does not give them their infallibility; the *maior pars* (printed 105–106)

**152** The acceptance by which the universal Church admits the propositions defined by the Pontiff as to be believed by divine faith **confirms them indeed infallibly, yet does not give them their first infallibility**, which they have from the assistance promised by Christ to the Roman Pontiff as head of the Church, distinct from her other members [...] Here, however, by the universal Church is to be understood **by far its greatest and principal part**: for morally the whole Church is reckoned to acquiesce in a Pontifical definition even though some bishops are unwilling, or hedge at subscribing [...]

## § 157 — the negative school, and what most of them nevertheless held (printed 109)

[...] On account of these and other objections of this kind Torquemada, Cajetan, Cano, Báñez, Castro, Vega, Córdoba hold that it is not of faith that this man in particular is the true Pontiff, but only that it is certain with a great moral certitude, beneath which falsehood cannot morally lie, so that consequently it cannot prudently be doubted. **Most of them, however, hold that all things which he proposes to the whole Church as to be believed are infallibly true; because by the very fact that he is commonly held legitimate, even if through a hidden defect he is illegitimate, he has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, lest the whole Church be bound by his decrees to err**, which is repugnant to the infallibility promised her by God.

## § 158 — loss of the pontificate (printed 109–110)

The Pontiff can be deprived of the pontificate, 1, by voluntary abdication, as St Peter Celestine, and that even without the consent of the Cardinals. 2. He is deprived also unwillingly by **external notorious heresy**, and indeed **more probably *ipso facto*, without any declaration of the Church** by those whose office it is to elect a Pontiff — which is required when the heresy is not notorious. It is clear from *cap. SI PAPA dist. 40*. **He cannot be deposed for other crimes**, as Cajetan, Suárez and the other Scholastics more commonly teach, against Panormitanus and various canonists. 3. On account of insanity which is prudently judged perpetual, **and a reasonable doubt concerning his election**.
