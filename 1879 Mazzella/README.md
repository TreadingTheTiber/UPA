*Note on this entry: Mazzella — professor and prefect of studies at the Gregorian, created cardinal in 1886 — states the question in its sharpest late-nineteenth-century form, and against a named contemporary opponent. He distinguishes throughout between a proposition's being of faith **quoad nos** (can we be certain enough of it?) and **quoad se** (is it revealed?), answers the first with de Lugo's grades of moral certitude, and then, on the second, sets **Schrader and "as it seems" Cardinal Franzelin** — the ecclesiastical-faith / mediately-divine camp, i.e. this repository's 1b-ii — against **Suárez, de Lugo, Valentia, Bellarmine, Salmerón, Ripalda, and Viva**, whose immediate-de-fide position he adopts. The mechanism is the "complex subject" analysis of nn. 437–439: the revealed universal ("all legitimate successors of St. Peter have authority over the universal Church") is revealed **absolutely**, not conditionally, whenever the verification of its condition can be known with certainty without a new revelation — Ripalda's principle that in that case "the absolute truth is altogether indistinct from the conditioned." Note also n. 431, which supplies the acceptance-condition in Mazzella's own terms: the question proceeds only of an **undoubted** pontiff, received as such by the Church; while the election is doubted on grounds, the singular is not of faith quoad nos. The excerpt begins mid-§ II (with the tail of the Christ's-risibility discussion of implicit revelation) because § III applies the principles established there; the elision is marked [...] at the head.*

## §. III. — Ex statutis principiis peculiares quædam quæstiones illustrantur

[...]

**425.** Theologians here commonly are wont to investigate whether it be of faith — 1) that this infant (baptized) is justified, — 2) that this individual host is consecrated — 3) **that this Pope (Leo XIII) has authority over the universal Church**, or that this Council (e.g. Trent) is legitimate, and hence has authority over the universal Church. [...] Now a twofold question must here be distinguished: 1st, whether it be of faith *quoad nos* that this infant is justified etc. — 2nd, whether it be of faith *quoad se*.

[...]

**431.** But what is to be said of the third proposed question [...]? For its solution, they note *first*, commonly, that **the question proceeds concerning an undoubted Pontiff, and one received as such by the Church: for as long as there is doubt with foundation concerning the truth of the election, it is not of faith *quoad nos* that this man is the true Pope** — See de Lugo.

**432.** But then all teach *secondly*, that **it is certain with a certitude worthy of the name, excluding every doubt and every prudent fear, that this undoubted Pope has authority over the universal Church** [...]

1) Because among the thirty articles of John Huss condemned by the Council of Constance and Martin V, the eleventh is: "It is not necessary to believe that this man, whoever he is, who is the Roman Pontiff, is the head of any particular holy Church, unless God have predestined him." — [...] and the twenty-fourth [interrogatory of *Inter cunctas*]: "**whether he believes that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being, *his name being expressed*, is the successor of blessed Peter, having supreme authority in the Church of God**"

2) Because, says de Lugo: **just as we must believe by faith the things which the Pontiff, received by the universal Church, defines and proposes, so we must believe without misgiving that he is the true Pontiff**: for either we can prudently have misgiving about this, or we cannot: if we can, then we cannot believe with divine faith the things which he defines [...]

3) It is evident, says the same de Lugo, that the Church cannot be deceived by believing some error universally, since she is "**the Pillar and ground of truth**" (1 Tim. 3:15), **in which infallibility of the universal Church it seems no less to be contained that the Church cannot err in recognizing the true visible rule of her faith, than that she cannot err in the other things to be believed through faith**: for an error concerning the very rule of truth and of faith would harm the Church more than one concerning other particular objects, since it would be an error in the very foundation of faith. Since, therefore, the visible rule which the Church follows in her faith, and altogether ought to follow, is her visible head, namely the supreme Pontiff, whose doctrine and definitions she must embrace, **the Church cannot be deceived by accepting as Pontiff and rule of faith one who was not truly Pontiff.**

[...]

**436.** In this matter, then, there are two opinions. The *first*, which, besides the Authors cited by Suarez, **Schrader** also follows among the more recent writers, **and, as it seems, Card. Franzelin**, teaches that the proposition in question is a *theological* truth, or is to be held by *Ecclesiastical faith*, or by faith *mediately divine*; but denies that it is of divine faith immediately *quoad se*. [...] But the *second* opinion, **which Suarez, de Lugo, Valentia, Bellarmine, Salmeron, Ripalda, Viva, etc. hold, affirms it**. For they say that **by the very fact that the universal proposition "all Pontiffs legitimately elected have authority over the universal Church" has been revealed, there has been revealed the particular proposition, formally and implicitly contained in it, "this Pontiff, legitimately elected, has authority over the universal Church."** Nor does it stand in the way that it is not revealed that this man has been legitimately elected; **for it suffices, they say, that this be *certainly* established** [...] "We say," says de Lugo, "that it has been revealed by God, by a universal revelation, that this individual man, namely our most holy Lord Urban VIII, is the supreme Pontiff:" whence he adds "that it can also be believed *immediately as of faith*."

[...]

**438.** [...] Now **the rule to which one must certainly attend**, in order to discern this, is "**whether the verification of the condition can, without a new revelation, be known with certainty, with that certitude with which the revelation must be foreknown in order that we may believe with divine faith.**" [...] But the same cannot be said [...] of the proposition "all legitimate Successors of St. Peter have authority over the universal Church:" and therefore **nothing prevents our saying that it is *absolutely* revealed** [...]

**439.** Ripalda confirms the matter somewhat differently, saying: **there can be *absolute* faith of the object (this Pontiff has authority over the universal Church), when it has been sufficiently established that the condition exists absolutely, and therefore that the conditioned truth exists as absolute. For the absolute truth is altogether indistinct from the conditioned, and the absolute cannot be denied without the conditioned being denied.** [...]
