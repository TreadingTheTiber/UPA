*Note on this entry: Sebastián Salelles, S.J. (Oliva, in the Kingdom of Valencia, 8 October 1574 – Malta, 8 January 1666) taught theology at the Jesuit college of Barcelona before sailing to Malta in 1604, where he spent the remaining sixty-two years of his life as professor of moral theology and* ***Consultor of the Inquisition of Malta***; *the patents of 1634 and 1646 reprinted in the front matter count him twenty-eight and then forty-two years in the office. The* De materiis tribunalium *is the summa of that career, three volumes (Rome 1651–1656), the first dedicated to* ***Fabio Chigi***, *Inquisitor of Malta 1634–1639 and, four years after this volume appeared, Alexander VII. It is written for the working consultor: Book I sets out the rules of faith and their objects as the tribunal's frame of reference, and Regula LXV is the rule on the Pope, placed after Scripture, tradition, the Church and general councils. Its authorities are Castro Palao, whom he follows "in almost so many words," Suárez, Valencia and Basilio Ponce.*

***The papal question as the capstone of the rule (n. 277).*** *Salelles raises the singular not as a separate question but "to indicate more fully the infallibility of the rule of belief in the person of the Supreme Pontiff": "that weighty doubt … whether it is of faith that this man, who for the time being governs the Church of God, is the true Pope? Concerning which there are two opposed opinions of the Doctors." His answer: "I have always been pleased by* ***the resolution under a distinction***, *which* ***thirty-five years ago I defended publicly in the church of the College of Barcelona****, in the theological theses proposed by me, as is customary every year for the honour of the College, with the Rev. Fr. Vincenzo Sigala,* ***then nuncio extraordinary of the Most Holy Clement VIII to the Catholic King Philip III, arguing against****, for the fuller elucidation of the truth: namely,* ***that it is of faith that this man is the true Supreme Pontiff, when he has been received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church, and so approved that all are bound to obey him, above all in receiving definitions of faith and ordinances concerning the morals of the whole Church; but otherwise before that****: which is clear, because in time of schisms it is almost always an altogether uncertain matter who is head of the Church, and whom one must then obey."*

***The dating, and what it adds to the history of the dispute.*** *Clement VIII reigned to March 1605 and Philip III from 1598; Salelles left Spain for Malta in 1604. The Barcelona disputation therefore falls* ***between 1598 and 1604***, *and the passage, written "thirty-five years" later, in the mid-1630s. That places a public Jesuit defence of the acceptance-conditioned affirmative, against a papal nuncio arguing the negative "for the fuller elucidation of the truth," within a few years of the [Alcalá theses of 1601–02](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1601%20Alcala%20Theses) — the same years in which, on [Oviedo's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Francis%20of%20Oviedo) report, the question was fought out at Alcalá and settled in the affirmative "from the time of Clement VIII." Barcelona and Alcalá were arguing the same thesis in the same pontificate. The distinction Salelles defended is the census's completeness clause in its early form: "received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church," with the schism case expressly excepted because there "it is almost always an altogether uncertain matter who is head."*

***What the acceptance is (n. 277).*** *Not bare recognition but* ***approval unto obedience***: *"so approved that all are bound to obey him, above all in receiving definitions of faith and ordinances concerning the morals of the whole Church." The pope received is the pope whose definitions the whole Church is bound to receive, and the reason the singular is of faith is the reason the rule is infallible: it would be no living rule if the Church could be bound to a man who was not it. This is the same reasoning [Albizzi](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1683%20Albizzi) makes canonical (cap. X n. 12) and [Carena](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1668%20Carena) pastoral (§ XVI n. 73); Salelles gives it in the register of the rule of faith.*

***The frame: the Pope as living rule (nn. 259–276).*** *The rule: "another rule of faith, a living, or animate, rule of faith, namely the Supreme Pontiff, the true Vicar of Christ, by himself alone without any council … inasmuch as he defines and ordains from the Chair … not only in expressing whatsoever things are to be believed by divine faith, but also in making known and handing down precepts, or moral matters, for the universal Catholic Church." It is "certain of faith" that this rule is the Roman Pontiff (n. 261); the promises would fail "if the Pope could err while he speaks from the chair in defining and proposing for the whole Church" (n. 262). The worked cases are those of the working tribunal: the Vulgate "received as certain of faith" by the universal Church (n. 265); Sixtus V's* Cum frequenter *on eunuchs, "held as certain of faith by the universal Catholic Church; although before that there was no lack of Catholic Doctors … who held that such marriages were valid" (n. 266); canonization and beatification, where to assert error is "equally rash and scandalous" (n. 267); the approval of religious orders, at least as to substantials, against Bañez (nn. 271–272); and the limit, "as to accidentals … it is not unfitting that some human defect be committed by him" (n. 273), whence one pope revokes what another ordained. The papal question follows as the last and most fundamental application: if the man is not certainly the rule, none of these is certainly of faith.*

***Classification 1a.*** *Divine faith of the singular, conditioned on reception "by the sufficient consent of the whole Church" and expressly denied before it. He is the earliest of the collection's three inquisitorial witnesses, and the only one to date his own conviction to a public act: the Barcelona theses of c. 1600.*

## Regula LXV — the rule (printed 135)

*Whether the Pope alone is another living rule of faith? And in what manner in certain cases?*

**259** BESIDES the one already set forth, there is necessarily assigned another rule of faith, **a living, or animate, rule of faith, namely the Supreme Pontiff, the true Vicar of Christ, by himself alone without any council, whether particular or general, inasmuch as he defines and ordains from the Chair**; and he is of such great certainty and infallibility that he can in no way err, not only in expressing whatsoever things are to be believed by divine faith, but also in making known and handing down precepts, or moral matters, for the universal Catholic Church [...]

## n. 277 — whether it is of faith that this man is the true Pope (printed 137)

**277** To indicate more fully the infallibility of the rule of belief in the person of the Supreme Pontiff, that weighty doubt is wont to be discussed here by some: **whether it is of faith that this man, who for the time being governs the Church of God, is the true Pope?** Concerning which there are two opposed opinions of the Doctors on the one side and the other: I have always been pleased by **the resolution under a distinction, which thirty-five years ago I defended publicly in the church of the College of Barcelona**, in the theological theses proposed by me, as is customary every year for the honour of the College, with the Rev. Fr. Vincenzo Sigala, **then nuncio extraordinary of the Most Holy Clement VIII to the Catholic King Philip III, arguing against**, for the fuller elucidation of the truth: namely, **that it is of faith that this man is the true Supreme Pontiff, when he has been received by the sufficient consent of the whole Church, and so approved that all are bound to obey him, above all in receiving definitions of faith and ordinances concerning the morals of the whole Church; but otherwise before that**: which is clear, because in time of schisms it is almost always an altogether uncertain matter who is head of the Church, **278** and whom one must then obey. And thus far concerning the Supreme Pontiff according to this consideration only, that he is a rule of faith: according to the others, the treatment of him is very copious indeed; see Suárez, disp. 10. de fide.
