| *An inter cætera Fidei Theologicæ objecta materialia sit etiam, Summum Pontificem, p. t. regnantem, esse verum Pontificem.* | | *Whether among the other material objects of Theological Faith there is also this: that the Supreme Pontiff, reigning for the time being, is a true Pontiff.* |
| 54. CErtum est: quòd sit remotum, ceu revelabile, si non revelatum, idque semper intrinsecè, ità, ut non possit unquam fieri irrevelabile, nisi extrinsecè, atque adeò saltèm remotè semper credibile, si non proximè, ùt liquet ex præced. subsect. Sed dubium est: num sit objectum proximum, seu non solùm revelabile, sed etiam revelatum, atque adeò proximè credibile, & defacto de fide: quòd Summus Pontifex, p. t. regnans, legitimè electus, & pacificè à tota Ecclesia acceptatus, absque justa contradictione, atque ità, ut legitimè, sic pacificè suum Pontificatum possidens, neque eidem authenticè renuntians, ( qualem suspicimus hodie Sanctissimum D. Clementem XII. ) sit verus Pontifex, D. Petri Successor, & Supremus Christi Vicarius in terris? In ordine ad quod resolvendum in præsent. subsect. | | 54. It is certain that it is a remote object, as being revealable if not revealed, and that always intrinsically, so that it can never become unrevealable except extrinsically, and therefore that it is always at least remotely credible, if not proximately, as is clear from the preceding subsection. But it is doubtful whether it is a proximate object, that is, not only revealable but also revealed, and therefore proximately credible and in fact of faith, namely this: that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being, legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the whole Church, without just contradiction, and thus possessing his Pontificate as legitimately so peacefully, and not authentically renouncing it (such as we look up to today the most holy lord Clement XII), is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, and the Supreme Vicar of Christ on earth. With a view to settling this in the present subsection: |
| 55. Prænoto primò: Quòd revelatio, quantum ad præsens, sit, alia *formalis explicita*, alia *formalis implicita*, alia *virtualis implicita*. *Formalis explicita revelatio* est: quando aliqua veritas *expressè* à DEO est manifestata, seu per scripturam, seu per traditionem, ùt: *DEUM esse unum, & trinum, Christum esse Filium DEI, & hominis, &c.* Revelatio deinde *formalis implicita* est: quando in una veritate, revelata explicitè, continetur altera, aut saltèm cum illa connectitur ista, *essentialiter*, ùt *in definito* continetur *definitio, in propositione universali* continetur *propositio particularis*, & *mons* connectitur *cum valle, &c.* Revelatio denique *virtualis implicita* est: quando in una veritate, revelata explicitè, continetur altera solummodo *realiter*, puta, in essentia proprietas, ùt *in homine risibilitas, flebilitas, &c.* Porrò ad determinandam fidei materiam proximam non requiritur necessariò *formalis explicita revelatio*, sed sufficit *formalis implicita*, per infallibilem conclusionem deducibilis ex *revelatione formali explicita*, licèt non sufficiat *virtualis implicita*, ùt interim præsuppono ex *Sect.* 2. debitè expositurus idipsum in *Sect.* ejusdem *Subsect.* 5. atque adeò omnis propositio revelata *formaliter*, seu *explicitè*, seu *implicitè*, licèt nulla propositio revelata tantummodo *virtualiter*, est fidei Theologicæ objectum materiale proximum, & de fide. Ulteriùs | | 55. I note first: that revelation, so far as concerns the present matter, is of three kinds: one *formal explicit*, another *formal implicit*, another *virtual implicit*. *Formal explicit revelation* is when some truth has been *expressly* manifested by God, whether through Scripture or through tradition, as: *that God is one and triune, that Christ is the Son of God and of man*, etc. Next, *formal implicit* revelation is when in one truth, explicitly revealed, another is contained, or at least the latter is connected with the former *essentially*, as *the definition* is contained *in the thing defined*, and *the particular proposition* is contained *in the universal proposition*, and *a mountain* is connected *with a valley*, etc. Finally, *virtual implicit* revelation is when in one truth, explicitly revealed, another is contained only *really*, for instance a property in an essence, as *risibility, the capacity for weeping*, etc., in *man*. Further, to determine the proximate matter of faith, *formal explicit revelation* is not necessarily required, but *formal implicit* revelation suffices, deducible by an infallible conclusion from *formal explicit revelation*, although *virtual implicit* revelation does not suffice, as for the moment I presuppose from *Section* 2, being about to expound this same point duly in *Subsection* 5 of the same *Section*. And therefore every proposition revealed *formally*, whether *explicitly* or *implicitly*, though no proposition revealed only *virtually*, is a proximate material object of Theological Faith, and of faith. | 56. Prænoto secundò: quòd hæc propositio: *omnis Pontifex legitimè electus, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptatus, est verus Pontifex, & Supremum Caput visibile militantis Ecclesiæ, Vicarium Christi*, tam in dogmatico, quàm in sensu politico, in persona nominata complectente personas innominatas, æquipollet propositioni huic: *Tu es Petrus, & super hanc Petram adifecabo[1] Ecclesiam meam. Matth. 16.* Et ratio est: quia aliàs, nisi sub *sensu litterali persona historica Petri* intelligeretur *in sensu spirituali persona mystica Petri*, id est, *omnis Pontifex, legitimè electus, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptatus*, non esset vera promissio illa Christi, quæ tamen non potest esse falsa, utpote ab æterna veritate facta, *immediatè* quidem Apostolis *mediatè* autem toti Ecclesiæ militanti: *Ego vobiscum sum usque ad consummationem sæculi. Matth. 18.*[2] utique non per se ipsum, proportionatô nempe modô d. Ecclesiæ, nimirum *visibiliter*, neque in *persona Petri historica*: ergo *in quolibet legitimè electo, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptato Pontifice*, tanquam in *persona Petri mystica*, & suo in terris Vicario; ut enim visibilem instituit Ecclesiam militantem, sic eam voluit etiam per visibilia gubernari Capita usque ad finem mundi: alioquin neque Petrô fuisset opus, si eam voluisset gubernatam *invisibiliter*. Ac proinde sic illa propositio: *omnis Pontifex legitimè electus, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptatus, &c.* Sicut propositio ista: *Tu es Petrus, &c.* utpote æquivalens una alteri, revelata est *formaliter explicitè* locô præcit. nempe *Matth. 16.* Et consequenter ex propositæ q. suppositô, quòd Summus Pontifex p. t. regnans, sit *legitimè electus, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptatus, &c.* ut omnem in communi *explicitè*, sic talem in particulari *formaliter implicitè*, revelatum est, esse verum Pontificem, D. Petri Successorem, &c. Ultimatè | | 56. I note secondly: that this proposition — *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church is a true Pontiff, and the Supreme visible Head of the Church militant, the Vicar of Christ* — both in the dogmatic and in the political sense, in a named person comprehending unnamed persons, is equivalent to this proposition: *Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build*[1] *my Church. Matth. 16.* And the reason is this: because otherwise, unless under the *literal sense of the historical person of Peter* there were understood, *in the spiritual sense, the mystical person of Peter*, that is, *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church*, that promise of Christ would not be true — a promise which nevertheless cannot be false, since it was made by eternal truth, *immediately* indeed to the Apostles, but *mediately* to the whole Church militant: *I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world. Matth. 18.*[2] And certainly He is with her not through Himself, that is, in the manner proportioned to the said Church, namely *visibly*, nor in the *historical person of Peter*: therefore *in every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church*, as in the *mystical person of Peter* and His Vicar on earth. For as He instituted the Church militant as visible, so He also willed her to be governed by visible Heads until the end of the world; otherwise there would have been no need even of Peter, if He had willed her to be governed *invisibly*. And accordingly that proposition, *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church*, etc., just like this proposition, *Thou art Peter*, etc. — the one being equivalent to the other — is revealed *formally explicitly* in the place just cited, namely *Matth. 16*. And consequently, on the supposition of the question proposed, that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being is *legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church*, etc., then, as of every such Pontiff in general it is revealed *explicitly*, so of this one in particular it is revealed *formally implicitly*, that he is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, etc. Lastly: |
| 57. Prænoto tertiò: Quòd detur omnino potestas in Ecclesia eligendi, & acceptandi Summum Pontificem, ùt probat continuus Ecclesiæ usus, in suavi, ac infallibili providentia divina, Ecclesiam regente, fundatus, quò à principio deligebat, & acceptabat ejusmodi Caput visibile, ac deligit, & acceptat defacto, atque delectura, & acceptura est usque ad consummationem sæculi, juxta eum modum, quem vidit, & videt, visuráque est accommodum: uti à principio quidem adhibita totius Cleri Romani, ac Vicinorum Romæ Episcoporum votatione, eáque exhibita, præstolando insuper populi consensum, non quidem permodùm suffragii, sed permodum testimonii de neo-electi vita, ac moribus; ex postfacto autem, ubi auctus esset plusculum Cleri Romani numerus, adhibita votatione pauciorum ex eodem ad id deputatorum, antea Regionariorum, à 14. Urbis Romanæ regionibus, quibus singuli erant præfecti, postea Cardinalium à Sylvestro I. quasi Cardinum Ecclesiæ, nuncupatorum: quos Innocentiùs IV. rubrô insignivit galerô, ut monerentur eó, non dubitare, profundendum sanguinem suum esse, pro libertate Ecclesiæ; defacto autem adhibita votatione eorundem quidem Cardinalium, sed in auctiore numero, idque, ex constitutione Alexandri III. requisitis ad electionem validam duabus suffragiorum, seu votorum, tertiis. S. Petrus quidem ipse delegit sibi Successorem, D. Clementem; Ecclesia tamen non acceptavit hanc electionem, sed elegit, & acceptavit S. Linum: quamvis ipsomet etiam D. Clemente, factæ sui à S. Petro delectioni, renuntiante, teste D. Epiphanio. Ex quo licet eruere, quòd tametsi competat Pontifici, utpote totius Ecclesiæ Capiti, eum ordinare modum novi Pontificis eligendi, quem judicaverit congruum, ità, ut exigente id necessitate ac utilitate, in aliquo casu extraordinario, possit ipse etiam deligere sibi ( ex mente Eximii Suarezii ) Successorem; nihilominus loquendo de cursu ordinario, sicut non est post S. Petrum attentata ejusmodi electio, sic neque imposterum facilè est ab ullo Pontifice attentanda, nisi, ùt dixi, in aliquo casu extraordinario; nè alioqui hæreditate possideretur Sanctuarium DEI. Et licèt etiam, exemplô S. Petri, deligeret sibi ipse Pontifex Successorem suum, adhuc posset Ecclesia, exemplô non acceptati Clementis, & substituti Lini, non acceptare eum, sed substituere alium; uti quôvis modo electum, & nondum acceptatum, potest repudiare: cùm electio non habeat se nisi permodum *dispositivæ conditionis* ad designandam personam Pontificiam intento fine frustrabilis, & consequenter in eodem obtinendo fallibilis, ùt dicam infrà n. 61. ubi dicam etiam, quòd acceptatio è contra totius Ecclesiæ, tanquam *columna veritatis*, habens se instar formalis designationis personæ Pontificiæ, tanquam necessarii Capitis sui, sinè quô tam parum potest visibiliter perennare, quàm parùm quodlibet corpus aliud sinè suo, non possit esse nisi infrustrabilis, & infallibilis. Quibus prænotatis, facilè est colligere q. propositæ resolutionem, quàm ponens, | | 57. I note thirdly: that there is altogether in the Church a power of electing and accepting the Supreme Pontiff, as the continuous usage of the Church proves, a usage founded in that sweet and infallible divine providence which governs the Church; by which usage she from the beginning chose and accepted such a visible Head, and in fact does choose and accept one, and will choose and accept one until the consummation of the world, according to that manner which she has seen, and sees, and will see to be suitable. Thus at the beginning the vote of the whole Roman clergy and of the bishops neighboring on Rome was employed, and once that vote had been given, the consent of the people was further awaited, not indeed by way of a suffrage, but by way of a testimony concerning the life and morals of the newly elected. Afterwards, when the number of the Roman clergy had grown somewhat larger, the vote of a smaller number deputed from that same clergy for this purpose was employed: earlier these were the *Regionarii*, named from the fourteen regions of the city of Rome, over each of which one of them was set; later they were called Cardinals by Sylvester I, as being, so to speak, the hinges (*cardines*) of the Church. Innocent IV distinguished them with the red hat, so that they might be warned by it not to hesitate that their own blood must be poured out for the liberty of the Church. But at present the vote of these same Cardinals is employed, though in a larger number, and this by the constitution of Alexander III, two thirds of the suffrages, or votes, being required for a valid election. St. Peter himself indeed chose a successor for himself, blessed Clement; the Church, however, did not accept this election, but elected and accepted St. Linus — although blessed Clement himself also renounced the choice of him made by St. Peter, as blessed Epiphanius testifies. From this it may be gathered that, although it belongs to the Pontiff, as Head of the whole Church, to lay down that manner of electing a new Pontiff which he judges fitting, so that, when necessity and utility demand it, in some extraordinary case he may even choose a successor for himself (according to the mind of the Eminent Suárez), nevertheless, speaking of the ordinary course, as no such election has been attempted since St. Peter, so neither is such an election lightly to be attempted hereafter by any Pontiff, except, as I said, in some extraordinary case, lest otherwise the sanctuary of God be possessed by inheritance. And even if, after the example of St. Peter, a Pontiff should himself choose his own successor, the Church could still, after the example of Clement who was not accepted and of Linus who was substituted, not accept him but substitute another; just as she can repudiate anyone elected in whatever manner and not yet accepted. For the election stands only by way of a *dispositive condition* for designating the person of the Pontiff, a condition that can be frustrated of its intended end and consequently is fallible in obtaining that end, as I shall say below at § 61. There I shall also say that, on the contrary, the acceptance by the whole Church, as *the pillar of truth*, standing as the formal designation of the person of the Pontiff as her necessary Head — without whom she can as little endure visibly as any other body can endure without its own head — cannot be otherwise than unfrustrable and infallible. These things being noted beforehand, it is easy to gather the resolution of the question proposed; and setting it down, |

Petri Successorem, & Supremum Caput visibile totius Ecclesiæ militantis, seu, quod est idem, Summum Christi Vicarium in terris. Ità cum P. Suarez hodie communis, imò jam à t. Clementis VIII. ùt testatur Oviedo, unanimis Theologorum, contra Cajetanum, Turrecrematam, Banezium, & alios nonnullos. P. 1. authoritate SS. Pontifices, ùt ipsa agnoverunt, sic fidelibus proposuerunt, credendos, tanquam Vicarios Christi, quemadmodum Chalcedonense Leonem IV. Milevitanum Innocentium III. Tridentinum Pium IV. P. secundò ratione Theologica: omnis veritas, formaliter implicitè revelata, est fidei Theologicæ objectum materiale proximum, & de fide, juxta *prænot. 1.* atqui talis veritas est, Summum Pontificem p. t. regnantem, legitimè electum, & pacificè acceptatum, &c. esse verum Pontificem, D. Petri Successorem, & Summum Christi Vicarium in terris, juxta *prænot. 2.* ergo. | | 58. *I say:* Among the other proximate material objects of Theological Faith, and therefore in fact of faith, is this: that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being, legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church, and thus possessing his Pontificate as legitimately so peacefully, and not authentically renouncing it (such we revere today's Clement XII to be), is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, and the Supreme visible Head of the whole Church militant, or, what is the same, the Supreme Vicar of Christ on earth. So, with Fr. Suárez, holds the common opinion today — indeed, already from the time of Clement VIII, as Oviedo testifies, the unanimous opinion of theologians — against Cajetan, Torquemada, Bañez, and some others. Proof 1, from the authority of the holy Councils, and above all of Constance, where the Bull of Martin V is set out, in which it is laid down that heretics wishing to be reconciled to the Church are to be asked whether they believe that the Pope canonically elected, whoever he shall be for the time being (expressing his name), is the successor of Peter and has supreme power in the Church of God. Therefore the same Martin V, Supreme Pontiff, supposes that among the other material objects of Theological Faith, and among the proximate ones, is this: the Pope canonically elected; otherwise he would not require this to be believed by heretics who are to be reconciled to the Church. And this not only in general, but also in particular, indeed in the singular, that is, in the individual, as the adduced words of the Bull indicate: *whoever he shall be for the time being*, and especially those placed within the parenthesis: *expressing his name*. Other Councils hold the same, which, as they themselves acknowledged the Pontiffs reigning for the time being, so proposed them to the faithful as men to be believed to be Vicars of Christ: as Chalcedon did Leo IV, Mileve Innocent III, Trent Pius IV. Proof 2, by theological reason: every truth formally implicitly revealed is a proximate material object of Theological Faith, and of faith, according to *note 1*; but such a truth is this: that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being, legitimately elected and peacefully accepted, etc., is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, and the Supreme Vicar of Christ on earth, according to *note 2*; therefore. |
| 59. Confirm. primò ab infallibilitate Ecclesiæ: quia de fide est, quòd Ecclesia sit *columna, & firmamentum veritatis, 1. Timoth. 3. contra quam nec porta inferi prævalebunt, Matth. 16.* atqui si non esset de fide, quòd Summus Pontifex, legitimè electus, & pacificè ab Ecclesia acceptatus, sit verus Pontifex, & Vicarius Christi in terris, non esset de fide, quòd Ecclesia sit columna veritatis, contra quam portæ inferi non prævalerent: ergo. Confirmation 1, from the infallibility of the Church: because it is of faith that the Church is *the pillar and ground of truth, 1 Tim. 3*, against which *the gates of hell shall not prevail, Matth. 16*. But if it were not of faith that the Supreme Pontiff, legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church, is a true Pontiff and the Vicar of Christ on earth, it would not be of faith that the Church is the pillar of truth against which the gates of hell would not prevail; therefore. I prove the minor: because, first, she could err in accepting the animate rule of faith; for everything that is neither evident nor of faith must lie open to the danger of error. And, consequently, secondly, such a Pontiff, though legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church, could err in defining matters of faith, being in the truth of the matter no Pontiff at all; and yet the Church is bound to believe his definition. And, consequently, further, thirdly, the Church could err by believing the definition of such a Pontiff; therefore. |

Tridentinum, esse verum Concilium; Secundò: omnia essent frustranea, quæ de Judice controversiarum fide divina creduntur in Ecclesia; Tertiò: non esset etiam de fide Benedictum XIII. regnantis hodie Clementis XII. Antecessorem, fuisse verum Pontificem, & sic de tota serie retrograda Pontificum, & consequenter neque fuisse, neque esse veros Episcopos, & veros Sacerdotes; atqui hæ sequelæ sunt omnino absurdæ: ergo & origo earum, quòd nempe non sit de fide regnantem p. t. Pontificem esse verum Pontificem, absurda est, & minimè toleranda. Quòd verò dd. sequelæ sint omnino absurdæ P. etiam facilè: quia primò confitemur in Symbolo: *credo - S. Ecclesiam Catholicam;* 2dò: quia de fide debeo credere dogmata à Concilio definita; jam autem sicut de fide debet esse certum, hoc esse verum Evangelium, ut credam de fide, quæ docet Evangelium: sic de fide debet esse certum, hoc esse verum Concilium, ut credam de fide, quæ docet Concilium; 3tiò: quia si frustranea essent ea, quæ fide divina creduntur de Judice controversiarum in Ecclesia, ipsa fides divina esset, quòd absit, falsa; 4tò: quia ruente Pontificum[3], ut posset ruere, si non esset de fide, & consequenter ruente clero, tota corrueret hierarchia Ecclesiastica, & sicut à Synagoga, sic etiam ab Ecclesia ablatum esset Sacerdotium, &c: ergo. | | 60. Confirmation 2, from absurd consequences: because, first, it would not be of faith that this individual Church is the true Church, nor likewise that this individual Council, for example Trent, is a true Council. Secondly: all those things would be vain which are believed in the Church by divine faith concerning the Judge of controversies. Thirdly: neither would it be of faith that Benedict XIII, the predecessor of Clement XII who reigns today, was a true Pontiff, and so on through the whole retrograde series of Pontiffs; and consequently neither that there have been or are true bishops and true priests. But these consequences are altogether absurd; therefore their origin also — namely, that it is not of faith that the Pontiff reigning for the time being is a true Pontiff — is absurd and by no means to be tolerated. But the said consequences are proved: because, first, as the true Church and the true Council in general depend on a true Head in general, so this individual true Church and this individual true Council depend on a true Pontiff in the individual, as on this individual true Head, so that neither a true Church nor a true Council can be believed unless a true Pontiff is believed, for the reason that their truth depends on his truth, as things founded depend on their foundation, or (to use the phrase of Truth himself) on the Rock. Secondly: because anyone could say: I believe indeed that there is in the Church some Judge of controversies, but I do not believe that this Judge is Clement XII; and consequently we shall neither have recourse to him for judgment, nor, when judgment has been received through him, shall we stand by it. Thirdly: because the reasoning is the same for one as for the other; and if the Pontiff falls, the bishops fall, and if these fall, the priests collapse; therefore. But that the said consequences are altogether absurd is likewise easily proved: because, first, we confess in the Creed: *I believe in the holy Catholic Church*. Secondly: because by faith I must believe the dogmas defined by a Council; and now, just as it must be certain by faith that this is a true Gospel, in order that I may believe by faith what the Gospel teaches, so it must be certain by faith that this is a true Council, in order that I may believe by faith what the Council teaches. Thirdly: because if those things were vain which are believed by divine faith concerning the Judge of controversies in the Church, divine faith itself would be — which God forbid — false. Fourthly: because if the Pontiff[3] fell, as he could fall if this were not of faith, and consequently if the clergy fell, the whole ecclesiastical hierarchy would collapse, and as from the Synagogue, so also from the Church, the priesthood would have been taken away, etc.; therefore. |

But I said, both in the very statement of the question proposed and in the resolution of it, first: *legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church*, etc., because without a legitimate election and a peaceful acceptance, and with just contradiction, those who occupy the Apostolic See are invaders of it rather than possessors, and are no Pontiffs at all. And this although (as I intimated above at § 57) the *election* stands only *by way of a dispositive condition* for designating the person of the Pontiff — which disposition, being not that of the whole Church, nor that of the Head, at least as such, that is, as speaking from the chair, but that of a partial Church, which does not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, can be deceived. But the *acceptance* stands *by way of a formal designation* of that person of the Pontiff on whom the principal Head, Christ, confers the Pontifical[4] dignity itself, the dignity of the Vicarial Head; and this designation, being that of the whole Church, and therefore of her who, as *the pillar of truth*, has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, cannot be deceived. Rather it is a kind of *virtual revelation*, applying to that universal proposition, *formally explicitly revealed*, *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted is a true Pontiff*, etc., by the deed itself as it were subsuming: but *this individual Pontiff is legitimately elected and peacefully accepted*; and, from that same proposition, infallibly explicating this particular, or singular, proposition, *formally implicitly revealed* in it, by the deed itself as it were concluding: therefore *this individual Pontiff is a true Pontiff*, etc. Indeed it is a kind of *formal revelation*, according to that saying: *vox Populi, vox DEI* (the voice of the people is the voice of God). Furthermore I said, secondly: *and not authentically renouncing it*, that is, his Pontificate; because, once an authentic renunciation has been made, he resumes the quality of a particular member, having laid down the dignity of universal Head of the whole Church. But in order that a Pope be reckoned to renounce the Papacy authentically, an internal or private renunciation does not suffice; an external and public renunciation is required. For a legitimate possessor is not reckoned to abdicate anything he possesses, and especially a thing such as the Pontificate, unless the abdication made is morally certain. Idem est respondendum ad alias paritates, huic, de baptismo, non dispares, seu respectu conficientium quodpiam Sacramentum, seu respectu illud suscipientium, seu respectu aliorum, cum certitudine, vel incertitudine ex ipso, nempe Sacramento, alióve revelato formaliter explicitè, deducentium quidpiam aliud, revelatum formaliter implicitè. | | To Confirmation 2. Reply: I distinguish the antecedent. It is not of faith with respect to the one who baptizes: I deny the antecedent; with respect to others: I concede the antecedent, or let it pass; and I deny the consequence. Given this truth formally explicitly revealed, *every duly baptized person obtains sanctifying grace*, the one who duly baptizes can, with physical evidence, or at least with great moral evidence, subsume: but *this man has been duly baptized by me*; and thereupon he can infallibly conclude this truth, formally implicitly revealed and therefore an object of faith: therefore *this man has obtained sanctifying grace*. And so the parity is on our side, though with a disparity that confirms our opinion: the disparity of a lesser infallibility, as being natural, standing for that minor, *but this man has been duly baptized by me*, and of a greater infallibility, as being supernatural, standing for this minor, *but this man has been legitimately elected as Pontiff and peacefully accepted by the Church*. For the rest, speaking of others, the parity collapses. For the one who baptizes can, from that formally explicitly revealed proposition, *every duly baptized person obtains sanctifying grace*, infallibly subsume: but *this man has been duly baptized by me*, and so apply this to that formally explicitly revealed proposition, and from it explicate this other, formally implicitly revealed: therefore *this man has obtained sanctifying grace*. Or again, a member of the faithful can, from that formally explicitly revealed proposition, *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church is a true Pontiff*, infallibly subsume: but *this man has been legitimately elected as Pontiff and peacefully accepted by the Church*, and so apply this to that formally explicitly revealed proposition, and from it explicate this other, formally implicitly revealed: therefore *this man is a true Pontiff*. Those who do not baptize, I say, cannot in this way infallibly subsume: but *this man has been duly baptized*; for the reason that those who do not baptize do not, as the one who baptizes does, know infallibly that the requisite intention was made, although they may perhaps know the rest infallibly — unless perhaps that very thing, namely the intention of the one baptizing, should become known to those who do not baptize as well, through a revelation, whether formally such or a revelation equivalently such. And in that case again (with a view to which I said *let it pass*) we should be released from the obligation of giving a disparity between that case and our own. The same answer is to be given to the other parities that are not disparate from this one about baptism, whether with respect to those who confect some sacrament, or with respect to those who receive it, or with respect to others, who, with certainty or uncertainty, deduce from that sacrament itself, or from something else formally explicitly revealed, something else that is formally implicitly revealed. |

To the proof: I distinguish the antecedent. Every infallibility which the Church enjoys through the assistance of the Holy Spirit *with a view to accepting her Head* she receives dependently on her Head himself: I deny the antecedent. The infallibility she enjoys *with a view to deciding controversies of faith*: I subdistinguish — *radically*: I deny the antecedent; *formally*: I subdistinguish still further — *before she legitimately has him*: I deny it; *after she legitimately has him*: I concede the antecedent; and I deny the consequence. For with a view to accepting her Head the Church, as *the pillar and ground of truth*, has promised to her the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit independently of her Head himself, lest otherwise, against her character as *the pillar and foundation of truth*, she should err in accepting that Head of hers, and consequently also in the controversies of faith decided through him, as through a putative Judge of controversies who in reality is none. But with a view to deciding those controversies of faith which are contradistinguished from this one about the true Pontiff having been accepted — a controversy decided by the acceptance itself, though not signately [*signate*], yet in exercise [*exercite*] — she does not at all have promised to her the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit independently of her Head himself, except *radically*: that is, insofar as she has the power of taking up such a Head, by force of which she requires infallibility in deciding controversies of faith; so that the infallibility which she roots *in the divided sense* of her Head, that is, *before she has him*, that same infallibility, namely in deciding controversies of faith, she has *formally* *in the composite sense* of her Head, that is, *after she has him*. |

Reply: let the antecedent pass, I deny the consequence. The disparity is this: because, granted but not conceded that the Church erred for a short time in recognizing the true Pope, that would nevertheless not expose her to the danger of false dogmas and of a well-nigh irreparable harm; for the acts of such a doubtful Pontiff neither have validity, for want of a true Pontificate, nor do they acquire force unless they are approved and established by a certain Pontiff — although indeed even this would not come about altogether without some detriment to the Church. But if the Church were to err for a longer time in accepting as a true Pontiff one who was in reality no Pontiff at all, say through the whole course of a Pontificate, that would expose her to the danger of false dogmas and of a well-nigh irreparable harm, because she could err in the same way about a second and a third, and so on. For the rest, I said: let the antecedent pass; for it is not true, but altogether false, that the Church ever erred, even for a short time, in accepting the true Pontiff, since in no historian, unless a forger, does there exist any testimony to this assertion. But as to the fact that it has sometimes happened that several occupied the see of Peter, note this concerning it: that such a schism can arise in three ways. First, that to one man legitimately elected and peacefully accepted a second is added; and in this case the first has prescribed and remains in possession of the true Papacy, against the later schismatic Antipope. Secondly, that the greatest part of the Church, and as it were the whole, accepts a Pontiff, apart from one or another king, the author of a schism, for whose sake some Cardinals, withdrawing from the true Pope, go over to a Pseudo-Pope; and in this case, although a partial Church errs, the whole Church nevertheless does not err, the right standing on the side of that Pontiff who was accepted by the greatest part of the Church, and so by what is as it were the whole, and who is in reality the true one, against the Pseudo-Popes. Thirdly, that when two or three are sitting, contradiction is made almost equally from the two or three parties; and in this case, as none is a peaceful possessor of the true Papacy, so none is a true Pope, but they are mere Antipopes. |

| Ad Confirm. 1. Resp. C. M. N. m. ad causalem dico: quòd, sicut ad divinam spectat providentiam impedire, ne incapax eligatur, sic ad eam spectat etiam, ne incapax retineatur. Reply: I concede the major, I deny the minor. To the causal clause I say that, as it belongs to divine providence to prevent an incapable man from being elected, so it belongs to it also to prevent an incapable man from being retained. Hence I say further that one who has become insane, and so is reckoned as a dead man (unless the insanity be temporary and not perpetual), just as he falls away from capacity, so also falls away from the power of governing, by natural law. But one who has turned unbeliever is, according to some, probably by that very fact deposed by Christ, who does not will his lambs to be fed by wolves; according to others, however, more probably not by that very fact, but only when the *sentence* of the Church supervenes, not indeed a *judicial* but a *declaratory* one. For it is nowhere read as expressed in divine law that a Pope who has become a heretic, even a manifest one, is by that very fact to be reckoned deposed by Christ; but rather, as it has been granted to the Church to take up a profitable Head, so also it has been granted to the Church to repudiate a pernicious one. And although faith is a necessary foundation of all ecclesiastical power *for its better being*, it is nevertheless not necessary *for its being simply*: as it is not necessary for the power of orders, as is plain in heretics who validly ordain, validly consecrate, etc., so neither for the power of jurisdiction. And consequently, when some decrees of the Pontiffs seem to hint that such unbelieving Pontiffs are deposed by the law itself, either they speak of inferiors, who receive their power from the Pope, or, if they speak of the supreme ones, who have their power from Christ, they must be understood to speak as regards the first act and not as regards the second act, that is, as regards a potential and not as regards an actual loss of the papal power. In which sense too it is true that Christ does not will his sheep to be fed by wolves. But if you say: a heretical Pontiff implicitly denies, granted not explicitly, Christ and the Church; therefore he denies himself also and his own dignity — I reply: I concede the antecedent, I distinguish the consequent. He denies himself *as a member united to Christ by faith with a view to justification*: I concede; he denies himself *as an instrument taken up by Christ with a view to the government of the Church*: I deny the consequent. The Pope remains in possession as long as there does not supervene a declaration of the Church about his nullity, on account of a lapse into manifest heresy — supposing, that is, that he can lapse into it. And this holds a fortiori in the case of the other crimes on account of which Suárez holds that the Pope cannot be arraigned by the Church with a view to his deposition. |

Reply: I concede the major, I deny the minor, and I distinguish the causal clause: in none of the said instants is a new revelation made that is *formally* such: I concede; that is *equivalently* such: I deny it, denying the causal clause of the minor and the consequence. When a new Pope is accepted by the Church, in that instant the ancient and common revelation is applied to a new and particular material object, so that the same Pope who *before* the instant of acceptance was credible *remotely* becomes credible *in* the instant of acceptance, and remains so *after* the instant of acceptance, *proximately*; the *acceptance* itself exercising a threefold office: with respect to Christ, the office of a *condition*, that he may confer the Pontifical power on the newly elected; with respect to the newly elected, the office of a *union* with the first truth, as of a material object with the formal one; and with respect to us, the office of an *application*, proposing the newly elected as an object no longer *remotely* but *proximately* credible. And so, although the first election can be annulled, the second election can no longer be annulled; but just as prescription, even though a particular injury intervene, brings it about, on account of the certitude of ownership, that what is prescribed in good faith is justly possessed, so the acceptance of the Church, on account of the certitude of a head necessary to her by law both natural and divine, adjusts and validates the first election — even one unjust and invalid by the lower human law — by the higher law, namely the natural and divine, as being a second election. But if you say: the Church cannot accept the man elected unless she knows that he was legitimately elected; but this she cannot know, since the electors do not have the Holy Spirit infallibly assisting them; therefore — I reply: I distinguish the major. Unless she knows it positively and from a direct principle: I deny the major; unless she knows it negatively and from a reflex principle: I concede the major. I distinguish the minor in the same way, and, the causal clause of the minor being conceded, I deny the consequence. For the Church to accept the man elected, it is not required that she be able to demonstrate the election made to be legitimate, philosophically or theologically, through a middle term naturally or supernaturally certain, and so to know it positively and from a direct principle; but it suffices that by prudent conjectures it be established that the election is not illegitimate, even if in reality it were so, with this dictate supervening: whoever, all circumstances prudently weighed, is judged canonically elected, he must be accepted; but such is this individual man judged to be, namely Cardinal Lorenzo Corsini, from now on canonically elected Supreme Pontiff, Clement XII; therefore he must be accepted — and so she knows it negatively and from a reflex principle. And when upon this the acceptance itself at last follows, the voice of the people, which has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, being equivalent to the divine voice, it comes about that such a man's being Pontiff is of faith. |

| Resp. ad argumentum N. A. licèt enim possit communis error suffragari, ut valeant acta putatitii principis politici, juxta *L. Barbarius*, non potest tamen communis error suffragari, ut valeant acta putatitij Principis *Supremi* Ecclesiastici; dico: *Supremi:* quia potest quidem d. error suffragari, ut valeant etiam acta putatitij Pastoris Ecclesiastici *non Supremi;* disparitas autem inter *Principem politicum*, & inter *Principem Ecclesiasticum*, eúmque *Supremum*, & *non Supremum*, est: quia Princeps quidem politicus accipit suam potestatem à communitate politica: quæ non habens appromissam Spiritûs S. assistentiam infallibilem, potest quidem putatitium acceptare Principem suum, sed ità tamen, ut, sicut potest legitimo conferre potestatem, sic possit illegitimi valorare acta invalida, uti valoravit Barbarij Philippi: pariter Pastor Ecclesiæ inferior accipit suam jurisdictionem à Pastore ejusdem superiore, ità, ut licèt partialis Ecclesia, non habens pariter infallibilem Spiritûs S. assistentiam, erret in acceptatione talis Pastoris inferioris, adhuc possit, & velit Pastor superior, authoritate totalis Ecclesiæ, tali pastori inferiori putatitio jurisdictionem conferre, exigente ità salute ovicularum, uti, supposita potestate ordinis, confert defacto potestatem jurisdictionis Parocho, occultè Simoniaco, id, quod datur colligi, à priori quidem ex ratione ipsius superioritatis, seu superioris potestatis, concessæ ad ædificandum, non ad destruendum, à posteriori autem ex eo, quòd non obliget fideles ad repetitionem ministratorum ipsis à tali Pastore putatitio actuum jurisdictionalium; N. suppositum A. quòd possit pro legitimo ab Ecclesia haberi Pontifex illegitimus, nisi cum eversione firmamenti veritatis. Inde etiam ad causalem dico: quòd Spiritus S. sicut non potest sponsæ suæ, Ecclesiæ, in acceptando Capite legitimo, non assistere, tanquam cum qua Christus promisit se mansurum usque ad consummationem sæculi, & quam constituit columnam, & firmamentum veritatis, sic non possit assistere non acceptatæ ab Ecclesia Petræ, atque ità illegitimæ, aut non assistere ab Ecclesia acceptatæ, atque ità jam legitimæ, & non ampliùs illegitimæ, ut hoc pacto contra eam, tanquam contra legitimam, & non tanquam contra illegitimam, portæ inferiori non prævaleant. | | Reply to the argument: I deny the antecedent. For although a common error can avail to make the acts of a putative political prince valid, according to the law *Barbarius*, a common error cannot avail to make valid the acts of a putative *Supreme* ecclesiastical Prince. I say *Supreme*, because the said error can indeed avail to make valid even the acts of a putative ecclesiastical pastor who is *not Supreme*. But the disparity between a *political prince* and an *ecclesiastical Prince*, and between the latter as *Supreme* and as *not Supreme*, is this: a political prince receives his power from the political community, which, not having the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit promised to it, can indeed accept a putative prince, yet in such a way that, as it can confer power on a legitimate one, so it can validate the invalid acts of an illegitimate one, as it validated the acts of Barbarius Philippus. Likewise an inferior pastor of the Church receives his jurisdiction from a superior pastor of the same Church, so that, although a partial Church, likewise not having the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, may err in the acceptance of such an inferior pastor, the superior pastor can still, and will, by the authority of the whole Church, confer jurisdiction on such a putative inferior pastor, the salvation of the little sheep so demanding; as, the power of orders being supposed, he in fact confers the power of jurisdiction on a parish priest who is secretly a simoniac. This may be gathered, a priori indeed from the very nature of superiority, that is, of the superior power, granted for building up and not for destroying, and a posteriori from the fact that he does not oblige the faithful to a repetition of the jurisdictional acts administered to them by such a putative pastor. But the ecclesiastical Prince and Supreme pastor of the Church receives his power from Christ, as a Vicar from his Principal; and as Christ promised to the ecclesiastical community the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit as regards accepting a legitimate Head, through whom he might be visibly with that same ecclesiastical community, as the principal Head in his Vicar, even to the consummation of the world, so to such a Vicarial Head of his, accepted by the Church, he promised the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit in defining all matters of faith, as to the Rock against which the gates of hell shall not prevail. And therefore the whole, or as it were the whole, ecclesiastical community can as little accept an illegitimate Vicarial Head of its principal Head as such an accepted Head can fail to be legitimate, or an unaccepted one fail to be illegitimate. Hence to the proof: I deny the supposition of the antecedent, that an illegitimate Pontiff could be held for legitimate by the Church, except with the overthrow of the ground of truth. Hence too, to the causal clause I say that, as the Holy Spirit cannot fail to assist his spouse, the Church, in accepting a legitimate Head — she being the one with whom Christ promised that he would remain to the consummation of the world, and whom he constituted the pillar and ground of truth — so he cannot assist a Rock not accepted by the Church and thus illegitimate, nor fail to assist one accepted by the Church and thus now legitimate and no longer illegitimate; so that in this way the gates of hell may not prevail against her as against a legitimate Rock, and not as against an illegitimate one. |
