*Note on this entry: Matěj Bartys (Latin* Mathias Bartis; *Ostrava, 1 Feb 1693 – Velehrad, 28 Feb 1770), a Cistercian of the royal Moravian abbey of Velehrad, professed 15 December 1715, taught theology at the Archiepiscopal College of St. Adalbert in Prague from 1724 to 1733 and later served his abbey as preacher and parish priest, publishing sermons in Czech and philosophy and theology in Latin. The* Nucleus *is a thesis-book: Bartis presided, the Benedictine Bernard Hein of Břevnov-Braunau defended it in September 1731, the Jesuit Maximilian Wietrowski approved it for the Archiepiscopal consistory on 23 August 1731, and the dedication is to St. John Nepomuk, canonized two years earlier. A fuller 690-page issue of the Secunda Secundae followed in 1732 (Google Books* -7Mc4fgHy6MC*); the 1731 fascicle, 204 pages, is the copy used here. The reigning pope named throughout is* ***Clement XII***, *elected 12 July 1730, with* ***Benedict XIII*** *as his predecessor (§ 60) and* ***Cardinal Lorenzo Corsini*** *as the worked example of the man to be accepted (§ 66); the present year is given as 1731 at printed 153. He is the second Prague witness in the collection, eighty years after [Arriaga](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Arriaga), whose "virtual revelation" is the doctrine he develops.*

***The thesis and its pedigree (§ 58).*** *"Among the other proximate material objects of theological faith, and therefore in fact of faith, is this: that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being,* ***legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church***, *and thus possessing his Pontificate as legitimately so peacefully, and not authentically renouncing it (such we revere today's Clement XII to be), is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, and the Supreme visible Head of the whole Church militant." The pedigree is stated as history: "with Fr. Suárez, holds the common opinion today —* ***indeed, already from the time of Clement VIII, as Oviedo testifies, the unanimous opinion of theologians*** *— against Cajetan, Torquemada, Bañez, and some others." That is [Oviedo's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Francis%20of%20Oviedo) report of the [Alcalá affair of 1601–02](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1601%20Alcala%20Theses), received in Prague as settled fact, and returned to at the end (§ 67): since Clement VIII "no one among the more recent authors has dared, on Oviedo's testimony, to teach the opposite … so that the opinion of the Adversaries seems in truth to be no longer tolerated by the Church." The first proof is* ***Martin V's bull at Constance***, *pressed on its parenthesis "expressing his name": the credendum is "not only in general, but also in particular, indeed in the singular, that is, in the individual."*

***The machinery: three kinds of revelation and the equivalence with Matt. 16:18 (§§ 55–56).*** *Revelation is formal explicit, formal implicit (one truth contained in or essentially connected with another, "as the particular proposition is contained in the universal"), or virtual implicit (a property in an essence). Formal implicit suffices for faith; virtual does not. The universal "every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church is a true Pontiff" is then declared* ***equivalent to "Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church"***, *read of the "mystical person of Peter": otherwise Christ's promise to be with the Church "all days" would be false, since He is with her "visibly", and "not in the historical person of Peter." So the universal is formally explicitly revealed, and the singular, once the minor is supplied, formally implicitly.*

***Election as dispositive condition, acceptance as formal designation (§§ 57, 61).*** *The praenotandum on the Church's power to elect and accept runs through the history of papal election, and its example is Peter's own designation of Clement: "the Church, however, did not accept this election, but elected and accepted St. Linus." Election "stands only by way of a* ***dispositive condition*** *for designating the person of the Pontiff, a condition that can be frustrated of its intended end and consequently is fallible"; it is the act of "a partial Church, which does not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit." Acceptance "stands by way of a* ***formal designation*** *of that person of the Pontiff on whom the principal Head, Christ, confers the Pontifical dignity itself", the act of the whole Church as pillar of truth, and so "cannot be deceived." It is "a kind of* ***virtual revelation***, *applying to that universal proposition … by the deed itself as it were subsuming … and infallibly explicating this particular proposition … by the deed itself as it were concluding" — "indeed it is a kind of* ***formal revelation***, *according to that saying:* vox Populi, vox DEI*." The renunciation clause is glossed too: an "internal or private" renunciation does not suffice; it must be "external and public" and "morally certain."*

***What the acceptance certifies (§ 64) — the point that divides this cluster.*** *The objection is the occult-defect argument: the universal does not reveal "that this individual Pontiff is a man, duly baptized, duly ordained." Bartis distinguishes before and after: "Before the election is confirmed by the acceptance, it is a human work, depending on the testimony of a few and subject to many doubts … But after the election is confirmed by the acceptance,* ***the elected Pontiff is now a divine work and no longer a human one***, *and by that very fact confirmed by Christ, so that the acceptance, being equivalent to a divine revelation, infallibly manifests to us that the elected Pontiff is a true Pontiff, subject to no doubts, and consequently that he is a man, duly baptized, duly ordained, etc.;* ***for all these are contained formally implicitly in the quiddity of a true Pontiff***." *This is [Platel's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1680%20Platel) § 156 and the exact denial of [Sendín's](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1699%20Sendin) § 79, which had held that "even after the peaceful acceptance" it is not heretical to say several electors were unbaptized. The two test cases follow: Pope Joan is "a notable fiction, imported, as they say, from Utopia", John VIII having been called a woman "in mockery" for his timidity over Photius; Formosus "was not only a true Pope, but moreover (as Bellarmine is witness) distinguished in doctrine and holiness", and Stephen VII's synod acted "not from the chair of Peter but from the chair of pestilence", "a matter not of law but of fact", reprobated by John IX.*

***The circularity, and the headless Church (§ 65).*** *The objection is the one every negative author presses: the Church's infallibility depends on her Head, and "the incomplete Church does not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit with a view to deciding controversies of faith." Bartis's reply is the distinction* radicaliter / formaliter*: with a view to accepting her Head the Church has the assistance "independently of her Head himself, lest otherwise, against her character as the pillar and foundation of truth, she should err in accepting that Head of hers, and consequently also in the controversies of faith decided through him, as through a putative Judge of controversies who in reality is none"; for every other controversy she has it only radically before she has a Head and formally after. The headless Church therefore decides "this very controversy about the true Pontiff having been accepted, and those not distinct from it — such as that this Pontiff is a man and not a woman, duly baptized, duly ordained, etc. — by the acceptance itself, as being equivalent to a divine utterance,* ***though not signately, yet in exercise***, *leaving that to be decided signately by the Church, now made complete through the Head she has taken up." The Great Schism is met with a* ***three-mode typology***: *an antipope added to a peacefully accepted pope, who "has prescribed and remains in possession"; "the greatest part of the Church, and as it were the whole" accepting a pope against a king's pseudo-pope, where "although a partial Church errs, the whole Church nevertheless does not err"; and an almost equal division among two or three claimants, where "as none is a peaceful possessor of the true Papacy, so none is a true Pope, but they are mere Antipopes." A brief error would not expose the Church to false dogmas, because a doubtful pope's acts "neither have validity … nor do they acquire force unless they are approved and established by a certain Pontiff"; an error "through the whole course of a Pontificate" would, "because she could err in the same way about a second and a third" — and in any case "in no historian, unless a forger, does there exist any testimony" that the Church ever erred even briefly.*

***The instant of acceptance, and how much the Church must know (§ 66).*** *Against the objection that no new revelation occurs before, in, or after the acceptance, he answers that "the ancient and common revelation is applied to a new and particular material object", the acceptance "exercising a* ***threefold office***: *with respect to Christ, the office of a condition, that he may confer the Pontifical power on the newly elected; with respect to the newly elected, the office of a union with the first truth, as of a material object with the formal one; and with respect to us, the office of an application, proposing the newly elected as an object no longer remotely but proximately credible." Hence "although the first election can be annulled, the* ***second election*** *can no longer be annulled", the acceptance validating the first election "even one unjust and invalid by the lower human law — by the higher law, namely the natural and divine", on the analogy of prescription. And the Church need not know the election legitimate "positively and from a direct principle" but only "negatively and from a reflex principle": "whoever, all circumstances prudently weighed, is judged canonically elected, he must be accepted; but such is this individual man judged to be, namely* ***Cardinal Lorenzo Corsini***, *from now on canonically elected Supreme Pontiff, Clement XII; therefore he must be accepted." Election by the Cardinals is "prerequired by positive law as a condition" but is "by no means an infallible testimony" — the infallibility rests on the assistance of the Holy Spirit alone. The same paragraph carries an* ***excursus on the heretical pope***: *the more probable view is that he is not deposed ipso facto but only "when the sentence of the Church supervenes, not indeed a judicial but a declaratory one", since "it is nowhere read as expressed in divine law that a Pope who has become a heretic, even a manifest one, is by that very fact to be reckoned deposed by Christ"; "the Pope remains in possession as long as there does not supervene a declaration of the Church about his nullity — supposing, that is, that he can lapse into it."*

***The effect-only answer rejected, and the censure (§ 67).*** *Objection VI is the effect-guarantee thesis of the collection's School 2 put as an objection: even an illegitimate pope's decrees "would nevertheless still be valid, and the Church would be bound to believe them", because "the Holy Spirit would assist the Church for her own good." Bartis denies it outright. The law* Barbarius *lets common error validate the acts of a putative political prince, and even of a putative inferior pastor, because a superior can supply jurisdiction; but the Supreme Pastor "receives his power from Christ, as a Vicar from his Principal", and "the whole, or as it were the whole, ecclesiastical community can as little accept an illegitimate Vicarial Head of its principal Head as such an accepted Head can fail to be legitimate, or an unaccepted one fail to be illegitimate." The denier of Clement XII "would in truth be a material heretic", but not a formal one if he "either did not penetrate that such a Pontiff is formally implicitly revealed, or judged that such a revelation does not suffice for faith, and this inculpably" — which also explains why the contrary opinion was tolerated in the schools: formally implicit revelations "are not proposed through any rule of faith … but only through discourse", and the discourse "can be penetrated by one man and not by another." Then the reconciling turn: "there can be agreement in the thing and disagreement only in the word, as seems to happen here. For the Adversaries grant to the Pontiff the power of defining matters of faith, and to us this power seems to be the moral form constitutive of a true Pontiff; and so* ***we differ not in the thing but in the word***." *Future popes are of faith only "remotely, indeterminately, and conditionally"; the present one "proximately, determinately, and absolutely", the acceptance being "a testimony of deed as well, which in these circumstances is equivalent to divine speech."*

***Where he sits.*** ***Classification 1a***, *in the acceptance-as-revelation line of Arriaga, [Ojea](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1661%20Ojea) and Platel: the acceptance is a "second election" that supplies the defects of the first (Ojea § 56), a revelation "in exercise" (Platel § 155), and it reaches the facts themselves (Platel § 156, against Sendín § 79). What Bartis adds is the systematic form — the threefold-revelation apparatus, the Matt. 16:18 equivalence, the* radicaliter/formaliter *answer to the circularity, the three-mode schism typology, the three offices of the instant of acceptance — and the explicit rejection of the effect-only answer as an objection. The completeness clause is built in: "peacefully accepted by the whole Church, without just contradiction", the second schism-mode allowing "the greatest part … as it were the whole." He is the structural opposite of [Neesen](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1678%20Neesen) at every joint, and on the Alcalá affair his information is Neesen's want: he knows the 1601–02 outcome through Oviedo and treats it as the end of the question.*

## § 58 — the thesis, its pedigree, and Constance (printed 60)

**58** *I say:* Among the other proximate material objects of Theological Faith, and therefore in fact of faith, is this: that the Supreme Pontiff reigning for the time being, **legitimately elected and peacefully accepted by the Church**, and thus possessing his Pontificate as legitimately so peacefully, and not authentically renouncing it (such we revere today's Clement XII to be), is a true Pontiff, the successor of blessed Peter, and the Supreme visible Head of the whole Church militant, or, what is the same, the Supreme Vicar of Christ on earth. So, with Fr. Suárez, holds the common opinion today — **indeed, already from the time of Clement VIII, as Oviedo testifies, the unanimous opinion of theologians** — against Cajetan, Torquemada, Bañez, and some others. Proof 1, from the authority of the holy Councils, and above all of Constance, where the Bull of Martin V is set out […] And this not only in general, but also in particular, indeed in the singular, that is, in the individual, as the adduced words of the Bull indicate: *whoever he shall be for the time being*, and especially those placed within the parenthesis: *expressing his name*. […]

## § 61 — election as dispositive condition, acceptance as formal designation (printed 62–63)

**61** […] the *election* stands only *by way of a dispositive condition* for designating the person of the Pontiff — which disposition, being not that of the whole Church, nor that of the Head, at least as such, that is, as speaking from the chair, but that of a partial Church, which does not have the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, can be deceived. But the *acceptance* stands *by way of a formal designation* of that person of the Pontiff on whom the principal Head, Christ, confers the Pontifical dignity itself, the dignity of the Vicarial Head; and this designation, being that of the whole Church, and therefore of her who, as *the pillar of truth*, has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, cannot be deceived. Rather it is a kind of *virtual revelation*, applying to that universal proposition, *formally explicitly revealed*, *every Pontiff legitimately elected and peacefully accepted is a true Pontiff*, etc., by the deed itself as it were subsuming: but *this individual Pontiff is legitimately elected and peacefully accepted*; and, from that same proposition, infallibly explicating this particular, or singular, proposition, *formally implicitly revealed* in it, by the deed itself as it were concluding: therefore *this individual Pontiff is a true Pontiff*, etc. **Indeed it is a kind of *formal revelation*, according to that saying: *vox Populi, vox DEI*.** […]

## § 64 — what the acceptance certifies (printed 66)

Reply to the argument: I distinguish the antecedent. *Before the acceptance*: I concede it; *after the acceptance*: I deny the antecedent and the consequence. Before the election is confirmed by the acceptance, it is a human work, depending on the testimony of a few and subject to many doubts, such as those mentioned in the objection. But after the election is confirmed by the acceptance, **the elected Pontiff is now a divine work and no longer a human one**, and by that very fact confirmed by Christ, so that the acceptance, being equivalent to a divine revelation, infallibly manifests to us that the elected Pontiff is a true Pontiff, subject to no doubts, and consequently that he is a man, duly baptized, duly ordained, etc.; **for all these are contained formally implicitly in the quiddity of a true Pontiff.**

## § 65 — the headless Church, and the three modes of schism (printed 68–69)

[…] although the incomplete Church cannot decide the other controversies of faith, distinct from this one about the true Pontiff having been accepted, except *radically*, nevertheless she decides this very controversy about the true Pontiff having been accepted, and those not distinct from it — such as that this Pontiff is a man and not a woman, duly baptized, duly ordained, etc. — by the acceptance itself, as being equivalent to a divine utterance, **though not *signately*, yet *in exercise***; leaving that to be decided *signately* by the Church, now made complete through the Head she has taken up. […]

[…] such a schism can arise in three ways. First, that to one man legitimately elected and peacefully accepted a second is added; and in this case the first has prescribed and remains in possession of the true Papacy, against the later schismatic Antipope. Secondly, that the greatest part of the Church, and as it were the whole, accepts a Pontiff, apart from one or another king, the author of a schism, for whose sake some Cardinals, withdrawing from the true Pope, go over to a Pseudo-Pope; and in this case, **although a partial Church errs, the whole Church nevertheless does not err**, the right standing on the side of that Pontiff who was accepted by the greatest part of the Church, and so by what is as it were the whole, and who is in reality the true one, against the Pseudo-Popes. Thirdly, that when two or three are sitting, contradiction is made almost equally from the two or three parties; and in this case, as none is a peaceful possessor of the true Papacy, so none is a true Pope, but they are mere Antipopes.

## § 66 — the instant of acceptance; the heretical pope; Cardinal Corsini (printed 71–73)

[…] one who has turned unbeliever is, according to some, probably by that very fact deposed by Christ, who does not will his lambs to be fed by wolves; according to others, however, more probably not by that very fact, but only when the *sentence* of the Church supervenes, not indeed a *judicial* but a *declaratory* one. For it is nowhere read as expressed in divine law that a Pope who has become a heretic, even a manifest one, is by that very fact to be reckoned deposed by Christ […] **The Pope remains in possession as long as there does not supervene a declaration of the Church about his nullity**, on account of a lapse into manifest heresy — supposing, that is, that he can lapse into it. […]

[…] When a new Pope is accepted by the Church, in that instant the ancient and common revelation is applied to a new and particular material object […] the *acceptance* itself exercising a threefold office: with respect to Christ, the office of a *condition*, that he may confer the Pontifical power on the newly elected; with respect to the newly elected, the office of a *union* with the first truth, as of a material object with the formal one; and with respect to us, the office of an *application*, proposing the newly elected as an object no longer *remotely* but *proximately* credible. And so, although the first election can be annulled, **the second election can no longer be annulled** […] For the Church to accept the man elected, it is not required that she be able to demonstrate the election made to be legitimate […] but it suffices that by prudent conjectures it be established that the election is not illegitimate, even if in reality it were so, with this dictate supervening: whoever, all circumstances prudently weighed, is judged canonically elected, he must be accepted; but such is this individual man judged to be, namely **Cardinal Lorenzo Corsini**, from now on canonically elected Supreme Pontiff, Clement XII; therefore he must be accepted […] And when upon this the acceptance itself at last follows, the voice of the people, which has the infallible assistance of the Holy Spirit, being equivalent to the divine voice, **it comes about that such a man's being Pontiff is of faith.**

## § 67 — the effect-only answer rejected; material and formal heresy; why the contrary was tolerated (printed 73–76)

**67** Objection VI. Even if the Church were to accept some illegitimate Pontiff, his decrees would nevertheless still be valid, and the Church would be bound to believe them; therefore. […] Reply to the argument: I deny the antecedent. For although a common error can avail to make the acts of a putative political prince valid, according to the law *Barbarius*, **a common error cannot avail to make valid the acts of a putative *Supreme* ecclesiastical Prince.** […] the whole, or as it were the whole, ecclesiastical community can as little accept an illegitimate Vicarial Head of its principal Head as such an accepted Head can fail to be legitimate, or an unaccepted one fail to be illegitimate. […]

[…] One who denied that the Pontiff reigning for the time being, legitimately elected and peacefully accepted, etc., is a true Pontiff would in truth be a *material heretic*, because he would deny an object in reality sufficiently revealed by God. If, however, he either did not penetrate that such a Pontiff is formally implicitly revealed, or judged that such a revelation does not suffice for faith, and this inculpably, he would not at all be a *formal heretic* […] there can be agreement in the thing and disagreement only in the word, as seems to happen here. For the Adversaries grant to the Pontiff the power of defining matters of faith, and to us this power seems to be the moral form constitutive of a true Pontiff; and so **we differ not in the thing but in the word.** […] although the older authors taught the opposite opinion, whether by way of disputation or by way of assertion, nevertheless **from the time of Clement VIII, as I intimated in the conclusion, no one among the more recent authors has dared, on Oviedo's testimony, to teach the opposite**, especially in view of the Councils cited in the same conclusion; so that the opinion of the Adversaries seems in truth to be no longer tolerated by the Church.
