*Note on this entry: Paolo Comitoli, S.J. (1545–1626), of Perugia, was among the first generation of Jesuit casuists; his* Responsa moralia *(Lyon 1609) digests decades of consultations into numbered questions. Quaestio XCIX is, so far as this collection reaches, the earliest* ***dedicated quaestio*** *on the UPA question — not a digression inside a treatise on faith or the pope, but a stand-alone question titled* "Whether it must be believed by divine and Catholic faith, or not, that the Roman Pontiff who at this or at another time rules the multitude of the faithful is the legitimate Vicar of Christ" *— and Comitoli tells us why he wrote it: "not once, nor twice, but frequently I have been pressed by many with this question," a dateable trace of how live the dispute was around 1600, in the same years the Protestant controversialists were impugning the titles of the reigning popes (see the 1616 Malderus "noui homines" passage and the 1632 Turnbull entry). His thesis makes both the electors and the Church's embrace constitutive: the pope "chosen by those to whom belongs the right of electing,* ***and whom the Christian Church has embraced*** *[quem est Christiana complexa Ecclesia], is the most true Vicar of Christ: and this must be believed by each and every one* ***by that faith which can admit nothing false beneath it***." Fourteen rationes follow — the visible Church requires a de fide visible head; the succession is believed "by Catholic faith, not merely human," and consists of these men; councils and ex-cathedra decrees hang on the convoking and decreeing pope; canonizations; the English martyrs under Gregory XIII died "for the cause of the faith" in dying for a particular pope's authority; the Roman cathedra of the Pius IV profession "is no cathedra except that which this or that Pontiff holds"; the monarchy needs an actual monarch; Trent sess. 23 cann. 6 and 8; and the* postrema*: the denier of Clement VIII's title "would sin not against human faith, but against the orthodox faith… and* ***would be burned in the flames as a true heretic***" — fixing composition under Clement VIII (1592–1605). The objections he answers are the classics: the private-priest disparity; the* ***baptism objection*** *("the Catholic faith imposes upon no one the necessity of believing that the Roman Pontiff has been baptized") — "shattered by* ***conversion*** *[conuersione eliditur argumentum]: since by the Catholic faith we are compelled to confess that there is a true and legitimate vicar of Christ… a necessary consequence now compels the conclusion that he is baptized, orthodox, a male"; and the simony-nullity constitutions of Julius II and Pius IV — answered by election-providence a decade before Coriolano (1621): God "either* ***will not suffer vices of this kind to fall upon the election; or, if they should fall upon it, will straightway uncover them***." Quaestio XCVIII is included as the frame: the pope cannot appoint his own successor (Turrecremata, Waldensis, Ockham, Almain, Cajetan, Panormitanus, St. Antoninus surveyed; if the college of Cardinals were extinct, St. Antoninus would have the princes gather a council of bishops for the election), closing on the canon of Anacletus: "The Lord reserved to Himself the election of the highest Priests" — the election is God's act carried out through the Church, which is why q. 99's providence-answer works. Comitoli's q. 99 became a standard census citation: Verricelli (1653, tract. 8 q. 24 n. 7 — the repo's 1653 entry) cites "Comitolus responsorum lib. 1. qu. 99" by name, and Diana's affirmative census (reported in the repo's 1684 Murga entry, n. 79) lists him. Classification is* ***1a*** *(divine and Catholic faith, "cui nullum potest subesse falsum," with denial censured as heresy).*

## Quaestio XCIX — *Romanum Pontificem qui hoc alióue tempore multitudinem regit fidelium, fide an ne diuina & Catholica credi oporteat legitimum esse Christi Vicarium*

**2.** [...] **This opinion, therefore, has for many years now been firmly settled in my mind: that the Roman Pontiff, chosen by those to whom belongs the right of electing, and whom the Christian Church has embraced, is the most true Vicar of Christ: and that this must be believed by each and every one by that faith which can admit nothing false beneath it.** [...]

**3.** [...] The eighth. **We believe by Catholic faith that the Roman chair is the interpreter of religion, the incorrupt mistress of sacred things and of morals: but there is no Roman chair except that which this or that Pontiff holds, whom the Church chooses for herself** [...] **therefore we must believe, by a faith which cannot err, that this or that man is the true and legitimate prelate of that same chair.** [...]

The last reason. **Whoever should utterly deny that Clement VIII, whom the Catholic Church obeys, is either the head of the Church, or the true successor of Peter, or the vicar of Christ, would sin not against human faith, but against the orthodox faith, which can admit nothing false beneath it, and would be burned in the flames as a true heretic** [...]

**4.** The subtle opiners add: And yet the Catholic faith imposes upon no one the necessity of believing that the Roman Pontiff has been baptized: but without baptism he cannot be the true and legitimate vicar of Christ [...] **But the argument is shattered by conversion: for since by the Catholic faith we are compelled to confess that there is a true and legitimate vicar of Christ in the Catholic Church, on account of the so many reasons adduced, a necessary consequence now compels the conclusion that he is baptized, that he is orthodox, that he is a male: and that he is all that without which the Pontifical power cannot stand.**

**5.** [...] I respond that **God, who by a certain special providence presides over the election of His vicar, and governs the inclinations and votes of the electors, either will not suffer vices of this kind to fall upon the election; or, if they should fall upon it, will straightway uncover them: lest her own legitimate spouse, parent, and ruler be lacking to the Church.** [...]
