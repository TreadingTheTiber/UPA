*Note on this entry: Jan van Malderen (Malderus, 1563–1633) held the royal chair of scholastic theology at Louvain from 1596 and was bishop of Antwerp from 1611; this commentary on the 2.2 (Antwerp 1616) distills his Louvain course, and its appended* Tractatus de Summo Pontifice *(eight disputations attached to q. 1 a. 10) is where the UPA question surfaces. The census lists him on the negative side, and he does deny the* de fide *note — but what he affirms in its place makes him a textbook* ***1b-iii***: *in Disp. V ("Whether the Roman Pontiff can err in deciding questions of faith"), the fourth objection is the classic hidden-defect argument ("it is not de fide, nor otherwise altogether most certainly established, that he was truly baptized, and hence neither whether he is capable of the Episcopal dignity"), and his answer concedes the note while rescuing the fact with an infallible-but-not-revealed certainty: for the pope's determinations to be de fide "it is not required that it be of divine faith that he who determines is the true Pontiff,* ***but it suffices that this be most certain [certissimum]. Now it is most certain that he whom the universal Church receives as Pontiff is the true Pontiff. For it pertains to the providence of God to prevent the Church from being deceived in a judgment of this kind.***" The certainty attending the* propositio fidei *need not equal the certainty of faith, "but it suffices that the credibility be made evident through arguments of the kind* ***that indeed cannot deceive***, *though they be not believed by divine faith. For this suffices* ***not only for the infallibility of faith, but also for the obligation: so that he who does not believe incurs the sin of heresy or of infidelity***" — the fact-guarantee entire, one note below divine faith, with denial still censured. And he tells us who was pressing the objection: "***In vain, therefore, do new men, certain blasphemous apostates, raise a doubt about the legitimate election of the Pontiffs of this age, whom they have experienced as opposed to themselves. Received by the Catholic Church, they are beyond the hazard of this calumny***" — direct testimony that impugning the reigning popes' elections was a Protestant weapon by 1616, fifteen years before Baron's* Apodixis *(see the 1632 Turnbull entry) and the popes in question being those who had moved against the Reformation (Pius V through Paul V). The Disp. IV Dub. III objection-block is included as the companion piece: it carries the conciliarist maxim ("the whole is greater than its part… therefore the Council which represents the whole universal Church is greater than the Pontiff") with Malderus's answer ("we confess the body is greater in bulk than the whole head, but* ***the head is greater in power than the rest of the body***"), and, at the solution to the third item, his handling of the genuinely doubtful case — the completeness clause: we contend only "that a Synod cannot judge concerning an* ***undoubted*** *Pontiff as its subject, leaving in the middle whether, in a schism,* ***when it is doubtful who is the true Pontiff and several claim to have been legitimately elected***, *a General Synod can compel such ambiguous Pontiffs to renounce their claimed right." Disp. V also carries his seven senses of Luke 22:32 and the rule that the pope, defining from the chair, never omits the due inquiry ("who promises the end promises also the necessary means"). Classification is* ***1b-iii*** *(fact-guarantee by infallible certainty below divine faith, grounded in providence, obliging under pain of the sin of heresy or infidelity).*

## Disputatio V — *An Romanus Pontifex possit errare in decidendis fidei quæstionibus?*

{marg. *Obiect. 4.*} It is objected, Fourthly: It is not of faith that this Pontiff is the true Pontiff: therefore his determinations will never be *de fide*. The antecedent is proved: because **it is not *de fide*, nor otherwise altogether most certainly established, that he was truly baptized, and hence neither whether he is capable of the Episcopal dignity.** [...]

{marg. *Respons.*} [...] I respond, therefore, rather by denying the consequence. For in order that what the Pope determines be of faith, **it is not required that it be of divine faith that he who determines is the true Pontiff, but it suffices that this be most certain. Now it is most certain that he whom the universal Church receives as Pontiff is the true Pontiff. For it pertains to the providence of God to prevent the Church from being deceived in a judgment of this kind. In vain, therefore, do new men, certain blasphemous apostates, raise a doubt about the legitimate election of the Pontiffs of this age, whom they have experienced as opposed to themselves. Received by the Catholic Church, they are beyond the hazard of this calumny.** [...] it suffices that the credibility be made evident through arguments of the kind **that indeed cannot deceive**, though they be not believed by divine faith. For this suffices **not only for the infallibility of faith, but also for the obligation: so that he who does not believe incurs the sin of heresy or of infidelity.**

## Disputatio IV, Dub. III — the third objection-block

{marg. 5.} Moreover, The whole, they say, is greater than its part, and the world greater than the city; therefore **the Council, which represents the whole universal Church, is greater than the Pontiff, who is only a part of the Church.** [...] {marg. *Ad 5.*} But as to the urging that the world is greater than the city: we confess that the body is greater in bulk than the whole head, but **the head is greater in power than the rest of the body.**

{marg. *Ad 3.*} To the deed of Henry I respond that we here contend only this, that **a Synod cannot judge concerning an undoubted Pontiff as one subject to itself; leaving in the middle whether, in a schism, when it is doubtful who is the true Pontiff, and several claim to have been legitimately elected, a General Synod can compel such ambiguous Pontiffs to renounce their claimed right.**
