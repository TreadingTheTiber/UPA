# Turrianus — Selectae Disputationes, Pars I, Disp. XXXV (Lyon 1634)

**Latin–English parallel text: Dubium unicum, "An sit de fide hunc numero hominem esse Pontificem" (printed pp. 218b–219a)**

| *Latin* | | *English* |
|-------|---|--------|
| [col. b] | | [col. b] |
| **DISPVTATIO XXXV. — SCHOLASTICA. — DVBIVM VNICVM.** | | **DISPUTATION XXXV. — SCHOLASTIC. — SOLE DOUBT.** |
| *An sit de fide hunc numero hominem esse Pontificem.* | | *Whether it is of faith that this-in-number man is Pontiff.* |
| DIFFICVLTAS hæc tractari solet ex instituto ab Scholasticis in materia *de fide* 2. 2. *ad quæstionem* 1. & ab iis, qui disputant contra hæreticos agentibus de dignitate Pontificis. | | THIS difficulty is wont to be treated of set purpose by the Scholastics in the matter *de fide*, on 2. 2. at question 1., and by those who dispute against heretics when they treat of the dignity of the Pontiff. |
| Et apud Catholicos certum est, esse non solùm temerarium, & scandalosum, sed errori proximum, negare, hunc numero hominem receptum sine controuersia ab Ecclesia ex electione, esse verum Pontificem. | | And among Catholics it is certain that it is **not only temerarious and scandalous, but proximate to error**, to deny that **this-in-number man, received without controversy by the Church from the election**, is the true Pontiff. |
| Quæstio tamen est, an sit de fide istum esse Pontificem, ita vt contrarium sit hæresis. | | The question, however, is whether it is **of faith** that this man is Pontiff, in such wise that the contrary is **heresy**. |
| Et sanè ante Clementem VIII. communis Scholasticorum sensus, præsertim eorum, qui scripta sua typis mandarunt, fuit, non esse de fide. & citantur à recentioribus ferè triginta autores ex diuersis Academiis Ecclesiæ, in Gallia, Germania, Italia, Hispania, qui ita senserunt, vt omittam multos alios, qui ita docuerunt in suis manuscriptis. Bellarmin. *libr. 4. de Pontific. cap.* 12. Canus ita docuit 6. *de locis. cap. vltimo*, quem vt magistrum secuti sunt religiosi doctissimi ex familia Dominicana, & Bañes, ita docuit Cani discipulos 2. 2. *ad quæstionem* 1. *in articulum* 10, *in prioribus commentarijs*, *dub.* 2. *ad* 2. & Vega, ex familia Franciscana, *libr. 9. in Tridentin. cap.* 39. & qui simul cum Cano interfuerunt Concilio Tridentino, vbi Patrum illius Concilij cognouerunt sententiam. | | And indeed, **before Clement VIII, the common sense of the Scholastics, especially of those who committed their writings to print, was that it is not of faith**. And **nearly thirty authors** are cited by more recent writers, from the various Academies of the Church, **in Gaul, Germany, Italy, and Spain**, who so held — to say nothing of many others who so taught in their manuscripts. Bellarmin. *libr. 4. de Pontific. cap.* 12. Cano so taught, 6. *de locis. cap. vltimo*, whom, as their master, most learned religious of the Dominican family have followed; and Bañes so taught, [a] disciple of Cano, 2. 2. *ad quæstionem* 1. *in articulum* 10, *in prioribus commentarijs*, *dub.* 2. *ad* 2. And Vega, of the Franciscan family, *libr. 9. in Tridentin. cap.* 39. And who, together with Cano, **were present at the Council of Trent, where they came to know the mind of the Fathers of that Council**. |
| Et præcipuum argumentum pro hac sententia illud fuit quòd non appareat principium aliquod de fide certum, quo probetur hunc numero hominem esse Pontificem ex certitudine fidei. nam licèt sit certum de fide, legitimum successorem S. Petri esse verum Pontificem, tamen quod iste [col. a] numero homo sit legitimus Petri successor, solùm videtur constare fide humana, & iudicio prudenti, quo iudicamus electionem illius fuisse legitimam. | | And the chief argument for this opinion was this: that there appears no principle certain of faith by which it might be proved, from the certitude of faith, that this-in-number man is Pontiff. For although it is certain of faith that the legitimate successor of St. Peter is the true Pontiff, nevertheless that this [col. a] in-number man is the legitimate successor of Peter seems to be established only by human faith, and by a prudent judgment, whereby we judge that his election was legitimate. |
| Et explicari potest à simili, quia licèt sit de fide hostiam ritè consecratam continere verum corpus Christi; tamen quòd hæc numero hostia contineat corpus Christi, non est de fide absolutè, quandiu non constat esse legitimè consecratam. | | And it can be explained from a similar case: for although it is of faith that a host duly consecrated contains the true body of Christ, nevertheless that this-in-number host contains the body of Christ is not of faith absolutely, so long as it is not established that it has been legitimately consecrated. |
| Sed hoc tempore post Clementem VIII. communis sententia est esse de fide vnum numero hominem, v. g. Vrbanum VIII. esse summum Ecclesiæ Pontificem: loquuntur autem Doctores huius opinionis de homine recepto ab Ecclesia, vt Pontifice communi sensu, & sine vlla controuersia. Præcipuum argumentum pro hac sententia est, quia ex contraria opinione sequeretur definitionem Pontificis circa doctrinam, & materiam fidei non habere sufficientem certitudinem, quia non esset certum definitionem illam procedere à vero Pontifice, & habente legitimam autoritatem ad definiendas, & declarandas res fidei. Ex quo dubio possent sequi plura absurda, & dubitationes animi etiam in hominibus piis, & bene affectis erga res Ecclesiæ. quam rationem fusè explicant aliqui recentiores. legantur P. Suarez in materia *de fide*, tractans hoc dubium, & alij non pauci, qui post Clementem VIII. scripsere. | | **But in this time, after Clement VIII, the common opinion is that it is of faith that one man in number — for example, Urban VIII — is the supreme Pontiff of the Church**: and the Doctors of this opinion speak **of a man received by the Church as Pontiff by common consent, and without any controversy**. The chief argument for this opinion is that from the contrary opinion it would follow that the Pontiff's definition concerning doctrine and the matter of faith does not have sufficient certitude, because it would not be certain that that definition proceeds from a true Pontiff, and from one having legitimate authority for defining and declaring matters of faith. From which doubt several absurdities could follow, and doubts of mind even in men who are pious and well affected toward the affairs of the Church. Which reason some more recent writers explain at length. Let Fr. Suarez be read, in the matter *de fide*, where he treats this doubt, and not a few others who have written after Clement VIII. |
| Mihi placet hæc posterior opinio, & ratio illius, quia quando homo receptus est ab Ecclesia sine vlla controuersia, recipit à Christo potestatem definiendi iurisdictionem in totam Ecclesiam ex Christi institutione, aliàs nihil esset firmum in Ecclesia ex determinatione, aut ordinatione Pontificis. | | **This latter opinion pleases me**, and its reason: because when a man has been **received by the Church without any controversy**, he receives from Christ the power of defining, jurisdiction over the whole Church, by Christ's institution; otherwise nothing would be firm in the Church from the Pontiff's determination or ordinance. |
| Et ideo non est necessarium quod existimauit P. Valentia, necessarium esse, vt hic homo definiat, vel faciat aliquid in ordine ad totam Ecclesiam, vt habeatur tanquam certus Pontifex, sed sufficit sola electio, & acceptatio Ecclesiæ sine vlla controuersia. | | And therefore it is not necessary, as Fr. Valencia thought it necessary, that this man should define, or do something in relation to the whole Church, in order that he be held as a certain Pontiff; **but election alone, and the acceptance of the Church without any controversy, suffice**. |
| Et ex hac ratione soluitur ratio contrariæ opinionis, quia in Sacramento Eucharistiæ non est aliquid in particulari certum, ex quo colligamus in hac numero hostia contineri Christum, certitudine fidei: at in nostro casu in homine sic electo est potestas definiendi & gubernandi totam Ecclesiam. Ex qua colligitur certitudine fidei hominem sic electum esse Pontificem. | | And from this reasoning the reason of the contrary opinion is resolved: because in the Sacrament of the Eucharist there is nothing certain in particular from which we might gather, with the certitude of faith, that Christ is contained in this-in-number host; whereas **in our case, in the man thus elected there is the power of defining and of governing the whole Church. From which is gathered, with the certitude of faith, that the man thus elected is Pontiff.** |
| Duo possent breuiter quæri, vnum est, an ante Clementem VIII. prima opinio fuerit probabilis. Alterum est, an post Clementem VIII. maneat probabilis. | | Two things might briefly be asked. One is whether, before Clement VIII, the first opinion was probable. The other is whether, after Clement VIII, it remains probable. |
| Circa primum tempore Clementis ab omnibus ferè viris doctis Hispaniæ iudicata est ea opinio vel vera, vel valde probabilis, nullámque merens censuram. | | As to the first: in the time of Clement, that opinion was judged by nearly all the learned men of Spain either true, or highly probable, and **deserving no censure**. |
| Circa secundum vnus, vel alter Doctor iudicauit non esse probabilem. Alij tamen communiter contrarium docuerunt, eo quòd non appareat vel damnatio Pontificis, vel prohibitio eius doctrinæ, vt vnusquisque ex principiis generalibus possit ferre iudicium. Ego verò iudico, hoc tempore non posse sine scandalo defendi rem non esse de fide. | | As to the second: one or another Doctor has judged it not to be probable. Others, however, have commonly taught the contrary, on the ground that there appears neither a condemnation by a Pontiff, nor a prohibition of his doctrine, such that each one may pass judgment from general principles. **But I judge that at this time it cannot be defended without scandal that the thing is not of faith.** |
