- *Selection: **Pars I, Disputatio XXXV, Scholastica, Dubium unicum, "An sit de fide hunc numero hominem esse Pontificem"** — the disputation entire, printed p. 218 col. b through p. 219 col. a. Printed page = PDF page − 28. The disputation begins below the rule closing Disp. XXXIV and ends at the foot of p. 219 col. a, in a tapering* cul-de-lampe *setting, immediately before Disp. XXXVI opens in the right column.* Two appendices carry the papal-inerrancy background from elsewhere in the same volume:* ***Appendix A***, **Disp. XXX, Dub. III, "An Ecclesia possit errare in assignandis censuris propositionum"** *(printed 149–150 — the locus* ***Cardinal Manning*** *cites as "pars i. disp. xxx. dub. 3, p. 149, Lyons, 1634"); and* ***Appendix B***, *the four propositions on the Pontiff from the volume's own* **Censura Theologica** *(printed 150, 157). The whole volume was swept: its res-and-verba index confirms the main locus under its own headword —* "**An sit de fide hunc numero hominem esse Pontificem. 218**" *— and there is* ***no occurrence of* schisma *anywhere in either Part***, *no treatment of the Great Western Schism, of Martin V, or of Constance except a citation of the Wyclif censures at printed 146, and no* acceptatio *or* receptus ab Ecclesia *locus outside Disp. XXXV itself.*

*Note on this entry: Luis de Torres, S.J. (1562–1655) held two offices that the title page prints in sequence, and both bear on what he writes:* ***formerly Primary Professor in the College of Alcalá*** (olim in Collegio Complutensi Professoris primarij), ***now by office Calificator of the Supreme Council of the Holy Inquisition*** (nunc supremi Senatus sanctæ Inquisitionis Calificatoris ex munere). *A calificador is precisely the officer whose competence is the* ***qualification of propositions*** *— the assigning of theological notes and censures — and this volume advertises on its own title page an appended* censura quædam Theologica de qualitate multarum propositionum, per centurias dubiorum distincta. *The disputation below is therefore a note-judgment delivered by the man whose profession was note-judgments, and it is the only text in this collection that* ***periodizes the whole controversy and takes a census of the other side***.*

*Its structure is a history in five moves.* ***First, the floor.*** *Before any dispute begins, Torres fixes what no Catholic may deny: it is "**not only temerarious and scandalous, but proximate to error**, to deny that **this-in-number man, received without controversy by the Church from the election**, is the true Pontiff." That is the* ***shared minimum of both schools***: *the disputed question was never whether the reigning pope may be denied, but only what note attaches to the denial. "The question, however, is whether it is* ***of faith*** *that this man is Pontiff, in such wise that the contrary is* ***heresy***."*

***Second, the census of the negative.*** *"Before Clement VIII, the common sense of the Scholastics, especially of those who committed their writings to print, was that it is* ***not*** *of faith." He then does what no other author in this collection does — he counts them: "**nearly thirty authors** are cited by more recent writers, from the various Academies of the Church, **in Gaul, Germany, Italy, and Spain**, who so held — to say nothing of many others who so taught in their manuscripts." Four are named, and every one of them but the last already has an entry here:* ***Bellarmine***, libr. 4. de Pontific. cap. 12 *(see below);* ***Cano***, 6. de locis, cap. vltimo *(the [1563 Cano](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1563%20Cano) entry), "whom, as their master, most learned religious of the Dominican family have followed";* ***Bañez***, 2. 2. ad quæstionem 1. in articulum 10, in prioribus commentarijs, dub. 2. ad 2. *(the [1586 Báñez](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1586%20Banez) entry — and the printed "in prioribus commentarijs" is exact, the* prior *commentary being the one Báñez himself clarified in 1602); and* ***Vega, of the Franciscan family***, libr. 9. in Tridentin. cap. 39 — *with the added weight that these "**were present, together with Cano, at the Council of Trent, where they came to know the mind of the Fathers of that Council**." This is the negative school's own roll-call, given by an opponent who does not diminish it.*

***Third, the turn, and its cause.*** *"**But in this time, after Clement VIII, the common opinion is that it is of faith that one man in number — for example, Urban VIII — is the supreme Pontiff of the Church**: and the Doctors of this opinion speak **of a man received by the Church as Pontiff by common consent, and without any controversy**." The reason is the definition-certitude argument: on the contrary opinion "the Pontiff's definition concerning doctrine and the matter of faith does not have sufficient certitude, because it would not be certain that that definition proceeds from a true Pontiff" — from which "several absurdities could follow, and doubts of mind even in men who are pious and well affected toward the affairs of the Church." And he names who did it: "**Let Fr. Suarez be read**, in the matter* de fide, *where he treats this doubt, and not a few others who have written after Clement VIII" — the [1583 Suárez](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1583%20Suarez) entry's* De fide *disp. 10, the same locus Carena would cite in 1668.*

***Fourth, his own adhesion, and two refinements that matter.*** *"**This latter opinion pleases me**, and its reason: because when a man has been **received by the Church without any controversy**, he receives from Christ the power of defining, jurisdiction over the whole Church, by Christ's institution; otherwise nothing would be firm in the Church from the Pontiff's determination or ordinance." The first refinement is a* ***named rejection of the exercise-condition***: *"it is not necessary, as* ***Fr. Valencia*** *thought it necessary, that this man should define, or do something in relation to the whole Church, in order that he be held as a certain Pontiff; **but election alone, and the acceptance of the Church without any controversy, suffice**." Gregory of Valencia's requirement that the claimant must first* act *toward the universal Church is the collection's* ***2→1a*** *position; Torres rejects it by name and dates the rejection. The second refinement is his answer to the negative side's best argument, the Eucharistic parallel — as one cannot know of faith that* this *host is consecrated, so not of* this *man that he is pope. Torres breaks the analogy on the* ***effect***: *"in the Sacrament of the Eucharist there is nothing certain in particular from which we might gather, with the certitude of faith, that Christ is contained in this-in-number host; whereas **in our case, in the man thus elected there is the power of defining and of governing the whole Church. From which is gathered, with the certitude of faith, that the man thus elected is Pontiff**." The jurisdiction actually exercised over the whole Church is the visible term the host lacks — the fact/effect disjunction of the later authors, compressed into two sentences.*

***Fifth, and most valuable, the censure-history — told straight.*** *He asks the two questions a calificador would ask: was the negative opinion probable* ***before*** *Clement VIII, and does it remain probable* ***after***? *On the first: "in the time of Clement, that opinion was judged by nearly all the learned men of Spain either true, or highly probable, and* ***deserving no censure***." *On the second: "one or another Doctor has judged it not to be probable. Others, however, have commonly taught the contrary, on the ground that* ***there appears neither a condemnation by a Pontiff, nor a prohibition of his doctrine***, *such that each one may pass judgment from general principles." And then his own verdict, set in the tapering close of the column: "**But I judge that at this time it cannot be defended without scandal that the thing is not of faith.**" The Inquisition's own qualifier concedes that no formal condemnation exists, declines to invent one, and rests the case on the note* ***scandalous*** *rather than* ***heretical*** *— the same candour as Pueroni's n. 54 ("no Pontiff has ever pronounced upon it... not even in time of schism") and the Salmanticenses' and Palanco's later admission that the negative opinion as such was never condemned.*

***The Alcalá connection — Torres was not an observer of the affair but a principal in it.*** *The proposition denounced to Rome from Alcalá in 1602 — the founding document of this whole dispute, printed in the [1601 Alcalá Theses](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1601%20Alcala%20Theses) dossier — reads, in these formal words: "**It is not of faith that this numerically singular Pope, for example Clement VIII, is the true Pope**." Three independent witnesses now place Torres inside that affair. [Oviedo](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Francis%20of%20Oviedo) (1651), narrating the Alcalá act, records that after Dean De la Cámara and the regent Villegas "*Subscripsere postea P. Vasquez, & P. Torres*" — the two Jesuits who subscribed the theses afterward, at what the dossier records as the Jesuit re-defence of 7 March 1602. Torres's own 1621* In secundam secundae *styles him "*Luisij Turriani **Complutensis**, è Societate Iesu*" throughout its licences, opens its preface "*in Complutensi schola nostra*," and speaks of Alcalá regents "*quem ego vidi… pugnacissime defendentem*" — he was the Jesuit Torres teaching in the Alcalá college in exactly those years (the patrologist Francisco de Torres, whom one of our translations had supplied as the subscriber, died in 1584). And the* New Catholic Encyclopedia *(P. K. Meagher) states it outright: Torres "**had his student D. de Oñate defend the thesis that it was not of Catholic faith that a particular person, for example, Clement VIII, was the legitimate pope**," the charge being that it "cast doubts upon the legitimacy of Clement's title," and that "**both Torres and Oñate were imprisoned by the Inquisition**" and "released in 1603 with a stern reprimand." (The accounts differ on that last point: Gaspar Hurtado, the original defendant, later denied that anyone was imprisoned, and both he and Ojea report that the accused were "**declared innocent and unharmed, and the doctrine sound**" by judges Clement VIII designated in Spain; the entry records both and does not adjudicate.) So when Torres writes in 1634 that in the time of Clement the negative "**was judged by nearly all the learned men of Spain either true, or highly probable, and deserving no censure**," he is describing his own case; and when he writes that the common opinion is now that it is of faith that "*one man in number, **for example Urban VIII**,*" is Pope, he is writing back — with the reigning pope substituted and the verdict reversed — the very sentence his own student had defended under him thirty-three years before.*

***The switch, and what it is worth.*** *The master of the negative thesis in 1601 is, in 1634, the Calificator who judges that the negative "**cannot be defended without scandal**." That is a change of position, and it should be counted as one — the collection's clearest instance of an author who held the negative and abandoned it, alongside Hurtado, whom Ojea reports "*retractata ea quam voce defenderat in Complutensi Academia*." Two cautions keep it in proportion. Torres's 1621 commentary, which treats the pope's dignity, election and deposition, doctrinal power, canonizations and beatifications across Disputations XV–XVIII, contains* ***no dubium on the singular pope at all*** *— so the 1634 text is his first treatment of the question in print, and the interval between the act and the retraction cannot be narrowed from his own works. And the* New Catholic Encyclopedia *adds that the Jesuit General* ***Mutius Vitelleschi had the 1634* Disputationes selectae *withdrawn from circulation***, *judging that Torres condemned opposing opinions "without taking the trouble to acquaint himself sufficiently with their foundations" — which is presumably the appended* Censura *of some 352 propositions, and which explains both why the volume is rare and why later authors cite his 1621 work rather than this one.*

***How Carena reads him.*** *The [1668 Carena](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1668%20Carena) entry — the Holy Office's Fiscal Advocate, directing confessors that whoever denies the reigning pope's title is "not a Schismatic but a true HERETIC" — supports that verdict by citing "*Turrian. in suis selectis disput. part. 1. disp. 35. dub. 5*, where [he notes] that* ***this is now more certain than it was before the times of Clement VIII***." *The gloss is a direct paraphrase of this page, and the disputation number is exact; the "dub. 5" is a slip, since the Lyon 1634 printing gives Disp. XXXV a single* Dubium unicum. *Note also that Carena hardens the note* ***beyond*** *what Torres would say: Torres judges the denial indefensible* without scandal *and expressly finds no papal condemnation, where Carena thirty years on calls the denier a true heretic. The two entries together show the note tightening across a generation, and show where each author stopped.*

*Classification is* ***1a*** *(of faith of the singular, the contrary heresy, on the condition of election plus the Church's acceptance without any controversy — the exercise-condition expressly rejected). What sets the entry apart is that it is also the collection's* ***internal history of its own question***: *the only text that names the negative school's size, spread, chief authorities and Tridentine credentials; dates its decline to Clement VIII; names Suárez as the cause; and states, from inside the Inquisition, exactly how far the censure did and did not go.*

***The Calificator's own casebook — why the office is not merely a title.*** *The volume does not only call Torres a qualifier; it prints his qualifying. Disputation XXX,* De qualitate propositionum *(printed 146), sets out the theory of censure-grades, taking its list from the Council of Constance's condemnation of Wyclif — "**quasdam esse hæreticas, quasdam erroneas, alias scandalosas, alias blasphemas, quasdam piarum aurium offensiuas, alias temerarias, & seditiosas**." It closes with this hinge: "**Annexed to this disputation is what will be brought forward in the following censure on the quality, in particular, of certain propositions, concerning which I was at various times consulted**" —* de quibus aliquando interrogatus fui. *There follows a* ***Censura Theologica, per dubiorum centurias digesta***: *roughly* ***352 numbered propositions*** *across four "centuries" (printed 150–194), each a real consultation answered with its grade, and a closing note that these are censures of the same kind he assigned against* ***Michael Baius***. *So the man delivering the verdict of Disp. XXXV had a working practice of exactly this kind of judgment, and the book proves it.* ***Appendix A*** *gives the governing principle from Disp. XXX dub. 3 — the locus* ***Cardinal Manning*** *cites — where Torres holds that the Pontiff cannot err in* assigning *censures, not merely in defining* de fide, *and grounds it on indefectibility: the opinion the Pontiff approves as probable "**the whole Church is bound to embrace as such, and to reason from it to other matters**," so that were he to err "**all would embrace and defend that opinion, and would think it true, and its probability would grow from day to day, and consequently the whole Church would be deceived — which is by no reasoning to be admitted**." That is the same inference-structure as Disp. XXXV: the Church's dependence on a papal judgment is what forbids the judgment to fail.* ***Appendix B*** *gives four of the applied propositions (printed 157), of which the last is the sharpest: to assert that the Pontiff can err concerning the censures of propositions is "**impious, scandalous, rash, and erroneous**" — a four-term formula worth noting, since* ***Verricelli*** *(the 1653 entry) attributes just this vocabulary to Turrianus.*

***A citation cleared up — and a correction to the first version of this entry.*** *Verricelli cites Turrianus at* disp. 17. dub. 6. in fine, *Murga at* disputatione 55. dubio quinto, *Moraes at* disp. 14. dub. ult. & disp. 56. dub. 9. *None of these belongs to this volume; they belong to the 1621* In secundam secundae, *whose index has now been read. But they do not concern the singular pope either: Disp. XVII dub. VI is "***Qua certitudine sit credendum Pontificem non posse errare in beatificatione Sanctorum***" — Verricelli's "temerarious, scandalous and impious" is Torres's censure on denying papal inerrancy in* ***beatification***, *not on denying the reigning pope; Disp. LV dub. V is the conclusion "*Pontifex summus non posse errare in assignandis censuris propositionum*"; and Disp. XIV is on schismatics as members of the Church. The 1621 index carries* ***no dubium on whether it is of faith that this man is Pope***. *Version 1.0 of this entry said the fuller treatment lay in the 1621 tract* de regulis orthodoxis; *that was wrong. Disp. XXXV of 1634 is the only place Torres treats the question.*

***Note on the scan, and two open readings.*** *The source PDF is an MRC scan in which all text lives in a* ***lossy, symbol-coded JBIG2 bitonal mask***; *the JPEG-2000 layer carries background only, so there is* ***no independent image witness***. *Symbol-coding interchanges visually similar sorts, and this scan does so systematically for* ***u/n/o*** *and* ***t/r*** *(the mask "shows"* iorisdictionem, aotoritatem, gubernaodi, certitudioe, oumero, receprum, rotam, ranquam). *Every restoration below is either grammatically compelled or corroborated by the machine layer, and the full sic-notes are carried in both the Latin and the English files. Two readings remain genuinely open and are flagged there rather than silently resolved: (i) the last word of the treatise-sentence,* rem *("that* ***the thing*** *is not of faith") — the initial is a stem with a top-right arm which the mask encodes with the same symbol as* t, *though* rem *alone is grammatical without positing a lost word; and (ii) the Bañez clause, printed* & Bañes, ita docuit Cani discipulos, *where the natural sense requires the nominative* discipulus *("and Bañes, a disciple of Cano, so taught") — the two differ only by the documented u→o substitution, and the citation that follows is to Bañez's* own *commentary, not to anything he taught to others. One further caution, positively resolved: "**potestatem definiendi iurisdictionem in totam Ecclesiam**" has* ***no*** *"&" between* definiendi *and* iurisdictionem, *confirmed at pixel level and in the machine layer, so the asyndeton is the printer's and is kept.*

***Two loci this entry supplies to the collection.*** ***Vega*** *is now identified: the citation* libr. 9. in Tridentin. cap. 39 *— given identically by Francesco Albertini, Gravina, Gaspar Hurtado and Oviedo — is Andrés de Vega, O.F.M.,* Tridentini decreti de iustificatione expositio et defensio, libri XV *(1548), where* ***liber IX*** *expounds Trent sess. VI cap. 9 under the verso side-title* ***De incertitudine gratiae***, *and* ***cap. XXXIX stands at printed p. 199***. *The chapter is not about popes at all: it is the* ***epistemology of inferred certitude*** *the negative school borrows — whether a proposition drawn from one revealed and one evident premise is to be believed* ex fide *(printed 200), and the parallel that "no one can know evidently, or with certainty of faith, that he has been baptized or absolved" (printed 202). Vega supplies the machinery; Cano, Báñez and Córdoba transfer it to the papal singular.* ***Bellarmine — verified, and the citation is sound***. *Torres names him* ***first*** *among the negatives, at* De Romano Pontifice ***libr. 4, cap. 12***. *That chapter has now been read in the* ***Ingolstadt 1588 first edition*** *— the same edition this repository's [1588 Bellarmine](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1588%20Bellarmine) entry is drawn from. (The volume is numbered by* ***column***, *two numbered columns to the page; cap. XII runs cols. 1001–1005.) Its heading is "***De aliis septem Pontificibus***" — on seven further pontiffs alleged to have erred — and it closes with the Cadaver Synod. There Bellarmine writes of Stephen VI and Sergius III against Formosus: "***Respondeo, errasse Stephanum VI. & Sergium III. sed in quæstione facti, non iuris; & malo exemplo, non falsa doctrina***"; and then the sentence that earns him his place at the head of Torres's list: "***præcipua ergo quæstio fuit; an Formosus fuisset Papa legitimus, nec ne. in qualibus quæstionibus non negamus posse errare Pontifices, & de facto errasse Stephanum & Sergium***" — "the chief question, therefore, was* ***whether Formosus had been a legitimate Pope or not***. *And in questions of that kind* ***we do not deny that Pontiffs can err***, *and that in fact Stephen and Sergius did err." Bellarmine thus classes* whether N. is the legitimate pope *as a* ***quæstio facti*** *and concedes without reserve that the papal charism does not reach it — precisely the premise the negative school needs. What cap. 12 does* ***not*** *contain is the formula* non esse de fide *applied to that proposition; its three uses of* de fide *all concern doctrinal definition. Torres's citation is therefore* ***substantively accurate but not verbatim***: *he summarizes Bellarmine's* quæstio facti *doctrine in the later school's vocabulary, and this entry says so rather than pretending Bellarmine wrote the phrase.*

## Dubium unicum — the shared minimum, before the dispute begins (printed 218b)

**And among Catholics it is certain that it is not only temerarious and scandalous, but proximate to error, to deny that this-in-number man, received without controversy by the Church from the election, is the true Pontiff.**

The question, however, is whether it is **of faith** that this man is Pontiff, in such wise that the contrary is **heresy**.

## The census of the negative school, and its Tridentine credentials (printed 218b)

And indeed, **before Clement VIII, the common sense of the Scholastics, especially of those who committed their writings to print, was that it is not of faith**. And **nearly thirty authors** are cited by more recent writers, from the various Academies of the Church, **in Gaul, Germany, Italy, and Spain**, who so held — to say nothing of many others who so taught in their manuscripts. *Bellarmin. libr. 4. de Pontific. cap. 12.* Cano so taught, *6. de locis. cap. vltimo*, whom, as their master, most learned religious of the Dominican family have followed; and Bañes so taught, [a] disciple of Cano, *2. 2. ad quæstionem 1. in articulum 10, in prioribus commentarijs, dub. 2. ad 2.* And Vega, of the Franciscan family, *libr. 9. in Tridentin. cap. 39.* And who, together with Cano, **were present at the Council of Trent, where they came to know the mind of the Fathers of that Council**.

## The turn after Clement VIII, and its cause (printed 219a)

**But in this time, after Clement VIII, the common opinion is that it is of faith that one man in number — for example, Urban VIII — is the supreme Pontiff of the Church**: and the Doctors of this opinion speak **of a man received by the Church as Pontiff by common consent, and without any controversy**. The chief argument for this opinion is that from the contrary opinion it would follow that the Pontiff's definition concerning doctrine and the matter of faith does not have sufficient certitude, because it would not be certain that that definition proceeds from a true Pontiff, and from one having legitimate authority for defining and declaring matters of faith. From which doubt several absurdities could follow, and doubts of mind even in men who are pious and well affected toward the affairs of the Church. Which reason some more recent writers explain at length. **Let Fr. Suarez be read**, in the matter *de fide*, where he treats this doubt, and not a few others who have written after Clement VIII.

## His own adhesion, and the rejection of Valencia's exercise-condition (printed 219a)

**This latter opinion pleases me**, and its reason: because when a man has been **received by the Church without any controversy**, he receives from Christ the power of defining, jurisdiction over the whole Church, by Christ's institution; otherwise nothing would be firm in the Church from the Pontiff's determination or ordinance.

And therefore it is not necessary, as Fr. Valencia thought it necessary, that this man should define, or do something in relation to the whole Church, in order that he be held as a certain Pontiff; **but election alone, and the acceptance of the Church without any controversy, suffice**.

## The Eucharistic analogy broken on the effect (printed 219a)

And from this reasoning the reason of the contrary opinion is resolved: because in the Sacrament of the Eucharist there is nothing certain in particular from which we might gather, with the certitude of faith, that Christ is contained in this-in-number host; whereas **in our case, in the man thus elected there is the power of defining and of governing the whole Church. From which is gathered, with the certitude of faith, that the man thus elected is Pontiff.**

## The censure-history, and the final verdict (printed 219a)

Two things might briefly be asked. One is whether, before Clement VIII, the first opinion was probable. The other is whether, after Clement VIII, it remains probable.

As to the first: in the time of Clement, that opinion was judged by nearly all the learned men of Spain either true, or highly probable, and **deserving no censure**.

As to the second: one or another Doctor has judged it not to be probable. Others, however, have commonly taught the contrary, on the ground that there appears **neither a condemnation by a Pontiff, nor a prohibition of his doctrine**, such that each one may pass judgment from general principles. **But I judge that at this time it cannot be defended without scandal that the thing is not of faith.**

## Appendix A — Disp. XXX, Dub. III: the Pontiff cannot err in *assigning* censures (printed 149–150)

**DOUBT III.** *Whether the Church can err in assigning the censures of propositions.*

Bañez, *2. 2. q. 11. in fine art. 2.*, teaches that it is an error, or proximate to error, to assert that the Church can err in assigning these censures. **To me indeed it is exceedingly certain that the Pontiff cannot err in these censures**: for it is certain that he cannot err in defining the truths of faith, as I have said; but to assign these censures pertains to the truths of faith [...] **and so I judge it to be an error to assert that the Pontiff can err in these censures.**

**And I judge that the Pontiff cannot err in assigning this censure** [that a proposition is *probable*], for this could tend to the detriment of faith, **because the opinion which the Pontiff approves as probable, or as more probable, the whole Church is bound to embrace as such, and to reason from it to other matters — whence a grave error concerning matters of faith could arise.** [...] For if in this the Pontiff could err, great detriment could follow in the Church, **for all would embrace and defend that opinion, and would think it true, and its probability would grow from day to day, and consequently the whole Church would be deceived — which is by no reasoning to be admitted.**

Annexed to this disputation is what will be brought forward in the following censure on the quality, in particular, of certain propositions, **concerning which I was at various times consulted**.

## Appendix B — the *Censura Theologica*: the propositions concerning the Pontiff (printed 157)

**DOUBT LXVI.** *Whether it deserves censure to assert that the Pontiff can err concerning the censures of propositions.* — I answer that **it is certain that the Pontiff cannot err in assigning the censure of any proposition, because in matters pertaining to doctrine he cannot err; and to assert the opposite is impious, scandalous, rash, and erroneous.**

**DOUBT LXII.** *Whether it deserves a grave censure to assert that the Pontiff can err in the approbation of a religious order.* — I answer that it deserves a grave censure, **not only of rashness, but also of error**.

**DOUBT LXV.** *Whether it deserves censure to assert that the Pontiff can err in approving some special rule concerning some special act in a religious order.* — I answer that it deserves the censure of rashness and of error, **because he cannot approve either false doctrine for true doctrine, nor a base act for an honest one**.
