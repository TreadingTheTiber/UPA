*Note on this entry: Francesco Bordoni (1595–1671) of Parma, of the Franciscan Third Order Regular, was a consultor of the Inquisition, and the* Sacrum Tribunal *(Rome 1648) is a working manual for judges in causes of faith — which makes him the collection's clearest specimen of the doctrine operating as* ***inquisitorial law*** *rather than school-theology. Four quaestiones are given whole. The centerpiece, cap. V Quaeres 6, asks by name "whether it is to be believed de fide that Innocent X is the true Roman Pontiff — which same thing is asked concerning each one individually," and answers (n. 16) that it is de fide that any canonical elect "de quo constet" is true pope, "and accordingly that such now is Innocent the Tenth,* ***elected Pope on the 15th day of September 1644, and crowned on the fourth day of October of the same year***" — the doctrine applied with notarial precision to dated facts. His third proof is the UPA argument in full ritual dress: "what is believed, held, and solemnized by the whole Church is said to be de fide… **the Church crowned him through the deputed Cardinals, and recognized him as such by receiving his Jubilee and indulgences; and he is solemnized in the prayer of the Mass and in other rites, and all call him Pope**." The fourth proof grounds it formally: the faith by which we believe the definer to be pope cannot be human faith, "which is uncertain and inconstant… **therefore it is de fide that this man is the true Pontiff**"; the fifth, from the assistance of the Holy Spirit, requires all three acts of believing to be "specified by the same divine revelation." Two boundary-rulings follow that the repository's classification framework prizes. First, the* ***completeness clause*** *stated as law: the thesis "supposes that the election of one man as Pontiff is established; for if the validity of the election is in question, or several have been elected, there is no obligation of believing" until a council determines — and cap. VII Quaeres 5 applies it: one who withholds recognition from a claimant "whom he probably thinks not to have been canonically elected"* ***is not a schismatic***, *and where plural claimants exist both are held as doubtful and "the judgment of a General Council is to be awaited." Second, the* ***coronation terminus*** *(n. 19), against faction-rumors of simony or defective vote-counts "at the beginning of someone's election": "***once the coronation of the Supreme Pontiff has been carried out, it is to be held de fide by all that the man crowned is the true Pope; for rumor, suspicions, and the lies of detracting men are not to be heeded***" — the earliest fixed terminus-date for the completed acceptance in the collection. The Dices-1 objection block answers the singulars objection (ignorance of the head is "the greatest absurdity… not to be able with certainty to point him out with the finger") and the baptism/requisites objection ("those other propositions are to be believed de fide* ***concomitantly***") — cf. the intention census in the root README — and n. 20 closes: the schismatic who disbelieves the unity of the head "is a heretic in this sense." Cap. II Quaeres 13 supplies the methodological frame: in his list of theological loci, the* ***consensus of the whole Church is a distinct locus (the fourth), "equivalent to the definitions of the Supreme Pontiff"*** *— and even short of definition its opponent is "most vehemently suspect." Cap. VI Quaeres 20 gives the reception-obligation for councils: all the faithful — "even the Supreme Pontiff himself," de iure divino — are bound to accept conciliar definitions of faith, the Fathers representing the whole Church and princes accepting "for themselves and their subjects." Bordoni appears in Diana's affirmative census (reported in the repo's 1684 Murga entry, n. 79). Classification is* ***1a*** *(de fide by divine faith, with the completeness clause and coronation terminus as its juridical edges).*

## Cap. V, Quaeres 6 — *An de fide credendum sit, Innocentium X. esse verum Pontificem Romanum? quod idem de singulis quęritur?*

**16.** *Resp.* **It is to be held *de fide* that anyone canonically elected Supreme Pontiff, about whom this is established, is a true Pope; and accordingly that such now is Innocent the Tenth, elected Pope on the 15th day of September 1644, and crowned on the fourth day of October of the same year.** [...]

Thirdly, it is proved, because **what is believed, held, and solemnized by the whole Church is said to be *de fide*; but all the faithful believe in the Supreme Pontiff, confessing that Innocent X is now the true Pope, for the Church crowned him through the deputed Cardinals, and recognized him as such by receiving his Jubilee and indulgences; and he is solemnized in the prayer of the Mass and in other rites, and all call him Pope; therefore this is to be believed *de fide*, for those things are known to be *de fide* which are embraced in the whole Church.** [...]

**18.** Fourthly, [...] **the foundation of believing that the Pope defines a proposition previously uncertain as of faith to be certain *de fide*, without erring in its truth and determination, consists in this, that the person defining it *de fide* is believed to be the true successor of Peter and a real Pope; but this belief cannot be had by human faith, which is uncertain and inconstant; therefore by divine faith; therefore it is *de fide* that this man is the true Pontiff** [...]

For the truth of this opinion, however, it is supposed that **the election of one man as Pontiff is established; for if the validity of the election is in question, or several have been elected Pontiff, there is no obligation of believing, but only then, when it is determined by some Council that this man, and not that one, is the true Pope** [...]

**19.** **I think that, once the coronation of the Supreme Pontiff has been carried out, it is to be held *de fide* by all that the man crowned is the true Pope; for rumor, suspicions, and the lies of detracting men are not to be heeded, since Schismatics are wont, in each and every election of a new Pontiff, to stir up dissensions and divisions, because such is the conventicle of impious men.** [...]

*To the first*, that there is a broad difference, for not to believe those singular things brings in no inconvenience, but **it is the greatest absurdity not to know the head of the Church, or not to be able with certainty to point him out with the finger without any controversy** [...] **20.** Nay, I say that **the Schismatic is a heretic in this sense of disbelieving the unity of the head.**

## Cap. II, Quaeres 13 (the theological loci)

**45.** **The fourth is from the consensus of the whole Church, for this consensus is equivalent to the definitions of the Supreme Pontiff, and argues that an opinion embraced by the whole Church is true, infallible, and undoubted.** But if it has not yet been defined as to be held *de fide*, one who opposes it is not proved formally a Heretic, but is nevertheless **most vehemently suspect**.

## Cap. VII, Quaeres 5 (the completeness clause)

**8.** *Resp.* **He is not a Schismatic who does not recognize as Pope one whom he probably thinks not to have been canonically elected Pope**, because it is withdrawal and rebellion from the true Pope that makes Schism. But if there are several Popes, and it is not known which of them is the true Pope [...] each is to be held, as doubtful, for the Vicar of Christ on earth [...] **the judgment of a General Council is to be awaited, to which the decision of this controversy pertains.**
