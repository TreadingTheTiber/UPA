*Note on this entry: two witnesses share it. **Diego de Simancas** (Córdoba, 29 January 1513 – 16 October 1583), doctor of both laws, consultor of the Inquisition at Valladolid and Córdoba while still young (as he says of himself at cap. 41), afterwards a member of the Council of the Supreme Inquisition and bishop of Ciudad Rodrigo, Badajoz and Zamora, wrote the* De catholicis institutionibus *as a working manual for the tribunals (Valladolid 1552; revised, Rome 1575), and reduced it to the* Theorice et praxis haereseos, *or Enchiridion of judges of violated religion. The 1640 Portuguese Inquisition made it required reading. **Francesco Castracani**, doctor of both laws, canon of the cathedral of Ferrara, perpetual vicar of the priory of S. Romano Martire and* ***Consultor of the Inquisition***, *gathered Simancas's scattered works into one folio (Ferrara, Pomatelli, 1692), "illustrated them with notes," added the recent constitutions for the tribunal, and dedicated the volume to Cardinal Flavio Chigi. His notes are keyed to Simancas's paragraph numbers and were written before October 1689: at tit. XLV n. 18 he counts "six Supreme Pontiffs" elected under Gregory XV's conclave rite, Urban VIII to "Innocent XI, a Pontiff of admirable piety, whom may God deign long to preserve." Nothing more is known of him here than the title page states; he is not the canonist of the same name who published on Curial offices in 1590.*

***Why this entry matters: a name on the negative roll-call, read.*** *Simancas is a fixture of the lists of authors who deny that the singular pope is of faith. His sentence, at tit. LVIII n. 9, does say that "although we are bound to believe that the true successor of Peter is the supreme Prince of the Catholic Church, we are nevertheless not bound by the same faith to believe that this or that man is the true successor of Peter" — but the sentence continues, "****when it is doubted with great probability whether he was rightly elected****. For those things do not pertain to the Catholic faith which depend on a doubtful fact, but those which arise from certain right." And the paragraph it closes is about the third kind of schismatic, the orthodox who "led by probable or invincible ignorance, think someone to be Supreme Pontiff who has not been rightly elected; which is wont to happen* ***when, on account of an ambiguous election, two or more contend for the Pontificate****, and some in good faith hold one of them for the true Pontiff." His authorities are Gerson on how to behave in time of schism, Torquemada, Alvaro Pelayo, Castro and Jacobazzi — the literature of the Great Schism. The Enchiridion says the same in fewer words: "when it is doubted with great probability which of them was more rightly elected … these men can be free of all fault, and are not truly schismatics." That is the case [Albizzi](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1683%20Albizzi) would later set out as the first two rungs of his ladder; Simancas, writing in 1552, does not reach the third. He affirms the office without qualification at tit. XLV n. 2 — "we constantly assert, and confess with the Catholic Church, that all Supreme Pontiffs rightly and justly elected are Vicars of Christ under heaven, and legitimate successors of Peter" — and says nothing of the pope received by the whole Church. What the roll-calls report as a negative is a limited statement about the contested election.*

***The annotator supplies the missing clause (note 9).*** *Castracani, a consultor of the same tribunal a hundred and forty years on, glosses the sentence: "It is disputed among theologians whether it is certain, with the certitude of faith beneath which falsehood can never lie, that this individual Supreme Pontiff is the true Pontiff.* ***The truer opinion is that which, speaking of the Supreme Pontiff after he has been received by the universal Church, affirms it****, which Simancas seems here to confirm, since, asserting that we are not bound by the same faith by which we believe the true successor of Peter to be the supreme Prince of the Catholic Church to believe this or that man to be the true successor of Peter, he adds:* When it is doubted with great probability whether he was rightly elected*.* ***For after the Supreme Pontiff has been received by the unanimous consent of the Church, all doubt about his right election is removed****. See Castro Palao, tract. 4 de Fide disp. 1 punct. 5 § 2 n. 9." Castro Palao is the authority [Salelles](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1651%20Salelles) follows for the same rule. The reading is fair to the text: Simancas's limiting clause is exactly the condition whose removal by universal reception Castracani asserts.*

***The rest of tit. LVIII.*** *Schism is "the sister of heresy," "the greatest evil, once punished by God himself more severely than parricide and more grievously than idolatry" (Dathan and Abiram, Core). Three kinds of schismatic: consummate heretics, who hold that departure from the pope is lawful; those "bordering on heretics," who keep the rule of faith but "desire to enlarge their own borders"; and the orthodox in a doubtful election. The second kind "differ little from heretics; for … by the fact itself they openly show that they believe … that the Supreme Pontiff is not to be obeyed; that the unity of the Church can be rent," whence Clement V's "an inveterate schism is heresy," and they are punished with almost the same penalties. The title closes with the Theodosian law banishing schismatics from Rome, Christ's prayer "that they may be one," and the sectarian confusion of the new heretics, "so that in one house you may find various faiths, the husband believing one thing, the wife another." Castracani's other notes correct the gravity claim (schism is graver than idolatry only by circumstance), record that schism "does not always involve an error of the intellect," and add that the reconciled schismatic remains irregular.*

***Castracani on the succession (note 18).*** *Beside Simancas's remark that some popes were designated by their predecessors, Castracani reviews the modes of election from Clement, "indeed designated by Peter" but ascending only after Linus and Cletus, through the Roman clergy, the Arian and Gothic kings, Symmachus, Stephen IV, Nicholas II, Alexander III, Innocent III and Gregory X, to "Gregory XV's constitution," under which "the election of the Supreme Pontiff is made only by secret scrutiny, with two-thirds of the votes of the Cardinals present in conclave"; and "no Supreme Pontiff can decree concerning the election of his successor, except by way of exhortation and counsel." The paragraph belongs beside [Bartis](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1731%20Bartis) § 57 and [Dens](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1829%20Dens) N. 93 on election as the condition and not the source of the office.*

***Classification 1a*** *on the annotator's verdict, with Simancas's own text recorded as a limited negative for the case of a contested election, which asserts nothing about the pope received by the whole Church. The entry counts two theologians. It is the collection's fourth inquisitorial witness, and the earliest text among them; read with Albizzi and Salelles it shows the manuals' negative tradition to be a tradition about the Great Schism.*

## De Catholicis Institutionibus, tit. LVIII n. 9 — the limited negative (printed 396)

**9** There are others who are schismatics, who are neither heretics nor incur any penalty of law, because they have a just excuse and are without fault. These are they who, led by probable or invincible ignorance, think someone to be Supreme Pontiff who has not been rightly elected; **which is wont to happen when, on account of an ambiguous election, two or more contend for the Pontificate**, and some in good faith hold one of them for the true Pontiff, who nevertheless has not been justly elected. For although we are bound to believe that the true successor of Peter is the supreme Prince of the Catholic Church, we are nevertheless not bound by the same faith to believe that this or that man is the true successor of Peter, **when it is doubted with great probability whether he was rightly elected**. For those things do not pertain to the Catholic faith which depend on a doubtful fact, but those which arise from certain right. [...]

## Castracani, Annotationes on tit. LVIII, note 9 (printed 398)

**9** *There are others, etc.* It is disputed among theologians whether it is certain, with the certitude of faith beneath which falsehood can never lie, that this individual Supreme Pontiff is the true Pontiff. **The truer opinion is that which, speaking of the Supreme Pontiff after he has been received by the universal Church, affirms it**, which Simancas seems here to confirm, since, asserting that we are not bound by the same faith by which we believe the true successor of Peter to be the supreme Prince of the Catholic Church to believe this or that man to be the true successor of Peter, he adds: *When it is doubted with great probability whether he was rightly elected.* **For after the Supreme Pontiff has been received by the unanimous consent of the Church, all doubt about his right election is removed.** See Castropal. tract. 4. de Fide disput. 1. punct. 5. §. 2. num. 9.

## Theorice et Praxis, tit. V n. 3 (printed 462)

**3** For although we are bound to believe that the Supreme Pontiff is the Vicar of Christ, we are nevertheless not bound by the same faith to believe that this or that man is Pope, when it is doubted with great probability which of them was more rightly elected; for those things do not pertain to the Catholic faith which depend on a doubtful fact: and so **these men can be free of all fault, and are not truly schismatics.**
