*Note on this entry: Francesco Albizzi (Cesena, 24 October 1593 – Rome, 5 October 1684), doctor in both laws of Bologna and its professor of civil and canon law from 1611, was called to Rome by Urban VIII and made* ***Assessor of the Holy Office*** *on 18 July 1635, the post he held through the Jansenist crisis until Innocent X created him Cardinal Priest of S. Prassede on 2 March 1654; he sat in the Congregation to the end of his life. The* De inconstantia in iure admittenda vel non *was planned in five tracts; only the first,* De inconstantia hominum circa virtutes fidei, spei et charitatis*, appeared in his lifetime (Amsterdam 1683), the second posthumously (1698). It is a canonist's treatise written from the Assessor's desk: cap. X cites Holy Office resolutions by date (1604, 1607, 1631, 1634, 1639, 1655) and pauses at n. 15 to record that "while I was writing this" Innocent X had suspended the Archbishop of Mechelen and the Bishop of Ghent for refusing to appear at Rome, the Jansenist affair of 1652–53. Its authorities on the papal question are the two he cites for nn. 11–12,* ***Suárez*** *(De fide disp. 10 sect. 5 n. 6) and* ***Pueroni*** *(Commentaria in Bullam Coenae, 1666, n. 241), with [Carena](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1668%20Carena), Del Bene, Santarelli, Farinacci and de Lugo throughout.*

***Why this entry matters.*** *The inquisitorial literature has been read as a store of negatives on the papal question: Simancas, Locati and Del Bene are quoted for the proposition that one who doubts the pope's election is no schismatic. Albizzi, the ranking practitioner of the corpus, sets the whole question out as a* ***ladder of cases***, *and the acceptance threshold is its hinge. The received conclusion that a probable doubter is not a schismatic (n. 9) is "approved by the reckoning of most learned men," but "is nevertheless to be understood with a grain of salt." Then three cases (nn. 10–12): (i) two elected pontiffs, schism pending, "until it has been defined by the Church whom one must obey" — no schism, "for such a man does not refuse to be subject to the Pontiff, but to this person, whom he thinks not to be Pontiff" (Simancas, Torquemada); (ii) a single elected pope, doubt arising at once, "and on that account he is not received by the Church" — no schism, on Suárez: "****until the Pontiff has been accepted by the whole Church, it is not of faith that this man is truly Pontiff, and that he is to be obeyed****"; (iii) "****a Pontiff elected and received by the universal Church; then, since it is not only a probable opinion but of faith that he is the legitimate Pontiff, it is a crime to withhold obedience from him without the vice of schism****." The Summarium states the same in headline form: "Obedience is not owed to a man as Pontiff, nor as such of faith, until he has been accepted by the whole Church"; "A legitimate Pontiff is to be believed of faith, who is elected as such and received by the universal Church, and obedience cannot be denied him without the vice of schism." Every negative in the manuals, on this reading, is a statement about cases (i) and (ii); the affirmative is the rule for case (iii). The doubter in the two doubtful cases is, moreover, bound to inquire: one "who did not employ diligence in inquiring who is the true Supreme Pontiff would be reputed a schismatic."*

***Where heresy begins (n. 21).*** *Schismatics "are almost always heretics, and if they are not formally such, they are nonetheless suspect of heresy." Heresy proper is the denial of the office: "if anyone, together with schism, should believe that the Roman Pontiff is not the Head of the Church, nor the Vicar of Christ, he would assuredly be a heretic" (Castro, Sylvester, Suárez). The denial of* this *occupant, once received, is schism (n. 12); the denial of the office is heresy (n. 21). Albizzi does not say the denier of the received pope is a heretic, and this is the settled canonists' line that Carena's "verus haereticus" for the pope in peaceful possession goes beyond.*

***Common reputation and the law Barbarius (n. 32).*** *In the section on privileges obtained from schismatic popes: where "one man alone should bear himself as Pope … in some Region, who, although he were a schismatic, or invalidly elected, would nevertheless there be held by common reputation for the true Pope," his acts stand, "because for the validity of acts common reputation suffices,* l. Barbarius*, whose provision holds even in the Pope." This is the effect-guarantee of the collection's School 2 stated as positive law, sitting a few pages after the fact-guarantee of n. 12; Albizzi holds both without tension, the one as a rule of faith for the received pope, the other as a rule of validity for acts done under a reputed one. Compare [Bartis](https://github.com/TreadingTheTiber/UPA/tree/main/1731%20Bartis) § 67, who denies that* Barbarius *reaches the Supreme Pastor.*

***"Not the See but this person" (n. 36).*** *Under the Bulla Coenae's excommunication of schismatics, Albizzi adopts de Lugo's reading: it strikes not only those "who elect another pseudo-pontiff," but also those "who, without the election of another Pontiff, separate themselves from obedience to the true Pontiff," and — Lugo "believes" — "****those who, cunningly trying to veil their malice, say that they do not deny obedience to the Apostolic See, but to this person for particular reasons, while nevertheless they will afterwards render obedience to his Successor****," which is why the bull reads "who withdraw themselves from obedience to us and to any Pontiff for the time being." The move that cases (i) and (ii) permit for a claimant not yet received is here named as a pretext when made against the received one.*

***Classification 1a, by adoption.*** *Albizzi does not argue the thesis; he receives it from Suárez and Pueroni as settled and builds the canonical consequences on it. The clause is the acceptance clause in its ordinary form, "ab universali Ecclesia receptus," and the negative conditions he allows are the completeness conditions of the census: two claimants, or a single claimant not yet received. He is the collection's second inquisitorial-manual witness after Carena, and the one who most completely states the division that the manuals' apparent negatives have been read without.*

## Summarium 9–12 (printed 45)

9. One denying subjection to the Pontiff because he with probability doubts his canonical election — whether, and when, he can be called a schismatic.

10. One refusing to obey one of two elected Pontiffs, while the schism is pending, until it is defined by the Church whom one must obey — whether, and when, he is called a schismatic.

11. One doubtful of the valid election of a Pope not yet received by the Church on account of that same doubt — whether, and when, he is called a schismatic. **Obedience is not owed to a man as Pontiff, nor as such of faith, until he has been accepted by the whole Church.**

12. **A legitimate Pontiff is to be believed of faith, who is elected as such and received by the universal Church. And obedience cannot be denied him without the vice of schism.**

## nn. 9–12 — the ladder (printed 47)

**9** Nor will he be a schismatic who denies subjection to the Pontiff because with probability he doubts his legitimate election [...]. But although this conclusion has been approved by the reckoning of most learned men, it is nevertheless **to be understood with a grain of salt.**

**10** For either we are in a schism on account of the election of two Pontiffs, and then the doubt is lawful as to which of them is to be obeyed, until it has been defined by the Church whom one must obey, and therefore he will not be a schismatic who acknowledges neither of them as Supreme Pontiff: **for such a man does not refuse to be subject to the Pontiff, but to this person, whom he thinks not to be Pontiff** [...].

**11** Or we are dealing with a single elected Pope, but immediately upon his election it happens that it is doubted whether he was canonically elected, and on that account he is not received by the Church. And in this case also it must be said that such a man, who together with others doubts about the valid election of the Pope, is not a schismatic, by reason of what Suárez hands down [...] where he says that **until the Pontiff has been accepted by the whole Church, it is not of faith that this man is truly Pontiff, and that he is to be obeyed.**

**12** Or we are dealing with **a Pontiff elected and received by the universal Church; then, since it is not only a probable opinion but of faith that he is the legitimate Pontiff** (Pueroni), **it is a crime to withhold obedience from him without the vice of schism** (Lugo). Indeed, in the two cases mentioned above, one who did not employ diligence in inquiring who is the true Supreme Pontiff would be reputed a schismatic [...].

## n. 36 — "not the See but this person" (printed 51)

Nay more, Cardinal de Lugo adds that he believes there are comprehended also **those who, cunningly trying to veil their malice, say that they do not deny obedience to the Apostolic See, but to this person for particular reasons, while nevertheless they will afterwards render obedience to his Successor**: and that for this reason those words were added: *Who withdraw themselves from obedience to us and to any Pontiff for the time being.*
